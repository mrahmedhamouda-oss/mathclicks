Core Concepts

1. What is an inscribed angle?

An inscribed angle is an angle whose vertex sits ON the circle and whose two sides are chords of that circle. Compare that with a central angle, whose vertex sits at the centre.

inscribed angle — an angle that has its vertex on a circle and sides that contain chords of the circle. In ☉C, ∠QRS is an inscribed angle.
intercepted arc — the part of a circle that lies between the two lines intersecting it. Arc QS is intercepted by ∠QRS.
inscribed polygon — a polygon all of whose vertices lie on a circle. Inscribed triangles and quadrilaterals have special properties.
central angle — vertex at the centre. Its measure equals its arc; an inscribed angle is only half its arc.
How to spot the intercepted arc: stand at the vertex, look out along the two sides. The piece of circle you can "see" between them — the piece that does not contain the vertex — is the intercepted arc.

2. There are three ways an angle can be inscribed

The centre of the circle can land in three different places relative to the angle. The theorem below is true in all three cases.

  • Case 1 — the centre P is on a side of the inscribed angle.
  • Case 2 — the centre P is inside the inscribed angle.
  • Case 3 — the centre P is in the exterior of the inscribed angle.
Theorem 10.7 — Inscribed Angle Theorem
If an angle is inscribed in a circle, then the measure of the angle equals one half the measure of its intercepted arc.
m∠1 = ½ · m(arc AB)
m(arc AB) = 2 · m∠1
Why it works (Case 1). Put the centre P on one side, so that side is a diameter. Draw the radius to the far vertex — it creates an isosceles triangle (two radii are equal), so its two base angles are both equal to the inscribed angle x. The exterior angle of that triangle at the centre is therefore x + x = 2x. But that central angle is the arc. So arc = 2·angle, i.e. angle = ½·arc.
Theorem 10.8 — Congruent Inscribed Angles
If two inscribed angles of a circle intercept the same arc or congruent arcs, then the angles are congruent.
∠B and ∠C both intercept arc AD  ∴  ∠B ≅ ∠C
This falls straight out of Theorem 10.7: both angles equal half of the same number, so they must be equal. It is what turns a picture into an equation such as 5x − 12 = 3x + 2.
Theorem 10.9 — Semicircles and Right Angles
An inscribed angle of a triangle intercepts a diameter or semicircle if and only if the angle is a right angle.
If arc FJH is a semicircle, then m∠G = 90°.
If m∠G = 90°, then arc FJH is a semicircle and FH is a diameter.
A semicircle measures 180°, and half of 180° is 90°. Because it is an if and only if, you may run it in either direction: diameter → right angle, and right angle → diameter.
Theorem 10.10 — Inscribed Quadrilaterals
If a quadrilateral is inscribed in a circle, then its opposite angles are supplementary.
KLMN inscribed in ☉A  ⇒  m∠K + m∠M = 180°  and  m∠L + m∠N = 180°
Reason: two opposite angles intercept the two arcs that together make up the whole circle, 360°. Half of 360° is 180°.
1
Angle = half arc. Every single problem in this lesson is this one sentence used forwards or backwards.
2
Same arc ⇒ equal angles. Two vertices on the circle looking at the same chord see the same angle.
3
Diameter ⇒ 90°. An angle standing on a diameter is a right angle, so the other two angles of that triangle are complementary.
4
Cyclic quadrilateral ⇒ opposite angles add to 180°. Not adjacent angles — opposite ones.
Watch Out! Do not confuse the two directions of the theorem. Given the arc, you divide by 2. Given the angle, you multiply by 2. Writing "arc = ½ angle" is the single most common error in this lesson.
Study Tip: if a central angle and an inscribed angle intercept the same arc, the central angle is exactly twice the inscribed angle.

In ☉O, CE is a diameter, m(arc DE) = 98° and m∠FEC = 40°.
Find (a) m(arc CF)  and  (b) m∠DCE.
1m(arc CF) = 2 · m∠FECInscribed Angle Theorem
2= 2 · 40 = 80Substitute and simplify
3m∠DCE = ½ m(arc DE)Inscribed Angle Theorem
4= ½ (98) = 49Substitute and simplify
m(arc CF) = 80°   and   m∠DCE = 49°
Check: the whole circle is arc CF + arc FE + arc ED + arc DC = 80 + 100 + 98 + 82 = 360 ✓
Trap: ∠FEC is an inscribed angle, not a central angle, so its arc is double it (80°), never equal to it.

Check: m∠D = 3(7) + 2 = 23° ✓   Both angles are 23°, so arc BC = 46°.

Quadrilateral DEFG is inscribed in a circle, with
m∠D = (5y + 16)°, m∠E = (3x − 16)°, m∠F = (3y + 4)°, m∠G = (x − 4)°.
Find m∠F and m∠G.
Set-up: because DEFG is inscribed in a circle, its opposite angles are supplementary. The opposite pairs are ∠D & ∠F and ∠E & ∠G — not ∠D & ∠E.

Step 1 — Find m∠F

m∠D + m∠F = 180
(5y + 16) + (3y + 4) = 180
8y + 20 = 180
8y = 160
y = 20
m∠F = 3(20) + 4 = 64

Step 2 — Find m∠G

m∠G + m∠E = 180
(x − 4) + (3x − 16) = 180
4x − 20 = 180
4x = 200
x = 50
m∠G = 50 − 4 = 46
m∠F = 64°   and   m∠G = 46°
Check: m∠D = 5(20) + 16 = 116° and m∠E = 3(50) − 16 = 134°. Then 116 + 64 = 180 ✓, 134 + 46 = 180 ✓, and 116 + 134 + 64 + 46 = 360 ✓

In ☉O, ∠MNP is inscribed and m(arc MP) = 138°. Find m∠MNP.

2= ½ (138)Substitute

Practice 2 — congruent inscribed angles

∠A and ∠B are inscribed in the same circle and both intercept arc CD. If m∠A = (4x + 9)° and m∠B = (6x − 7)°, find x and m∠A.
14x + 9 = 6x − 7Theorem 10.8: ∠A ≅ ∠B
216 = 2x  →  x = 8Solve
3m∠A = 4(8) + 9 = 41Substitute
x = 8, m∠A = 41° (and arc CD = 82°)

Practice 3 — right triangle in a circle

△RST is inscribed in a circle and RT is a diameter. If m∠R = (3x + 7)° and m∠T = (2x + 8)°, find m∠T.
1m∠S = 90°∠S intercepts a semicircle (Thm 10.9)
2(3x + 7) + (2x + 8) = 90Acute ∠s are complementary
35x + 15 = 90 → 5x = 75 → x = 15Solve
4m∠T = 2(15) + 8 = 38Substitute
m∠T = 38°  (check: m∠R = 52°, 52 + 38 = 90 ✓)

Practice 4 — inscribed quadrilateral

Quadrilateral WXYZ is inscribed in a circle. If m∠W = (2y + 15)° and m∠Y = (3y + 15)°, find m∠Y.
1m∠W + m∠Y = 180Opposite ∠s of a cyclic quad (Thm 10.10)
2(2y + 15) + (3y + 15) = 180Substitution
35y + 30 = 180 → 5y = 150 → y = 30Solve
4m∠Y = 3(30) + 15 = 105Substitute
m∠Y = 105°  (check: m∠W = 75°, 75 + 105 = 180 ✓)

Practice 5 — central and inscribed together

In ☉O, central ∠AOC = (4x)° and inscribed ∠ABC intercepts the same arc AC with m∠ABC = (x + 15)°. Find x, m∠ABC and m(arc AC).
1m(arc AC) = m∠AOC = 4xCentral angle equals its arc
2x + 15 = ½ (4x)Inscribed Angle Theorem
3x + 15 = 2x  →  x = 15Solve
4m∠ABC = 30, m(arc AC) = 60Substitute
x = 15, m∠ABC = 30°, m(arc AC) = 60°

Practice 6 — multi-step

△ABC is inscribed in a circle so that m(arc AB) = 100° and m(arc BC) = 140°. Find m∠A, m∠B and m∠C.
1m(arc CA) = 360 − 100 − 140 = 120Arcs of a circle sum to 360°
2m∠A = ½(140) = 70∠A intercepts arc BC
3m∠B = ½(120) = 60∠B intercepts arc CA
4m∠C = ½(100) = 50∠C intercepts arc AB
m∠A = 70°, m∠B = 60°, m∠C = 50°
Check: 70 + 60 + 50 = 180° ✓ — the angles of the inscribed triangle are exactly half of the three arcs, which sum to 360°.

Common mistakes to avoid:
  • Treating an inscribed angle like a central angle (forgetting the ½).
  • Doubling when you should halve. Arc is always the bigger number.
  • Adding adjacent angles of a cyclic quadrilateral to 180° instead of opposite ones.
  • Assuming a chord is a diameter without being told — you need the right angle or the word "diameter"/"semicircle".
  • Using the arc that contains the vertex. The intercepted arc never contains the vertex.
  • Forgetting that a major arc is 360° − minor arc before halving it.
