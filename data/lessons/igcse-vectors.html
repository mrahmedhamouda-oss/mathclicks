A vector carries two pieces of information at once — how far, and which way. Everything on this page grows from one rule: $\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}$, "end point minus start point". Add and scale vectors in the first lab, then use the route method to write any vector in a diagram in terms of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ — which is where the Paper 4 marks live.

◎ What you will be able to do

  • Read and write vectors in all three notations.
  • Add, subtract and scale column vectors.
  • Find the magnitude of a vector with Pythagoras.
  • Use position vectors, and find a vector by walking a route through a diagram.
  • Handle midpoints and points that divide a line in a given ratio.
  • Prove that two lines are parallel, and that three points are collinear.

1 Vector notation

A vector has both a size (magnitude) and a direction. A scalar has size only. "5 km north" is a vector; "5 km" is a scalar.

NotationExampleMeaning
Column vector$\begin{pmatrix}3\\-2\end{pmatrix}$$3$ in the $x$ direction, $2$ in the negative $y$ direction
Single bold letter$\mathbf{a}$A named vector, used for algebra
Two points with an arrow$\overrightarrow{AB}$The vector that takes you from $A$ to $B$
!
Handwriting matters. In print a vector is bold, but you cannot write bold — underline the letter instead. An unmarked letter is read as a scalar.

2 Adding, subtracting and scaling Interactive

Work component by component — top with top, bottom with bottom.

$\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}c\\d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a+c\\b+d\end{pmatrix}\qquad k\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}ka\\kb\end{pmatrix}$

Geometrically you add nose to tail: draw $\mathbf{a}$, start $\mathbf{b}$ where $\mathbf{a}$ finished, and the vector from the start of $\mathbf{a}$ to the end of $\mathbf{b}$ is $\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}$. Subtracting is adding the reverse: $\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{a}+(-\mathbf{b})$.

Parallel test. Two vectors are parallel exactly when one is a scalar multiple of the other: if $\mathbf{v}=k\mathbf{u}$ then $\mathbf{v}$ is parallel to $\mathbf{u}$.

3 Magnitude

The magnitude (or modulus) of a vector is its length, and it comes straight from Pythagoras.

$\left|\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}\right|=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}$
VectorWorkingMagnitude
$\begin{pmatrix}3\\4\end{pmatrix}$$\sqrt{9+16}=\sqrt{25}$$5$
$\begin{pmatrix}-5\\12\end{pmatrix}$$\sqrt{25+144}=\sqrt{169}$$13$
$\begin{pmatrix}6\\-8\end{pmatrix}$$\sqrt{36+64}=\sqrt{100}$$10$
$\begin{pmatrix}2\\3\end{pmatrix}$$\sqrt{4+9}=\sqrt{13}$$3.61$ (3 s.f.)
!
Squaring removes the minus sign, so a magnitude is never negative. A negative answer means a slip in the arithmetic.
✎
Order of operations. For $|3\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}|$, do the vector arithmetic first to reach a single column vector, and only then take the magnitude. $|3\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}|$ is not $3|\mathbf{a}|-|\mathbf{b}|$.

4 Position vectors and finding routes Interactive

The position vector of a point $A$ is $\overrightarrow{OA}$, the vector from the origin to $A$. If $A$ is $(3,-2)$ its position vector is $\begin{pmatrix}3\\-2\end{pmatrix}$.

$\overrightarrow{AB}=\overrightarrow{OB}-\overrightarrow{OA}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}$  — end point minus start point

It works because you can always travel backwards to the origin and out again: $\overrightarrow{AB}=\overrightarrow{AO}+\overrightarrow{OB}=-\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}$.

Route finding in a diagram
  • Write the vector you want with its start and end points, for example $\overrightarrow{MN}$.
  • Find a chain of known vectors from $M$ to $N$, travelling backwards where you have to — a backwards step carries a minus sign.
  • Add the chain and collect like terms.
  • Check the joins cancel: $\overrightarrow{MP}+\overrightarrow{PN}=\overrightarrow{MN}$.
✎
In a parallelogram. Opposite sides are equal vectors. In $OACB$ with $\overrightarrow{OA}=\mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB}=\mathbf{b}$: $\overrightarrow{AC}=\mathbf{b}$ and $\overrightarrow{BC}=\mathbf{a}$ — that is what lets you cross the shape.

Pick the vector you want and the lab walks the route, marks it on the diagram and sets out the algebra line by line.

5 Midpoints, ratios and proof Interactive

Midpoints
$\overrightarrow{OM}=\mathbf{a}+\tfrac12\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf{a}+\tfrac12(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a})=\tfrac12(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})$
Dividing a line in a given ratio

If $C$ lies on $AB$ with $AC:CB=m:n$, then $C$ is $\dfrac{m}{m+n}$ of the way along $AB$:

$\overrightarrow{OC}=\mathbf{a}+\dfrac{m}{m+n}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a})$
Ratio $AC:CB$Fraction along $AB$$\overrightarrow{OC}$
$1:1$$\tfrac12$$\tfrac12\mathbf{a}+\tfrac12\mathbf{b}$
$1:2$$\tfrac13$$\tfrac23\mathbf{a}+\tfrac13\mathbf{b}$
$2:1$$\tfrac23$$\tfrac13\mathbf{a}+\tfrac23\mathbf{b}$
$3:1$$\tfrac34$$\tfrac14\mathbf{a}+\tfrac34\mathbf{b}$
!
The fractions swap. For $AC:CB=m:n$ the coefficient of $\mathbf{a}$ is $\dfrac{n}{m+n}$ and the coefficient of $\mathbf{b}$ is $\dfrac{m}{m+n}$. They must always add to $1$ for a point on $AB$ — a free check on every answer.
Proving lines parallel, and points collinear
  • Parallel: find both vectors in terms of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$, show one is a scalar multiple of the other, then say so — "$\overrightarrow{PQ}=3\overrightarrow{RS}$, therefore $PQ$ is parallel to $RS$".
  • Collinear: find two vectors that share a point, show $\overrightarrow{XZ}=k\,\overrightarrow{XY}$, then state that they are parallel and share the common point $X$, so $X$, $Y$, $Z$ lie on a straight line.
!
The word "and" earns the mark. Parallel on its own is not collinearity — you must also say the two vectors share a common point.

✍️ Worked examples

Example 1
Column vector arithmetic
 ⌄

$\mathbf{a}=\begin{pmatrix}4\\-1\end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{b}=\begin{pmatrix}-3\\5\end{pmatrix}$. Find $2\mathbf{a}-3\mathbf{b}$.

1

Scale first: $2\mathbf{a}=\begin{pmatrix}8\\-2\end{pmatrix}$ and $3\mathbf{b}=\begin{pmatrix}-9\\15\end{pmatrix}$.

2

Subtract component by component: $8-(-9)=17$ and $-2-15=-17$.

Answer. $\begin{pmatrix}17\\-17\end{pmatrix}$
!
Subtracting a negative component is the commonest slip — write the double negative out in full before simplifying.
Example 2
Finding a magnitude
 ⌄

Find $|2\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}|$ where $\mathbf{a}=\begin{pmatrix}3\\-1\end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{b}=\begin{pmatrix}-2\\5\end{pmatrix}$.

1

Vector arithmetic first: $2\mathbf{a}=\begin{pmatrix}6\\-2\end{pmatrix}$.

2

$2\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}=\begin{pmatrix}6+(-2)\\-2+5\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}4\\3\end{pmatrix}$.

3

Now the magnitude: $\sqrt{4^{2}+3^{2}}=\sqrt{25}=5$.

Answer. $5$
✎
Here the answer is exact. Had it been $\sqrt{26}$ you would write $5.10$ to 3 significant figures.
Example 3
From coordinates to a vector and its length
 ⌄

$P$ is $(-1,4)$ and $Q$ is $(5,-4)$. Find $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ as a column vector and calculate $|\overrightarrow{PQ}|$.

1

End minus start: $\overrightarrow{PQ}=\overrightarrow{OQ}-\overrightarrow{OP}$.

2

Components: $5-(-1)=6$ and $-4-4=-8$.

3

$|\overrightarrow{PQ}|=\sqrt{6^{2}+(-8)^{2}}=\sqrt{36+64}=\sqrt{100}=10$.

Answer. $\overrightarrow{PQ}=\begin{pmatrix}6\\-8\end{pmatrix}$ and $|\overrightarrow{PQ}|=10$
!
$\overrightarrow{QP}$ would be $\begin{pmatrix}-6\\8\end{pmatrix}$ — same length, opposite direction. Read which way round the question wants.
Example 4
A midpoint in a triangle
 ⌄

In triangle $OAB$, $\overrightarrow{OA}=\mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB}=\mathbf{b}$. $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$. Express $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{OM}$ in terms of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$.

1

$\overrightarrow{AB}$: travel back to $O$, then out to $B$: $\overrightarrow{AO}+\overrightarrow{OB}=-\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}$.

2

$\overrightarrow{AM}$: $M$ is halfway, so $\overrightarrow{AM}=\tfrac12(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a})$.

3

$\overrightarrow{OM}$: $\mathbf{a}+\tfrac12(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a})=\mathbf{a}-\tfrac12\mathbf{a}+\tfrac12\mathbf{b}$.

Answer. $\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OM}=\tfrac12(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})$
✎
Check. The coefficients $\tfrac12$ and $\tfrac12$ add to $1$, as they must for a point on $AB$. ✓
Example 5
Dividing a line in a ratio
 ⌄

In triangle $OAB$, $\overrightarrow{OA}=\mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB}=\mathbf{b}$. $C$ lies on $AB$ with $AC:CB=1:3$. Express $\overrightarrow{OC}$ in terms of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$.

1

The ratio $1:3$ means $C$ is $\tfrac14$ of the way from $A$ to $B$.

2

$\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}$, so $\overrightarrow{AC}=\tfrac14(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a})$.

3

$\overrightarrow{OC}=\mathbf{a}+\tfrac14(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a})=\mathbf{a}-\tfrac14\mathbf{a}+\tfrac14\mathbf{b}$.

Answer. $\overrightarrow{OC}=\tfrac34\mathbf{a}+\tfrac14\mathbf{b}$
✎
Check. $\tfrac34+\tfrac14=1$ ✓. Notice the coefficient of $\mathbf{a}$ is the second number of the ratio over the total — the fractions swap over. Set Lab 2 to $AP:PB=1:3$ and compare.
Example 6
Proving points are collinear
 ⌄

$\overrightarrow{OX}=\mathbf{a}+2\mathbf{b}$ and $\overrightarrow{OY}=3\mathbf{a}+6\mathbf{b}$. Show that $O$, $X$ and $Y$ are collinear and state the ratio $OX:XY$.

1

Factorise: $\overrightarrow{OY}=3\mathbf{a}+6\mathbf{b}=3(\mathbf{a}+2\mathbf{b})$.

2

So $\overrightarrow{OY}=3\,\overrightarrow{OX}$ — a scalar multiple, so the two vectors are parallel.

3

They also share the common point $O$, so the three points lie on a straight line.

4

$\overrightarrow{XY}=\overrightarrow{OY}-\overrightarrow{OX}=3(\mathbf{a}+2\mathbf{b})-(\mathbf{a}+2\mathbf{b})=2(\mathbf{a}+2\mathbf{b})$.

Answer. $O$, $X$, $Y$ are collinear, and $OX:XY=1:2$
!
Marks are for the words. The final mark needs both "parallel" and "common point". The algebra alone is not a complete proof.

! Common mistakes to avoid

  • Writing a column vector as a coordinate. A vector goes one number above the other with no comma; a coordinate is a position.
  • Getting $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{BA}$ the wrong way round. $\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}$ is end minus start; $\overrightarrow{BA}$ is its negative.
  • Taking the magnitude too early. Simplify to one column vector first, then use Pythagoras.
  • Giving a negative magnitude. A length is never negative.
  • Losing a root. If $k^{2}=9$ then $k=3$ or $k=-3$.
  • Swapping the ratio fractions. For $AC:CB=m:n$, $C$ is $\frac{m}{m+n}$ of the way from $A$, and the coefficient of $\mathbf{a}$ is $\frac{n}{m+n}$.
  • Saying "parallel" and stopping. Collinearity also needs the common point.
  • Not simplifying. Collect the $\mathbf{a}$ terms and the $\mathbf{b}$ terms — the last mark is usually for the simplest form.
