Trigonometry connects the angles of a triangle to the lengths of its sides. Three ratios do all the work in a right-angled triangle; two rules extend them to every other triangle; and the sine and cosine curves let angles run past 90°. Build the triangles below and watch which ratio the question is really asking for.

◎ What you will be able to do

  • Name the hypotenuse, opposite and adjacent sides against a chosen angle.
  • Use sine, cosine and tangent to find a missing side or a missing angle.
  • Work with angles of elevation and depression.
  • Recall and apply the exact values of sin, cos and tan at $0°$, $30°$, $45°$, $60°$ and $90°$.
  • Solve trigonometric equations for angles beyond $90°$, giving every solution in the range.
  • Use the sine rule and the cosine rule — and spot the ambiguous case.

1 The three ratios

In a right-angled triangle the three sides get names, and those names depend on which angle you are standing at.

  • The hypotenuse is always opposite the right angle. It never changes and it is always the longest side.
  • The opposite is the side facing your angle $\theta$.
  • The adjacent is the remaining side, next to $\theta$.
!
Opposite and adjacent swap over if you switch to the other acute angle. Only the hypotenuse stays put — so always mark the angle first, then label.
$\sin\theta=\dfrac{\text{O}}{\text{H}}\qquad \cos\theta=\dfrac{\text{A}}{\text{H}}\qquad \tan\theta=\dfrac{\text{O}}{\text{A}}$
Memory wordRatioUse it when you know / want
SOH$\sin\theta=\text{O}\div\text{H}$opposite and hypotenuse
CAH$\cos\theta=\text{A}\div\text{H}$adjacent and hypotenuse
TOA$\tan\theta=\text{O}\div\text{A}$opposite and adjacent
✎
Choosing the ratio. Label H, O and A, then tick the two sides in play — the one you know and the one you want. The pair you ticked names the ratio. You never need the third side.
!
These three ratios only work in a right-angled triangle. Any other triangle needs the sine rule or the cosine rule (section 6).

2 Finding sides and angles Interactive

Finding a side
  • Label H, O, A against the given angle.
  • Pick the ratio joining the known side to the wanted one.
  • Substitute, then rearrange.
  • Unknown on top → multiply. Unknown on the bottom → divide.
$x=12\sin 35°\qquad$ or $\qquad x=\dfrac{12}{\sin 35°}$
Finding an angle
  • Label H, O, A.
  • Write the ratio of the two sides you know.
  • Turn it into a decimal.
  • Use the inverse: $\sin^{-1}$, $\cos^{-1}$ or $\tan^{-1}$.
$\tan\theta=\tfrac{5}{8}\Rightarrow\theta=\tan^{-1}(0.625)=32.0°$

Accuracy
  • Angles to 1 decimal place, lengths to 3 significant figures, unless told otherwise.
  • Never round part-way through — store the full value and use it in the next line.
  • Check the calculator says DEG, not RAD or GRAD.
✎
Sense check. The hypotenuse must be the longest side, and the longer of the other two sides must face the larger acute angle. If your answer breaks either rule, look again.

3 Angles of elevation and depression

Both are measured from a horizontal line — never from a vertical one.

  • Elevation — you look up from the horizontal to the object.
  • Depression — you look down from the horizontal to the object.
✎
The two horizontal lines (yours and the object's) are parallel, so the angle of elevation from the bottom equals the angle of depression from the top — alternate angles.
!
Watch out. The angle of depression is not the angle inside the triangle at the top of the building. That interior angle is $90°-\text{depression}$.

Once the sketch is drawn, an elevation problem is an ordinary SOH CAH TOA problem — feed it into Lab 1 as "angle + adjacent, find the opposite".

5 Angles greater than 90° Interactive

Sine, cosine and tangent are defined for every angle, not just those that fit inside a right-angled triangle. Their graphs show why an equation has more than one answer.

The sine curve
  • Maximum $1$ at $90°$, minimum $-1$ at $270°$.
  • Zeros at $0°,180°,360°$.
  • Symmetrical about $x=90°$, so $\sin\theta=\sin(180°-\theta)$.
  • Positive on $0°$–$180°$, negative on $180°$–$360°$.
The cosine curve
  • Starts at $1$, minimum $-1$ at $180°$.
  • Zeros at $90°$ and $270°$.
  • Symmetrical about $x=180°$, so $\cos\theta=\cos(360°-\theta)$.
  • Positive on $0°$–$90°$ and $270°$–$360°$, negative in between.
Lab 3 — every solution in $0°\le x\le 360°$

Choose a curve, drag the line $y=k$, and read off all the crossings. The working underneath shows the calculator's principal value and the symmetry rule that produces the rest.

Watch out. A calculator only ever returns one answer. For $\cos x=-0.5$ it gives $120°$, but $240°$ is just as valid. Always check the curve or the quadrant signs before you write the final line.

6 The sine rule and the cosine rule Interactive

For triangles with no right angle — and where obtuse angles turn up most often.

Sine rule
$\dfrac{a}{\sin A}=\dfrac{b}{\sin B}=\dfrac{c}{\sin C}$

Use it when you have a matching side-and-angle pair. Turn it upside down when the unknown is an angle:

$\dfrac{\sin A}{a}=\dfrac{\sin B}{b}$
Cosine rule
$a^{2}=b^{2}+c^{2}-2bc\cos A$

Use it for two sides and the included angle, or for all three sides. Rearranged for an angle:

$\cos A=\dfrac{b^{2}+c^{2}-a^{2}}{2bc}$
Lab 4 — the rule chooser

Pick which rule and which unknown, type the numbers, and read the working. Try Sine rule — find an angle with $A=40°$, $a=8$, $b=11$ to meet the ambiguous case.

Obtuse angles from the rules
  • Cosine rule. If $\cos A$ comes out negative, the angle is obtuse — the calculator returns it correctly between $90°$ and $180°$, so no extra work is needed.
  • Sine rule — the ambiguous case. Because $\sin\theta=\sin(180°-\theta)$ there may be two possible triangles. Always test whether $180°-\theta$ also fits, by checking the three angles still total less than $180°$.
✎
Area of any triangle: $\text{Area}=\tfrac12ab\sin C$. This works for an obtuse $C$ too, because $\sin C$ stays positive all the way to $180°$.

✍️ Worked examples

Example 1
Finding a side
 ⌄

The hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle is $12$ cm and one angle is $35°$. Find the side opposite that angle.

1

Known: hypotenuse. Wanted: opposite. That pair is SOH.

2

$\sin 35°=\dfrac{x}{12}$

3

The unknown is on top, so multiply: $x=12\sin 35°$.

4

$x=12\times0.5736=6.8829\ldots$

Answer. $6.88$ cm (3 s.f.)
✎
Sense check. The answer must be shorter than the hypotenuse of $12$ cm. ✔
Example 2
Finding an angle
 ⌄

A right-angled triangle has an opposite side of $5$ cm and an adjacent side of $8$ cm. Find the angle $\theta$.

1

Opposite and adjacent are known, so use TOA.

2

$\tan\theta=\dfrac{5}{8}=0.625$

3

$\theta=\tan^{-1}(0.625)=32.005\ldots°$

Answer. $\theta=32.0°$ (1 d.p.)
!
Use $\tan^{-1}$, not $\tan$. Pressing $\tan(0.625)$ gives a meaningless answer.
Example 3
Angle of elevation
 ⌄

From a point $40$ m from the base of a tower, the angle of elevation of the top is $28°$. Find the height of the tower.

1

Sketch it: the tower is the opposite side, the $40$ m is the adjacent side.

2

Opposite and adjacent → TOA.

3

$\tan 28°=\dfrac{h}{40}\Rightarrow h=40\tan 28°$

4

$h=40\times0.5317=21.27\ldots$

Answer. The tower is $21.3$ m tall (3 s.f.)
Example 4
Using exact values
 ⌄

(a) A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse $12$ cm and an angle of $30°$. Find the opposite side exactly.

1

$\sin 30°=\dfrac{x}{12}$, and $\sin 30°=\tfrac12$ exactly.

2

$x=12\times\tfrac12=6$ cm — exact, no rounding.

(b) Evaluate $\sin 60°\cos 30°+\cos 60°\sin 30°$ exactly.

1

Substitute exact values: $\left(\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)\left(\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)+\left(\tfrac12\right)\left(\tfrac12\right)$

2

First term $\tfrac34$, second term $\tfrac14$.

3

$\tfrac34+\tfrac14=1$

Answer. (a) exactly $6$ cm · (b) $1$ — which is $\sin 90°$, since the expression is the sine addition formula for $60°+30°$.
Example 5
Solving trigonometric equations
 ⌄

Solve for $0°\le x\le 360°$: (a) $\sin x=0.5$, (b) $\cos x=-0.5$, (c) $\tan x=1$.

1

(a) $x=\sin^{-1}(0.5)=30°$. Sine is positive in the first two quadrants, so also $180°-30°=150°$.
$x=30°$ or $150°$.

2

(b) $x=\cos^{-1}(-0.5)=120°$. Cosine is symmetrical about $180°$, so also $360°-120°=240°$.
$x=120°$ or $240°$.

3

(c) $x=\tan^{-1}(1)=45°$. Tangent repeats every $180°$, so also $45°+180°=225°$.
$x=45°$ or $225°$.

Answer. (a) $30°,150°$ · (b) $120°,240°$ · (c) $45°,225°$
!
Each of these has two answers in the range. Losing the second one loses marks — check each against Lab 3.
Example 6
Sine rule and the ambiguous case
 ⌄

In triangle $ABC$, $A=40°$, $a=8$ cm and $b=11$ cm. Find the possible values of angle $B$.

1

Sine rule, angle way up: $\dfrac{\sin B}{11}=\dfrac{\sin 40°}{8}$

2

$\sin B=\dfrac{11\sin 40°}{8}=\dfrac{11\times0.6428}{8}=0.8838$

3

$B=\sin^{-1}(0.8838)=62.1°$

4

Since $\sin\theta=\sin(180°-\theta)$, also test $180°-62.1°=117.9°$.

5

Check both fit: $40+62.1=102.1<180$ ✔ and $40+117.9=157.9<180$ ✔ — both triangles exist.

Answer. $B=62.1°$ or $B=117.9°$ (1 d.p.)
✎
Why two answers. Because $b>a$ and angle $A$ is acute, two different triangles can be drawn from these measurements.
Example 7
Cosine rule giving an obtuse angle
 ⌄

A triangle has sides $a=5$ cm, $b=6$ cm and $c=10$ cm. Find angle $C$.

1

$C$ is opposite side $c$, so $\cos C=\dfrac{a^{2}+b^{2}-c^{2}}{2ab}$

2

$\cos C=\dfrac{25+36-100}{2\times5\times6}=\dfrac{-39}{60}$

3

$\cos C=-0.65$

4

$C=\cos^{-1}(-0.65)=130.54\ldots°$

Answer. $C=130.5°$ (1 d.p.) — obtuse, exactly as the negative cosine predicted.
✎
Sense check. $C$ is opposite the longest side ($10$ cm), so it must be the largest angle in the triangle. ✔

! Common mistakes to avoid

  • Calculator in radians. Check for DEG on the display before you start.
  • Mislabelling opposite and adjacent. They swap over when you change which angle you are using.
  • Multiplying when you should divide. If the unknown sits on the bottom of the fraction, divide.
  • Using SOH CAH TOA in a triangle with no right angle. Switch to the sine or cosine rule.
  • Giving only one solution to a trigonometric equation. Sine, cosine and tangent all repeat — check the curve.
  • Ignoring the ambiguous case of the sine rule. Always test $180°-\theta$ as well.
  • Square-rooting the cosine rule too early. Work out the whole right-hand side first, then take the root.
  • Rounding too early. Store intermediate values; round only the final answer.
