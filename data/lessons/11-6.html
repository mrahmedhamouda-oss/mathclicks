Core Concepts

1. What volume actually measures

The volume of a solid is the measure of the amount of space that the solid encloses. Volume is measured in cubic units.

Think of filling a rectangular prism with unit cubes. If the bottom layer holds 6 · 4 = 24 cubic units, and the prism is 2 layers tall, the total volume is 24 · 2 = 48 cubic units. That "one layer × number of layers" idea is the volume formula.

bottom layer = area of the base = B
number of layers = height = h
total = B · h
B — the area of the base of the solid, in square units. Not the length of a side.
h — the height: the perpendicular distance between the two bases (prism) or from the apex straight down to the base plane (pyramid).
Right solid — the lateral edges are perpendicular to the base.
Oblique solid — the lateral edges lean; the height is still measured vertically, not along the slanted edge.
Key Concept · Volume of a Prism
Words: The volume V of a prism is V = Bh, where B is the area of a base and h is the height of the prism.
V = Bh
This works for every prism — rectangular, triangular, trapezoidal, pentagonal, hexagonal. Only the way you compute B changes.

2. Computing B for the common bases

rectangle: B = ℓw
triangle: B = ½bhbase
trapezoid: B = ½hbase(b1 + b2)
regular polygon: B = ½aP  (a = apothem, P = perimeter)
Study Tip: The base of a prism is not always the face resting on the table. It is one of the two congruent parallel faces. For a triangular prism lying on a rectangular face, the base is still the triangle.
Key Concept · Cavalieri's Principle
Words: If two solids have the same height h and the same cross-sectional area B at every level, then they have the same volume.
same h  +  same cross-section at every level  →  same V
This is why V = Bh works for an oblique prism too. Leaning a stack of cards to one side does not change how much space the stack takes up.
Watch Out! Cross-Sectional Area. For solids with the same height to have the same volume, their cross sections must have the same area. The cross sections of the different solids do not have to be congruent polygons — a square and a rectangle of equal area work just as well.
Key Concept · Volume of a Pyramid
Words: The volume V of a pyramid is V = ⅓Bh, where B is the area of the base and h is the height of the pyramid.
V = ⅓Bh   or   V = Bh ÷ 3
Exactly three pyramids with the same base and the same height fill a prism with that base and height. So a pyramid holds one third of its "parent" prism.
1
Prism: V = Bh. Pyramid: V = ⅓Bh. Same base, same height → the pyramid is exactly ⅓ of the prism.
2
Find B first, as a single number in square units. Only then multiply by h.
3
h is always the perpendicular (vertical) distance between the bases — never the slanted lateral edge.
4
Cavalieri: an oblique prism and a right prism with the same B and the same h have equal volumes.
5
For a composite solid, split it into a prism plus a pyramid, find each volume, then add.

3. The method, every time

Identify the base: the pair of congruent parallel faces (prism) or the single face opposite the apex (pyramid).
Compute B, the area of that base, using the correct 2-D area formula. Label the units as square units.
Identify h, the perpendicular height. If the solid is oblique, do not use the slant edge.
Substitute into V = Bh (prism) or V = ⅓Bh (pyramid).
Report the answer in cubic units.
Watch Out! Area and Volume. Area is two-dimensional, so it is measured in square units. Volume is three-dimensional, so it is measured in cubic units. Writing 340 mm2 for a volume is a scoring error even when the number is right.

Worked Examples

Find the volume of the prism. The base is a regular pentagon with apothem a = 3.4 mm and perimeter P = 25 mm (side 5 mm). The height of the prism is 8 mm.

Step 1 — Find the area of the base B.

1B = ½aParea of a regular polygon
2B = ½(3.4)(25)a = 3.4 and P = 25
3B = 42.5the base is 42.5 mm²

Step 2 — Find the volume of the prism.

4V = Bhvolume of a prism
5V = 42.5(8)B = 42.5 and h = 8
V = 340 cubic millimetres (340 mm3)
Check: 42.5 × 8 = 40(8) + 2.5(8) = 320 + 20 = 340 ✓  Units: mm2 × mm = mm3 ✓
Trap: P is the whole perimeter (5 sides × 5 mm = 25 mm), not one side. Using P = 5 would give B = 8.5 and a volume five times too small.
Find the volume of the oblique prism. The base is a trapezoid with parallel sides b1 = 4 cm and b2 = 2 cm and base height 5 cm. The prism's height is 8 cm.

Part A — Find the area of the base. The base of the prism is a trapezoid.

1A = ½h(b1 + b2)area of a trapezoid
2A = ½(5)(4 + 2)h = 5, b1 = 4, b2 = 2
3A = ½(5)(6) = 15the base is 15 cm²

Part B — Find the volume.

4V = Bhvolume of a prism (valid by Cavalieri)
5V = (15)(8)B = 15 and h = 8
V = 120 cm3
Trap: The trapezoid's own height (5 cm) and the prism's height (8 cm) are two different numbers. The 5 belongs inside the area formula; only the 8 multiplies B.
A rectangular prism has a base measuring 4x by x and a height of 4x + 3. Part A Find the volume in terms of x. Part B Find the volume if x = 2.

Part A, Step 1 — Find the area of the base. The base is a rectangle.

1A = ℓwarea of a rectangle
2A = (4x)(x)substitution
3A = 4x²solve

Part A, Step 2 — Find the volume.

4V = Bhvolume of a prism
5V = (4x²)(4x + 3)substitute
6V = 16x³ + 12x²distribute and simplify

Part B — Evaluate at x = 2.

7V = 16(2)³ + 12(2)²substitute x = 2
8V = 16(8) + 12(4) = 128 + 48simplify
V = 16x3 + 12x2   →   V = 176 in3 when x = 2
Check: Substitute first instead: B = 4(2)2 = 16, h = 4(2) + 3 = 11, so V = 16(11) = 176 ✓
Find the volume of the pyramid whose base area is B = 22 cm2 and whose height is 5 cm.
1V = ⅓Bhvolume of a pyramid
2V = ⅓(22)(5)B = 22 and h = 5
3V = 110/3 ≈ 36.7divide, round to the nearest tenth
V ≈ 36.7 cm3
Check: A prism with the same base and height would hold 22(5) = 110 cm3. One third of 110 is about 36.7 ✓
A square pyramid has base edge 8x. A right triangle inside the pyramid has legs 4x (centre of the base to the midpoint of an edge) and h, with hypotenuse 5x. Part A Find V in terms of x. Part B Find V if x = 4.

Part A, Step 1 — Find the area B of the square base.

1B = (8x)² = 64x²area of a square

Part A, Step 2 — Find the height with the Pythagorean Theorem.

2a² + b² = c²Pythagorean Theorem
3(4x)² + h² = (5x)²a = 4x, b = h, c = 5x
416x² + h² = 25x²square each term
5h² = 9x², so h = 3xsolve for h (lengths are positive)

Part A, Step 3 — Find the volume.

6V = ⅓Bh = ⅓(64x²)(3x)substitute
7V = 64x³⅓ · 3 = 1

Part B — Evaluate at x = 4.

8V = 64(4)³ = 64(64)substitute x = 4
V = 64x3   →   V = 4096 in3 when x = 4
Study Tip: 3-4-5 shows up again: legs 4x and 3x, hypotenuse 5x. Recognising the triple saves you the arithmetic.
Find the volume of the composite solid: a rectangular prism with a 6 in. by 6 in. base and height 3 in., topped by a square pyramid of height 4 in.

Part A — the prism

V = Bh
= 36(3)
= 108 in3

Part B — the pyramid

V = ⅓Bh
= ⅓(36)(4)
= 48 in3

Part C — the composite solid. The two pieces do not overlap, so add the volumes.

1V = 108 + 48prism + pyramid
V = 156 in3
Trap: Do not use 5 in. (a slanted edge) as the pyramid's height. Only the vertical 4 in. goes into V = ⅓Bh.
A monument-shaped solid is modelled by a square prism with base edge 6.5 units and height 36 units, topped by a square pyramid with the same base and height 6.5 units. Approximate the total volume to the nearest tenth.

Step 1 — the square prism. The base is a square of edge 6.5, so B = 6.52 = 42.25.

1V = Bh = 42.25(36)B = 42.25 and h = 36
2V = 1521volume of the square prism

Step 2 — the square pyramid.

3V = ⅓Bh = ⅓(42.25)(6.5)B = 42.25 and h = 6.5
4V = 274.625/3 ≈ 91.5simplify, round to the nearest tenth

Step 3 — the whole sculpture.

5V ≈ 1521 + 91.5add the two pieces
V ≈ 1612.5 cubic units
Study Tip: Round only at the very end. Rounding 91.541… to 92 before adding would shift the final answer by half a cubic unit.

Try It Yourself

Practice 1 — prism with a triangular base

A prism has a right-triangular base with legs 6 cm and 9 cm. The prism's height is 12 cm. Find V.
1B = ½(6)(9) = 27area of a right triangle
2V = Bh = 27(12)volume of a prism
V = 324 cm3

Practice 2 — regular hexagonal prism

A prism's base is a regular hexagon with apothem 5√3 in. and side 10 in. The prism is 7 in. tall. Find V.
1P = 6(10) = 60perimeter of the hexagon
2B = ½aP = ½(5√3)(60) = 150√3regular polygon area
3V = Bh = 150√3(7) = 1050√3volume of a prism
V = 1050√3 ≈ 1818.7 in3

Practice 3 — oblique prism (Cavalieri)

An oblique prism has a trapezoidal base with parallel sides 7 m and 3 m and base height 4 m. Its vertical height is 9 m; each lateral edge measures 11 m. Find V.
1B = ½(4)(7 + 3) = 20area of a trapezoid
2V = Bh = 20(9)Cavalieri: use 9, not the 11 m slant
V = 180 m3

Practice 4 — pyramid in terms of x

A pyramid has a rectangular base 6x by 2x and height 5x. Find V in terms of x, then evaluate at x = 3.
1B = (6x)(2x) = 12x²area of a rectangle
2V = ⅓(12x²)(5x) = 20x³volume of a pyramid
3V = 20(3)³ = 20(27)substitute x = 3
V = 20x3, and V = 540 cubic units when x = 3

Practice 5 — solve backwards for h

A pyramid has a volume of 270 ft3 and a base area of 45 ft2. Find its height.
1270 = ⅓(45)hsubstitute into V = ⅓Bh
2270 = 15h⅓(45) = 15
3h = 18divide each side by 15
h = 18 ft (the same answer whether the pyramid is right or oblique)

Practice 6 — composite solid

A cube of edge 4 in. has a square pyramid of height 9 in. built on its top face. Find the total volume.
1B = 4² = 16shared square face
2Vcube = 16(4) = 64V = Bh
3Vpyr = ⅓(16)(9) = 48V = ⅓Bh
V = 64 + 48 = 112 in3

Common mistakes to avoid:
  • Using a side length where the formula wants the base area B.
  • Using the slant (lateral) edge instead of the perpendicular height on an oblique solid or a pyramid.
  • Forgetting the ⅓ on a pyramid — that answer is exactly 3 times too big.
  • Mixing up the base's internal height (inside a triangle or trapezoid area formula) with the solid's height.
  • Using the perimeter of one side instead of the whole polygon in B = ½aP.
  • Reporting the answer in square units instead of cubic units.
  • Rounding partway through a composite-solid calculation instead of at the end.
