Core Concepts

What "surface area" actually means

Imagine cutting a solid along its edges and flattening it onto a table. The flat shape you get is called a net, and the total area of that net is the surface area of the solid. So surface area is never a new idea — it is always just areas of flat polygons and circles, added up.

Every solid in this lesson splits into two pieces:

lateral faces — the faces that join the bases of a solid (the "sides").
lateral area (L) — the sum of the areas of the lateral faces. Bases are not included.
altitude of a prism or cylinder — the segment perpendicular to the bases that joins the planes of the bases. Its length is the height h.
altitude of a pyramid or cone — the segment perpendicular to the base with the vertex as one endpoint and a point in the plane of the base as the other.
regular pyramid — a pyramid whose base is a regular polygon.
composite solid — a three-dimensional solid built out of simpler solids.
Surface area = lateral area + area of the base(s)
S = L + 2B  (two bases: prism, cylinder)  ·  S = L + B  (one base: pyramid, cone)

Prisms and cylinders

Unroll the sides of a right prism and you get one long rectangle. Its height is h and its width is the whole way around the base — the perimeter P. That is the entire reason L = Ph works. A cylinder is the same story with P = 2πr.

Key Concept · Lateral Area of a Right Prism
The lateral area L of a right prism is L = Ph, where h is the height of the prism and P is the perimeter of a base.
Key Concept · Lateral Area of a Right Cylinder
The lateral area L of a right cylinder is L = 2πrh, where r is the radius of a base and h is the height of the cylinder.
Key Concept · Surface Area of a Right Prism
The surface area S of a right prism is S = L + 2B, where L is the lateral area and B is the area of a base. Substituting L = Ph gives S = Ph + 2B.
Key Concept · Surface Area of a Right Cylinder
The surface area S of a right cylinder is S = L + 2B. Substituting L = 2πrh and B = πr2 gives S = 2πrh + 2πr2.
Study Tip · Right Solids and Oblique Solids. A solid is a right solid if the segment connecting the centers of the bases (prisms and cylinders) or the center of the base to the vertex (pyramids and cones) is perpendicular to the base(s). Otherwise it is an oblique solid. Every formula in this lesson assumes a right solid.

Pyramids and cones

The lateral faces of a regular pyramid are congruent triangles. Each one has base s (a base edge) and height ℓ, the slant height. With n faces, L = n · ½sℓ = ½(ns)ℓ = ½Pℓ. A cone is the limit of that idea, and ½(2πr)ℓ = πrℓ.

Key Concept · Lateral Area of a Regular Pyramid
The lateral area L of a regular pyramid is L = ½Pℓ, where ℓ is the slant height and P is the perimeter of the base.
Key Concept · Lateral Area of a Right Cone
The lateral area L of a right circular cone is L = πrℓ, where r is the radius of the base and ℓ is the slant height.
Key Concept · Surface Area of a Regular Pyramid / Right Cone
Pyramid: S = L + B = ½Pℓ + B.
Cone: S = L + B = πrℓ + πr2.
A pyramid or cone has only one base, so you add B once, not twice.
Watch Out! Slant height ≠ height. The height h runs from the vertex straight down to the center of the base; the slant height ℓ runs from the vertex down the face to the midpoint of a base edge (or to the edge of the circle). They form a right triangle with the apothem a of the base: ℓ2 = h2 + a2 for a pyramid and ℓ2 = h2 + r2 for a cone.

Spheres

A sphere is the set of all points in space that are a given distance from a given point called the center. A sphere has no bases and no lateral faces — only one curved surface. A great circle is the cross-section made by a plane through the center; its radius and circumference are the radius and circumference of the sphere.

Key Concept · Surface Area of a Sphere
The surface area S of a sphere is S = 4πr2, where r is the radius. Equivalently, the surface of a sphere equals exactly four of its great circles.

How to attack any surface-area problem

Name the solid and decide how many bases it has (2, 1, or 0).
Find the base: its perimeter P (or circumference) and its area B. For a regular polygon, B = ½Pa, where a is the apothem.
Find the missing right-triangle piece if needed — use the Pythagorean Theorem or a trigonometric ratio to get a, h, or ℓ.
Compute the lateral area L with the matching formula.
Add the base(s): S = L + 2B or S = L + B. Keep π exact until the last step, then round.
1
L = Ph and L = 2πrh — two bases, so S = L + 2B.
2
L = ½Pℓ and L = πrℓ — one base, so S = L + B.
3
S = 4πr2 for a sphere — no bases at all.
4
Area of a regular polygon: B = ½Pa, with apothem a found from a central angle of 360°/n.
5
For a composite solid, add the exposed pieces and subtract or omit any face that is hidden where two solids meet.

Find the lateral area and surface area of the regular octagonal prism. Round to the nearest tenth, if necessary. (base edge 6 in., height 11 in.)

Part A — Find the lateral area.

1L = Phlateral area of a prism
2L = (8 × 6)11the base has 8 sides
3L = 48(11) = 528solve
The lateral area is 528 in²

Part B — Find the surface area. Step 1: find the area of the base.

1central angle = 360°/8 = 45°, so half is 22.5°split the octagon into 8 triangles
2tan 22.5° = 3/atrig ratio for the apothem
3a = 3 / tan 22.5° ≈ 7.24solve for a
4B = ½Pa = ½(48)(7.24) ≈ 173.8area of a regular polygon

Step 2: find the surface area.

5S = L + 2Bsurface area of a right prism
6S ≈ 528 + 2(173.8)L = 528 and B ≈ 173.8
7S ≈ 875.6simplify
S ≈ 875.6 in²
Watch Out! The apothem is the distance from the center to the midpoint of a side, so the leg of the little right triangle is half of 6, not 6.
Find the lateral area and surface area of a cylinder with diameter 10 in. and height 9 in. Round to the nearest tenth.

Part A — Find the lateral area.

1L = 2πrhlateral area of a cylinder
2L = 2π(5)(9) = 90πsubstitution, r = 10 ÷ 2 = 5
3L ≈ 282.7solve
The lateral area is about 282.7 in²

Part B — Find the surface area.

4S = 2πrh + 2πr2surface area of a cylinder
5S ≈ 282.7 + 2π(5)2substitute
6S ≈ 282.7 + 157.1 ≈ 439.8solve
S ≈ 439.8 in²
Study Tip. Exact form is 90π + 50π = 140π. Rounding only at the very end avoids a build-up of error.
Find the lateral area and surface area of a regular pyramid whose base is an equilateral triangle with side 6 ft and whose slant height is 8 ft. Round to the nearest tenth.

Part A — Find the lateral area.

1P = 6 × 3 = 18perimeter of the equilateral base
2L = ½Pℓlateral area of a regular pyramid
3L = ½(18)(8) = 72P = 18 and ℓ = 8
The lateral area is 72 ft²

Part B — Find the surface area. Step 1: height of the triangular base.

4a2 + b2 = c2Pythagorean Theorem
5a2 = 62 − 32 = 27c = 6 and b = 3
6a = √27 ≈ 5.196solve
7B = ½bh = ½(6)(√27) ≈ 15.6area of a triangle

Step 2: surface area of the pyramid.

S ≈ 87.6 ft²
Watch Out! A pyramid has one base, so it is S = L + B, never L + 2B.

A regular triangular pyramid has base edge 2x and slant height 4x − 4. (a) Find S in terms of x. (b) Evaluate S when x = 6.
1P = 3(2x) = 6xperimeter of the equilateral base
2height of base = x√330°-60°-90° triangle: legs x and x√3

5S = ½(6x)(4x − 4) + √3x2substitute

(7) Find the surface area of a sphere of radius 7.1 mm to the nearest tenth. (8) If the circumference of a sphere is C = 3πx2, find the surface area in terms of x and π.

Example 7 — from the radius

S = 4πr2
S = 4π(7.1)2
S = 4π(50.41) = 201.64π
S ≈ 633.5 mm2

Example 8 — from the circumference

C = 2πr
3πx2 = 2πr
r = (3/2)x2
S = 4π[(3/2)x2]2 = 4π(9/4)x4

A solid is modeled by a square prism 7 in. by 7 in. by 12 in. with no top face, capped by half of a cylinder of radius 3.5 in. and length 12 in. Find the surface area.
1prism: 3 rectangles 12 × 7 and 2 squares 7 × 7the top face is missing
2half cylinder: rectangle 12 × 3.5π and two half circles r = 3.5half of 2πr is 3.5π
3S = 3(12 × 7) + 2(7 × 7) + (12 × 3.5π) + π(3.5)2add every exposed piece
4S = 252 + 98 + 42π + 12.25π = 350 + 54.25πsimplify

Watch Out! Two half circles make one whole circle, so their combined area is π(3.5)2, not 2π(3.5)2. And the face where the two solids meet is not part of the surface at all.

Try It Yourself

Problem 1

A right prism has a rectangular base 8 units by 3 units and height 15 units. Find L and S.
1P = 2(8 + 3) = 22, B = 8(3) = 24base perimeter and area
2L = Ph = 22(15) = 330L = Ph
3S = 330 + 2(24) = 378S = L + 2B
L = 330 units², S = 378 units²

Problem 2

A right cylinder has r = 4 cm and h = 10 cm. Find L and S in exact form and to the nearest tenth.
1L = 2π(4)(10) = 80π ≈ 251.3L = 2πrh
22B = 2π(4)2 = 32πtwo circular bases
3S = 80π + 32π = 112π ≈ 351.9S = L + 2B
L = 80π ≈ 251.3 cm², S = 112π ≈ 351.9 cm²

Problem 3

A regular square pyramid has base edge 10 m and slant height 13 m. Find S.
1P = 4(10) = 40, B = 102 = 100square base
2L = ½(40)(13) = 260L = ½Pℓ
3S = 260 + 100 = 360S = L + B (one base)
S = 360 m²

Problem 4

A right cone has r = 6 in. and altitude h = 8 in. Find the surface area in exact form.
1ℓ2 = 62 + 82 = 100, so ℓ = 10Pythagorean Theorem (r, h, ℓ)
2L = π(6)(10) = 60πL = πrℓ
3S = 60π + π(6)2 = 96π ≈ 301.6S = πrℓ + πr2
S = 96π ≈ 301.6 in²

Problem 5

A great circle of a sphere has circumference 12π units. Find the surface area of the sphere.
12πr = 12πcircumference of a great circle
2r = 6divide by 2π
3S = 4π(6)2 = 144π ≈ 452.4S = 4πr2
S = 144π ≈ 452.4 units²

Problem 6 — work backwards

A cylinder has lateral area 120π m² and height 7 m. Find the radius to the nearest tenth.
1L = 2πrh → 120π = 2πr(7)substitute what you know
2120π = 14πrsimplify
3r = 120/14 = 60/7 ≈ 8.6divide by 14π
r ≈ 8.6 m

Common mistakes to avoid
  • Using the height h where the slant height ℓ belongs (pyramids and cones).
  • Adding 2B for a pyramid or cone — they have only one base.
  • Forgetting to halve a diameter before using r.
  • Writing 4πr2 for a circle's area or πr2 for a sphere — keep them straight.
  • Using the side length instead of the apothem in B = ½Pa.
  • Counting a face that is covered where two solids are joined in a composite solid.
  • Rounding π early, then rounding again — carry exact values to the last step.
