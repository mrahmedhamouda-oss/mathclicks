Core Concepts

What "varies" actually means

A variation equation says that two or more quantities are locked together by one fixed number. That fixed number is the constant of variation, written k. Nothing else is allowed in the equation — no + 3, no − 7. The only question in this lesson is where the variables sit: multiplied by k (directly), or divided into k (inversely).

direct variation — y = kx. As x grows, y grows by the same factor.
constant of variation — the nonzero constant k in a variation equation.
joint variation — y = kxz. One quantity varies directly as the product of two or more others.
inverse variation — xy = k, or y = k/x. As x grows, y shrinks.
combined variation — y = kx/z. One quantity varies directly and/or inversely as two or more others.
proportional — another way of saying "varies directly as."

Direct variation

Two quantities x and y are related by a direct variation if y is equal to a constant k times x.

Key Concept · Direct Variation
Words: y varies directly as x if there is some nonzero constant k such that y = kx.
Symbols: y = kx,  k ≠ 0

The graph of a direct variation function such as f(x) = 4x is a line through the origin. A direct variation is just the special case of slope-intercept form y = mx + b in which m = k and b = 0 — so the slope of a direct variation equation is its constant of variation. It passes through the origin because substituting x = 0 forces y = k(0) = 0.

y = kx  →  k = y/x   (the ratio y : x never changes)
Proportion for two sets of values:   y1/x1 = y2/x2

Joint variation

Joint variation occurs when one quantity varies directly as the product of two or more other quantities. It is still "direct" — there are simply more factors riding along with k.

Key Concept · Joint Variation
Words: y varies jointly as x and z if there is some nonzero constant k such that y = kxz.
Symbols: y = kxz,  k ≠ 0
y = kxz  →  k = y/(xz)
Proportion:   y1/(x1z1) = y2/(x2z2)
Think about it: if y varies jointly as x and z and y = 0, then kxz = 0. Since k ≠ 0, at least one of x or z must be 0.

Inverse variation

Two quantities x and y are related by an inverse variation if their product is equal to a constant k. Bigger x forces smaller y, because the product has to stay the same.

Key Concept · Inverse Variation
Words: y varies inversely as x if there is some nonzero constant k such that xy = k or y = k/x, where x ≠ 0 and y ≠ 0.
Example: If xy = 8 and x = 12, then y = 8/12 or 2/3.

When k is positive, one quantity increases while the other decreases. Because the two products must match, x1y1 = x2y2, which can be rearranged into the proportion below.

xy = k  →  y = k/x
Proportion:   x1/y2 = x2/y1   (same as x1y1 = x2y2)
Study Tip · Proportions: several different proportions can be used to solve an inverse variation problem with the same result. What matters is that the two products x1y1 and x2y2 end up equal.

Combined variation

Combined variation occurs when one quantity varies directly and/or inversely as two or more other quantities. Everything the quantity varies directly as goes in the numerator with k; everything it varies inversely as goes in the denominator.

"y varies directly as x and inversely as z"  →  y = kx/z
Solve for k:   k = yz/x
Proportion:   y1z1/x1 = y2z2/x2

The derivation is worth seeing once: if y1 = kx1/z1 and y2 = kx2/z2, then solving each for k gives y1z1/x1 = k and y2z2/x2 = k. Two things equal to the same k are equal to each other.

The four types side by side

x doubles → y is halved

Combined — y directly as x, inversely as z

y = kx/z
k = yz/x
numerator = direct, denominator = inverse

The one procedure that solves every problem

Translate the sentence into an equation containing k. Direct/joint factors go on top with k; inverse factors go on the bottom.
Substitute the complete set of values you were given (the set where you know every variable).
Solve for k. This is the constant of variation and it never changes for the rest of the problem.
Rewrite the equation using that value of k.
Substitute the second set of values and solve for the unknown.
Check: for direct variation both ratios y/x must match; for inverse variation both products xy must match.
Study Tip · Subscripts: when writing the initial proportion, use subscripts with the variables (x1, y1, x2, y2). This will help you substitute the correct value for each of the variables.
1
The constant of variation is a ratio in direct variation and a product in inverse variation. In y = kx the quotient y/x equals k for every point. In y = k/x the product xy equals k for every point. Compute both from any given pair — whichever one comes out the same twice tells you the type of variation.
2
k must be nonzero. In an inverse variation neither x nor y may be 0 either, since xy = k ≠ 0. A direct or joint variation, however, is perfectly happy at the origin.
3
Every direct variation graph passes through (0, 0) and has slope k. Every inverse variation graph is a hyperbola with the x-axis and y-axis as asymptotes — it approaches them forever and never reaches them.
Watch Out!
  • y = 3x + 5 is not a direct variation. The + 5 pushes the line off the origin.
  • "Varies inversely" means xy = k, not y/x = k. Multiply, do not divide.
  • In an inverse proportion the subscripts cross: x1/y2 = x2/y1. Writing x1/y1 = x2/y2 is a direct variation and is the single most common error in this lesson.
  • Joint variation is y = kxz, not y = kx + kz.

Common mistakes to avoid
  • Calling y = kx + b a direct variation. Only b = 0 qualifies.
  • Using the direct proportion x1/y1 = x2/y2 on an inverse variation problem.
  • Naming the wrong constant: in y = 2rgt the constant is 2, not 2rg.
  • Forgetting to solve for the requested variable before naming the type. In rw = 15, r varies inversely as w even though no fraction is visible.
  • Letting a value be 0 in an inverse variation. Since xy = k ≠ 0, neither x nor y can be 0.
  • Substituting the incomplete set of values when solving for k. Always use the set where every variable is known.
