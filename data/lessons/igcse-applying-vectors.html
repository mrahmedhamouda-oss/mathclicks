This is the Paper 4 lesson: everything from Vectors put to work. Almost every 0580 vectors question is one of six types, and recognising the type tells you the method before you write anything. The labs below run the two techniques that carry the hardest marks — comparing coefficients to find where two lines cross, and area ratios.

◎ What you will be able to do

  • Recognise which of the six standard question types you are looking at.
  • Compare coefficients to find two unknowns — and say why you are allowed to.
  • Find the intersection of two lines using two independent parameters.
  • Turn a scalar multiple into a length ratio.
  • Use the same-height rule and the similar-triangle rule for area ratios.
  • Write the concluding sentence that earns the final mark in a proof.

1 The toolkit, on one page

ToolStatementUse it when…
Route rule$\overrightarrow{AB}=\overrightarrow{AO}+\overrightarrow{OB}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}$You need a vector between two labelled points

Parallel test$\mathbf{v}=k\mathbf{u}$You must prove two lines parallel, or find a ratio

!
The condition that makes it all work. Comparing coefficients is valid only when $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are not parallel. Every IGCSE question sets up two non-parallel base vectors precisely so you can do this — and the mark scheme expects you to say so.
✎
Two habits worth having. Always write a vector with its start and end letters ($\overrightarrow{MN}$, not "the vector to N"), and always simplify to the form $\alpha\mathbf{a}+\beta\mathbf{b}$ before comparing anything.

2 The six question types

TypeWhat it looks likeMethod
A — Arithmetic"Find $3\mathbf{a}-2\mathbf{b}$ and $|3\mathbf{a}-2\mathbf{b}|$"Component arithmetic first, then Pythagoras
B — Unknowns"Find $k$ given the vectors are parallel"Set one vector $=\lambda\times$ the other; compare
C — Express"Express $\overrightarrow{MN}$ in terms of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$"Walk a route, then collect terms
D — Prove"Show $PQ$ is parallel to $AB$" · "Show $X$, $Y$, $Z$ are collinear"Factorise to $k\times(\text{something})$, then write the conclusion

!
Read the command word. "Write down" needs no working. "Find" or "Express" needs the route. "Show that" needs the given answer to appear and a concluding statement. "Explain" or "Justify" awards marks for the words, not the algebra.

3 Comparing coefficients Interactive

If $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are not parallel, the only way two expressions in $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ can be equal is for the $\mathbf{a}$ parts to match and the $\mathbf{b}$ parts to match separately. One vector equation therefore carries two ordinary equations.

$\lambda\mathbf{a}+\mu\mathbf{b}=p\mathbf{a}+q\mathbf{b}\quad\Rightarrow\quad \lambda=p\ \text{ and }\ \mu=q$
Lab 1 — the coefficient solver

Type the coefficients of any equation of this shape and the lab splits it into its two equations and solves them, with the check at the end.

4 Finding an intersection Interactive

  • Let $X$ be the intersection. Write $\overrightarrow{OX}$ along the first line using a parameter $t$.
  • Write $\overrightarrow{OX}$ again along the second line using a different parameter $s$.
  • Simplify both into the form $\alpha\mathbf{a}+\beta\mathbf{b}$.
  • Set the $\mathbf{a}$ coefficients equal and the $\mathbf{b}$ coefficients equal — two equations.
  • Solve the pair, then substitute back to get $\overrightarrow{OX}$.
!
Why two different letters. $X$ sits at a different fraction along each line, so the parameters must be independent. Using $t$ for both is the most common way to lose every mark in the part.
Lab 2 — where the two lines cross

Move $M$ along $OA$ and $N$ along $AB$. The lab runs the full method — both routes, both comparison equations, the value of $t$, and the ratio in which $X$ divides $ON$. With both at the midpoint you get the median result: $OX:XN=2:1$.

5 Ratios of lengths and areas Interactive

Length ratios from a scalar multiple

If $\overrightarrow{OX}=k\,\overrightarrow{OY}$ then $X$ and $Y$ lie on the same line through $O$, and

$OX:XY=k:(1-k)$

For example $\overrightarrow{OX}=\tfrac23\overrightarrow{ON}$ gives $OX:XN=2:1$.

Two different area rules
Same height
Triangles $OAC$ and $OCB$ share the perpendicular height from $O$ to $AB$, so their areas are in the ratio of their bases: $AC:CB$.
Similar triangles
Lengths in the ratio $1:k$ give areas in the ratio $1:k^{2}$. Showing $\overrightarrow{PQ}=k\,\overrightarrow{AB}$ is what proves the similarity.
What you knowLength ratioArea ratio
$\overrightarrow{PQ}=\tfrac12\overrightarrow{AB}$$1:2$$1:4$
$\overrightarrow{PQ}=\tfrac13\overrightarrow{AB}$$1:3$$1:9$
$\overrightarrow{PQ}=\tfrac34\overrightarrow{AB}$$3:4$$9:16$
!
Do not mix the two rules. Triangles sharing a height use the base ratio. Similar triangles use the square of the length ratio. Decide which situation you are in before writing an area ratio.

The first tab splits a triangle at a ratio point and shares the area out by the bases; the second tab shrinks the triangle by a scale factor and squares it. Both tabs give you the sentence the mark scheme wants.

6 Exam technique

A Paper 4 vectors question is a ladder: each part feeds the next. Part (a) is nearly always one mark for a route you can see immediately; the last part is the proof or ratio that uses everything above it.

  • Label the diagram as you go — write each answer onto the sketch next to the point.
  • Never skip an early part. Later parts are marked follow-through from your answer.
  • Simplify every answer to the form $\alpha\mathbf{a}+\beta\mathbf{b}$; unsimplified answers lose the accuracy mark.
  • Check the coefficients add to $1$ whenever the point lies on the line joining the two base points.
  • Finish a proof with a sentence. The algebra earns the method marks; the conclusion earns the last one.
Sentences worth memorising
To prove…Write…
Parallel"$\overrightarrow{PQ}=k\,\overrightarrow{AB}$, so $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ is a scalar multiple of $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and therefore $PQ$ is parallel to $AB$."
Collinear"$\overrightarrow{XZ}=k\,\overrightarrow{XY}$, so they are parallel, and they share the common point $X$; therefore $X$, $Y$ and $Z$ lie on a straight line."
A ratio"Since $\overrightarrow{OX}=\tfrac23\overrightarrow{ON}$, the point $X$ divides $ON$ so that $OX:XN=2:1$."
Equal areas"The two triangles have the same height and equal bases, so their areas are equal."
!
Notation still counts. Underline your vector letters, and write column vectors with one number above the other and no comma. An examiner cannot award a mark for something they cannot read as a vector.

✍️ Worked examples

Example 1
Two unknowns by comparing coefficients
 ⌄

$\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are not parallel. Given $(2x+y)\mathbf{a}+(x-3y)\mathbf{b}=8\mathbf{a}-3\mathbf{b}$, find $x$ and $y$.

1

Because $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are not parallel, the coefficients must match separately.

2

The $\mathbf{a}$ terms: $2x+y=8$.

3

The $\mathbf{b}$ terms: $x-3y=-3$.

4

From the first, $y=8-2x$. Substituting: $x-3(8-2x)=-3$.

5

$x-24+6x=-3\Rightarrow 7x=21\Rightarrow x=3$, so $y=8-6=2$.

Answer. $x=3$ and $y=2$
✎
Check. $2(3)+2=8$ ✓ and $3-3(2)=-3$ ✓. Enter these coefficients into Lab 1 to see the same two equations appear.
Example 2
Finding an intersection point
 ⌄

In triangle $OAB$, $\overrightarrow{OA}=\mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB}=\mathbf{b}$. $M$ is the midpoint of $OA$ and $N$ is the midpoint of $AB$. $BM$ and $ON$ intersect at $X$. Find $\overrightarrow{OX}$.

1

Set up the two lines. $\overrightarrow{OM}=\tfrac12\mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{ON}=\tfrac12(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})$.

2

$X$ on $ON$: $\overrightarrow{OX}=t\,\overrightarrow{ON}=\tfrac12t\,\mathbf{a}+\tfrac12t\,\mathbf{b}$.

3

$X$ on $BM$: $\overrightarrow{BM}=-\mathbf{b}+\tfrac12\mathbf{a}$, so $\overrightarrow{OX}=\mathbf{b}+s\left(\tfrac12\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\right)=\tfrac12s\,\mathbf{a}+(1-s)\mathbf{b}$.

4

Compare $\mathbf{a}$: $\tfrac12t=\tfrac12s$, so $t=s$.

5

Compare $\mathbf{b}$: $\tfrac12t=1-s=1-t$, so $\tfrac32t=1$ and $t=\tfrac23$.

6

Substitute back: $\overrightarrow{OX}=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac23\mathbf{a}+\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac23\mathbf{b}$.

Answer. $\overrightarrow{OX}=\tfrac13\mathbf{a}+\tfrac13\mathbf{b}=\tfrac13(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})$
✎
Bonus result. Since $\overrightarrow{OX}=\tfrac23\overrightarrow{ON}$, the ratio $OX:XN=2:1$. $X$ is the centroid, and every median splits this way. Lab 2 starts on exactly this case.
Example 3
Proving parallel and stating the ratio
 ⌄

In triangle $OAB$, $P$ lies on $OA$ with $OP:PA=3:1$ and $Q$ lies on $OB$ with $OQ:QB=3:1$. Show that $PQ$ is parallel to $AB$ and find $PQ:AB$.

1

$OP:PA=3:1$ means $P$ is $\tfrac34$ of the way along $OA$, so $\overrightarrow{OP}=\tfrac34\mathbf{a}$.

2

Similarly $\overrightarrow{OQ}=\tfrac34\mathbf{b}$.

3

$\overrightarrow{PQ}=\overrightarrow{PO}+\overrightarrow{OQ}=-\tfrac34\mathbf{a}+\tfrac34\mathbf{b}=\tfrac34(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a})$.

4

$\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}$, so $\overrightarrow{PQ}=\tfrac34\overrightarrow{AB}$.

Answer. $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ is a scalar multiple of $\overrightarrow{AB}$, therefore $PQ$ is parallel to $AB$, and $PQ:AB=3:4$
✎
Extension. Triangles $OPQ$ and $OAB$ are similar with lengths $3:4$, so their areas are in the ratio $9:16$. Check it on the second tab of Lab 3.
Example 4
An unknown from a collinearity condition
 ⌄

$\overrightarrow{OM}=2\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}$ and $\overrightarrow{ON}=4\mathbf{a}+k\mathbf{b}$. Given that $O$, $M$ and $N$ are collinear, find $k$ and the ratio $OM:MN$.

1

Collinear through $O$ means $\overrightarrow{ON}=\lambda\,\overrightarrow{OM}$.

2

$4\mathbf{a}+k\mathbf{b}=\lambda(2\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})=2\lambda\mathbf{a}+\lambda\mathbf{b}$.

3

Compare $\mathbf{a}$: $4=2\lambda$, so $\lambda=2$.

4

Compare $\mathbf{b}$: $k=\lambda=2$.

5

Since $\overrightarrow{ON}=2\overrightarrow{OM}$, $M$ is the midpoint of $ON$, so $MN=OM$.

Answer. $k=2$ and $OM:MN=1:1$
!
The question asks for $OM:MN$, not $OM:ON$ (which would be $1:2$). Mark the points on the line before writing a ratio down.
Example 5
An area ratio from a ratio point
 ⌄

In triangle $OAB$, $C$ lies on $AB$ with $AC:CB=3:1$. The area of $OAB$ is $32\text{ cm}^{2}$. Find the area of triangle $OAC$.

1

$OAC$ and $OCB$ share the same perpendicular height from $O$ to the line $AB$.

2

So their areas are in the ratio of their bases: $\text{area }OAC:\text{area }OCB=3:1$.

3

$OAC$ therefore takes $3$ parts out of $3+1=4$.

4

$\tfrac34\times32=24$.

Answer. $24\text{ cm}^{2}$
!
Reasoning mark. The mark scheme wants the words "same height" or "same perpendicular distance from $O$ to $AB$". The arithmetic alone is not a justification.
Example 6
A magnitude with a direction condition
 ⌄

$\mathbf{a}=\begin{pmatrix}3\\-4\end{pmatrix}$. Find the vector $\mathbf{b}$ that is parallel to $\mathbf{a}$, points in the same direction, and has magnitude $15$.

1

$|\mathbf{a}|=\sqrt{3^{2}+(-4)^{2}}=\sqrt{25}=5$.

2

Parallel means $\mathbf{b}=\lambda\mathbf{a}$ for some scalar $\lambda$.

3

$|\mathbf{b}|=|\lambda|\times|\mathbf{a}|=5|\lambda|=15$, so $|\lambda|=3$.

4

Same direction means $\lambda$ is positive, so $\lambda=3$.

Answer. $\mathbf{b}=3\mathbf{a}=\begin{pmatrix}9\\-12\end{pmatrix}$
!
Had the question said "opposite direction", the answer would be $-3\mathbf{a}=\begin{pmatrix}-9\\12\end{pmatrix}$. The direction condition is what fixes the sign.
Example 7
A full multi-part question
 ⌄

$OPQR$ is a parallelogram with $\overrightarrow{OP}=\mathbf{p}$ and $\overrightarrow{OR}=\mathbf{r}$. $M$ is the midpoint of $PQ$ and $N$ is the midpoint of $QR$. Find $\overrightarrow{MN}$ and state its relationship to $\overrightarrow{PR}$.

1

The diagonal. $\overrightarrow{PQ}=\overrightarrow{OR}=\mathbf{r}$ (opposite sides of a parallelogram), so $\overrightarrow{OQ}=\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{r}$.

2

Point $M$. $\overrightarrow{OM}=\overrightarrow{OP}+\tfrac12\overrightarrow{PQ}=\mathbf{p}+\tfrac12\mathbf{r}$.

3

Point $N$. $\overrightarrow{QR}=-\mathbf{p}$, so $\overrightarrow{ON}=\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{r}-\tfrac12\mathbf{p}=\tfrac12\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{r}$.

4

The vector $\overrightarrow{MN}$. $\left(\tfrac12\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{r}\right)-\left(\mathbf{p}+\tfrac12\mathbf{r}\right)=-\tfrac12\mathbf{p}+\tfrac12\mathbf{r}=\tfrac12(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{p})$.

5

Compare with $\overrightarrow{PR}$. $\overrightarrow{PR}=-\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{p}$, so $\overrightarrow{MN}=\tfrac12\overrightarrow{PR}$.

Answer. $\overrightarrow{MN}=\tfrac12(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{p})$; $MN$ is parallel to $PR$ with $MN:PR=1:2$
✎
This is the midpoint theorem. The line joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle (here $PQR$) is parallel to the third side and half its length — proved entirely with vectors.

! Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using the same parameter for both lines in an intersection question — you need $t$ and $s$.
  • Comparing coefficients without the condition. It is valid only because $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are not parallel — say so.
  • Answering the wrong ratio. $OX:XN$ is not $OX:ON$.
  • Subtracting magnitudes. $|\overrightarrow{AB}|$ is not $|\mathbf{b}|-|\mathbf{a}|$ — find the vector first, then its length.
  • Using the base ratio for similar triangles. Similar triangles need $k^{2}$; only triangles sharing a height use the bases directly.
  • Stopping at the algebra in a proof. "Parallel" and "share a common point" are separate marks.
  • Leaving an answer unsimplified. The last accuracy mark usually depends on collecting the terms.
  • Skipping an early part you find hard. Later parts are marked follow-through, so always write something.
