Proportion questions look wordy, but every single one — direct or inverse, any power — is solved by the same four steps. This lesson drills those steps, and the labs let you watch what actually happens to $y$ when $x$ doubles, for every form of proportion on the syllabus.

◎ What you will be able to do

  • Tell direct and inverse proportion apart.
  • Solve proportion problems algebraically with the four-step method.
  • Translate word problems into proportion equations.
  • Identify the relationship from a table of data.

1 Direct proportion

As one quantity increases, the other increases in the same ratio.

✎
Definition. $y$ is directly proportional to $x$ means $y\div x$ is always the same number.
$y\propto x\ \longrightarrow\ y=kx\ \longrightarrow\ k=\dfrac{y}{x}$

The symbol $\propto$ means "is proportional to". The number $k$ is the constant of proportionality — it never changes within a problem.

Every direct proportion graph passes through $(0,0)$. When $x=0$, $y=0$. If a graph misses the origin, it is not proportional.

2 Inverse proportion

As one quantity increases, the other decreases in the same ratio.

✎
Definition. $y$ is inversely proportional to $x$ means $y\times x$ is always the same number.
$y\propto \dfrac{1}{x}\ \longrightarrow\ y=\dfrac{k}{x}\ \longrightarrow\ k=xy$
StatementEquationConstant testGraph shape
$y\propto \frac{1}{x}$$y=\frac{k}{x}$$xy$Hyperbola, asymptotes on both axes
$y\propto \frac{1}{x^{2}}$$y=\frac{k}{x^{2}}$$x^{2}y$Falls away much more steeply
$y\propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$$y=\frac{k}{\sqrt{x}}$$\sqrt{x}\cdot y$Falls away more gently
!
Watch out. "Inversely proportional" never means "subtract". $y=k-x$ is not inverse proportion. Inverse proportion always puts $x$ in the denominator.

Powers change the scale factor. If $y\propto x^{2}$ and $x$ doubles, $y$ is multiplied by $2^{2}=4$. If $y\propto 1/x^{2}$ and $x$ doubles, $y$ is divided by 4.

3 The four-step method Interactive

Every proportion question — direct or inverse, any power — is solved the same way.

  • Step 1. Write the equation with $k$: e.g. $y=kx^{2}$ or $y=k/x$.
  • Step 2. Substitute the given pair of values to find $k$.
  • Step 3. Write the complete formula with the value of $k$ in place.
  • Step 4. Use the formula — substitute the new value and solve.
!
Never skip step 3. Writing the full formula earns marks on its own and prevents mistakes later in the question.

4 Spotting the type from data Interactive

  • Work out $y\div x$ for each pair. Constant → direct, $y=kx$.
  • If not, work out $x\times y$. Constant → inverse, $y=k/x$.
  • If neither, try $y\div x^{2}$ (direct with the square), then $x^{2}\times y$ (inverse square).

5 Translating word problems

Wording in the questionWrite
"…is proportional to…", "…varies directly as…"$y=kx$
"…is proportional to the square of…"$y=kx^{2}$
"…is proportional to the square root of…"$y=k\sqrt{x}$
"…is inversely proportional to…", "…varies inversely as…"$y=\frac{k}{x}$
"…is inversely proportional to the square of…"$y=\frac{k}{x^{2}}$
"…is inversely proportional to the cube of…"$y=\frac{k}{x^{3}}$
  • Name the two quantities and give each a sensible letter ($C$ for cost, $t$ for time).
  • Decide which quantity depends on which — the one being predicted goes on the left.
  • Read the wording carefully: direct or inverse, and is there a power?
  • Write the equation, then follow the four-step method.
  • Answer in words, with the correct units.
✎
Sense check. In an inverse problem a larger input must give a smaller output. If your answer went the wrong way, you have probably used the wrong type. Classic inverse contexts: more workers → less time; higher speed → less journey time; further away → weaker force.

✍️ Worked examples

Example 1
Simple direct proportion
 ⌄

$y$ is directly proportional to $x$. When $x=3$, $y=12$. Find $y$ when $x=7$, and find $x$ when $y=30$.

1

Write the equation: $y=kx$.

2

Substitute $x=3$, $y=12$: $12=3k$, so $k=4$.

3

Full formula: $y=4x$.

4

When $x=7$: $y=4\times7=28$. When $y=30$: $30=4x$, so $x=7.5$.

Answer. $y=28$ · $x=7.5$. Reproduce it in Lab 2 with known pair $(3,12)$.
Example 2
Direct proportion to a square
 ⌄

$y$ is proportional to $x^{2}$. When $x=3$, $y=18$. Find $y$ when $x=4$, and the positive value of $x$ when $y=50$.

$y=kx^{2}$ → $18=k\times3^{2}=9k$ → $k=2$ → $y=2x^{2}$.

When $x=4$: $y=2\times16=32$. When $y=50$: $50=2x^{2}$ → $x^{2}=25$ → $x=5$.

!
$k\times3^{2}$ means $k\times9$, not $(3k)^{2}=9k^{2}$. Square the number first, then multiply by $k$.
Answer. $y=32$ · $x=5$.
Example 3
Simple inverse proportion
 ⌄

$y$ is inversely proportional to $x$. When $x=5$, $y=8$. Find $y$ when $x=2$, and $x$ when $y=10$.

$y=\dfrac{k}{x}$ → $8=\dfrac{k}{5}$ → $k=40$ → $y=\dfrac{40}{x}$.

When $x=2$: $y=40\div2=20$. When $y=10$: $10=\dfrac{40}{x}$ → $x=4$.

Sense check. $x$ fell from 5 to 2, so $y$ should rise — and it did, from 8 to 20. ✔
Example 4
Inverse proportion to a square
 ⌄

$y$ is inversely proportional to $x^{2}$. When $x=3$, $y=4$. Find $y$ when $x=6$.

$y=\dfrac{k}{x^{2}}$ → $4=\dfrac{k}{9}$ → $k=36$ → $y=\dfrac{36}{x^{2}}$. When $x=6$: $y=\dfrac{36}{36}=1$.

Shortcut. $x$ doubled (3 → 6), so for an inverse square $y$ is divided by $2^{2}=4$: $4\div4=1$ ✔ — the same rule Lab 1 shows in the "scale-factor rule" row.
Example 5
Contextual problem — inverse square
 ⌄

The force $F$ newtons between two magnets is inversely proportional to the square of the distance $d$ cm between them. When $d=3$, $F=20$. (a) Find $F$ when $d=5$. (b) Find $d$ when $F=45$.

1

Translate the wording: $F=\dfrac{k}{d^{2}}$.

2

Substitute $d=3$, $F=20$: $20=\dfrac{k}{9}$, so $k=180$.

3

$F=\dfrac{180}{d^{2}}$.

4

(a) $d=5$: $F=\dfrac{180}{25}=7.2$ N.
(b) $45=\dfrac{180}{d^{2}}$ → $d^{2}=4$ → $d=2$ cm (a distance must be positive).

Answer. (a) 7.2 N · (b) 2 cm. Both make sense: further apart → weaker force; stronger force → closer together.
Example 6
Word problem — workers and time
 ⌄

It takes 6 workers 10 days to build a wall. Assuming the time is inversely proportional to the number of workers, how long would 4 workers take? How many workers are needed to finish in 5 days?

Let $t$ = time in days and $n$ = number of workers. More workers means less time, so $t=\dfrac{k}{n}$.

$10=\dfrac{k}{6}$ → $k=60$ (the total "worker-days" of effort) → $t=\dfrac{60}{n}$.

With $n=4$: $t=15$ days. For $t=5$: $5=\dfrac{60}{n}$ → $n=12$ workers.

Answer. 4 workers take 15 days; 12 workers are needed to finish in 5 days.
!
Answers must be sensible in context — you cannot have 11.4 workers, so round up to a whole number of people if the numbers are not exact.
Example 7
Identifying the relationship from a table
 ⌄

Find the relationship connecting $x$ and $y$: $(2,40)\ (4,10)\ (5,6.4)\ (8,2.5)$.

Try $y\div x$: $20,\ 2.5,\ 1.28,\ 0.3125$ — not constant, so not direct.

Try $x\times y$: $80,\ 40,\ 32,\ 20$ — not constant, so not simple inverse.

Try $x^{2}\times y$: $4\times40=160$, $16\times10=160$, $25\times6.4=160$, $64\times2.5=160$ — constant.

Answer. $y$ is inversely proportional to $x^{2}$, with $y=\dfrac{160}{x^{2}}$. This exact set is loaded in Lab 3.

! Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using $y=kx$ for an inverse problem. "Inversely" always puts $x$ on the bottom.
  • Leaving out $k$. $y\propto x$ is a statement, not an equation — you cannot calculate with it until it becomes $y=kx$.
  • Squaring $k$ by mistake. In $y=kx^{2}$, only $x$ is squared.
  • Doubling $y$ when $x$ doubles in a squared relationship. For $y\propto x^{2}$ the factor is $2^{2}=4$.
  • Forgetting the negative root — or keeping it. $x^{2}=25$ gives $x=\pm5$, but a length, mass or distance must be positive.
  • Not sense-checking the direction. In an inverse problem a bigger input must give a smaller output.
