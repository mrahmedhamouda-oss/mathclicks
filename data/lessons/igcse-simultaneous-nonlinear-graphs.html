Where two graphs cross is where two equations are true at once. This lesson turns that one idea into a full method — solving simultaneous equations from a graph, spotting tangents, sketching the four standard non-linear shapes, and reading domain and range. Six labs let you move the line yourself and watch the number of solutions change.

◎ What you will be able to do

  • Solve simultaneous linear and non-linear equations graphically.
  • Interpret points of intersection — and know when there are 2, 1 or 0 of them.
  • Sketch and label the four standard non-linear curves.
  • Identify domains, ranges and key graph features.

1 Solving simultaneous equations graphically Interactive

✎
Key rule. A point lies on a graph only if its coordinates satisfy that equation. So a point lying on both graphs satisfies both equations — and that is exactly what a simultaneous solution is.
Solutions $=$ the coordinates of the points of intersection

Each intersection gives a pair of values: an $x$ and its matching $y$. A curve and a line usually meet twice, so you normally get two pairs.

The method
  • Draw the non-linear graph accurately, usually from a table of values.
  • Draw the straight line on the same axes — plot two or three points and join with a ruler.
  • Mark every point where the two graphs cross.
  • Read off the $x$- and $y$-coordinate of each crossing and write the answers as pairs.
  • Check by substituting into both original equations.
!
Watch out. Answers go in pairs — $x=2,\ y=4$ belong together and $x=-1,\ y=1$ belong together. Never mix an $x$ from one point with a $y$ from the other.
Lab 1 — the intersection explorer

Pick a curve, then move the line with the $m$ and $c$ sliders. The panel reports how many solutions there are and the coordinate pairs. Try to find, for each curve, a line that gives 2 solutions, one that gives 1, and one that gives none.

Algebraic check (substitution)
  • Substitute the linear equation into the non-linear one to remove $y$.
  • Rearrange to the form $ax^{2}+bx+c=0$.
  • Solve by factorising or with the quadratic formula.
  • Put each $x$ back into the linear equation (it is easier) to find the matching $y$.

  • The $x$-coordinate is the value that makes the two expressions equal.
  • The $y$-coordinate is the common value of both expressions at that point.
  • Either side of an intersection, one graph is above the other — that tells you which expression is greater in that region (useful for inequalities).

3 Sketching non-linear graphs Interactive

A sketch is not a plot — it needs the correct shape and the key points labelled, not accurate scales.

Quadratic $y=ax^{2}+bx+c$ — parabola. $a>0$ → ∪ with a minimum; $a<0$ → ∩ with a maximum. Symmetrical about $x=\frac{-b}{2a}$.
Cubic $y=ax^{3}+\dots$ — S-shaped. For $a>0$ it rises from bottom-left to top-right. Up to two turning points and up to three roots.
Reciprocal $y=\frac{a}{x}$ — two branches with asymptotes $x=0$ and $y=0$. Never crosses either axis.
Exponential $y=a^{x}$ — always positive, passes through $(0,1)$, asymptote $y=0$. Grows for $a>1$, decays for $0

Example 1
Curve meets line — two solutions
 ⌄

Solve graphically the simultaneous equations $y=x^{2}$ and $y=x+2$ for $-3\le x\le 3$.

x−3−2−10123
$y=x^2$9410149

For the line $y=x+2$, plot $(-2,0)$, $(0,2)$ and $(3,5)$ and join with a ruler. Mark the two crossings and read their coordinates: $(-1,1)$ and $(2,4)$. Set $m=1$, $c=2$ on the $y=x^{2}$ curve in Lab 1 to see it.

Answer. $x=2,\ y=4$  or  $x=-1,\ y=1$.

Example 3
Reciprocal curve meets a line
 ⌄

Solve graphically $y=\dfrac{4}{x}$ and $y=x+3$.

x−4−2−1124
y−1−2−4421

Draw both branches, leaving a gap at $x=0$, then draw the line through $(0,3)$ and $(-3,0)$. The line meets each branch once — select $y=4/x$ in Lab 1 and set $m=1$, $c=3$.

Example 4
Sketching a cubic
 ⌄

Sketch $y=x^{3}-4x$, showing the roots and describing the shape.

1

Factorise: $x^{3}-4x=x(x^{2}-4)=x(x-2)(x+2)$.

2

Set each factor to zero: $x=0$, $x=2$, $x=-2$ — three roots.

3

y-intercept: $x=0\Rightarrow y=0$, so it passes through the origin.

4

The coefficient of $x^{3}$ is positive, so the curve comes up from the bottom-left and heads to the top-right.

5

Between the roots there is a local maximum (between $-2$ and $0$) and a local minimum (between $0$ and $2$).

Answer. An S-shaped cubic crossing the x-axis at $-2,\ 0$ and $2$, with a local maximum near $(-1.15,\ 3.1)$ and a local minimum near $(1.15,\ -3.1)$. Domain: all real $x$. Range: all real $y$. Select $y=x^{3}-4x$ in Lab 1 to compare with your sketch.
Example 5
Domain and range
 ⌄

State the domain and range of (a) $y=x^{2}$ for $-1\le x\le 3$, (b) $y=\dfrac{4}{x}$, (c) $y=2^{x}$.

(a) The domain is given. Over that stretch the smallest value is at the vertex $x=0$, giving $y=0$; the largest is at $x=3$, giving $y=9$.

Domain: $-1\le x\le 3$  ·  Range: $0\le y\le 9$

(b) Division by zero is not allowed, and the curve never reaches the x-axis.

Domain: $x\neq 0$  ·  Range: $y\neq 0$

(c) Any power of 2 is positive; the curve sits entirely above its asymptote $y=0$ and passes through $(0,1)$.

Domain: all real $x$  ·  Range: $y>0$
Watch out. Don't just test the endpoints — the vertex sits inside the domain in part (a) and produces the minimum. Check all three in Lab 5.

! Common mistakes to avoid

  • Giving only the x-values. Simultaneous equations need both coordinates, paired correctly.
  • Stopping after one intersection. A line and a curve usually meet twice — check the whole grid, including the second branch of a reciprocal graph.
  • Confusing domain and range. Domain is horizontal ($x$), range is vertical ($y$).
  • Using only endpoints for the range. If a turning point lies inside the domain, it gives the maximum or minimum.
  • Drawing a curve through an asymptote. The curve approaches it but never touches or crosses it.
  • Sketching a cubic as a parabola. A cubic is S-shaped, with the two ends going in opposite directions.

1. Two graphs are drawn on the same axes. The solutions of the simultaneous equations are:

  • The x-intercepts of both graphs
  • Only the x-coordinates where they cross
  • The coordinates of every point of intersection
  • The y-intercepts of both graphs

2. A line touches a curve at exactly one point. This means:

  • There are no solutions
  • There is one repeated solution and the line is a tangent
  • There are two solutions very close together
  • The line is parallel to the curve
