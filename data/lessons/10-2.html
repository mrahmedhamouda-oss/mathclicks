Core Concepts

1. Central Angles — the angle that "owns" the arc

A central angle is an angle that sits right at the middle of the circle. Its vertex is the center, and both of its sides are radii. Because the two sides reach all the way out to the circle, a central angle "cuts out" a piece of the circle — and the size of that piece is exactly the size of the angle.

central angle of a circle — an angle with a vertex at the center of a circle and sides that are radii. In ☉B, ∠ABC is a central angle.
degree — 1⁄360 of the circular rotation about a point. That is why one full turn is 360°.
Key Concept · Sum of Central Angles
Words: The sum of the measures of the central angles of a circle with no interior points in common is 360°.
Example: m∠1 + m∠2 + m∠3 = 360°
Why it works: "No interior points in common" just means the angles do not overlap. Laid side by side they sweep out one complete rotation, and one complete rotation is 360 degrees.

2. Arcs: minor, major, and semicircle

An arc is a piece of the circle itself — the curved part, not the region inside. Any two points on a circle split it into two arcs: a short one and a long one.

arc — part of a circle defined by two endpoints.
minor arc — an arc measuring less than 180°. Its measure equals its related central angle: m(arc PR) = m∠PQR.
major arc — an arc measuring more than 180°. m(arc PSR) = 360° − m(arc PR).
semicircle — an arc measuring exactly 180°. Its endpoints lie on a diameter: m(arc RST) = 180°.
congruent arcs — arcs in the same circle, or in congruent circles, that have the same measure.
adjacent arcs — arcs of a circle that have exactly one point in common.
Study Tip — Naming Arcs: Minor arcs are named by just their two endpoints (arc PR). Major arcs and semicircles need three letters: the two endpoints plus a point on the arc that lies between them (arc PSR). Without the middle letter nobody can tell which way around the circle you went.
Theorem 10.2
Words: In the same circle or in congruent circles, two minor arcs are congruent if and only if their central angles are congruent.
Example: If ∠1 ≅ ∠2, then arc FG ≅ arc HJ. If arc FG ≅ arc HJ, then ∠1 ≅ ∠2.
Watch Out! "If and only if" means the statement works in both directions. Equal central angles force equal arcs, and equal arcs force equal central angles — but only inside the same circle or congruent circles. A 60° arc on a tiny circle and a 60° arc on a huge circle have the same measure but very different lengths.

3. Adding arcs

If two arcs touch at exactly one point, you can glue them together and just add their measures — exactly like adjacent angles.

Postulate 10.2 · Arc Addition Postulate
Words: The measure of an arc formed by two adjacent arcs is the sum of the measures of the two arcs.
Example: m(arc XZ) = m(arc XY) + m(arc YZ)

4. Arc length — how far is it, really?

The measure of an arc is in degrees. The arc length is the actual distance you would walk along the curve, in centimetres, metres, inches … An arc is a fraction of the whole circle, so its length is that same fraction of the circumference.

Key Concept · Arc Length in Degrees
Words: The ratio of the length of an arc ℓ to the circumference of the circle is equal to the ratio of the degree measure of the arc to 360°.
Proportion:   ℓ ÷ (2πr) = x ÷ 360
Equation:     ℓ = (x / 360) · 2πr
Watch Out! — Arc Length vs. Arc Measure: The length of an arc is given in linear units such as centimetres. The measure of an arc is given in degrees. They are different quantities and they are never interchangeable.
Problem-Solving Tip: If you forget the formula, rebuild it. An arc is a portion of a circle, so its length is a fraction of the circumference. That fraction is (arc measure)/360. Multiply it by 2πr and you are done.

5. Radian measure

Degrees are an arbitrary human choice. A radian is the natural unit: it is the angle you get when the arc you cut off is exactly as long as the radius.

arc length — the distance between the endpoints of an arc measured along the arc, in linear units.
radian — a unit of angular measurement equal to 180°/π, or about 57.296°.
Key Concept · Radian Measure
The radian measure θ of a central angle is the ratio of the arc length to the radius of the circle.
θ = ℓ / r   radians   ⇒   ℓ = rθ
Key Concept · Degree and Radian Conversion Rules
To convert a degree measure to radians, multiply by π radians / 180°.
To convert a radian measure to degrees, multiply by 180° / π radians.

Both rules are just multiplying by 1, because 180° = π radians. Put the unit you want on top.

1
Central angles that do not overlap add to 360°.
2
A minor arc has the same measure as its central angle. A major arc is 360° − the minor arc. A semicircle is 180°.
3
Adjacent arcs add: m(arc XZ) = m(arc XY) + m(arc YZ).
4
Arc length: ℓ = (x/360) · 2πr  (degrees)  or  ℓ = rθ  (radians).
5
180° = π radians. Degrees → radians: × π/180. Radians → degrees: × 180/π.

Common mistakes to avoid:
  • Forgetting that a right-angle square in the figure is worth 90° in the sum.
  • Naming a major arc with only two letters — it becomes ambiguous with the minor arc.
  • Confusing arc measure (degrees) with arc length (cm, m, in.).
  • Plugging the diameter into 2πr and doubling the answer.
  • Using ℓ = rθ while θ is still in degrees — that formula only works in radians.
  • Applying Theorem 10.2 across circles of different sizes; it needs the same circle or congruent circles.
  • Rounding halfway through. Keep the exact value (like 27π/4) until the last step.
