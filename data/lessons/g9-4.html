Core Concepts

Why "special"?

Two right triangles show up so often that memorising their side ratios is faster than running the Pythagorean Theorem every time. Because all triangles with the same three angles are similar, every 45°-45°-90° triangle in the world has the same side ratio, and so does every 30°-60°-90° triangle. Learn the ratio once, use it forever.

45°-45°-90° triangle — an isosceles right triangle; its two acute angles are each 45°.
30°-60°-90° triangle — half of an equilateral triangle, cut along an altitude.

1. The 45°-45°-90° triangle

Draw a diagonal in a square. The diagonal cuts the square into two congruent isosceles right triangles. The base angles of an isosceles triangle are congruent, so each acute angle measures 90° ÷ 2 = 45°.

Theorem 9.8 · 45°-45°-90° Triangle Theorem
In a 45°-45°-90° triangle, the legs ℓ are congruent and the length of the hypotenuse h is √2 times the length of a leg.
h = ℓ√2   (find the hypotenuse from a leg)
ℓ = h / √2 = (h√2)/2   (find a leg from the hypotenuse)

Why it works: with legs ℓ and ℓ, h² = ℓ² + ℓ² = 2ℓ², so h = ℓ√2.

2. The 30°-60°-90° triangle

Drop an altitude from any vertex of an equilateral triangle. It splits the triangle into two congruent 30°-60°-90° triangles. In △ABC equilateral with altitude BD, △ABD ≅ △CBD, so AD ≅ CD. If AD = x then CD = x and AC = 2x; since the triangle is equilateral, AB = 2x as well.

a² + x² = (2x)²   Pythagorean Theorem
a² + x² = 4x²   Simplify
a² = 3x²   Subtract x² from each side
a = x√3   Simplify
Theorem 9.9 · 30°-60°-90° Triangle Theorem
In a 30°-60°-90° triangle, the length of the hypotenuse h is 2 times the length of the shorter leg s, and the longer leg ℓ is √3 times the length of the shorter leg.
h = 2s     ℓ = s√3     s : ℓ : h = 1 : √3 : 2

3. Matching a side to its angle

Every rule above is really about which angle a side is opposite. Find the shorter leg first — everything else is measured from it.

Identify the right angle. The side opposite it is the hypotenuse — always the longest side.
Find the 30° angle. The side opposite the 30° angle is the shorter leg s.
The side opposite the 60° angle is the longer leg ℓ = s√3.
The hypotenuse is h = 2s. If you are given ℓ or h, solve for s first, then get the rest.
Watch Out! — the classic mix-up. Ian claims that "sometimes the 30° angle is opposite the longer leg." Disagree. In a triangle, the largest angle is always opposite the longest side. Since 30° < 60° < 90°, the side opposite 30° is always the shortest, the side opposite 60° is always the middle, and the side opposite 90° is always the longest — with no exceptions.

4. Rationalizing the denominator

When you divide by a radical you get an answer like 18/√2. That is correct, but the conventional form has no radical downstairs. Multiply the numerator and denominator by the radical that is in the denominator.

18/√2 = (18/√2)·(√2/√2) = 18√2 / 2 = 9√2
12/√3 = (12/√3)·(√3/√3) = 12√3 / 3 = 4√3
Study Tip. Dividing by √2 is the same as multiplying by √2 and then halving. Dividing by √3 is the same as multiplying by √3 and then dividing by 3.
1
45-45-90: ℓ : ℓ : h = 1 : 1 : √2. Multiply a leg by √2 to go up; divide the hypotenuse by √2 to come back down.
2
30-60-90: s : ℓ : h = 1 : √3 : 2. The shorter leg is the key that unlocks the other two sides.
3
The diagonal of a square of side s is s√2. The altitude of an equilateral triangle of side s is (s√3)/2.
4
Never use √2 on a 30-60-90 triangle and never use √3 on a 45-45-90 triangle. Check the angles before you pick the radical.

Common mistakes to avoid
  • Multiplying by √2 when you were given the hypotenuse. Given h, you must divide.
  • Using √2 on a 30-60-90 triangle (or √3 on a 45-45-90). Read the angles first.
  • Treating the longer leg as the shorter leg. Shorter leg is opposite 30°, longer leg is opposite 60°.
  • Doubling the longer leg to get the hypotenuse. Only the shorter leg doubles: h = 2s.
  • Forgetting that the altitude of an equilateral triangle bisects the base, so the shorter leg is s/2, not s.
  • Leaving a radical in a denominator: write 18/√2 as 9√2.
