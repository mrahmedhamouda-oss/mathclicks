Core Concepts

What trigonometry actually is

The word trigonometry comes from the Greek trigon (triangle) and metron (measure) — literally "triangle measurement." A trigonometric ratio is a ratio of the lengths of two sides of a right triangle.

The whole subject rests on one fact from similarity: if two right triangles share an acute angle, they are similar (AA), so their corresponding sides are proportional. That means the ratio opp/hyp depends only on the angle, not on how big the triangle is. So we can give that ratio a name and put it in a calculator.

trigonometry — the study of the relationships between the sides and angles of triangles.
trigonometric ratio — a ratio of the lengths of two sides of a right triangle.
solving a triangle — finding every unknown side length and angle measure of a triangle.
inverse sine / cosine / tangent — the operation that takes a ratio back to the angle that produced it.

Naming the sides: it depends on which angle you stand at

Before you can write any ratio you must label the three sides relative to a chosen acute angle, called the reference angle.

  • The hypotenuse is always the side across from the right angle. It never changes.
  • The opposite leg is the leg that does not touch the reference angle.
  • The adjacent leg is the leg that does touch the reference angle (and is not the hypotenuse).
Watch out! "Opposite" and "adjacent" swap the moment you switch reference angles. In the same triangle, the leg that is opposite ∠A is adjacent to ∠B. See the two figures below — same triangle, opposite colours.

Key Concept · Trigonometric Ratios

Sine
If △ABC is a right triangle, then the sine of each acute angle in △ABC is the ratio of the length of the leg opposite that angle to the length of the hypotenuse.
sin A = opp / hyp = a/c    sin B = opp / hyp = b/c
Cosine
If △ABC is a right triangle, then the cosine of each acute angle in △ABC is the ratio of the length of the leg adjacent to that angle to the length of the hypotenuse.
cos A = adj / hyp = b/c    cos B = adj / hyp = a/c
Tangent
If △ABC is a right triangle, then the tangent of each acute angle in △ABC is the ratio of the length of the leg opposite that angle to the length of the leg adjacent to that angle.
tan A = opp / adj = a/b    tan B = opp / adj = b/a
Study Tip — memorising the ratios: SOH‑CAH‑TOA. Sine = Opposite over Hypotenuse, Cosine = Adjacent over Hypotenuse, Tangent = Opposite over Adjacent. Take the first letter of each word in the ratio.

Key Concept · Inverse Trigonometric Ratios

A trig ratio turns an angle into a number. The inverse turns the number back into the angle.

Inverse SineInverse CosineInverse Tangent
If ∠A is acute and the sine of A is x, then the inverse sine of x is the measure of ∠A. If ∠A is acute and the cosine of A is x, then the inverse cosine of x is the measure of ∠A. If ∠A is acute and the tangent of A is x, then the inverse tangent of x is the measure of ∠A.
If sin A = x, then sin−1x = m∠A If cos A = x, then cos−1x = m∠A If tan A = x, then tan−1x = m∠A
Watch out! sin−1x is read "the inverse sine of x". It is not a negative exponent: sin−1x ≠ 1/(sin x). The notation copies the inverse-function notation f−1(x).

Solving a right triangle

When you are given measurements and asked to find every unknown side and angle, that is called solving a triangle. To solve a right triangle you need to know either

two side lengths, or
one side length and the measure of one acute angle.

From there the toolkit is: a trig ratio (for a side or an inverse for an angle), the fact that the two acute angles are complementary, and the Pythagorean Theorem.

1
Choose your ratio by looking at which two sides are involved — the one you know and the one you want.
2
To find a side, use sin / cos / tan. To find an angle, use sin−1 / cos−1 / tan−1.
3
The acute angles of a right triangle are complementary: m∠A + m∠B = 90°. One inverse gives you both angles.
4
sin A = cos B and cos A = sin B whenever A and B are complementary — the sine of an angle equals the cosine of its complement.
5
Every trig ratio of an acute angle is a positive number, and sine and cosine are always less than 1 (a leg is always shorter than the hypotenuse).
Study Tip — graphing calculator: make sure the calculator is in degree mode, not radian mode. The inverse keys sin−1, cos−1, tan−1 are usually the second functions of the sin, cos, tan keys.
Study Tip — approximation: if you use a calculated measure to find another measure, do not round until the very last step. Rounding early snowballs.

Worked Examples

In right △JKL, ∠L = 90°, KL = 5, LJ = 12, KJ = 13. Find sin J, cos J, tan J, sin K, cos K, tan K. Express each ratio as a fraction and as a decimal to the nearest hundredth.
1From ∠J: opp = KL = 5, adj = LJ = 12, hyp = KJ = 13name the sides
2sin J = opp/hyp = 5/13 ≈ 0.38SOH
3cos J = adj/hyp = 12/13 ≈ 0.92CAH
4tan J = opp/adj = 5/12 ≈ 0.42TOA
5From ∠K: opp = LJ = 12, adj = KL = 5, hyp = 13sides swap roles
6sin K = 12/13 ≈ 0.92   cos K = 5/13 ≈ 0.38   tan K = 12/5 = 2.40SOH-CAH-TOA at K
sin J = 5/13, cos J = 12/13, tan J = 5/12; sin K = 12/13, cos K = 5/13, tan K = 12/5
Think about it: sin J = cos K and cos J = sin K. That is no accident — ∠J and ∠K are complementary, and tan J · tan K = (5/12)(12/5) = 1.
Use a special right triangle to express the sine of 60° as a fraction and as a decimal to the nearest hundredth.
1shorter leg = x, longer leg = x√3, hyp = 2x30°-60°-90° Triangle Theorem
2sin 60° = opp/hypdefinition of sine ratio
3= x√3 / 2xsubstitution (opposite 60° is the longer leg)
4= √3 / 2 ≈ 0.87divide out x; use a calculator
sin 60° = √3/2 ≈ 0.87
The trap: the side opposite the 60° angle is the longer leg x√3, not the shorter leg. Pairing 60° with x gives 1/2, which is actually sin 30°. Always look at the picture (see the 30–60–90 figure above).
Check: the x cancels, so the answer does not depend on the size of the triangle — exactly what similarity predicts.
In right △ABC the right angle is at B, the hypotenuse AC = 12 and m∠A = 4°. Find the leg adjacent to ∠A (call it x) and the leg opposite ∠A (call it y). Round to the nearest hundredth.

Find x (adjacent leg)

cos A = adj / hyp
cos 4° = x / 12
12 cos 4° = x
x ≈ 11.97

Find y (opposite leg)

sin A = opp / hyp
sin 4° = y / 12
12 sin 4° = y
y ≈ 0.84
1Known: hyp and one acute anglechoose cosine for adj, sine for opp
2Multiply each side by 12 to isolate the unknownthe unknown starts in the numerator
x ≈ 11.97  and  y ≈ 0.84
Check: 11.97² + 0.84² = 143.28 + 0.71 = 143.99 ≈ 12². ✓ A 4° angle is tiny, so the adjacent leg should be almost the whole hypotenuse and the opposite leg almost nothing — and it is.
In right △ABC the right angle is at C, AC = 3 and AB = 15. Use a calculator to find m∠A to the nearest tenth.
1Given lengths are the leg adjacent to ∠A and the hypotenuseso use the cosine ratio
2cos A = adj/hyp = 3/15 = 1/5substitution and simplify
3cos−1(1/5) = m∠Ause the inverse cosine function
4m∠A ≈ 78.5°use a calculator (degree mode)
m∠A ≈ 78.5°
Talk about it: another route is BC = √(15² − 3²) = √216 ≈ 14.7, then tan−1(14.7/3) ≈ 78.5°. Same answer, more steps — and more rounding risk.
Solve right △RST: the right angle is at S, ST = 6 and RT = 9. Round side measures to the nearest tenth and angle measures to the nearest tenth of a degree.
1sin R = opp/hyp = 6/9definition of sine ratio
2sin−1(6/9) = m∠Ruse the inverse sine function
3m∠R ≈ 41.8°use a calculator
4m∠R + m∠T = 90°acute ∠s of a rt △ are complementary
541.8 + m∠T ≈ 90° → m∠T ≈ 48.2°subtract 41.8 from each side
6(RS)² + (ST)² = (RT)²Pythagorean Theorem
7(RS)² + 6² = 9² → (RS)² = 45simplify
8RS = √45 ≈ 6.7use a calculator
m∠R ≈ 41.8°, m∠T ≈ 48.2°, RS ≈ 6.7
Alternative methods: m∠T could have been found first with cos−1(6/9), and once you know m∠R you could get RS from tan 41.8° = 6/RS. Using the Pythagorean Theorem is safer because it uses only the given numbers, not a rounded one.
Watch out! Do not use the rounded 41.8° to compute RS and then round again — you would get 6.71… drifting off. Keep exact values until the final step.

Common mistakes to avoid
  • Labelling opp / adj from the wrong vertex. Always fix the reference angle first.
  • Reading sin−1x as 1/sin x. It is an inverse operation, not a reciprocal.
  • Leaving the calculator in radian mode. All the angles in this lesson are degrees.
  • Using sine or cosine when both known sides are legs — that situation calls for tangent.
  • Getting sin θ or cos θ greater than 1. A leg can never exceed the hypotenuse, so that answer is impossible.
  • Rounding an intermediate value and then using it. Round once, at the end.
  • Applying trig ratios to a triangle that is not right-angled. Every rule here needs a 90° angle.
