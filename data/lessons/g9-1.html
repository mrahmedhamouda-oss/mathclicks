Core Concepts

1. What a geometric mean is

The arithmetic mean of two numbers is what you get by adding and dividing. The geometric mean is what you get by multiplying and taking a root — it is the number that sits in the middle of a proportion instead of in the middle of a sum.

Formally: the geometric mean of a set of n numbers is the nth root of their product. So the geometric mean of two numbers is the positive square root of their product.

geometric mean — the positive number x between a and b such that a/x = x/b.
altitude to the hypotenuse — the segment from the right-angle vertex, ⊥ to the hypotenuse.
Key Concept · Geometric Mean
The geometric mean of two positive numbers a and b is x such that
  a / x = x / b  →  x² = ab  →  x = √(ab)

Why the cross-multiplication works: a/x = x/b means the two ratios are equal, so x · x = a · b. Notice x appears in both middle positions of the proportion — that is the signature of a geometric mean. Whenever you see a variable sitting in both means of a proportion, you are looking at a geometric mean.

Study Tip: Geometric mean of 4 and 9 is √36 = 6. Arithmetic mean of 4 and 9 is 6.5. They are different numbers — and they are equal only when the two numbers are equal.

2. Splitting a right triangle with its altitude

Drop the altitude from the right angle of a right triangle onto the hypotenuse. You now have three right triangles — the original one and two smaller ones — and remarkably, all three have exactly the same shape.

Theorem 9.1
If the altitude is drawn to the hypotenuse of a right triangle, then the two triangles formed are similar to the original triangle and to each other.

Why: the small triangle on the left shares ∠A with the big triangle, and both have a right angle — so they are similar by AA. The same argument on the right gives the second pair. Similar to the same triangle ∴ similar to each other.

Watch Out! Similarity statements must list vertices in corresponding order. Do not write △ADC ∼ △BDC just because the letters look convenient — match equal angles: right angle to right angle, and the shared acute angle to its twin.

3. The two geometric mean theorems

Because those three triangles are similar, proportions between their sides appear everywhere. Two of those proportions are used so often that they get their own names.

Theorem 9.2 · Geometric Mean (Altitude) Theorem
Words: The altitude drawn to the hypotenuse of a right triangle separates the hypotenuse into two segments. The length of this altitude is the geometric mean between the lengths of these two segments.
Symbols: if CD is the altitude to hypotenuse AB of right △ABC, with AD = x and DB = y, then
x / h = h / y  →  h² = xy  →  h = √(xy)
Theorem 9.3 · Geometric Mean (Leg) Theorem
Words: The altitude drawn to the hypotenuse of a right triangle separates the hypotenuse into two segments. The length of a leg of the triangle is the geometric mean between the length of the hypotenuse and the length of the segment of the hypotenuse adjacent to that leg.
Symbols: with AD = x, DB = y, AB = c, leg b = AC, leg a = CB,
c / b = b / x  →  b = √(xc)
c / a = a / y  →  a = √(yc)
1
Altitude pairs with the two pieces of the hypotenuse: h = √(x·y).
2
Leg pairs with the whole hypotenuse and the piece touching that leg: leg = √(piece · whole).
3
The piece "adjacent to a leg" is the one that shares an endpoint with that leg. Leg AC touches A, so its piece is AD.
Watch Out! The most common error is putting the whole hypotenuse into the altitude formula. h = √(x·y) uses the two pieces only, never c. And b = √(x·c) uses the whole c, never the far piece y.

4. A reliable procedure

Redraw or mentally separate the three triangles so you can see which sides correspond.
Label the two hypotenuse pieces x and y, the whole hypotenuse c = x + y, the altitude h, and the legs a, b.
Decide what you are solving for. Altitude → use h = √(xy). A leg → use leg = √(adjacent piece · c).
Substitute, then simplify the radical (pull out perfect-square factors) before reaching for a calculator.
Check with the Pythagorean Theorem: a² + b² must equal c².

Common mistakes to avoid
  • Averaging instead of multiplying — the geometric mean of 4 and 9 is 6, not 6.5.
  • Using the whole hypotenuse inside the altitude formula. It takes the two pieces only.
  • Using the far piece inside the leg formula. A leg is paired with the piece it touches, times the whole hypotenuse.
  • Reporting a hypotenuse piece as the whole hypotenuse — remember to add both pieces.
  • Leaving radicals unsimplified: write 2√66, not √264.
  • Writing a similarity statement with the vertices out of correspondence.
  • Accepting a negative root — lengths and geometric means are positive.
