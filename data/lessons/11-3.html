Core Concepts

1. Area of a Circle

Every point on a circle sits the same distance — the radius r — from the centre. The circumference formula C = 2πr measures the rim; the area formula measures the whole flat disk inside that rim.

Key Concept · Area of a Circle
Words: The area A of a circle is equal to π times the square of the radius r.
Symbols: A = πr²

Why r²? Slice the disk into many thin pie wedges and re-arrange them into a near-rectangle. The rectangle's height is r and its base is half the circumference, ½(2πr) = πr. So the area is (πr)(r) = πr². That is exactly the regular-polygon idea A = ½Pa pushed to infinitely many sides.

A = πr²   |   C = 2πr   |   r = d/2
In terms of the diameter: A = π(d/2)² = πd²/4

2. Working Backward from the Area

If you are handed the area and asked for r or d, just undo the formula one step at a time.

Write A = πr² and substitute the known area.
Divide both sides by π to isolate r².
Take the positive square root: r = √(A/π).
If the question asks for the diameter, double it: d = 2r.
Watch Out! A length can never be negative, so we discard the negative square root. And √(A/π) is not the same as √A / π — divide first, then take the root.

3. Sectors

A sector is the "pizza slice" piece of a circle — the region bounded by a central angle and its intercepted arc. It is a fraction of the whole disk, and the fraction is decided entirely by the central angle.

sector — a region of a circle bounded by a central angle and its intercepted arc.
segment of a circle — the region bounded by an arc and its chord (not by two radii).
Key Concept · Area of a Sector
The ratio of the area A of a sector to the area of the whole circle πr² is equal to the ratio of the degree measure x of the intercepted arc to 360°.

Proportion: A / (πr²) = x° / 360°
Equation: A = (x°/360°) · πr²

Notice the family resemblance to arc length, ℓ = (x°/360°) · 2πr. Same fraction, different "whole": length uses the circumference, area uses πr².

Study Tip · Units of Area. When you convert between units of area you must multiply or divide by the square of the length conversion. Since 1 yd = 3 ft, 1 yd² = (3 ft)(3 ft) = 9 ft².

4. Reversing the Sector Formula

Two backward questions show up constantly:

  • Given a sector's area and arc, find the circle's area. Since Asector = (x/360)Acircle, simply multiply: Acircle = Asector · (360/x).
  • Given a sector's area and arc, find the radius. Solve r = √( Asector · (360/x) · (1/π) ).
Acircle = (360/x) · Asector
r = √( Asector (360/x)(1/π) )
1
A = πr² uses the radius. If the problem gives a diameter, halve it first.
2
Asector = (x°/360°)πr². The fraction x/360 is the share of the circle you own.
3
A sector is bounded by two radii and an arc. A segment is bounded by a chord and an arc.
4
Area of a segment = area of its sector − area of the triangle formed by the two radii and the chord.
5
Area units are squared: ft², cm², in². Converting ft² → yd² divides by 9, not 3.

Common mistakes to avoid
  • Substituting the diameter into A = πr². That makes the answer 4 times too big.
  • Answering with the radius when the question asked for the diameter (or vice versa).
  • Flipping the sector fraction — writing (360/x)πr² when finding a sector's area.
  • Using the arc-length formula (x/360)·2πr when the question wants area. Areas need πr².
  • Confusing a sector (two radii + arc) with a segment (chord + arc).
  • Converting square units with the linear factor (dividing ft² by 3 instead of 9).
  • Rounding π too early. Carry the exact value and round only at the last step.
