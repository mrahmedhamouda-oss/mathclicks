1.4  Directed Numbers

Reading, ordering and calculating with positive and negative numbers — the sign rules, the powers trap, and how to key it all correctly into a calculator.

In context Add & subtract Multiply & divide Examples Your turn Key points

Directed numbers in the real world

A directed number carries a direction as well as a size. The minus sign is not decoration — it tells you which way to go from zero.

Temperature

−7°C is 7 degrees below freezing. A rise of 12° takes it to 5°C.

Depth and height

Sea level is 0. A submarine at −180 m is 180 m below the surface; a cliff top at +45 m is above it.

Money

A balance of −$45.20 means you owe $45.20. A deposit is positive, a withdrawal negative.

Other contexts

Floors below ground, time before an event, gain and loss in a game, and change in a share price all use directed numbers.

Key idea On the number line, numbers get larger as you move right and smaller as you move left. This is the whole of ordering: a < b simply means a lies to the left of b.

The number line

Watch out The bigger the digits, the smaller a negative number is. −9 < −2 is true, and −0.5 > −0.8 is also true. When you are ordering, never compare the digits on their own — picture the line every time.

1

then 0, then the positives: 2, 5

Adding and subtracting

Every addition or subtraction is a journey along the number line. Start at the first number; the second number tells you how far and which way.

Move right when you…

  • add a positive: −5 + 3 = −2
  • subtract a negative: 4 − (−7) = 11

Move left when you…

  • subtract a positive: 2 − 6 = −4
  • add a negative: −3 + (−5) = −8

The two-signs-together rule

When an operation sign lands next to the sign of a number, replace the pair with a single sign, then carry out an ordinary calculation.

You seeReplace withExampleAnswer
+ (+a)+ a7 + (+3)7 + 3 = 10
+ (−a)− a7 + (−3)7 − 3 = 4
− (+a)− a7 − (+3)7 − 3 = 4
− (−a)+ a7 − (−3)7 + 3 = 10
Watch out The rule applies only to two signs standing next to each other. In −5 + 3 there is a single sign in front of the 5 and a single operation sign, so nothing combines: the answer is −2, not −8. Students who chant “two minuses make a plus” without checking lose marks here every single year.

Number-line journey

Set a start value and a second number, choose add or subtract, and watch the journey drawn on the line. Try 4 subtract −7, then −3 add −5.

Example 2Work out 6 − (−4) + (−9).
1
Simplify each pair of adjacent signs before doing any arithmetic. − (−4) → + 4
+ (−9) → − 9
2
Rewrite the whole expression on one clean line. 6 + 4 − 9
3
Work from left to right. 6 + 4 = 10
10 − 9 = 1
6 − (−4) + (−9) = 1
Exam tip Write out the simplified line 6 + 4 − 9. It is a method mark, and it stops you from losing track of the signs.
Example 3Marta's bank balance is −$45.20. She pays in $120 and then a direct debit of $88.50 is taken out. Find her new balance.
1
Translate the words into directed numbers: paying in is +, money taken out is −. −45.20 + 120 − 88.50
2
Left to right. The first step clears the overdraft and leaves credit. −45.20 + 120 = 74.80
3
Now subtract the direct debit. It is larger than the balance, so she goes back into debt. 74.80 − 88.50 = −13.70
New balance = −$13.70, i.e. she is $13.70 overdrawn
Watch out Money answers must be given to 2 decimal places, and the sign is part of the answer — “$13.70” on its own would not gain the accuracy mark.

Multiplying, dividing and powers

For × and ÷ the rule is far simpler than for + and −: work out the answer ignoring signs, then decide the sign at the end.

Exam tip For a long string of multiplications and divisions, just count the negative signs. An odd number of negatives gives a negative answer; an even number gives a positive one. So −2 × −3 × −5 has three negatives, and the answer is −30.

Negative numbers and powers

This is the single most common slip in the whole topic, and it is worth a mark in almost every substitution question.

(−3)² = 9

The brackets say the whole of −3 is squared.

(−3)² = (−3) × (−3) = 9

−3² = −9

Without brackets the power applies to the 3 only; the minus is applied afterwards.

−3² = −(3 × 3) = −9

Key idea A negative number raised to an even power is positive; raised to an odd power it is negative. (−2)² = 4, (−2)³ = −8, (−2)4 = 16, (−2)5 = −32. This is just the odd-or-even count of negatives again.

Using your calculator without losing marks

  • Use the (−) key for a negative number and the − key for the operation of subtracting. They are different keys and swapping them gives a syntax error or a wrong answer.
  • Always put a negative number in brackets before a power or before multiplying: key ( (−) 3 ) x² to get 9. Keying (−) 3 x² gives −9, because the calculator follows the same rule as the mathematics.
  • Put brackets round the whole numerator and the whole denominator of a fraction before dividing.
  • Do not round part-way through. Keep the full display value and round only the final answer, to 3 significant figures unless the question says otherwise.

Your turn

Practice set

  1. Write in order, smallest first: −4, 3, −11, 0, −1, 7.
  2. Work out (a) −8 + 15  (b) −6 − 9  (c) 4 − (−7)  (d) −3 + (−5)
  3. Work out (a) −6 × 7  (b) −48 ÷ (−6)  (c) (−2)4  (d) −24
  4. At midday the temperature in Astana is −19°C and in Cairo it is 24°C. How much warmer is Cairo?
  5. Find the value of b² − 4ac when a = 3, b = −8 and c = −2.
  6. A submarine is at a depth of 180 m. It rises 65 m, then makes three further dives of 40 m each. Write its final position as a directed number and state its depth.

  1. Negatives first, largest digits furthest left → −11, −4, −1, 0, 3, 7
  2. (a) start at −8, move 15 right → 7; (b) move 9 further left → −15; (c) −(−7) → +7, so 4 + 7 = 11; (d) +(−5) → −5, so −3 − 5 = −8
  3. (a) different signs → −42; (b) same signs → 8; (c) even power with brackets → 16; (d) no brackets, so −(24) = −16
  4. 24 − (−19) = 24 + 19 = 43°C warmer
  5. (−8)² − 4 × 3 × (−2) = 64 − (−24) = 64 + 24 = 88
  6. Depth 180 m below the surface is −180; −180 + 65 = −115; three dives of 40 m give −115 − 120 = −235, so the submarine is at a depth of 235 m

Key points to remember

  • A negative number is left of zero. Ordering is always decided by position on the line, never by the size of the digits.
  • Adding moves right, subtracting moves left — and a negative second number reverses that direction.
  • The two-signs-together rule applies only to adjacent signs: −(−a) = +a and +(−a) = −a. Rewrite the expression on one clean line before calculating.
  • For × and ÷: same signs give positive, different signs give negative. For a long chain, count the negatives — odd means negative.
  • (−3)² = 9 but −3² = −9. The brackets decide what is being squared.
  • A negative to an even power is positive; to an odd power it is negative.
  • Substitute negative values in brackets, always — especially in b² − 4ac.
  • On the calculator use the (−) key for negative numbers, bracket whole numerators and denominators, and round only at the very end.
