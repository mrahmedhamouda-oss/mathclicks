5.3  Percentages

Percentages of a quantity, increase and decrease by multiplier, percentage change, and the reverse percentages that Paper 4 loves to set.

Basics Multipliers Percentage change Reverse Examples Your turn Key points

Percentages, fractions and decimals

A percentage is simply a fraction with denominator 100. 37% means 37 parts out of 100, so 37% = 37100 = 0.37. Everything in this lesson comes back to that one idea, so if a question ever confuses you, write the percentage as a decimal and read the sentence again.

Definition To change a percentage to a decimal, divide by 100. To change a decimal to a percentage, multiply by 100. To change a percentage to a fraction, put it over 100 and cancel.

A percentage of a quantity

Turn the percentage into a decimal and multiply.

18% of 350 = 0.18 × 350 = 63

On a non-calculator paper, build it from 10%, 5% and 1% instead.

One quantity as a percentage of another

Write the fraction partwhole, then multiply by 100.

63 out of 180 = 63180 × 100 = 35%

The quantity after the word of goes on the bottom.

Watch out Both quantities must be in the same units before you divide. To write 45 cm as a percentage of 2 m, change the 2 m into 200 cm first: 45200 × 100 = 22.5%, not 452.
Example 1(a) Find 18% of 350 kg.  (b) Write 63 kg as a percentage of 180 kg.
1
(a) Write the percentage as a decimal. 18% = 18 ÷ 100 = 0.18
2
Multiply by the quantity. 0.18 × 350 = 63
3
(b) Put the part over the whole. The words “of 180 kg” tell you 180 is the denominator. 63180 = 0.35
4
Multiply by 100 to turn the decimal into a percentage. 0.35 × 100 = 35
(a) 63 kg     (b) 35%
Exam tip Notice how parts (a) and (b) run in opposite directions. If you are ever unsure which way round to divide, check whether your answer is sensible: 63 is roughly a third of 180, so 35% is believable.

Increase and decrease with a multiplier

Weaker candidates find the percentage, then add or subtract it. That is two steps, two chances to slip, and it collapses completely when the question asks for a repeated change. Use a multiplier instead: one number that does the whole job.

increase by p%  →  × (1 + p100)      decrease by p%  →  × (1 − p100) The multiplier does the change in a single step
ChangeMultiplierChangeMultiplier
increase by 5%1.05decrease by 5%0.95
increase by 4.5%1.045decrease by 12%0.88
increase by 17.5%1.175decrease by 7.5%0.925
increase by 100%2decrease by 65%0.35
Note A multiplier bigger than 1 always means an increase; a multiplier between 0 and 1 always means a decrease. Reading a multiplier the wrong way round is the single most common error in this topic.
Example 2A teacher earns $32 400 per year. Her salary rises by 4.5%. Calculate her new salary.
1
This is an increase, so the multiplier is 1 + 0.045. multiplier = 1.045
2
Multiply the original salary by it. 32 400 × 1.045 = 33 858
new salary = $33 858
Check The rise itself is 0.045 × 32 400 = 1458, and 32 400 + 1458 = 33 858. Same answer, but the multiplier got there in one line.

Percentage change, profit and loss

When a quantity changes you are often asked by what percentage. The rule never varies: compare the change with the original amount — never with the new one.

percentage change = changeoriginal amount × 100 change = new value − original value

Profit

An item is bought for the cost price and sold for the selling price.

% profit = selling − costcost × 100

Loss

If the selling price is lower, the change is negative and you have a loss.

% loss = cost − sellingcost × 100

Watch out The denominator for profit and loss is always the cost price, because that is what the trader started with. Dividing by the selling price is worth zero marks even if the rest of the working is perfect.
Example 3A trader buys 24 melons for a total of $96 and sells them all at $5.20 each. Calculate his percentage profit.
1
Find the total money received. 24 × 5.20 = 124.80
2
Find the profit: selling total minus cost total. 124.80 − 96 = 28.80
3
Divide by the cost price and multiply by 100. 28.8096 × 100 = 0.3 × 100 = 30
percentage profit = 30%
Exam tip Questions like this carry 3 marks: one for the total selling price, one for the profit, one for the percentage. Write all three lines even if your calculator does it in one go.

Repeated percentage change

If the same percentage change happens again and again, multiply by the multiplier again and again. That is just a power.

final amount = original × (multiplier)n n = the number of times the change happens

Growth

5% a year for 3 years:

1.053 = 1.157625

An overall increase of 15.7625%, not 15%.

Depreciation

15% a year for 3 years:

0.853 = 0.614125

The item keeps 61.4125% of its value.

Different changes

Up 8% then down 5%:

1.08 × 0.95 = 1.026

An overall increase of 2.6%.

Watch out An increase of 20% followed by a decrease of 20% does not return you to the start: 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96, a 4% loss overall. The second percentage is taken of a different amount, so percentages never simply cancel.
Exam tip To find the overall percentage change from a multiplier, subtract 1 and multiply by 100. Multiplier 1.026 → +2.6%; multiplier 0.614125 → −38.5875%, so a 38.6% loss to 3 significant figures.

Reverse percentages

Here you are given the amount after a change and asked for the amount before it. This is the part of the topic that separates grade 7 from grade 5, and it appears on almost every Extended paper.

Key idea Going forwards you multiply by the multiplier, so going backwards you divide by it.
original = amount after the changemultiplier

Why you cannot just add the percentage back

The 25% was taken off the original price, which you do not know yet. Adding 25% of the sale price gives 63 × 1.25 = 78.75 — the wrong answer.

Example 4In a sale all prices are reduced by 25%. A coat costs $63 in the sale. Calculate its price before the sale.
1
Decide the multiplier for the change that has already happened. 25% off → multiplier = 1 − 0.25 = 0.75
2
The sale price is 0.75 × the original, so divide to undo it. original = 63 ÷ 0.75
3
Work it out. 63 ÷ 0.75 = 84
4
Check by going forwards again. 84 × 0.75 = 63  ✓
price before the sale = $84
Exam trap The word that tells you it is a reverse percentage is a small one: “after a 25% reduction”, “including 15% tax”, “this is a 12% increase on last year”. Underline it before you start.

More worked examples

Example 5A shop adds sales tax of 15% to all its prices. A television is marked $632.50 including tax. Calculate the price before tax was added.
1
The word “including” means the increase has already been applied, so this is a reverse percentage. multiplier = 1 + 0.15 = 1.15
2
Divide the amount after the change by the multiplier. 632.50 ÷ 1.15 = 550
3
Check forwards. 550 × 1.15 = 632.50  ✓
price before tax = $550.00
Watch out Taking 15% off $632.50 gives 632.50 × 0.85 = 537.625, which is wrong. The tax was 15% of $550, not 15% of $632.50.
Example 6A car is bought for $18 000. Its value depreciates by 15% each year. (a) Find its value after 3 years. (b) Find the overall percentage loss in value.
1
(a) A 15% decrease each year gives a multiplier of 0.85, applied 3 times. value = 18 000 × 0.853
2
Work out the power first, keeping every digit. 0.853 = 0.614125
3
Multiply. 18 000 × 0.614125 = 11 054.25
4
(b) The overall multiplier is 0.614125, so subtract 1. 0.614125 − 1 = −0.385875 → −38.5875%
(a) $11 054.25     (b) a loss of 38.6% (3 s.f.)
Exam tip Do not round 0.853 to 0.61 part-way through. Rounding early here changes the answer by about $25 and loses the accuracy mark.
Example 7The value of a house increased by 8% during 2022 and then decreased by 5% during 2023. At the end of 2023 it was worth $205 200. (a) Find the overall percentage change over the two years. (b) Find the value of the house at the start of 2022.
1
(a) Write a multiplier for each year and multiply them. 1.08 × 0.95 = 1.026
2
Subtract 1 and turn into a percentage. 1.026 − 1 = 0.026 → +2.6%
3
(b) $205 200 is the value after both changes, so divide by the overall multiplier. value at start = 205 200 ÷ 1.026
4
Evaluate and check. 205 200 ÷ 1.026 = 200 000
200 000 × 1.08 = 216 000,  216 000 × 0.95 = 205 200  ✓
(a) an increase of 2.6%     (b) $200 000
Note Adding the percentages to get 8 − 5 = 3% is wrong, because the 5% is taken from the larger, already increased value. Only multipliers may be combined.

Key points to remember

  • Percentage to decimal: divide by 100. Decimal to percentage: multiply by 100.
  • To find p% of an amount, multiply by p ÷ 100. To write one quantity as a percentage of another, divide by the quantity after the word of, then multiply by 100 — with both in the same units.
  • Use a multiplier: 1 + p/100 to increase, 1 − p/100 to decrease. Above 1 means growth, below 1 means decline.
  • Percentage change = changeoriginal × 100. For profit or loss the denominator is always the cost price.
  • Reverse percentages: if you are given the amount after the change, divide by the multiplier. Never add the percentage back on to the new amount.
  • Repeated change: multiply by (multiplier)n. Combine different changes by multiplying their multipliers — percentages themselves can never be added.
  • Subtract 1 from an overall multiplier and multiply by 100 to state the overall percentage change.
  • Keep full accuracy until the last line; give money to 2 decimal places and other answers to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.
