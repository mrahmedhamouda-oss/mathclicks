3.4  Polygons

Naming polygons, the interior and exterior angle rules, finding the number of sides from a single angle, and why only some shapes tessellate.

What counts as a polygon

A polygon is a closed flat shape made only of straight sides. No curves, no gaps, no crossing lines. You are expected to know the names up to twelve sides — examiners use them without explanation, so “the interior angles of a nonagon” must tell you n = 9 immediately.

The regular polygons you should recognise on sight

Definition A polygon is regular when both conditions hold: all sides are the same length and all interior angles are equal. A rhombus has equal sides but unequal angles, and a rectangle has equal angles but unequal sides — neither is regular.

Convex

Every interior angle is < 180°. All the vertices point outwards, and any straight line you draw across the shape crosses the boundary at most twice.

Concave (re-entrant)

At least one interior angle is reflex — bigger than 180°. The shape has a dent. The angle sum rule still works perfectly.

The interior angle sum

Pick one vertex of a polygon and join it to every vertex it is not already joined to. The polygon splits into triangles — and because each triangle contributes 180°, that is where the whole rule comes from.

Why the formula works

Sum of interior angles = (n − 2) × 180° n is the number of sides
Exam tip This works for every polygon — regular or irregular, convex or concave. If a shape has a reflex angle, use the reflex value in the sum, not the angle outside it.
Example 1Four angles of a pentagon are 100°, 115°, 130° and 90°. Find the fifth angle.
1
Work out the angle sum for n = 5. (5 − 2) × 180° = 3 × 180° = 540°
2
Add the four angles you are given. 100 + 115 + 130 + 90 = 435°
3
Subtract from the total. Show this subtraction line — it is where the method mark sits. x = 540 − 435 = 105°
The fifth angle is 105°
Example 2The interior angles of a polygon add up to 2340°. How many sides has it?
1
Set the formula equal to the sum you are given. (n − 2) × 180 = 2340
2
Divide both sides by 180. n − 2 = 2340 ÷ 180 = 13
3
Add 2. n = 13 + 2 = 15
4
Check by substituting back. (15 − 2) × 180 = 13 × 180 = 2340  ✓
The polygon has 15 sides
Watch out Do not forget the “+ 2”. Writing n = 13 is the single most common error on this question type.

Exterior angles and regular polygons

Extend one side of a polygon past a vertex. The angle between that extension and the next side is the exterior angle. Interior and exterior angle at the same vertex sit on a straight line, so they always add to 180°.

Walk once round the shape and you turn through 360°

Sum of exterior angles = 360° True for every convex polygon, however many sides — and however irregular

Regular polygon: one exterior angle

exterior angle = 360°n

All n exterior angles are equal, and together they make 360°, so share it out.

Regular polygon: one interior angle

interior angle = 180° − 360°n

Or divide the total: (n − 2) × 180°n. Both give the same answer — use whichever is quicker.

Exam tip Nearly every “how many sides?” question is fastest through the exterior angle, because n = 360 ÷ exterior is a single division. Convert an interior angle to its exterior angle first by subtracting from 180°.
Example 3Work out the interior and exterior angles of a regular dodecagon (12 sides).
1
Exterior angle first — it is one division. 360 ÷ 12 = 30°
2
Interior angle is the supplement. 180 − 30 = 150°
3
Check with the angle sum. (12 − 2) × 180 = 1800°
1800 ÷ 12 = 150°  ✓
exterior = 30°, interior = 150°
Example 4Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 156°. How many sides does it have?
1
Turn the interior angle into an exterior angle using the straight line. exterior = 180 − 156 = 24°
2
The exterior angles share out 360°. n = 360 ÷ 24 = 15
3
Check: a regular 15-gon. 180 − (360 ÷ 15) = 180 − 24 = 156°  ✓
15 sides
Watch out If your division does not give a whole number, no such regular polygon exists — say so. An interior angle of 155° gives 360 ÷ 25 = 14.4, which is impossible.

Fitting polygons round a point

Shapes tessellate when copies of them cover the plane with no gaps and no overlaps. At every meeting point the angles must total exactly 360° — that one condition decides everything.

Hexagons fit: 3 × 120° = 360°

Pentagons do not: 3 × 108° = 324°

Note Only three regular polygons tessellate on their own: the equilateral triangle (6 × 60 = 360), the square (4 × 90 = 360) and the regular hexagon (3 × 120 = 360). They are the only ones whose interior angle divides exactly into 360°. Every triangle and every quadrilateral tessellates, even irregular ones, because their angles total 180° and 360°.
Example 5Two regular octagons and one square meet at a point. Show that they fit exactly.
1
Interior angle of a regular octagon. exterior = 360 ÷ 8 = 45°
interior = 180 − 45 = 135°
2
A square has interior angle 90°. Add the three angles at the point. 135 + 135 + 90 = 360°
3
The angles total exactly 360°, so there is no gap and no overlap.
135° + 135° + 90° = 360°, so the three shapes tessellate together

More worked examples

Example 6The angles of a hexagon are x, 2x, 2x, 110°, 130° and 150°. Find x and the largest angle.
1
Angle sum of a hexagon. (6 − 2) × 180 = 4 × 180 = 720°
2
Form an equation, collecting the x terms and the numbers. x + 2x + 2x + 110 + 130 + 150 = 720
5x + 390 = 720
3
Solve. 5x = 330
x = 66°
4
The angles are 66, 132, 132, 110, 130, 150. Check they total 720. 66 + 132 + 132 + 110 + 130 + 150 = 720  ✓
x = 66°, and the largest angle is 150°
Exam tip Always add your finished angles back up. It costs ten seconds and catches an arithmetic slip that would otherwise lose every mark after step 2.
Example 7In a regular polygon each interior angle is 8 times the size of each exterior angle. Find the number of sides.
1
Let the exterior angle be e. Then the interior angle is 8e, and they lie on a straight line. e + 8e = 180
2
Solve for e. 9e = 180
e = 20°
3
Use the exterior angle rule. n = 360 ÷ 20 = 18
4
Check: interior angle = 180 − 20 = 160°, and 160 ÷ 20 = 8 ✓
The polygon has 18 sides
Example 8A regular pentagon and a regular hexagon share an edge, as shown. Find the angle x between them.
1
Interior angle of the regular pentagon. 180 − (360 ÷ 5) = 180 − 72 = 108°
2
Interior angle of the regular hexagon. 180 − (360 ÷ 6) = 180 − 60 = 120°
3
The three angles at the shared vertex go all the way round a point. x + 108 + 120 = 360
4
Solve. x = 360 − 228 = 132°
x = 132°
Watch out The angles at that vertex make a full turn (360°), not a straight line. Students who write x + 108 + 120 = 180 get a negative angle — an instant sign that something is wrong.

Your turn

Practice set

  1. Work out the sum of the interior angles of (a) an octagon (b) a 20-sided polygon.
  2. Find each interior angle and each exterior angle of a regular nonagon (9 sides).
  3. Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is 15°. How many sides has it?
  4. Five angles of a hexagon are 140°, 95°, 120°, 160° and 85°. Find the sixth angle.
  5. Explain why a regular octagon cannot tessellate on its own.
  6. The interior angle of a regular polygon P is 140°. (a) Find the number of sides of P. (b) Two of these polygons and one other regular polygon Q fit exactly around a point. Find the number of sides of Q.

  1. (a) (8 − 2) × 180 = 1080°  (b) (20 − 2) × 180 = 18 × 180 = 3240°
  2. exterior = 360 ÷ 9 = 40°; interior = 180 − 40 = 140°
  3. n = 360 ÷ 15 = 24 sides
  4. Sum = 720°; the five given angles total 600°, so the sixth is 720 − 600 = 120°
  5. Interior angle = 135° and 360 ÷ 135 = 2.6̇, not a whole number, so copies leave a gap — 135° does not divide exactly into 360°
  6. (a) exterior = 180 − 140 = 40°, so n = 360 ÷ 40 = 9 sides  (b) angles at the point: 140 + 140 = 280°, so Q has interior angle 360 − 280 = 80°; exterior = 100° and 360 ÷ 100 = 3.6 — no such regular polygon exists

Key points to remember

  • Sum of interior angles = (n − 2) × 180°, because the polygon splits into n − 2 triangles.
  • Sum of exterior angles = 360° for every polygon, whatever n is.
  • At any vertex, interior + exterior = 180° — they are on a straight line.
  • For a regular polygon: exterior = 360 ÷ n and interior = 180 − 360 ÷ n.
  • To find n from an angle, always go via the exterior angle: n = 360 ÷ exterior. A non-integer answer means no such polygon exists.
  • Regular means equal sides and equal angles; a concave polygon has one reflex interior angle but still obeys the angle sum.
  • Shapes fit round a point only when the angles total exactly 360°; among regular polygons only the triangle, square and hexagon tessellate alone.
