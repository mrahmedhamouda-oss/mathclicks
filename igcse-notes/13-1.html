13.1  Understanding Units

Metric units of length, mass and capacity, converting areas and volumes, and the compound units of speed, density and pressure.

The metric system

Every metric unit is built from a base unit and a prefix that tells you how many of the base unit you have. Once you know the prefixes you never need to memorise a conversion table again — you can rebuild it in the margin of the exam paper in about ten seconds.

PrefixSymbolMeaningExample
kilok× 10001 km = 1000 m
centic× 11001 cm = 0.01 m, so 100 cm = 1 m
millim× 110001 mm = 0.001 m, so 1000 mm = 1 m

Length

  • 1 cm = 10 mm
  • 1 m = 100 cm = 1000 mm
  • 1 km = 1000 m

Mass

  • 1 g = 1000 mg
  • 1 kg = 1000 g
  • 1 tonne = 1000 kg

Capacity

  • 1 litre = 1000 ml
  • 1 litre = 100 cl
  • 1 cl = 10 ml
The only rule you need Going to a smaller unit, the number gets bigger, so you multiply. Going to a bigger unit, the number gets smaller, so you divide. A distance of 3 m is 300 cm (bigger number, smaller unit) but only 0.003 km.

Converting areas and volumes

This is where most marks are lost. Students who happily write 1 m = 100 cm then write “1 m² = 100 cm²”, which is wrong by a factor of one hundred. The reason is simple: an area is a length times a length, so the conversion factor gets used twice.

1 cm² = (10 mm) × (10 mm) = 10² mm² = 100 mm² Square the linear factor for area
1 cm³ = (10 mm) × (10 mm) × (10 mm) = 10³ mm³ = 1000 mm³ Cube the linear factor for volume

Why the factor is squared and cubed

LinearAreaVolume
1 cm = 10 mm1 cm² = 100 mm²1 cm³ = 1000 mm³
1 m = 100 cm1 m² = 10 000 cm²1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³
1 km = 1000 m1 km² = 1 000 000 m²1 km³ = 109 m³
Watch out 1 m² is not 100 cm². Picture a square metre: it is 100 cm along each edge, so it holds 100 × 100 = 10 000 centimetre squares.
Capacity and volume are the same thing 1 ml = 1 cm³  ·  1 litre = 1000 cm³  ·  1 m³ = 1000 litres. A “capacity” question is a volume question in disguise.
Example 1Convert (a) 3.7 m² to cm², (b) 45 000 mm² to cm², (c) 2.4 m³ to cm³.
1
(a) The linear factor is 1 m = 100 cm. Square it. 100² = 10 000
3.7 × 10 000 = 37 000 cm²
2
(b) Going from mm² to cm² is going to a bigger unit, so divide by 10² = 100. 45 000 ÷ 100 = 450 cm²
3
(c) Volume, so cube the linear factor. 100³ = 1 000 000
2.4 × 1 000 000 = 2 400 000 cm³
(a) 37 000 cm²    (b) 450 cm²    (c) 2 400 000 cm³
Exam tip Check the direction with a sentence: “a centimetre square is small, so I need lots of them” → the number must go up.
Example 2A tank is a cuboid measuring 0.8 m by 45 cm by 300 mm. Find its capacity in litres.
1
Put every length in the same unit first. Centimetres is the natural choice because the answer is wanted in litres and 1 litre = 1000 cm³. 0.8 m = 80 cm  ·  45 cm  ·  300 mm = 30 cm
2
Volume of a cuboid = l × w × h. 80 × 45 × 30 = 108 000 cm³
3
Convert to litres by dividing by 1000. 108 000 ÷ 1000 = 108
Capacity = 108 litres
Watch out Never multiply lengths that are in different units. Convert first, multiply second.

Compound units

A compound unit is built from two other units, and the word “per” (or the slash) always means divide. If you can read the unit you can write the formula.

Speed

speed = distancetime

Units: m/s, km/h. “Metres per second” = metres divided by seconds.

Density

density = massvolume

Units: g/cm³, kg/m³. Rearrange to mass = density × volume.

Pressure

pressure = forcearea

Units: N/m² (also called the pascal), N/cm².

Exam tip The unit tells you the operation. If an answer must be in g/cm³, the mass must be in grams and the volume in cm³ before you divide. Convert first, then divide.

Converting m/s into km/h

Do it in two separate steps and it never goes wrong.

1 m/s = 0.001 km13600 h = 0.001 × 3600 km/h = 3.6 km/h Multiply by 3.6 to go m/s → km/h; divide by 3.6 to come back

Example 3(a) A cheetah runs at 28 m/s. Write this in km/h. (b) A speed limit is 90 km/h. Write this in m/s.
1
(a) In one second it covers 28 m, so in one hour (3600 s) it covers 28 × 3600 = 100 800 m
2
Change metres to kilometres by dividing by 1000. 100 800 ÷ 1000 = 100.8 km/h
3
(b) Reverse it: divide by 3.6. 90 ÷ 3.6 = 25 m/s
(a) 100.8 km/h    (b) 25 m/s
Sense check A number of km/h is always larger than the same speed in m/s, because a kilometre per hour is a much smaller speed than a metre per second.

More worked examples

Example 4A steel block measures 12 cm by 5 cm by 4 cm. Steel has density 7.8 g/cm³. Find the mass of the block in kilograms.
1
Volume of the cuboid. V = 12 × 5 × 4 = 240 cm³
2
Rearrange density = mass ÷ volume to give mass = density × volume. The units match already (g and cm³). mass = 7.8 × 240 = 1872 g
3
The question asks for kilograms, so divide by 1000. 1872 ÷ 1000 = 1.872 kg
mass = 1.872 kg
Example 5A crate has weight 250 N and its base is a rectangle 80 cm by 50 cm. Find the pressure on the floor in N/m².
1
Area of the base, in cm² first. A = 80 × 50 = 4000 cm²
2
The answer must be in N/m², so the area must be in m². Divide by 10 000, not by 100. 4000 ÷ 10 000 = 0.4 m²
3
Now divide force by area. pressure = 250 ÷ 0.4 = 625
pressure = 625 N/m²
Exam trap Dividing 4000 by 100 gives 40 m² and a “pressure” of 6.25 N/m² — a hundred times too small. Area conversions use the squared factor.
Example 6A tin of paint covers 12 m² per litre. A wall measures 4.5 m by 2.4 m and needs two coats. How many litres of paint are needed?
1
Area of one coat. 4.5 × 2.4 = 10.8 m²
2
Two coats means twice the area to cover. 10.8 × 2 = 21.6 m²
3
“12 m² per litre” means divide the area by 12. 21.6 ÷ 12 = 1.8 litres
1.8 litres (so 2 whole tins must be bought)
Exam tip When a question ends “how many tins?” you must round up, whatever the decimal says. You cannot buy 0.8 of a tin.
Example 7A water tank holds 2.5 m³. Water flows in at 20 litres per minute. How long, in hours and minutes, does it take to fill the empty tank?
1
The rate is in litres, so convert the capacity to litres. 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³ = 1000 litres. 2.5 × 1000 = 2500 litres
2
Time = amount ÷ rate. 2500 ÷ 20 = 125 minutes
3
Convert to hours and minutes. 125 = 60 + 60 + 5 = 2 h 5 min
2 hours 5 minutes
Note 125 min written as a decimal number of hours is 125 ÷ 60 = 2.08̇3̇ h — never write it as 2.05 h. There is more on this in 13.2.

Key points to remember

  • Smaller unit → bigger number (multiply); bigger unit → smaller number (divide).
  • Length: 1 cm = 10 mm, 1 m = 100 cm, 1 km = 1000 m. Mass: 1 kg = 1000 g, 1 t = 1000 kg.
  • For area, square the linear factor: 1 cm² = 100 mm², 1 m² = 10 000 cm².
  • For volume, cube it: 1 cm³ = 1000 mm³, 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³.
  • 1 ml = 1 cm³, 1 litre = 1000 cm³, 1 m³ = 1000 litres.
  • Compound units: “per” means divide — speed = d ÷ t, density = m ÷ V, pressure = F ÷ A.
  • m/s → km/h multiply by 3.6; km/h → m/s divide by 3.6.
  • Always convert every quantity into matching units before you multiply or divide.
