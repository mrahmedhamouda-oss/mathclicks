11.3  Understanding Similar Shapes

How lengths, areas and volumes change under an enlargement, and how to move between the scale factors k, k² and k³ with confidence.

Similar shapes Area and volume Working backwards Examples Your turn Key points

What “similar” means

Two shapes are similar if one is an exact enlargement of the other. The shape is unchanged; only the size is different. In 0580 this idea runs from simple missing-side questions right up to three-dimensional model and container problems in Paper 4.

Definition Two shapes are similar when their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding sides are in the same ratio. That common ratio is the linear scale factor, written k.
k = length on the large shapecorresponding length on the small shape Always put matching sides on top and bottom

Corresponding sides and angles

Exam tip Before you calculate anything, write the two triangles down in corresponding order, for example “ABC is similar to PQR”. Then AB matches PQ, and you cannot pair the wrong sides.
Example 1Triangle ABC has sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm. It is similar to triangle PQR, in which the side corresponding to the 6 cm side is 15 cm. Find the other two sides of PQR.
1
Find the linear scale factor from the pair you know, large over small. k = 15 ÷ 6 = 2.5
2
Multiply each remaining side of the small triangle by k. x = 8 × 2.5 = 20
3
y = 10 × 2.5 = 25
4
Check the answer is consistent: 15, 20, 25 is 5 × (3, 4, 5), so it is still a right-angled triangle.
x = 20 cm,   y = 25 cm
Watch out If you are going from the large shape to the small one, divide by k (or use a scale factor less than 1). Getting the direction wrong turns a 4-mark question into 0.

The area and volume scale factors

Here is the idea that examiners test hardest. If every length is multiplied by k, then area is multiplied by k² and volume is multiplied by k³.

length × k     area × k²     volume × k³ k is always the LINEAR scale factor

Why area uses k²

An area is two lengths multiplied together, so it picks up the factor k twice: k × k = k².

Why volume uses k³

A volume is three lengths multiplied together, so the factor appears three times: k × k × k = k³.

Watch out — the classic trap If the lengths of a shape are doubled, its area is not doubled. It is multiplied by 4. Never apply the linear factor to an area or a volume. Surface area counts as an area, so it uses k² too.

Example 2Two similar rectangles have corresponding sides of 4 cm and 10 cm. The area of the smaller rectangle is 24 cm². Find the area of the larger.
1
Find the linear scale factor first. k = 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5
2
Areas use k², so square it. k² = 2.5² = 6.25
3
Multiply the known area by the area scale factor. area = 24 × 6.25 = 150
4
Check independently: the small rectangle must be 4 × 6, so the large one is 10 × 15 = 150. ✓
area of the larger rectangle = 150 cm²
Watch out 24 × 2.5 = 60 is the trap answer. It uses the linear factor on an area.
Example 3Two similar cones have heights 6 cm and 9 cm. The volume of the smaller cone is 120 cm³. Find the volume of the larger cone.
1
Heights are lengths, so they give the linear factor directly. k = 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5
2
Volumes use k³. k³ = 1.5³ = 3.375
3
Multiply. V = 120 × 3.375 = 405
volume of the larger cone = 405 cm³
Exam tip You never need the radius here. If a question gives two similar solids and one length in each, that pair alone gives k.

Going backwards: from an area or volume ratio to k

Questions often give you the areas or the volumes and ask for a length. Then you must undo the squaring or cubing: take the square root of an area ratio, or the cube root of a volume ratio, to get back to k.

k = area factor      k = 3volume factor Always return to the linear factor before touching a length

Given lengths

k → square it for areas, cube it for volumes.

Given areas

Divide to get k², square root to get k, then work on.

Given volumes

Divide to get k³, cube root to get k, then work on.

Example 4Two similar triangles have areas 45 cm² and 125 cm². The base of the smaller triangle is 12 cm. Find the base of the larger triangle.
1
Divide large by small to get the area scale factor. k² = 12545 = 259
2
Square root to come back to the linear factor. k = 25/9 = 53
3
Now, and only now, use it on a length. b = 12 × 53 = 20
4
Check: (5/3)² = 25/9 = 2.777… and 45 × 2.777… = 125. ✓
base of the larger triangle = 20 cm
Exam tip Keep the ratio as a fraction rather than a rounded decimal. Here 125 ÷ 45 = 2.7̇ — rounding it early costs accuracy marks.

More worked examples

Example 5Two mathematically similar jugs hold 250 ml and 2000 ml. The smaller jug is 12 cm high and has a surface area of 180 cm². Find the height and the surface area of the larger jug.
1
Capacity is a volume, so the ratio given is k³. k³ = 2000 ÷ 250 = 8
2
Cube root. k = 38 = 2
3
Height is a length, so multiply by k. h = 12 × 2 = 24
4
Surface area is an area, so multiply by k² = 4. S = 180 × 4 = 720
height = 24 cm,   surface area = 720 cm²
Watch out Three different factors appear in one question: k for the height, k² for the surface area, k³ for the capacity. Decide what kind of quantity each part is before you multiply.
Example 6A model of a statue is made to a scale of 1 : 20. The model has a surface area of 250 cm² and a volume of 400 cm³. Find the surface area of the statue in m² and its volume in m³.
1
A scale of 1 : 20 is a linear ratio, so k = 20. k² = 400,   k³ = 8000
2
Surface area of the statue. 250 × 400 = 100 000 cm²
3
Convert. Since 1 m = 100 cm, 1 m² = 100² = 10 000 cm². 100 000 ÷ 10 000 = 10 m²
4
Volume of the statue. 400 × 8000 = 3 200 000 cm³
5
Convert, using 1 m³ = 100³ = 1 000 000 cm³. 3 200 000 ÷ 1 000 000 = 3.2 m³
surface area = 10 m²,   volume = 3.2 m³
Note The unit conversion is itself a scale-factor question: metres to centimetres is k = 100, so areas differ by 100² and volumes by 100³.
Example 7Two similar cylinders have curved surface areas of 50π cm² and 98π cm². The volume of the smaller cylinder is 250 cm³. Find the volume of the larger cylinder.
1
Curved surface area is an area, so the ratio of the two is k². The π cancels. k² = 98π50π = 1.96
2
Square root to reach the linear factor. k = 1.96 = 1.4
3
Cube it for the volume factor. k³ = 1.4³ = 2.744
4
Multiply the known volume. V = 250 × 2.744 = 686
volume of the larger cylinder = 686 cm³
Exam tip Two steps, both essential: area ratio → k → volume ratio. Never jump straight from an area ratio to a volume ratio.

Your turn

Practice set

  1. Two similar triangles have corresponding sides 5 cm and 12.5 cm. Another side of the smaller triangle is 7 cm. Find the corresponding side of the larger.
  2. Two similar shapes have a linear scale factor of 4. The area of the smaller is 15 cm². Find the area of the larger.
  3. Two similar cylinders have heights 10 cm and 25 cm. The volume of the smaller is 400 cm³. Find the volume of the larger.
  4. Two similar shapes have areas 20 cm² and 45 cm². A length on the smaller shape is 8 cm. Find the corresponding length on the larger.
  5. Two similar solids have volumes 54 cm³ and 128 cm³. The surface area of the smaller is 27 cm². Find the surface area of the larger.
  6. A model ship is built to a scale of 1 : 50. The model has a deck of area 32 cm² and a volume of 1500 cm³. Find the deck area of the real ship in m² and its volume in m³.

  1. k = 12.5 ÷ 5 = 2.5; 7 × 2.5 → 17.5 cm
  2. k² = 16; 15 × 16 → 240 cm²
  3. k = 2.5, k³ = 15.625; 400 × 15.625 → 6250 cm³
  4. k² = 45 ÷ 20 = 2.25, k = 1.5; 8 × 1.5 → 12 cm
  5. k³ = 128 ÷ 54 = 64/27, so k = 4/3 and k² = 16/9; 27 × 16/9 → 48 cm²
  6. k = 50: area 32 × 2500 = 80 000 cm² → 8 m²; volume 1500 × 125 000 = 187 500 000 cm³ → 187.5 m³

Key points to remember

  • Similar shapes have equal angles and sides in the same ratio; that ratio is the linear scale factor k.
  • Always compute k as large ÷ small using a pair of corresponding lengths.
  • Lengths × k, areas × k², volumes × k³. Surface area is an area, so it uses k².
  • Area picks up k twice and volume three times because they are made of two and three lengths.
  • Given an area ratio, square root it to get k; given a volume ratio, cube root it.
  • Never apply the linear factor straight to an area or volume — that is the most common error in this topic.
  • A map or model scale such as 1 : 20 is always a linear ratio.
  • Unit changes obey the same rule: 1 m² = 10 000 cm² and 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³.
