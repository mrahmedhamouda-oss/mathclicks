3.1  Lines and Angles

The angle facts every geometry question is built from, and how to write the reason the examiner is looking for.

Naming and describing angles

An angle measures turn. Two rays meeting at a point create an angle, and the whole of geometry in this course is built on knowing what to call each one and how big it can be.

NameSizeHow to spot it
Acute0° < a < 90°Sharp — smaller than a corner of a page
Righta = 90°Marked with a small square, never with an arc
Obtuse90° < a < 180°Open, but not yet a straight line
Straighta = 180°A half turn — the two rays form one straight line
Reflex180° < a < 360°The angle measured the “long way round”

The four angle types

Naming with three letters The angle written ∠ABC (or AB̂C) is the angle at B, between the arms BA and BC. The middle letter is always the vertex. If only one angle sits at a vertex you may write ∠B.

Perpendicular lines

Lines that cross at 90°. Written AB ⊥ CD.

Parallel lines

Lines that never meet, always the same distance apart. Matching arrows mark each parallel pair; a second pair gets double arrows.

The four angle facts you build everything on

1. Angles on a straight line

Angles that sit side by side on a straight line add up to 180°.

Reason: angles on a straight line

2. Angles at a point

Angles that fill a complete turn about a point add up to 360°.

Reason: angles at a point

3. Vertically opposite angles

When two straight lines cross, the angles opposite each other are equal.

Reason: vertically opposite angles

4. Complementary / supplementary

Two angles are complementary if they add to 90° and supplementary if they add to 180°.

On a straight line: 50 + 60 + 70 = 180

At a point: 80 + 75 + 95 + 110 = 360

Vertically opposite angles are equal

The two orange angles are equal and the two blue angles are equal. You can prove it: each pair of neighbours lies on a straight line, so both orange angles equal 180° − 105°.

Example 1Three angles (x + 30)°, 3x° and (2x + 30)° lie on a straight line. Find x and all three angles.
1
Write the fact as an equation. The three angles sit on a straight line, so they total 180°. (x + 30) + 3x + (2x + 30) = 180
2
Collect like terms. 6x + 60 = 180
3
Solve. 6x = 120  →  x = 20
4
Substitute back into each expression — and check the total. x + 30 = 50°,   3x = 60°,   2x + 30 = 70°
50 + 60 + 70 = 180 ✓
x = 20, and the angles are 50°, 60° and 70°
Exam tip Always substitute back. It costs ten seconds and catches almost every arithmetic slip before the examiner does.
Example 2Four angles meet at a point. They are a right angle, 2x°, 3x° and (x + 30)°. Find x and all four angles.
1
The four angles fill a complete turn, so they add to 360°. The small square means that angle is exactly 90°. 90 + 2x + 3x + (x + 30) = 360
2
Collect like terms. 6x + 120 = 360
3
Solve. 6x = 240  →  x = 40
4
Substitute back and check the total is 360°. 2x = 80°,   3x = 120°,   x + 30 = 70°
90 + 80 + 120 + 70 = 360 ✓
x = 40; the angles are 90°, 80°, 120° and 70°
Watch out A right angle is marked with a square, never with a number and never with an arc. If you see the square you may use 90° without being told.

Parallel lines and a transversal

A line that cuts across two parallel lines is called a transversal. It creates eight angles — but only two different sizes, and they always add to 180°. Every parallel-line question is just a matter of deciding which of the two sizes each angle is.

The eight angles

Here b = c = f = g and a = d = e = h, and any orange plus any blue makes 180°.

The three named relationships

Corresponding (F)

Corresponding angles are equal. Same corner at each crossing. The highlighted lines make an F (which may be back to front or upside down).

Alternate (Z)

Alternate angles are equal. They sit between the parallels, on opposite sides of the transversal. The highlighted path makes a Z.

Co-interior / allied (C)

Co-interior angles add to 180°. Between the parallels, on the same side of the transversal. The highlighted path makes a C. Here 62 + 118 = 180.

Watch out F, Z and C are memory aids, not reasons. In the exam you must write the words “corresponding angles”, “alternate angles” or “co-interior angles”. And every one of them needs the lines to be parallel — if the diagram has no arrows, you cannot use these facts at all.

Example 3In the diagram the two lines are parallel. Find p, q and r, giving a reason for each.
1
p is directly opposite the 68° angle at the same crossing. p = 68°  (vertically opposite angles)
2
q and p both lie between the parallels, on the same side of the transversal. q = 180 − 68 = 112°  (co-interior angles)
3
r is opposite q at the lower crossing. r = 112°  (vertically opposite angles)
p = 68°,   q = 112°,   r = 112°
Exam tip There is usually more than one valid route. Marks are for a correct value with a correct reason, so pick whichever single step you can justify most cleanly.

Multi-step angle chases — and the reasons

A “chase” is a chain of small steps. Examiners award marks for each value with its reason, so never write a bare number. Use this vocabulary and nothing else:

What you usedWrite exactly this
Two angles making a half turnangles on a straight line (sum 180°)
Angles filling a full turnangles at a point (sum 360°)
Angles opposite at a crossingvertically opposite angles
Same corner, F shapecorresponding angles
Opposite sides, Z shapealternate angles
Same side, between the parallelsco-interior angles (sum 180°)
Exam tip If a diagram has a bend or a zig-zag between two parallel lines, draw a third line through the corner, parallel to the other two. It turns one hard angle into two easy alternate angles. Draw it dashed and label it “parallel to AB” — the construction itself earns credit.
Example 4In the diagram AB is parallel to CD, ∠APX = 42° and ∠CQX = 35°. Find ∠PXQ.
1
Draw a dashed line through X parallel to AB (and therefore parallel to CD as well). construction line through X, parallel to AB and CD
2
The upper part of ∠PXQ and ∠APX are alternate angles between AB and the dashed line. upper part = 42°  (alternate angles)
3
The lower part and ∠CQX are alternate angles between the dashed line and CD. lower part = 35°  (alternate angles)
4
Add the two parts. ∠PXQ = 42 + 35 = 77°
∠PXQ = 77°
The pattern For a single zig-zag between parallel lines, the bend angle is simply the sum of the two outer angles. That shortcut is worth knowing — but still show the construction line, because the reason marks live there.

More worked examples

Example 5Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. Two co-interior angles are (4x − 20)° and (2x + 20)°. Find x and both angles.
1
Co-interior angles between parallel lines add to 180°. (4x − 20) + (2x + 20) = 180
2
The −20 and +20 cancel. 6x = 180  →  x = 30
3
Substitute and check. 4(30) − 20 = 100°
2(30) + 20 = 80°
100 + 80 = 180 ✓
x = 30; the angles are 100° and 80°
Watch out Students often set co-interior angles equal. They are equal only in the special case 90° and 90°. Same side between the parallels always means add to 180°.
Example 6The two horizontal lines are parallel. Find a, b, c and d, giving a reason for each step.
1
a and 116° sit next to each other on the upper parallel line. a = 180 − 116 = 64°  (angles on a straight line)
2
b is opposite the 116° angle at the same crossing. b = 116°  (vertically opposite angles)
3
c occupies the same corner at the lower crossing as a does at the upper one. c = 64°  (corresponding angles)
4
d is opposite c. d = 64°  (vertically opposite angles)
5
Sanity check: at the lower crossing the four angles are 64, 116, 64, 116. 64 + 116 + 64 + 116 = 360 ✓
a = 64°,   b = 116°,   c = 64°,   d = 64°
Example 7Angles (3t + 5)° and (5t − 25)° are alternate angles between two parallel lines. A third angle w is co-interior with the first. Find t and w.
1
Alternate angles are equal, so form an equation. 3t + 5 = 5t − 25
2
Collect the t terms on the side where they stay positive. 5 + 25 = 5t − 3t
30 = 2t  →  t = 15
3
Check both expressions give the same angle. 3(15) + 5 = 50°  and  5(15) − 25 = 50° ✓
4
Co-interior with a 50° angle means they total 180°. w = 180 − 50 = 130°
t = 15,   w = 130°
Exam tip Read the words before you look at the picture. “Alternate” means set them equal; “co-interior” or “allied” means make them add to 180°.

Your turn

Practice set

  1. Name each angle type: (a) 47°  (b) 90°  (c) 163°  (d) 275°  (e) 180°
  2. Three angles on a straight line are 4x°, (3x + 10)° and (2x − 10)°. Find x and the three angles.
  3. Four angles at a point are 6y°, 5y°, 4y° and 3y°. Find y and the largest angle.
  4. Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, and one angle is 73°. Write down, with reasons, the size of (a) the corresponding angle, (b) the alternate angle, (c) the co-interior angle.
  5. AB is parallel to CD. A zig-zag runs from P on AB to X to Q on CD, with ∠APX = 28° and ∠CQX = 47°. Find ∠PXQ.
  6. Two co-interior angles between parallel lines are (2x + 15)° and (3x − 20)°. Find x and both angles, and state which is obtuse.

  1. (a) acute  (b) right  (c) obtuse  (d) reflex  (e) straight (a half turn)
  2. 4x + 3x + 10 + 2x − 10 = 180 → 9x = 180 → x = 20; angles 80°, 70°, 30° (check 80 + 70 + 30 = 180).
  3. 6y + 5y + 4y + 3y = 360 → 18y = 360 → y = 20; largest = 6(20) = 120°.
  4. (a) 73° (corresponding angles)  (b) 73° (alternate angles)  (c) 180 − 73 = 107° (co-interior angles).
  5. Draw a line through X parallel to AB: the two parts are 28° and 47° (alternate angles each time), so ∠PXQ = 28 + 47 = 75°.
  6. 2x + 15 + 3x − 20 = 180 → 5x − 5 = 180 → 5x = 185 → x = 37; angles 89° and 91° (check 89 + 91 = 180); the obtuse one is 91°.

Key points to remember

  • Acute < 90°, right = 90°, obtuse between 90° and 180°, reflex between 180° and 360°.
  • Angles on a straight line add to 180°; angles at a point add to 360°; vertically opposite angles are equal.
  • Complementary angles add to 90°; supplementary angles add to 180°.
  • With parallel lines: corresponding (F) equal, alternate (Z) equal, co-interior (C) add to 180°.
  • A transversal across parallel lines makes only two different angle sizes, and they are supplementary.
  • No arrows on the diagram means no parallel-line reasons — check before you use them.
  • For a zig-zag between parallels, draw a dashed line through the bend parallel to the others; the bend angle is the sum of the two outer angles.
  • Every step in an angle chase needs a value and a reason. A number on its own scores nothing in the reasoning marks.
