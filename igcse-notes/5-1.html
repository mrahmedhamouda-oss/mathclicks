5.1  Revisiting Fractions

Equivalent fractions, lowest terms, mixed numbers, ordering with a common denominator, and the link between fractions and decimals.

What it means Comparing Decimals Examples Your turn Key points

What a fraction actually means

A fraction is a division waiting to happen. The line between the two numbers is a division sign, so 34 = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75. That single idea explains everything else in this chapter, so hold on to it.

Definition In ab the numerator a counts how many parts you have, and the denominator b says how many equal parts the whole was cut into. The denominator can never be 0.

Equivalent fractions

Multiplying the numerator and denominator by the same number does not change the value — you are just cutting the same amount into more, smaller pieces. Dividing both by the same number does not change it either.

34 = 3 × 24 × 2 = 68 = 912 = 75100 = 0.75 Whatever you do to the top, do to the bottom

The same amount cut three different ways

Lowest terms

A fraction is in its lowest terms (or simplest form) when the only number that divides both the numerator and the denominator is 1. The fastest route there is to divide both by their HCF.

Example 1Write 84126 in its lowest terms.
1
Break both numbers into prime factors. 84 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7
126 = 2 × 3 × 3 × 7
2
The HCF is the product of the factors they share: one 2, one 3, one 7. HCF = 2 × 3 × 7 = 42
3
Divide top and bottom by 42. 84 ÷ 42 = 2    126 ÷ 42 = 3
84126 = 23   (both equal 0.6̇ — a good check)
Exam tip If you cannot spot the HCF, just keep halving or dividing by 3, 7, 11… until nothing works. Repeated simplifying gets you to the same place; it only costs you time, not marks.

Proper, improper and mixed

Proper fraction

Numerator smaller than denominator, so the value is less than 1.

38, 1120

Improper fraction

Numerator at least as big as the denominator — sometimes called top-heavy.

358, 476

Mixed number

A whole number and a proper fraction written side by side; the two parts are added.

438 = 4 + 38

mixed → improper:   abc = (a × c) + bc Multiply the whole by the denominator, add the numerator, keep the denominator
Example 2(a) Write 438 as an improper fraction. (b) Write 476 as a mixed number.
1
(a) There are 8 eighths in every whole, so 4 wholes are 4 × 8 = 32 eighths. Add the 3 you already have. (4 × 8) + 3 = 35  →  35/8
2
Check with decimals. 35 ÷ 8 = 4.375  =  4 + 0.375  ✓
3
(b) Going back is a division with a remainder. 47 ÷ 6 = 7 remainder 5
4
The quotient is the whole number, the remainder is the new numerator, the denominator is unchanged. 47/6 = 7 5/6  (both are 7.83̇)
(a) 358     (b) 756
Watch out 438 means 4 + 38, not 4 × 38. In algebra, writing two things next to each other does mean multiply — which is exactly why examiners never use mixed numbers inside algebraic expressions.

Comparing and ordering fractions

You cannot compare fifths with eighths any more than you can compare metres with pounds. Rewrite every fraction over the same denominator first — the LCM of the denominators is the smallest one that works — then simply compare the numerators.

Note Once the denominators match, the bigger numerator is the bigger fraction. If the question mixes fractions with decimals, converting everything to decimals is also acceptable — but show the comparison line either way, because that is where the method mark sits.
Example 3Write 58, 712, 35 and 0.62 in order, smallest first.
1
Find the LCM of 8, 12 and 5. 8 = 2³,  12 = 2² × 3,  5 = 5
LCM = 2³ × 3 × 5 = 120
2
Rewrite each fraction over 120. 5/8 = 75/120  (×15)
7/12 = 70/120  (×10)
3/5 = 72/120  (×24)
3
Deal with the decimal by turning it into 120ths too: 0.62 × 120 = 74.4. 0.62 = 74.4/120
4
Order the numerators: 70, 72, 74.4, 75. 70 < 72 < 74.4 < 75
712, 35, 0.62, 58
Watch out The question asked for the original numbers in order. Writing 70/120, 72/120, … as your final answer loses the accuracy mark. Convert back.
Exam tip For just two fractions you can cross-multiply: ab versus cd — compare a × d with c × b. It is the same as using the common denominator bd, only quicker.

Fraction comparer

Type two fractions. The tool shows the common denominator, which one is larger, and each fraction in its lowest terms and as a decimal. Try 7/11 against 9/14.

Fractions, decimals and quantities

Because the fraction line is a division sign, converting a fraction to a decimal needs no new skill: divide the top by the bottom.

740 = 7 ÷ 40 = 0.175      0.175 = 1751000 = 740 Going back: put the digits over 10, 100, 1000… then simplify

Terminating or recurring?

Write the fraction in its lowest terms first, then look at the denominator's prime factors.

Terminating

The denominator's only prime factors are 2 and/or 5 — the primes that build 10.

740, 40 = 2³ × 5 → 0.175

316, 16 = 24 → 0.1875

Recurring

The denominator contains any other prime (3, 7, 11, 13…).

512, 12 = 2² × 3 → 0.416̇

27 → 0.2̇85714̇

Example 4Convert 740 and 512 to decimals, stating whether each terminates or recurs.
1
Both are already in lowest terms, so factorise the denominators. 40 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 5
12 = 2 × 2 × 3
2
40 uses only 2s and a 5, so 740 terminates. Divide it out. 7 ÷ 40 = 0.175
3
12 contains a 3, so 512 must recur. 5 ÷ 12 = 0.41666…
4
Only the 6 repeats, so the dot goes on the 6 alone. 0.41666… = 0.416̇
740 = 0.175 (terminating)     512 = 0.416̇ (recurring)

Finding a fraction of a quantity

In mathematics the word of means multiply. In practice, divide by the denominator and then multiply by the numerator — dividing first keeps the numbers small.

38 of 240 = 240 ÷ 8 × 3 = 30 × 3 = 90 Divide by the bottom, multiply by the top
Exam tip If a question says “25 of the students walk to school”, the fraction that do not walk is 1 − 25 = 35. Nearly every “the rest” question is solved by subtracting from 1.

More worked examples

Example 5Write (a) 0.325 and (b) 2.6 as fractions in their lowest terms.
1
(a) There are three decimal places, so the last digit is thousandths. 0.325 = 325/1000
2
Simplify. 325 = 5² × 13 and 1000 = 2³ × 5³, so the HCF is 25. 325 ÷ 25 = 13    1000 ÷ 25 = 40
0.325 = 13/40
3
Check: 13 ÷ 40 = 0.325 ✓
4
(b) Keep the whole number to one side. 2.6 = 2 + 6/10 = 2 + 3/5 = 2 3/5
5
As an improper fraction if asked: (2 × 5) + 3 = 13  →  13/5 = 2.6 ✓
(a) 1340     (b) 235 = 135
Example 6A school has 1260 students. 49 walk to school. Of the remainder, 35 come by bus. How many students come by bus?
1
Find the number who walk: divide by 9, multiply by 4. 1260 ÷ 9 = 140
140 × 4 = 560 walk
2
The remainder is everyone else. 1260 − 560 = 700
3
Take 35 of the remainder, not of 1260. 700 ÷ 5 = 140
140 × 3 = 420
420 students come by bus
Exam trap “Of the remainder” is the single most common way this question is made harder. Taking 35 of 1260 gives 756 — wrong, and worth no marks.
Example 7In a survey of 180 people, 25 chose tea and 512 chose coffee; the rest chose juice. (a) Which drink was more popular, tea or coffee? (b) How many chose juice?
1
(a) Put both fractions over the LCM of 5 and 12, which is 60. 2/5 = 24/60    5/12 = 25/60
2
25 > 24, so coffee wins — only just. 5/12 > 2/5, so coffee
3
(b) The whole survey is 1, or 6060. Subtract the two known fractions. 60/60 − 24/60 − 25/60 = 11/60
4
Find 1160 of 180. 180 ÷ 60 = 3
3 × 11 = 33
(a) coffee     (b) 33 people chose juice
Check it Tea = 180 × 25 = 72, coffee = 180 × 512 = 75, juice = 33, and 72 + 75 + 33 = 180 ✓

Key points to remember

  • The fraction line is a division sign: ab = a ÷ b. Use it to check any answer in decimals.
  • Multiplying or dividing both numerator and denominator by the same number gives an equivalent fraction — the value is unchanged.
  • Lowest terms: divide top and bottom by their HCF. Always give final answers simplified.
  • Mixed to improper: (whole × denominator) + numerator. Improper to mixed: divide and keep the remainder as the new numerator.
  • To compare or order fractions, rewrite them over the LCM of the denominators, then compare numerators — and give the answer using the original numbers.
  • In lowest terms, a denominator built only from 2s and 5s gives a terminating decimal; any other prime factor gives a recurring one.
  • Of means multiply: divide by the denominator, then multiply by the numerator.
  • “The rest” means 1 − (the fractions you already have), and “of the remainder” never means “of the original total”.
