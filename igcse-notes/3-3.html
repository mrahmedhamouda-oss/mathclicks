The angle sum of a quadrilateral

a + b + c + d = 360° The four interior angles of any quadrilateral

Why it is 360°

Draw one diagonal. It cuts the quadrilateral into two triangles, and every angle of those two triangles is part of an angle of the quadrilateral. Two lots of 180° gives 360°.

Same idea, any polygon An n-sided polygon splits into n − 2 triangles, so its angles total (n − 2) × 180°. For n = 4 that is 2 × 180 = 360°.
Example 1The angles of a quadrilateral are x°, 2x°, 3x° and 4x°. Find all four angles.
1
The four interior angles of a quadrilateral add to 360°. x + 2x + 3x + 4x = 360
2
Collect and solve. 10x = 360  →  x = 36
3
Substitute back and check the total. 36°, 72°, 108°, 144°
36 + 72 + 108 + 144 = 360 ✓
36°, 72°, 108° and 144°
Watch out A quadrilateral may contain a reflex angle (an arrowhead shape). The 360° rule still holds — you just use the reflex value in the sum.

The special quadrilaterals

Each shape below is defined by its sides; the angle, diagonal and symmetry properties then follow. Learn them as a family: a square is a rectangle and a rhombus, and both of those are parallelograms.

Square

4 equal sides, 4 right angles. Diagonals equal, bisect at 90° and bisect the corner angles. 4 lines of symmetry, order 4.

Rectangle

Opposite sides equal and parallel, 4 right angles. Diagonals equal and bisect each other. 2 lines of symmetry, order 2.

Parallelogram

Opposite sides equal and parallel, opposite angles equal, adjacent angles add to 180°. Diagonals bisect each other. No symmetry lines, order 2.

Rhombus

A parallelogram with 4 equal sides. Diagonals bisect each other at 90° and bisect the corner angles. 2 lines of symmetry, order 2.

Trapezium

One pair of parallel sides. The two angles on each slanted side are co-interior, so they add to 180°. No symmetry, order 1.

Kite

Two pairs of adjacent equal sides. One pair of opposite angles equal. Diagonals cross at 90°; the long one bisects the short one. 1 line of symmetry, order 1.

Isosceles trapezium A trapezium whose two non-parallel sides are equal. It gains a line of symmetry, its base angles come in equal pairs, and its diagonals are equal. Exam diagrams often mark it only with two tick marks — spot them and you gain a whole set of facts.

Using the properties to find angles

Because a parallelogram and a trapezium contain parallel sides, every one of them hides a pair of co-interior angles. That is the fact that turns one given angle into all four.

Co-interior angles inside a parallelogram

Example 2In parallelogram ABCD above, ∠DAB = 62°. Find x, y and z with reasons.
1
AB is parallel to DC, and AD crosses them both. The angles at A and D are on the same side of that transversal. z = 180 − 62 = 118°  (co-interior angles)
2
Opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal. y = 62°  (opposite angles of a parallelogram)
x = 118°  (opposite angles of a parallelogram)
3
Check with the angle sum of a quadrilateral. 62 + 118 + 62 + 118 = 360 ✓
x = 118°,   y = 62°,   z = 118°
The short version In a parallelogram the four angles are always a, 180 − a, a, 180 − a as you go round. One given angle is enough.
Example 3Three angles of a quadrilateral are 87°, 113° and 64°. Find the fourth, and state whether the quadrilateral could be a trapezium.
1
Subtract the known angles from 360°. 87 + 113 + 64 = 264
fourth angle = 360 − 264 = 96°
2
For a trapezium we need a pair of angles on the same leg adding to 180°. Test the pairs. 87 + 113 = 200,   87 + 64 = 151,   87 + 96 = 183
113 + 64 = 177,   113 + 96 = 209,   64 + 96 = 160
3
No pair totals 180°, so no pair of sides can be parallel. it cannot be a trapezium
Fourth angle = 96°; the shape cannot be a trapezium

More worked examples

Example 4A kite has one angle of 116° and the opposite angle 68°. Find the other two angles.
1
In a kite the two angles between the pairs of unequal sides are equal. Call each of them x. the two side angles are equal (kite has a line of symmetry)
2
Use the angle sum of a quadrilateral. 116 + 68 + x + x = 360
3
Solve. 2x = 360 − 184 = 176  →  x = 88°
4
Check. 116 + 68 + 88 + 88 = 360 ✓
The other two angles are both 88°
Watch out Only one pair of opposite angles in a kite is equal — the pair the line of symmetry does not pass through. The angles at the top and bottom of the symmetry line are usually different.
Example 5An isosceles trapezium has its parallel sides horizontal, and one angle at the longer base is 72°. Find the other three angles.
1
The line of symmetry maps one base angle onto the other. r = 72°  (base angles of an isosceles trapezium)
2
The left-hand leg is a transversal of the two parallel sides, so the angles at its two ends are co-interior. p = 180 − 72 = 108°  (co-interior angles)
3
By symmetry (or by co-interior angles on the right-hand leg). q = 108°
4
Check with the angle sum. 72 + 72 + 108 + 108 = 360 ✓
r = 72°,   p = q = 108°
Example 6ABCD is a rhombus with ∠ABC = 116°. Find ∠BAC, giving your reasons.
1
All four sides of a rhombus are equal, so in triangle ABC we have AB = BC. triangle ABC is isosceles
2
The base angles opposite those equal sides are equal. ∠BAC = ∠BCA = x  (base angles of an isosceles triangle)
3
Use the angle sum of triangle ABC. 116 + x + x = 180  →  2x = 64  →  x = 32°
4
Check by the diagonal property. Adjacent angles of a rhombus are co-interior, so ∠DAB = 180 − 116 = 64°, and the diagonal AC bisects it. 64 ÷ 2 = 32° ✓
∠BAC = 32°
Example 7The angles of a quadrilateral are (2x + 10)°, (3x − 20)°, (x + 50)° and 4x°. Find x, list the four angles and say whether the shape could be a parallelogram.
1
The angles of a quadrilateral total 360°. (2x + 10) + (3x − 20) + (x + 50) + 4x = 360
2
Collect the x terms and the numbers separately. 10x + 40 = 360
3
Solve. 10x = 320  →  x = 32
4
Substitute into each expression. 2(32) + 10 = 74°
3(32) − 20 = 76°
32 + 50 = 82°
4(32) = 128°
74 + 76 + 82 + 128 = 360 ✓
5
A parallelogram needs two pairs of equal angles. Here all four values are different. no two angles are equal → not a parallelogram
x = 32; the angles are 74°, 76°, 82° and 128°, and the shape is not a parallelogram
Exam tip When a question adds “is it a parallelogram / trapezium?”, test the angle pairs: parallelogram needs two equal pairs, trapezium needs one pair summing to 180°. State the test you used.

Your turn

Practice set

  1. Three angles of a quadrilateral are 104°, 39° and 151°. Find the fourth.
  2. One angle of a parallelogram is 47°. Write down the other three, with a reason for each.
  3. A kite has angles 124° and 36° at the two ends of its line of symmetry. Find the other two angles.
  4. Name the quadrilateral: (a) diagonals equal and bisect each other at 90°; (b) diagonals bisect each other but are not equal and not perpendicular; (c) exactly one line of symmetry and one pair of parallel sides.
  5. In an isosceles trapezium the two angles at the shorter parallel side are 115° each. Find the other two angles.
  6. The angles of a quadrilateral are (3x − 15)°, (2x + 25)°, (4x)° and (x + 30)°. Find x and the largest angle.

  1. 104 + 39 + 151 = 294, so the fourth is 360 − 294 = 66°.
  2. Opposite angle 47° (opposite angles of a parallelogram); the other two are 180 − 47 = 133° each (co-interior angles). Check 47 + 133 + 47 + 133 = 360.
  3. 360 − 124 − 36 = 200; the two remaining angles are equal, so each is 200 ÷ 2 = 100°.
  4. (a) square  (b) parallelogram  (c) isosceles trapezium.
  5. Each leg gives co-interior angles: 180 − 115 = 65° each. Check 115 + 115 + 65 + 65 = 360.
  6. 3x − 15 + 2x + 25 + 4x + x + 30 = 360 → 10x + 40 = 360 → x = 32; angles 81°, 89°, 128°, 62° (total 360), largest 128°.

Key points to remember

  • The interior angles of any quadrilateral add to 360°, because one diagonal splits it into two triangles.
  • Parallelogram: opposite sides parallel and equal, opposite angles equal, adjacent angles co-interior so they sum to 180°.
  • Rhombus = parallelogram with 4 equal sides; its diagonals cross at 90° and bisect the corner angles.
  • Rectangle: 4 right angles and equal diagonals; a square has every property of both a rectangle and a rhombus.
  • Trapezium: one parallel pair, so the two angles on each leg add to 180°. An isosceles trapezium adds equal legs, equal base angles, equal diagonals and one line of symmetry.
  • Kite: two pairs of adjacent equal sides, one pair of equal opposite angles, diagonals meeting at 90°.
  • A diagonal of a rhombus, square or kite creates an isosceles triangle — that is usually the fastest route to a missing angle.
  • Give the most specific name when asked to identify a shape, and a reason for every angle you write down.
