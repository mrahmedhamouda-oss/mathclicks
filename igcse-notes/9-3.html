9.3  Surds

Simplifying roots, multiplying and dividing them, expanding brackets, rationalising denominators and giving exact answers in surd form.

What a surd is, and why we keep them

Definition A surd is a root that cannot be written exactly as a rational number, such as 2, 18 or 35. 16 = 4 is not a surd, because the root comes out exactly.

A calculator gives 2 = 1.414213562…, which is rounded and therefore slightly wrong. Writing 2 instead keeps the value exact. That is why questions say “give your answer in surd form” or “give an exact answer” — a decimal, however many digits, would lose the mark.

a × b = ab      ab = ab      (a)² = a The three rules that drive everything in this lesson
Watch out There is no rule for adding. 9 + 16 = 3 + 4 = 7, but 9 + 16 = 25 = 5. So a + b ≠ a + b. This is the most heavily penalised error in the whole topic.

Exact answers in action

A square has area 50 cm². Its side is √50 = 5√2 cm, so the perimeter is 20√2 cm and, by Pythagoras, the diagonal d satisfies d² = (5√2)² + (5√2)² = 50 + 50 = 100, giving d = 10 cm exactly.

Round the side to 7.07 instead and the diagonal comes out as 9.998… The surd form gives the clean, correct answer, which is why the syllabus asks for it.

Simplifying a surd

To simplify n, split n into the largest perfect square factor times whatever is left, then take the square root of the square part outside.

1

Exam tip If you pick a smaller square factor you still get there — just keep simplifying until the number under the root has no square factors left.

Adding, subtracting and expanding

Surds behave exactly like algebraic terms. You can only collect like surds — ones with the same number under the root — just as you can only collect like terms in algebra.

Like surds collect

5√2 + 3√2 = 8√2

7√3 − √3 = 6√3

Think of √2 as x: 5x + 3x = 8x.

Unlike surds do not

√2 + √3

cannot be simplified — leave it exactly as it is. Always simplify first, though: surds that look unlike often are not, as √12 + √27 = 2√3 + 3√3 = 5√3.

Example 2Simplify √50 + √18 − √8.
1
Simplify each surd separately, using the largest square factor each time. √50 = √25 × √2 = 5√2
√18 = √9 × √2 = 3√2
√8 = √4 × √2 = 2√2
2
They are now all multiples of √2, so collect the coefficients. 5√2 + 3√2 − 2√2 = (5 + 3 − 2)√2 = 6√2
3
Check on a calculator. 7.0711 + 4.2426 − 2.8284 = 8.4853
6√2 = 8.4853 ✓
6√2

Expanding brackets

Expand exactly as you would in algebra, then use (√a)² = a to tidy up.

Note The third line is the key one. (a + b√c)(a − b√c) = a² − b²c is always rational: the two middle terms cancel. This pair is called a pair of conjugates, and it is the engine behind rationalising denominators.

Rationalising the denominator

Mathematical convention says a final answer should have no surd in the denominator. Removing it is called rationalising, and it comes in two versions.

Multiply the top and bottom by that surd. Since √a × √a = a, the bottom becomes rational.

ka = ka × aa = kaa

6/√3 = 6√3/3 = 2√3

Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate: change the sign in the middle. The bottom becomes a difference of two squares and so is rational.

ka + bc × a − bca − bc = k(a − bc)a² − b²c

1/(3 + √2) = (3 − √2)/7

Example 3Rationalise the denominator of 126.
1
Multiply top and bottom by √6 — that is multiplying by 1, so the value is unchanged. 12/√6 × √6/√6 = 12√6 / 6
2
Cancel the rational numbers. 12 ÷ 6 = 2  →  2√6
3
Check: 12 ÷ 2.4495 = 4.899 and 2 × 2.4495 = 4.899 ✓
2√6
Exam tip Do not multiply by √24 or anything clever — the smallest surd that clears the bottom is always the surd itself.
Example 4Rationalise the denominator of 83 − 5, giving your answer in the form a + b√5.
1
The conjugate of 3 − √5 is 3 + √5. Multiply top and bottom by it. 8/(3 − √5) × (3 + √5)/(3 + √5)
2
Bottom first — difference of two squares, so no surds survive. (3 − √5)(3 + √5) = 9 − 5 = 4
3
Now the top. 8(3 + √5) = 24 + 8√5
4
Divide every term by 4. (24 + 8√5)/4 = 6 + 2√5
5
Check: 8 ÷ (3 − 2.2361) = 8 ÷ 0.7639 = 10.472, and 6 + 2(2.2361) = 10.472 ✓
6 + 2√5
Watch out Divide every term of the numerator by the denominator, not just the first. Writing (24 + 8√5)/4 = 6 + 8√5 throws away the accuracy mark.

Rationalising tool

Set the fraction k ÷ (a + b√c). Put a = 0 for a single surd on the bottom. Try k = 8, a = 3, b = −1, c = 5.

More worked examples

Example 5Work out (a) √8 × √6 (b) √32 ÷ √2 (c) 3√5 × 4√10, giving each answer in its simplest surd form.
1
(a) Put both under one root, then simplify. √8 × √6 = √48 = √16 × √3 = 4√3
2
(b) Division works the same way, and here the answer is not a surd at all. √32 ÷ √2 = √(32/2) = √16 = 4
3
(c) Multiply the outside numbers together and the inside numbers together. 3 × 4 = 12    √5 × √10 = √50 = 5√2
12 × 5√2 = 60√2
(a) 4√3    (b) 4    (c) 60√2
Example 6Expand and simplify (a) (3 + √5)(2 − √5) (b) (2√3 − 1)².
1
(a) Multiply every term in the first bracket by every term in the second. 3 × 2 = 6
3 × (−√5) = −3√5
√5 × 2 = 2√5
√5 × (−√5) = −(√5)² = −5
2
Collect the rational parts and the surd parts separately. (6 − 5) + (−3 + 2)√5 = 1 − √5
3
Check: (3 + 2.2361)(2 − 2.2361) = 5.2361 × (−0.2361) = −1.2361, and 1 − 2.2361 = −1.2361 ✓
4
(b) Write the square out as two brackets — never square the terms separately. (2√3 − 1)(2√3 − 1)
5
Expand term by term, using (√3)² = 3. 2√3 × 2√3 = 4 × 3 = 12
2√3 × (−1) = −2√3  (this happens twice)
(−1) × (−1) = 1
6
Collect. 12 + 1 − 4√3 = 13 − 4√3 Check: (3.4641 − 1)² = 2.4641² = 6.0718 and 13 − 4(1.7321) = 6.0718 ✓
(a) 1 − √5   ·   (b) 13 − 4√3
Watch out √5 × √5 = 5, not √25 left unsimplified and certainly not 25. And (2√3 − 1)² ≠ 12 + 1 — the middle terms matter.
Example 7A rectangle has area 24 cm² and one side of length (3 + √3) cm. Find the other side in the form a + b√3.
1
Other side = area ÷ known side. other side = 24 / (3 + √3)
2
Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate 3 − √3. bottom: (3 + √3)(3 − √3) = 9 − 3 = 6
top: 24(3 − √3) = 72 − 24√3
3
Divide each term by 6. (72 − 24√3)/6 = 12 − 4√3
4
Sense-check the size: 12 − 6.928 = 5.07 cm, and (3 + 1.732) × 5.07 = 24.0 ✓
(12 − 4√3) cm
Exam tip Length answers must be positive. If a rationalised length comes out negative, you have almost certainly used the wrong conjugate sign.

Key points to remember

  • A surd is an exact root that is irrational. “Exact answer” or “surd form” means do not use a decimal.
  • Simplify by taking out the largest perfect square factor: √72 = √36 × √2 = 6√2.
  • √a × √b = √(ab) and √a ÷ √b = √(a/b), but √a + √b ≠ √(a + b).
  • Only like surds can be added or subtracted — simplify each one first, then collect the coefficients.
  • Expand brackets exactly as in algebra and use (√a)² = a.
  • (a + b√c)(a − b√c) = a² − b²c is always rational — this is what makes rationalising work.
  • To rationalise: multiply top and bottom by the surd itself, or by the conjugate when the denominator has two terms.
  • Finish by dividing every term of the numerator by the rational denominator, and cancel.
