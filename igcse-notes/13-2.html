The 12-hour and 24-hour clock

Time is the one topic in 0580 that is not decimal. There are 60 seconds in a minute, 60 minutes in an hour and 24 hours in a day, so a calculator will happily give you an answer that looks right and is completely wrong. Start by being fluent with the two ways of writing a time.

12-hour clock

  • Hours run 12, 1, 2, … 11 and then start again.
  • Always needs am (midnight to noon) or pm (noon to midnight).
  • Written 7.45 pm or 7:45 pm.

24-hour clock

  • Hours run 00 to 23; no am or pm.
  • Always four digits: 08:05, not 8:05.
  • Midnight is 00:00; noon is 12:00.
Converting am times: keep the hours the same, but write midnight hours as 00 (12:30 am = 00:30).
pm times: add 12 to the hours — except 12-something pm, which is already right (12:40 pm = 12:40).

The 24-hour clock face

Example 1Write (a) 8:05 pm and (b) 12:15 am in 24-hour clock time; write (c) 03:40 and (d) 19:08 in 12-hour clock time.
1
(a) It is pm and the hour is not 12, so add 12 to the hours. 8 + 12 = 20  →  20:05
2
(b) 12-something am is just after midnight, so the hour becomes 00. 12:15 am → 00:15
3
(c) The hour is less than 12, so it is a morning time. 03:40 → 3:40 am
4
(d) The hour is more than 12, so subtract 12 and write pm. 19 − 12 = 7  →  7:08 pm
(a) 20:05   (b) 00:15   (c) 3:40 am   (d) 7:08 pm
Watch out 12:15 am is a quarter past midnight, while 12:15 pm is a quarter past noon. They are twelve hours apart.

Adding and subtracting times

Never do time arithmetic in a straight line on the calculator. Split the journey at the whole hour and count in two easy pieces — that is exactly how the mark scheme expects to see it.

Adding a duration

  1. Add the whole hours first.
  2. Then add the minutes.
  3. If the minutes go past 60, subtract 60 and carry 1 hour.

09:47 + 3 h 40 min → 12:47 → 12:87 → 13:27

Finding a duration

  1. Count from the start time up to the next whole hour.
  2. Count whole hours.
  3. Count the last few minutes.

14:35 → 15:00 is 25 min; 15:00 → 17:00 is 2 h; 17:00 → 17:10 is 10 min → 2 h 35 min

Watch out 10:50 + 25 min is not 10:75. Once the minutes reach 60 you carry an hour: 10:75 = 11:15. Subtracting works the same way in reverse — borrow 60, not 100.

Crossing midnight

If the finishing time is earlier in the day than the starting time, the clock has gone past midnight. Split at 24:00.

Example 2A nurse begins a night shift at 21:45 and finishes at 06:30 the next morning. How long is the shift?
1
The finish is earlier on the clock than the start, so the shift crosses midnight. Count up to midnight first. 21:45 → 22:00 is 15 min
22:00 → 24:00 is 2 h
so 21:45 → 00:00 is 2 h 15 min
2
Now count from midnight to the finish. 00:00 → 06:30 is 6 h 30 min
3
Add the two pieces, carrying an hour when the minutes reach 60. 2 h 15 min + 6 h 30 min = 8 h 45 min
The shift lasts 8 hours 45 minutes
Exam tip A quick check: 21:45 + 8 h = 05:45, and 05:45 + 45 min = 06:30. Always run the addition back to confirm the subtraction.

Minutes as decimals of an hour

Speed, distance and time questions need the time as a decimal number of hours, and this is where the single most common error in the whole of Chapter 13 lives.

The classic mistake 2 h 45 min is not 2.45 hours. There are 60 minutes in an hour, not 100, so 45 min = 4560 = 0.75 h and 2 h 45 min = 2.75 h.

Example 3(a) Write 3 h 24 min as a decimal number of hours. (b) Write 2.15 hours in hours and minutes.
1
(a) Only the minutes change; divide them by 60. 24 ÷ 60 = 0.4
2
Put the whole hours back on the front. 3 h 24 min = 3.4 h
3
(b) The 2 is 2 whole hours. Multiply the decimal part by 60. 0.15 × 60 = 9 min
(a) 3.4 hours    (b) 2 hours 9 minutes
Watch out 2.15 h is not 2 h 15 min. Two hours and fifteen minutes would be 2.25 h.

Using time in speed calculations

The speed formula only works when the units agree. If a speed is in km/h then the time must be in hours.

speed = distancetime    distance = speed × time    time = distancespeed Rearrange, then convert the time before substituting
Exam tip When the answer for a time comes out as a decimal, convert it back into hours and minutes if the question asks for a clock time. 3.75 h = 3 h 45 min, so leaving at 09:20 means arriving at 13:05.

Minutes and decimal hours

Convert either way and see the division or multiplication by 60 written out in full.

Timetables and time zones

A timetable is just a table of 24-hour clock times. Each column is one bus or train; each row is one place. To find how long a journey takes, read the two times in the same column and find the difference.

Harbour07:3508:5012:15
Market07:5209:0712:32
Station08:1409:2912:54
Airport08:4710:0213:27
Note Reading down a column gives a journey; reading across a row gives the gap between buses. Bus A leaves the Harbour at 07:35 and Bus B at 08:50, so the buses are 1 h 15 min apart.

Time zones

Different places set their clocks to different local times. A time zone is written as an offset from UTC, for example UTC+4. To compare two places, move both clocks into the same zone before you subtract.

Going east (a bigger offset)

Local time is ahead. If London is UTC+1 and Dubai is UTC+4, Dubai is 4 − 1 = 3 hours ahead, so add 3 hours to a London time.

Going west (a smaller offset)

Local time is behind. New York at UTC−4 is 2 − (−4) = 6 hours behind Cairo at UTC+2, so subtract 6 hours from a Cairo time.

Flight duration The duration of a journey is a real length of time, so it never depends on the time zones. Convert the arrival time back into the departure city's clock, then subtract.

More worked examples

Example 4A train travels 153 km in 1 hour 48 minutes. Find its average speed in km/h.
1
The speed is wanted in km/h, so the time must be in hours. 48 ÷ 60 = 0.8  →  1 h 48 min = 1.8 h
2
Substitute into speed = distance ÷ time. speed = 153 ÷ 1.8 = 85
3
Check by reversing: 85 × 1.8 = 153 ✓
average speed = 85 km/h
Exam trap Using 1.48 instead of 1.8 gives 103.4 km/h — a whole question thrown away for one careless conversion.
Example 5Use the bus timetable above. (a) How long does Bus C take from the Harbour to the Airport? (b) Ravi must be at the Airport by 10:00. He catches a bus from the Market. Which bus must he take, and how long does he then wait at the Airport?
1
(a) Read down the Bus C column: Harbour 12:15, Airport 13:27. 12:15 → 13:00 is 45 min
13:00 → 13:27 is 27 min
45 + 27 = 72 min = 1 h 12 min
2
(b) Bus B reaches the Airport at 10:02, which is after 10:00, so it is too late. Bus A reaches the Airport at 08:47, in time. Ravi takes Bus A from the Market at 07:52
3
Waiting time is from arrival until 10:00. 08:47 → 09:00 is 13 min
09:00 → 10:00 is 1 h
total 1 h 13 min
(a) 1 hour 12 minutes    (b) Bus A (07:52 from the Market); he waits 1 h 13 min
Exam tip “Must arrive by 10:00” excludes 10:02. Two minutes late is late.
Example 6A plane leaves Dubai (UTC+4) at 09:35 local time. The flight takes 7 hours 40 minutes. London is UTC+1. At what local time does the plane land in London?
1
Work out the arrival time on the Dubai clock first — the flight length is the same everywhere. 09:35 + 7 h = 16:35
16:35 + 40 min = 17:15 Dubai time
2
Find the time difference between the zones. 4 − 1 = 3 hours; London is 3 hours behind Dubai
3
Subtract 3 hours to change to London local time. 17:15 − 3 h = 14:15
The plane lands at 14:15 local time (2:15 pm)
Example 7A flight leaves Cairo (UTC+2) at 23:40 and lands in New York (UTC−4) at 05:25 the next day, local time. Find the length of the flight.
1
Put both times on the same clock. New York is 2 − (−4) = 6 hours behind Cairo, so change the Cairo departure into New York time. 23:40 − 6 h = 17:40 New York time (same day)
2
The landing is at 05:25 the next morning, so count up to midnight. 17:40 → 24:00 is 6 h 20 min
3
Then count on from midnight to the landing time and add. 00:00 → 05:25 is 5 h 25 min
6 h 20 min + 5 h 25 min = 11 h 45 min
The flight lasts 11 hours 45 minutes
Why it works Once both times are measured on one clock, a duration is an ordinary subtraction. Converting the departure rather than the arrival is usually neater when the flight crosses midnight.

Your turn

Practice set

  1. Write in 24-hour clock time: (a) 6:20 pm  (b) 12:05 am. Write in 12-hour clock time: (c) 14:50  (d) 00:45.
  2. A lesson starts at 11:35 and lasts 50 minutes. When does it end?
  3. A ferry leaves at 22:20 and arrives at 04:05 the next day. How long is the crossing?
  4. Write (a) 4 h 12 min in decimal hours and (b) 3.25 h in hours and minutes.
  5. A cyclist rides 63 km in 2 hours 6 minutes. Find her average speed in km/h.
  6. A coach leaves Delhi at 08:45 and travels 294 km at an average speed of 56 km/h. It stops for 25 minutes on the way. At what time does it arrive?

  1. (a) 6 + 12 = 18 → 18:20  (b) just after midnight → 00:05  (c) 14 − 12 = 2 → 2:50 pm  (d) 12:45 am
  2. 11:35 → 12:00 is 25 min, 25 min left → 12:25
  3. 22:20 → 00:00 is 1 h 40 min; plus 4 h 5 min → 5 h 45 min
  4. (a) 12 ÷ 60 = 0.2 → 4.2 h  (b) 0.25 × 60 = 15 → 3 h 15 min
  5. 6 ÷ 60 = 0.1, so time = 2.1 h; 63 ÷ 2.1 = 30 km/h
  6. 294 ÷ 56 = 5.25 h = 5 h 15 min; 08:45 + 5 h 15 min = 14:00; plus the 25 min stop → 14:25

Key points to remember

  • 24-hour times always have four digits: 08:05, 00:30, 23:59. No am or pm is used.
  • pm → 24-hour: add 12 to the hours (but 12:xx pm stays as 12:xx). 12:xx am becomes 00:xx.
  • Add or subtract times in two pieces, splitting at the whole hour; carry or borrow 60, never 100.
  • If the finish time is earlier than the start time, the clock has passed midnight — split the count at 00:00.
  • Minutes → decimal hours: divide by 60. Decimal hours → minutes: multiply by 60. 2 h 45 min = 2.75 h, never 2.45 h.
  • For speed = distance ÷ time in km/h, the time must be in hours; convert the minutes first.
  • In a timetable, read down a column for one journey and find the difference between the two times.
  • A journey length does not change with time zones — put both clock times in the same zone, then subtract.
