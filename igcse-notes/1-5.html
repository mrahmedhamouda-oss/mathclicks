1.5  Powers, Roots and Laws of Indices

Squares, cubes, higher powers and their roots, plus the index laws that let you simplify and evaluate powers without a calculator.

Powers and roots

An index (plural: indices) is the small raised number that tells you how many times to multiply a number by itself. In 54 the base is 5 and the index is 4, so 54 = 5 × 5 × 5 × 5 = 625.

Definition an means a multiplied by itself n times. Taking the nth root undoes this: if bn = a then b is an nth root of a.

Squares and square roots

1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144, 169, 196, 225

144 = 12 because 12² = 144.

Learn the squares up to 15² = 225 by heart — and 20² = 400, 25² = 625.

Cubes and cube roots

1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, 343, 512, 729, 1000

364 = 4 because 4³ = 64.

Cube roots of negatives do exist: 3−27 = −3.

Watch out Every positive number has two square roots. x² = 49 gives x = ±7, but the symbol 49 means only the positive one, 7. So when you solve an equation write ±; when you evaluate a root sign, do not.

The laws of indices

All the laws come from counting factors. For example a³ × a² = (a·a·a)(a·a) = a5 — you simply add the number of factors. Every rule below can be checked the same way.

Watch out The multiplying and dividing laws only work when the bases are the same. 2³ × 3² does not simplify to a single power — it is just 8 × 9 = 72. Also, am + an has no index law: you cannot add indices when the terms are added.

Where a0 = 1 comes from

Divide a power by itself in two different ways:

a³a³ = a3−3 = a0   and   a³a³ = 1 so a0 must equal 1 for every non-zero a

The same trick explains negative indices: a² ÷ a5 = a−3, but writing out the factors gives a·aa·a·a·a·a = 1a³. So a−3 = 1a³.

Negative and fractional indices

A negative index means reciprocal, not “negative answer”. A fractional index means root. Put them together and you can evaluate things like 16−3/4 in your head.

a−n = 1an     (ab)−n = (ba)n     am/n = (na)m Flip for a negative index; the denominator is the root, the numerator is the power

Reading a fractional index

Exam tip Always take the root before the power. 272/3 done as (327)² = 3² = 9 is easy; done as 3729 it is not. Both give 9, but only one is doable without a calculator.

Key points to remember

  • Same base only: am × an = am+n, am ÷ an = am−n, (am)n = amn.
  • An index outside a bracket applies to every factor inside: (3x²)³ = 27x6.
  • a0 = 1 for any non-zero a, however complicated the expression.
  • A negative index means reciprocal: a−n = 1 ÷ an. It never makes the answer negative.
  • In am/n the denominator is the root and the numerator is the power — take the root first to keep the numbers small.
  • For a fraction base, a negative index just turns the fraction upside down.
  • If the unknown is in the index, rewrite both sides with the same base and then equate the indices.
  • Learn squares to 15², cubes to 10³ and the powers of 2 and 3 — they turn hard-looking questions into one-liners.
