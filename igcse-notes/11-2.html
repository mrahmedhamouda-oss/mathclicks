11.2  Understanding Similar Triangles

Spotting similar triangles, naming them in the right order, and using the scale factor to calculate missing sides.

What similar means Naming Spotting them Examples Your turn Key points

What “similar” means

Two shapes are similar if one is an enlargement of the other: same shape, possibly different size. In mathematics that has a precise meaning.

Definition Two figures are similar if (i) their corresponding angles are equal, and (ii) their corresponding sides are in the same ratio. That common ratio is the scale factor, usually written k.

The same shape at two sizes

9.6 ÷ 6 = 7.2 ÷ 4.5 = 12 ÷ 7.5 = 1.6 every pair of corresponding sides gives the same scale factor

How much do you need to check?

For triangles you do not need both conditions — either one forces the other:

  • If the three angles of one triangle equal the three angles of another, the triangles are similar. This is the condition you will use almost every time, and it is usually called AAA (or just “equiangular”).
  • Because the angle sum is 180°, showing two pairs of equal angles is enough — the third pair follows automatically.
  • If all three pairs of sides are in the same ratio (SSS similarity), the triangles are similar.
  • If two pairs of sides are in the same ratio and the angles between them are equal (SAS similarity), the triangles are similar.
Exam tip To prove similarity in 0580 you almost always list two pairs of equal angles with a reason for each. That is a complete proof.
Similar is not congruent Congruent triangles are identical in shape and size — that is the special case k = 1. Similar triangles need only the same shape. See lesson 11.4 for congruence.

Naming triangles in the correct order

This is where most marks are lost. When you write triangle ABC is similar to triangle PQR, you are stating that A corresponds to P, B to Q and C to R. Write the letters in the wrong order and every ratio you build afterwards will be wrong.

How to get the order right

  1. Match the equal angles first, not the sides.
  2. Write the two triangles one above the other so the matching vertices line up.
  3. Then read the sides straight off, pair by pair.
PQAB = QRBC = PRAC = k corresponding sides, in matching order
Note A side is described by the two vertices at its ends. So the side between the vertices matching A and B is the side between P and Q. Line up the letters and the sides pair themselves.
Example 1In triangles ABC and DEF, angle A = angle D = 55° and angle B = angle E = 68°. AB = 8 cm, DE = 12 cm and BC = 10 cm. Find EF.
1
Two pairs of angles are equal, so the third pair must be equal too and the triangles are similar in the order given. triangle ABC is similar to triangle DEF
2
Find the scale factor from the pair of sides you know completely. AB corresponds to DE. k = 12 ÷ 8 = 1.5
3
BC corresponds to EF, so multiply by the scale factor. EF = 10 × 1.5
EF = 15 cm
Exam tip Before you calculate, decide whether the answer should be bigger or smaller. Here DEF is the larger triangle, so EF must exceed 10 cm.

Where similar triangles hide

Exam diagrams reuse the same three arrangements over and over. Learn to see them.

1. Nested triangles — a line parallel to one side

Because DE is parallel to BC:

  • angle ADE = angle ABC  (corresponding angles)
  • angle AED = angle ACB  (corresponding angles)
  • angle A is shared
triangle ADE is similar to triangle ABC equiangular — two pairs of corresponding angles plus the common angle
Watch out The ratio uses whole sides from the apex: AD / AB, not AD / DB. If the question gives you DB, add it to AD first to get AB.

2. The bow tie — two lines crossing between parallel lines

With AB parallel to DC, the alternate angles at A and C are equal, the alternate angles at B and D are equal, and the angles at X are vertically opposite. So triangle ABX is similar to triangle CDX.

3. Two triangles sharing an angle

If two triangles share an angle and one more pair of angles is equal — often because of a right angle, or a cyclic quadrilateral, or parallel lines — they are similar. This is the arrangement behind “the perpendicular from the right angle to the hypotenuse” problems, where the two smaller triangles are each similar to the original.

Worked examples

Example 2In triangle ABC, D lies on AB and E lies on AC with DE parallel to BC. AD = 6 cm, DB = 4 cm and DE = 7.5 cm. Find BC.
1
DE is parallel to BC, so triangle ADE is similar to triangle ABC (corresponding angles, common angle A).
2
Work out the whole side AB, not just AD. AB = AD + DB = 6 + 4 = 10 cm
3
Scale factor from small to large. k = AB / AD = 10 / 6 = 5/3
4
DE corresponds to BC. BC = 7.5 × 5/3 = 12.5 cm
BC = 12.5 cm
Watch out Using 6/4 as the scale factor is the single most common error here. That ratio compares a part with a part, not a triangle with a triangle.
Example 3AB is parallel to DC. The diagonals of trapezium ABCD meet at X. AB = 5 cm, DC = 12 cm and AX = 3.5 cm. Find XC, correct to 3 significant figures.
1
This is the bow tie. Alternate angles give angle BAX = angle DCX and angle ABX = angle CDX, and the angles at X are vertically opposite. triangle ABX is similar to triangle CDX
2
AB corresponds to CD, so the scale factor is k = 12 / 5 = 2.4
3
AX corresponds to CX. XC = 3.5 × 2.4 = 8.4 cm
XC = 8.40 cm (3 s.f.)
Example 4Two triangles have sides 4 cm, 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm, 15 cm, 20 cm. Show that they are similar.
1
Put the sides of each in order and pair the smallest with the smallest. 4 → 10,   6 → 15,   8 → 20
2
Work out each ratio. 10/4 = 2.5,   15/6 = 2.5,   20/8 = 2.5
All three ratios equal 2.5, so the triangles are similar (SSS), with k = 2.5
Note If even one ratio differed, they would not be similar — you must check all three.
Example 5In triangle PQR, angle Q = 90°. The perpendicular from Q meets PR at S. PS = 4 cm and SR = 9 cm. Find QS.
1
Angle PSQ = angle QSR = 90°. In triangle PSQ and triangle QSR, angle SPQ = angle SQR because each is 90° minus angle PQS. triangle PSQ is similar to triangle QSR
2
Match the sides: PS corresponds to QS, and QS corresponds to RS. QS / PS = RS / QS
3
Cross-multiply. QS² = PS × RS = 4 × 9 = 36
QS = 6 cm
Exam tip This result — the perpendicular height is the square root of the product of the two parts of the hypotenuse — is worth recognising, but always show the similar triangles that produce it.
Example 6A vertical stick 1.5 m tall casts a shadow 2.4 m long. At the same moment a tower casts a shadow 36 m long. How tall is the tower?
1
The sun's rays make the same angle with the ground in both cases, and both objects are vertical, so the two right-angled triangles are equiangular and therefore similar.
2
Scale factor from the shadows. k = 36 ÷ 2.4 = 15
3
Apply it to the heights. height = 1.5 × 15
The tower is 22.5 m tall
Example 7Exam style. In triangle ABC, X lies on AB and Y lies on AC. AX = 5 cm, XB = 7 cm, AY = 6 cm and YC = 8.4 cm. (a) Show that XY is parallel to BC. (b) Given XY = 4 cm, find BC.
1
(a) Compare the whole sides from A. AB = 5 + 7 = 12,   AC = 6 + 8.4 = 14.4
2
Work out both ratios. AB / AX = 12 / 5 = 2.4
AC / AY = 14.4 / 6 = 2.4
3
The ratios are equal and angle A is common, so triangle AXY is similar to triangle ABC (SAS similarity). Equal corresponding angles AXY and ABC then make XY parallel to BC.
4
(b) XY corresponds to BC with k = 2.4. BC = 4 × 2.4
(a) proved    (b) BC = 9.6 cm

Your turn

Practice set

  1. Triangle ABC is similar to triangle PQR with AB = 5 cm, PQ = 15 cm and BC = 7 cm. Find QR.
  2. In triangle ABC, DE is parallel to BC with D on AB and E on AC. AD = 8 cm, AB = 20 cm and BC = 15 cm. Find DE.
  3. Two triangles have sides 3, 5, 7 and 9, 15, 20. Are they similar? Justify your answer.
  4. AB is parallel to DC and the diagonals of trapezium ABCD meet at X. AB = 6 cm, DC = 9 cm and DX = 7.2 cm. Find BX.
  5. A photograph 15 cm wide and 10 cm high is enlarged so that its width becomes 24 cm. Find the new height.
  6. In triangle LMN, angle M = 90° and the perpendicular from M meets LN at K. LK = 3 cm and MK = 6 cm. Find KN.

  1. k = 15 / 5 = 3, so QR = 7 × 3 = 21 cm
  2. k = 8 / 20 = 0.4 (large to small), so DE = 15 × 0.4 = 6 cm
  3. 9/3 = 3, 15/5 = 3, but 20/7 = 2.857… — not similar, because the third ratio differs.
  4. Bow tie: triangle ABX is similar to triangle CDX, so BX / DX = AB / DC = 6/9, giving BX = 7.2 × 6/9 = 4.8 cm
  5. k = 24 / 15 = 1.6, so 10 × 1.6 = 16 cm
  6. MK² = LK × KN, so 36 = 3 × KN and KN = 12 cm

Key points to remember

  • Similar means equal angles and sides in the same ratio. For triangles, equal angles alone is enough.
  • Showing two pairs of equal angles proves two triangles are similar — the third pair follows from the 180° angle sum.
  • Write the vertices in corresponding order; every ratio you build depends on it.
  • The scale factor k = a length on the second shape divided by the matching length on the first. Multiply by k to enlarge, divide to reduce.
  • A line parallel to one side of a triangle creates a similar triangle — use whole sides from the apex, never part-to-part.
  • Look for the bow tie whenever two lines cross between a pair of parallel lines.
  • Check your answer is sensibly bigger or smaller before you write it down.
  • Similar with k = 1 means congruent (lesson 11.4); area and volume scale factors are k² and k³ (lesson 11.3).
