19.4  Angle Relationships in Circles

The circle theorems — each one with the exact wording the examiner expects you to quote as your reason.

The theorems Using them Examples Your turn Key points

The circle theorems

Circle questions in Paper 4 almost always ask you to find an angle and give a reason. The angle is usually the easy part; the marks are in the wording. Learn each theorem with the phrase printed underneath its diagram — that phrase is the reason.

Before you start An angle is at the centre if its vertex is at O. It is at the circumference if its vertex is a point on the circle. A chord joins two points on the circle; a chord splits the circle into a major and a minor segment.

1. The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference

angle AOB = 2 × angle APB Reason: the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference
Watch out Both angles must stand on the same arc AB. Look for the “arrowhead” shape pointing at O and the same arrowhead pointing at P.

2. The angle in a semicircle is 90°

AB is a diameter  ⇒  angle ACB = 90° Reason: the angle in a semicircle is 90°

This is really theorem 1 with the centre angle equal to 180°. The moment you see a line through the centre, look for a right angle at the circumference.

3. Angles in the same segment are equal

angle APB = angle AQB Reason: angles in the same segment are equal
Exam tip The shape to look for is a bow tie (or hourglass): two triangles sharing the same chord, with their apexes on the same side of it.

4. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°

angle A + angle C = 180°   and   angle B + angle D = 180° Reason: opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary

It follows that the exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral equals the interior opposite angle — useful, and worth quoting by name.

Watch out All four vertices must lie on the circle. A quadrilateral with one vertex inside or outside is not cyclic and the theorem does not apply.

5. The alternate segment theorem

angle between tangent and chord = angle in the alternate segment Reason: alternate segment theorem

The angle between the tangent T and the chord TA equals the angle TBA in the segment on the other side of that chord.

6. The tangent facts you also need

  • A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact — so a right angle appears whenever a radius meets a tangent.
  • The two tangents from an external point are equal in length, so the triangle they form with the chord of contact is isosceles.
Note These are the symmetry properties from lesson 19.3. In angle questions they usually supply the right angle or the pair of equal base angles that unlocks everything else.

Using the theorems in a problem

A method that always works

  1. Mark what you are given on the diagram — the centre, any diameter, any tangent, equal radii.
  2. Hunt for the shapes: arrowhead (centre theorem), bow tie (same segment), semicircle (right angle), four points on the circle (cyclic quadrilateral), tangent touching (alternate segment or perpendicular radius).
  3. Fill in every angle you can, one at a time, writing the reason next to each.
  4. Remember the ordinary facts too: angles in a triangle add to 180°, angles on a straight line add to 180°, and any triangle with two radii as sides is isosceles.
Exam tip One mark is for the angle, one is for the reason. Write the reason in full — “angles in the same segment” scores; “circle theorem” does not.
Example 1A, B and P lie on a circle centre O. Angle APB = 34°. Find angle AOB.
1
Both angles stand on the same arc AB, with one vertex at the centre and one on the circumference. angle AOB = 2 × angle APB
2
Substitute. angle AOB = 2 × 34° = 68°
angle AOB = 68° (angle at centre is twice the angle at the circumference)
Example 2PQ is a diameter of a circle and R lies on the circle. Angle RPQ = 27°. Find angle PQR.
1
PQ is a diameter, so the angle at R is a right angle. angle PRQ = 90°   (angle in a semicircle)
2
Use the angle sum of triangle PQR. angle PQR = 180° − 90° − 27°
angle PQR = 63°
Exam tip Whenever a question mentions a diameter, write “90° — angle in a semicircle” on the diagram before you do anything else.

More worked examples

Example 3ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral. Angle ABC = 96° and angle BCD = 71°. Find angle ADC and angle DAB, and the exterior angle of the quadrilateral at B.
1
ABC and ADC are opposite angles. angle ADC = 180° − 96° = 84°
2
BCD and DAB are opposite angles. angle DAB = 180° − 71° = 109°
3
Check: the four angles of any quadrilateral add to 360°. 96 + 71 + 84 + 109 = 360  ✓
4
The exterior angle at B is on a straight line with the interior angle ABC — and it equals the interior opposite angle ADC, as expected. 180° − 96° = 84° = angle ADC  ✓
angle ADC = 84°, angle DAB = 109°, exterior angle at B = 84°
Example 4O is the centre of a circle. TA is a tangent touching the circle at A, and OT = 25 cm with radius OA = 7 cm. Find angle OTA, correct to 1 decimal place.
1
The tangent meets the radius at A. angle OAT = 90°   (tangent ⊥ radius)
2
Triangle OAT is right-angled at A, with OT as the hypotenuse. Relative to angle T, OA is the opposite side. sin T = opp / hyp = 7 / 25 = 0.28
3
Use the inverse sine. T = sin⁻¹(0.28) = 16.2602…°
angle OTA = 16.3° (1 d.p.)
Note The tangent length itself is 25² − 7² = 576 = 24 cm, by Pythagoras.
Example 5A, B and C lie on a circle centre O. Angle OBC = 28°. Find angle BAC.
1
OB and OC are both radii, so triangle OBC is isosceles. angle OCB = angle OBC = 28°
2
Angle sum of triangle OBC. angle BOC = 180° − 28° − 28° = 124°
3
Angle BOC is at the centre and angle BAC is at the circumference, both on arc BC. angle BAC = 124° ÷ 2
angle BAC = 62°
Exam tip Two radii always make an isosceles triangle. That single observation unlocks a large fraction of circle questions.
Example 6TA is a tangent to a circle at A. B and C are points on the circle, and the angle between the tangent TA and the chord AB is 63°. Angle BAC = 44°. Find angle ACB and angle ABC.
1
The angle between the tangent and chord AB equals the angle in the alternate segment, which is the angle subtended by AB at C. angle ACB = 63°   (alternate segment theorem)
2
Now use the angle sum of triangle ABC. angle ABC = 180° − 63° − 44°
angle ACB = 63°, angle ABC = 73°
Watch out The alternate segment is the one on the other side of the chord from the angle you are given. Shade it lightly on the diagram if you are unsure.
Example 7Exam style. A, B, C and D lie on a circle centre O. BD is a diameter. Angle ADB = 32° and angle DBC = 47°. Find angle ABD, angle BCD and angle BAD, giving a reason for each.
1
BD is a diameter, so the angle at A in triangle ABD is a right angle. angle BAD = 90°   (angle in a semicircle)
2
Angle sum of triangle ABD. angle ABD = 180° − 90° − 32° = 58°
3
BD is also a diameter for triangle BCD, so the angle at C is a right angle. angle BCD = 90°   (angle in a semicircle)
4
Check with the cyclic quadrilateral ABCD: angle BAD and angle BCD are opposite. 90° + 90° = 180°  ✓
angle BAD = 90°, angle ABD = 58°, angle BCD = 90°
Note The 47° is not needed for these three parts — it would be used to find angle BDC = 180° − 90° − 47° = 43°. Exam diagrams often carry extra information; take only what you need.

Your turn

Practice set

  1. P, Q and R are on a circle centre O. Angle POQ = 146°. Find angle PRQ.
  2. AB is a diameter and C is on the circle. Angle ABC = 41°. Find angle BAC.
  3. WXYZ is a cyclic quadrilateral with angle WXY = 103°. Find angle WZY.
  4. A, B, C and D lie on a circle. Angle ACB = 25°. Write down angle ADB and give a reason.
  5. A tangent touches a circle centre O at T. The point S is outside the circle with OS = 17 cm and radius 8 cm. Find the length ST.
  6. The exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral PQRS at vertex R is 78°. Find the interior angle at P, and explain why.

  1. Angle at the circumference is half the angle at the centre: 146 ÷ 2 = 73°
  2. Angle ACB = 90° (angle in a semicircle), so 180 − 90 − 41 = 49°
  3. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral: 180 − 103 = 77°
  4. 25° — angles in the same segment are equal (both stand on chord AB).
  5. Angle OTS = 90° (tangent ⊥ radius), so ST = 17² − 8² = 225 = 15 cm
  6. The exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral equals the interior opposite angle, and P is opposite R, so angle P = 78°. (Check: the interior angle at R is 180 − 78 = 102°, and 78 + 102 = 180.)

Key points to remember

  • Angle at the centre = 2 × angle at the circumference, when both stand on the same arc.
  • The angle in a semicircle is 90° — look for it every time a diameter appears.
  • Angles in the same segment are equal — the bow-tie shape.
  • Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°, and the exterior angle equals the interior opposite angle.
  • Alternate segment theorem: the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.
  • A tangent is perpendicular to the radius, and the two tangents from a point are equal, giving an isosceles triangle.
  • Any triangle made from two radii is isosceles — often the missing step.
  • Always write the reason. Half the marks in a circle question are for the wording.
