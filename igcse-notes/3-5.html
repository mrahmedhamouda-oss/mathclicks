3.5  Circles

The vocabulary of the circle, circumference and area, working backwards to a radius, and arcs, sectors and composite shapes.

Vocabulary C and A Arcs and sectors Composite Examples Your turn Key points

The language of the circle

Every circle question begins with a word: chord, sector, tangent, segment. If you misread one of them you solve the wrong problem, so learn this diagram until you can redraw it from memory.

Lines in and around a circle

Arcs and regions

Definition An arc is part of the circumference. A sector is the pizza-slice region between two radii and an arc. A segment is the region between a chord and an arc. A semicircle is half a circle (180°) and a quadrant is a quarter (90°).
Watch out Sector and segment sound alike but are cut differently: a sector is cut by two radii, a segment by a chord. Remember “seCtor has a Centre in it”.

Circumference and area

Two formulas do most of the work. They are on the formula sheet, but you should know them cold — and know which one is a length (cm) and which is an area (cm²).

Circumference — a length

C = πd = 2πr

Use πd when the question gives you the diameter, and 2πr when it gives you the radius. Answer in cm, m, km.

Area — a region

A = πr²

Always the radius, and always squared before multiplying by π. Answer in cm², m².

Watch out The single most expensive circle error is putting the diameter into A = πr². If a question says “a circle of diameter 10 cm”, write r = 5 on the page before you touch the calculator.
Exam tip Use the π key, keep the full value in your calculator, and round only at the very end — to 3 significant figures unless the question says otherwise. If a question asks for an answer “in terms of π”, do not press the π key at all: write 36π.
Example 1A circle has radius 7.5 cm. Find its circumference and its area.
1
Circumference uses 2πr. C = 2 × π × 7.5 = 47.1238…
2
Round to 3 significant figures and give the unit. C = 47.1 cm
3
Area uses πr² — square the radius first. A = π × 7.5² = π × 56.25 = 176.714…
C = 47.1 cm  (3 s.f.)    A = 177 cm²  (3 s.f.)
Note In terms of π these are 15π cm and 56.25π cm².
Example 2(a) A circle has circumference 44 cm. Find its radius and its area. (b) A different circle has area 200 cm². Find its radius.
1
(a) Start from the formula and substitute what you know. 2πr = 44
2
Divide by 2π. Keep the exact value in the calculator. r = 44 ÷ (2π) = 7.00281… = 7.00 cm
3
Now the area, using the unrounded radius. A = π × 7.00281…² = 154.056… = 154 cm²
4
(b) Reverse A = πr²: divide by π, then square root. r² = 200 ÷ π = 63.6619…
r = √63.6619… = 7.97884…
(a) r = 7.00 cm, A = 154 cm²     (b) r = 7.98 cm
Exam tip Working backwards is just “undo in reverse order”: for area, divide by π then square root — never square root first.

Arcs and sectors: fractions of a circle

A sector of angle θ is simply the fraction θ360 of the whole circle. Work out the whole circle first, then take that fraction — every sector question in 0580 is that one idea.

arc length = θ360 × 2πr      sector area = θ360 × πr² θ is the angle at the centre, in degrees

Perimeter of a sector

The boundary is the arc plus the two straight radii:

P = arc + 2r

Forgetting the + 2r is the classic dropped mark.

Semicircle and quadrant

Semicircle (θ = 180°): area ½πr², perimeter πr + 2r (the curve plus the diameter).

Quadrant (θ = 90°): area ¼πr², perimeter πr2 + 2r.

Example 3A sector has radius 9 cm and angle 140°. Find its arc length, its area and its perimeter.
1
Write down the fraction of the circle. 140360 = 718
2
Arc length is that fraction of the whole circumference. arc = 140360 × 2 × π × 9 = 21.9911… = 22.0 cm
3
Sector area is the same fraction of the whole area. A = 140360 × π × 9² = 98.9601… = 99.0 cm²
4
Perimeter = arc + the two radii. Use the unrounded arc. P = 21.9911… + 9 + 9 = 39.9911… = 40.0 cm
arc = 22.0 cm   area = 99.0 cm²   perimeter = 40.0 cm
Watch out 22.0 and 40.0 keep the trailing zero — that is what 3 significant figures looks like here. Do not write 22 when the question demands 3 s.f.

Composite shapes

Paper 4 rarely gives you a plain circle. It gives you a rectangle with a semicircular end, or a square with a circle cut out. The method never changes:

The three-step method

  1. Split the shape into a rectangle, triangle, semicircle, quadrant … and mark the radius of each curved piece on the diagram.
  2. Add or subtract the areas. A hole is a subtraction.
  3. For a perimeter, walk round the outside and add only the edges you actually travel along. Cut edges that are now inside the shape do not count.
Example 4A shape is a 20 cm by 12 cm rectangle with a semicircle joined to one short end. Find its area and its perimeter.
1
The semicircle sits on the 12 cm end, so its diameter is 12 and its radius is 6. r = 12 ÷ 2 = 6 cm
2
Area of the rectangle. 20 × 12 = 240 cm²
3
Area of the semicircle — half of πr². ½ × π × 6² = 56.5486… cm²
4
Add. A = 240 + 56.5486… = 296.548… = 297 cm²
5
Perimeter: walk round. You travel the left end (12), two long sides (20 each) and the curve — but not the dashed join, because it is inside the shape. curve = ½ × 2π × 6 = π × 6 = 18.8495…
P = 12 + 20 + 20 + 18.8495… = 70.8495…
Area = 297 cm²    Perimeter = 70.8 cm
Watch out Adding the dashed 12 cm join into the perimeter is the standard trap. Trace the outline with your pencil before you add anything up.

More worked examples

Example 5Find the perimeter and area of (a) a semicircle of radius 6 cm (b) a quadrant of radius 8 cm.
1
(a) Semicircle: half the area of the full circle. A = ½ × π × 6² = 56.5486… = 56.5 cm²
2
Its perimeter is the curved half plus the diameter. P = ½ × 2π × 6 + 12 = 18.8495… + 12 = 30.8 cm
3
(b) Quadrant: a quarter of the circle. A = ¼ × π × 8² = 50.2654… = 50.3 cm²
4
Its perimeter is the quarter-arc plus two radii. P = ¼ × 2π × 8 + 8 + 8 = 12.5663… + 16 = 28.6 cm
(a) A = 56.5 cm², P = 30.8 cm    (b) A = 50.3 cm², P = 28.6 cm
Example 6An arc of length 12 cm lies on a circle of radius 10 cm. Find the angle at the centre, and the area of the sector.
1
Put what you know into the arc formula and leave θ as the unknown. θ360 × 2 × π × 10 = 12
2
The whole circumference is 2π × 10 = 62.8318…, so the arc is that fraction of it. θ360 = 1262.8318… = 0.190985…
3
Multiply by 360. θ = 0.190985… × 360 = 68.7549… = 68.8°
4
Sector area uses the same fraction — keep it unrounded. A = 0.190985… × π × 10² = 60 cm²
θ = 68.8° (3 s.f.)    sector area = 60 cm²
Note The area came out exactly 60 because A = ½ × r × arc = ½ × 10 × 12. That shortcut is a quick check whenever you already know the arc.
Example 7A circle of the largest possible size is cut from a square of side 14 cm. Find the area of the shaded card that is left, and the total perimeter of that shaded piece.
1
The largest circle touches all four sides, so its diameter equals the side of the square. d = 14 cm  →  r = 7 cm
2
Area of the square. 14 × 14 = 196 cm²
3
Area of the circle. π × 7² = 153.938… cm²
4
Subtract — write the subtraction line out, it carries the method mark. A = 196 − 153.938… = 42.0619… = 42.1 cm²
5
The shaded piece has two boundaries: the outside of the square and the circular hole. square: 4 × 14 = 56 cm
circle: 2 × π × 7 = 43.9822… cm
P = 56 + 43.9822… = 99.98… = 100 cm
Area = 42.1 cm²    Perimeter = 100 cm (3 s.f.)
Exam tip “Largest possible circle in a square” always means d = side length. Write r = 7 on the diagram straight away and the rest is routine.

Your turn

Practice set

  1. A circle has diameter 18 cm. Find its circumference and its area, correct to 3 significant figures.
  2. A circle has circumference 62 cm. Find its radius.
  3. A circle has area 95 cm². Find its diameter.
  4. A sector has radius 12 cm and angle 45°. Find its arc length, its area and its perimeter.
  5. Find the perimeter of a semicircle of diameter 20 cm.
  6. A running lane is made from a 40 m by 25 m rectangle with a semicircle of diameter 25 m at each end. Find the total area and the total perimeter of the shape.

  1. C = π × 18 = 56.548… → 56.5 cm; r = 9, A = π × 81 = 254.469… → 254 cm²
  2. 2πr = 62 → r = 62 ÷ (2π) = 9.8676… → 9.87 cm
  3. r² = 95 ÷ π = 30.2394…, r = 5.4990…, d = 2r → 11.0 cm
  4. fraction = 45/360 = 1/8; arc = 1/8 × 2π × 12 = 9.4247… → 9.42 cm; area = 1/8 × π × 144 = 56.548… → 56.5 cm²; perimeter = 9.4247… + 24 → 33.4 cm
  5. r = 10; P = π × 10 + 20 = 31.4159… + 20 → 51.4 cm
  6. The two semicircles make one full circle of radius 12.5 m. Area = 40 × 25 + π × 12.5² = 1000 + 490.87… → 1490 m² (3 s.f.); perimeter = 40 + 40 + 2π × 12.5 = 80 + 78.539… → 159 m (the 25 m ends are inside the shape)

Key points to remember

  • C = πd = 2πr is a length; A = πr² is an area. Halve a diameter before using the area formula.
  • To go backwards from C, divide by 2π. From A, divide by π then take the square root.
  • Arc length = θ/360 × 2πr and sector area = θ/360 × πr² — always write the fraction down first.
  • Perimeter of a sector = arc + 2r; perimeter of a semicircle = πr + 2r. The straight edges always count.
  • For composite shapes, split into standard pieces, add or subtract the areas, and for the perimeter add only the edges on the outside.
  • Keep full calculator accuracy throughout and round once, at the end, to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.
  • Know the vocabulary: a sector is cut by two radii, a segment by a chord, and a tangent meets the radius at 90°.
