14.6  Factorising Quadratics

Factorising ax² + bx + c when a is not 1, using the ac method, the difference of two squares, and factorisation to solve quadratic equations.

The ac method Signs & factors Two squares Solving Examples Your turn Key points

Factorising when a is not 1

You already know how to factorise x² + bx + c: find two numbers with product c and sum b. When the coefficient of x² is not 1 that shortcut fails, because the two brackets now also have to multiply to give a. The reliable method — the one that never leaves you guessing — is to split the middle term.

The ac method To factorise ax² + bx + c:
  1. Work out ac (multiply the first and last coefficients).
  2. Find two numbers with product ac and sum b.
  3. Use them to split the bx term into two terms.
  4. Factorise the four terms in two pairs — the bracket that appears twice is a factor.
6x² + 11x + 3  →  ac = 6 × 3 = 18,   b = 11  →  9 × 2 = 18  and  9 + 2 = 11 The two numbers you need are 9 and 2

Factorise 6x² + 11x + 3 by splitting.

ac = 18 and b = 11, so the pair is 9 and 2. Replace 11x by 9x + 2x:

6x² + 9x + 2x + 3

Now factorise in pairs. From 6x² + 9x take out 3x; from 2x + 3 take out 1:

3x(2x + 3) + 1(2x + 3) = (2x + 3)(3x + 1)
Exam tip The identical bracket (2x + 3) is your proof that the split was correct. If the two brackets are not identical, you have taken out the wrong common factor — or written the pair in the wrong order.

Factorise 6x² + 11x + 3 by trying brackets.

The x terms must multiply to 6x² (1 × 6 or 2 × 3) and the numbers must multiply to 3 (1 × 3). All signs are positive.

TrialMiddle term
(x + 1)(6x + 3)3x + 6x = 9x✗
(x + 3)(6x + 1)x + 18x = 19x✗
(2x + 1)(3x + 3)6x + 3x = 9x✗
(2x + 3)(3x + 1)2x + 9x = 11x✓
Speed trick The original expression has no common factor, so no bracket may have one either. That kills (6x + 3) and (3x + 3) instantly and halves the number of trials.

Seeing it as an area

The four terms of the split are the four pieces of a rectangle whose sides are the two brackets.

Reading the sides: (3x + 1)(2x + 3). The two green pieces are the split middle term — that is why 9x + 2x must add to 11x.

Example 1Factorise (a) 3x² − 10x + 8  (b) 5x² + 9x − 2.
1
(a) ac = 3 × 8 = 24 and b = −10. The product is positive and the sum is negative, so both numbers are negative. −6 × −4 = 24  and  −6 + (−4) = −10
2
Split the middle term and factorise in pairs. 3x² − 6x − 4x + 8
= 3x(x − 2) − 4(x − 2)
= (x − 2)(3x − 4)
3
(b) ac = 5 × (−2) = −10 and b = 9. The product is negative, so one number is positive and one is negative. 10 × (−1) = −10  and  10 + (−1) = 9
4
Split and group. Watch the sign when you take out the second factor. 5x² + 10x − x − 2
= 5x(x + 2) − 1(x + 2)
= (x + 2)(5x − 1)
(a) (x − 2)(3x − 4)     (b) (x + 2)(5x − 1)
Watch out In (b) the last pair is −x − 2. You must take out −1, not +1, or the brackets will not match. Whenever the third term is negative, expect a − in front of the second group.

Signs, and always look for a common factor first

Getting the signs right is most of the battle. The signs of b and c tell you the signs of the pair of numbers before you start searching.

Sign of cSign of bThe two numbers are…Example
positivepositiveboth positive2x² + 7x + 3 → 6 and 1
positivenegativeboth negative3x² − 10x + 8 → −6 and −4
negativepositiveone of each; the larger is positive5x² + 9x − 2 → 10 and −1
negativenegativeone of each; the larger is negative6x² − 5x − 6 → −9 and 4
Watch out “Factorise fully” means take out any numerical common factor before you start the ac method. 12x² + 22x + 6 left as (4x + 6)(3x + 1) would lose a mark — the first bracket still has a factor of 2 in it.
Example 2Factorise fully 12x² + 22x + 6.
1
Every coefficient is even, so take out 2 first. The numbers left are much smaller. 12x² + 22x + 6 = 2(6x² + 11x + 3)
2
Factorise the bracket: ac = 18, b = 11, giving 9 and 2. 6x² + 9x + 2x + 3 = 3x(2x + 3) + 1(2x + 3)
3
Write the answer with the common factor still in front. = 2(2x + 3)(3x + 1)
12x² + 22x + 6 = 2(2x + 3)(3x + 1)
Check by expanding (2x + 3)(3x + 1) = 6x² + 2x + 9x + 3 = 6x² + 11x + 3, and doubling gives 12x² + 22x + 6. Ten seconds well spent.
Negative x² term If a is negative, take out −1 as well, then factorise the positive quadratic: −2x² + 7x + 4 = −(2x² − 7x − 4) = −(x − 4)(2x + 1).

The difference of two squares

When the middle term is missing and the two remaining terms are squares with a minus sign between them, you can write the factors down in one line. This is the most heavily tested factorisation in Paper 2.

a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b) The difference of two squares

It works because the middle terms cancel

(3x − 4)(3x + 4) = 9x² + 12x − 12x − 16 = 9x² − 16

So 9x² − 16 = (3x)² − 4² = (3x − 4)(3x + 4).

Two things it is not

  • x² + 16 does not factorise — a sum of two squares never does.
  • 2x² − 50 is not itself a difference of two squares, because 2x² is not a perfect square. Take out the 2 first.
Example 3Factorise fully (a) 9x² − 16  (b) 2x² − 50  (c) 49 − 25y².
1
(a) Write each term as a square. 9x² − 16 = (3x)² − 4² = (3x − 4)(3x + 4)
2
(b) There is a common factor of 2. Take it out, and a difference of two squares appears. 2x² − 50 = 2(x² − 25) = 2(x − 5)(x + 5)
3
(c) The square being subtracted is the second term this time, so the number comes first. 49 − 25y² = 7² − (5y)² = (7 − 5y)(7 + 5y)
(a) (3x − 4)(3x + 4)    (b) 2(x − 5)(x + 5)    (c) (7 − 5y)(7 + 5y)
Exam tip A difference of two squares is just the ac method with b = 0: for (a), ac = −144 and the pair summing to 0 is 12 and −12. If you ever forget the rule, the method still gets you there.

Using the factorisation to solve

Factorising is not the point — solving is. Once the quadratic is written as a product equal to zero, one of the brackets must be zero.

Zero product property If A × B = 0 then A = 0 or B = 0. So set each bracket equal to zero in turn and solve the two small linear equations.
Watch out The right-hand side must be 0 before you factorise. From (x − 2)(3x − 4) = 6 you can say nothing useful — there are endless pairs of numbers multiplying to 6. Rearrange to … = 0 first, every time.
Example 4Solve 6x² − 5x − 6 = 0.
1
ac = 6 × (−6) = −36, b = −5. One number of each sign, the larger negative. −9 × 4 = −36  and  −9 + 4 = −5
2
Split and group. 6x² − 9x + 4x − 6
= 3x(2x − 3) + 2(2x − 3)
= (2x − 3)(3x + 2)
3
Set each bracket to zero. 2x − 3 = 0 → x = 32
3x + 2 = 0 → x = −23
4
Check the first root by substituting. 6(1.5)² − 5(1.5) − 6 = 13.5 − 7.5 − 6 = 0  ✓
x = 1.5  or  x = −23
Exam tip Fractional roots are perfectly normal when a ≠ 1. Leave them as exact fractions unless the question asks for decimals — −2/3 is exact, −0.67 is not.

More worked examples

Example 5Factorise 8x² − 26x + 15.
1
ac = 8 × 15 = 120 and b = −26, so both numbers are negative and multiply to 120. Pairs of 120: 1×120, 2×60, 3×40, 4×30, 5×24, 6×20, 8×15, 10×12
2
The pair adding to 26 is 6 and 20; make both negative. −6 + (−20) = −26  ✓
3
Split and factorise in pairs. 8x² − 6x − 20x + 15
= 2x(4x − 3) − 5(4x − 3)
= (4x − 3)(2x − 5)
4
Expand mentally to check. (4x − 3)(2x − 5) = 8x² − 20x − 6x + 15 = 8x² − 26x + 15  ✓
(4x − 3)(2x − 5)
Being systematic pays Writing out the factor pairs of ac in order guarantees you find the right pair; hunting at random does not.
Example 6Solve 3x² = 10x − 8.
1
Collect everything on one side so the equation equals zero. Keep the x² term positive. 3x² − 10x + 8 = 0
2
ac = 24, b = −10 → −6 and −4. 3x² − 6x − 4x + 8 = 3x(x − 2) − 4(x − 2)
3
Factorise and solve each bracket. (x − 2)(3x − 4) = 0
x = 2  or  3x = 4 → x = 43
4
Check the awkward root in the original equation. 3(43)² = 163  and  10(43) − 8 = 403 − 243 = 163  ✓
x = 2  or  x = 43
Example 7A rectangle has area (10x² + 11x − 6) cm² and width (2x + 3) cm. Find its length, and hence its perimeter, in terms of x.
1
Area = length × width, so factorising the area gives the two sides. ac = 10 × (−6) = −60, b = 11. 15 × (−4) = −60  and  15 + (−4) = 11
2
Split and group. 10x² + 15x − 4x − 6
= 5x(2x + 3) − 2(2x + 3)
= (2x + 3)(5x − 2)
3
The width is (2x + 3), so the other bracket must be the length. length = (5x − 2) cm
4
Perimeter is twice the sum of the sides. Collect like terms inside the bracket first. P = 2[(2x + 3) + (5x − 2)] = 2(7x + 1) = (14x + 2) cm
length = (5x − 2) cm,   perimeter = (14x + 2) cm
Watch out Give the units the question uses: the area was in cm², so lengths are in cm. A length must also be positive, which here means x > 0.4 — worth a comment if the question asks about possible values of x.
When it will not factorise If no integer pair multiplies to ac and adds to b, the quadratic has no integer factorisation. Stop searching and use the formula x = −b ± √(b² − 4ac)2a, giving your answers to 2 decimal places unless told otherwise.

Your turn

Practice set

  1. Factorise 2x² + 11x + 5.
  2. Factorise 3x² − 11x − 4.
  3. Factorise 25x² − 49.
  4. Factorise fully 18x² − 8.
  5. Solve 4x² + 4x − 15 = 0.
  6. The area of a rectangle is (6x² − 5x − 6) cm² and its length is (3x + 2) cm. Find the width, and the value of x when the width is 4 cm.

  1. ac = 10, sum 11 → 10 and 1. 2x² + 10x + x + 5 = 2x(x + 5) + 1(x + 5) → (x + 5)(2x + 1)
  2. ac = −12, sum −11 → −12 and 1. 3x² − 12x + x − 4 = 3x(x − 4) + 1(x − 4) → (x − 4)(3x + 1)
  3. Difference of two squares: (5x)² − 7² → (5x − 7)(5x + 7)
  4. Common factor 2 first: 2(9x² − 4) → 2(3x − 2)(3x + 2)
  5. ac = −60, sum 4 → 10 and −6. 4x² + 10x − 6x − 15 = 2x(2x + 5) − 3(2x + 5), so (2x + 5)(2x − 3) = 0 → x = −2.5 or x = 1.5
  6. 6x² − 5x − 6 = (3x + 2)(2x − 3), so width = (2x − 3) cm. Then 2x − 3 = 4 → 2x = 7 → x = 3.5

Key points to remember

  • Common factor first, always. “Factorise fully” means nothing is left inside a bracket that could still come out.
  • For ax² + bx + c: find two numbers with product ac and sum b, split the middle term, then factorise in pairs.
  • The two brackets you create when grouping must be identical — if they are not, fix the sign you took out, not the pair of numbers.
  • Signs: c > 0 means the two numbers match b's sign; c < 0 means one of each, with the larger taking b's sign.
  • a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b). A sum of two squares never factorises.
  • Always expand to check — it takes seconds and catches almost every slip.
  • To solve, rearrange to … = 0, factorise, then set each bracket to zero. Roots are often fractions when a ≠ 1; leave them exact.
  • No integer pair works? It does not factorise — switch to the quadratic formula.
