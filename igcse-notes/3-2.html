3.2  Triangles

The angle sum, the exterior angle rule and isosceles reasoning — the three tools that unlock almost every geometry question.

Types Angle sum Exterior angle Isosceles Examples Your turn Key points

Types of triangle

A triangle is named twice: once by its sides and once by its angles. A question may say “the triangle is isosceles” and expect you to supply the equal angles yourself, so learn both lists properly.

Equilateral

All three sides equal, all three angles 60°. Rotational symmetry of order 3.

Isosceles

Two sides equal. The base angles (opposite the equal sides) are equal.

Scalene

All three sides different, all three angles different. No lines of symmetry.

Right-angled

One angle is 90°. The longest side, opposite the right angle, is the hypotenuse. It may also be isosceles (90, 45, 45).

By angles as well A triangle with all angles below 90° is acute-angled; one with an angle above 90° is obtuse-angled. A triangle can have at most one angle of 90° or more — two would already use up the whole 180°.

The angle sum is always 180°

a + b + c = 180° The three interior angles of any triangle

Why it is 180°

Draw a line through the top vertex parallel to the base. The two outer angles at the top are alternate angles with the base angles, so the three angles at the top vertex are just a, c and b rearranged — and they lie on a straight line.

That is the whole proof, and it is worth knowing: it shows why the rule needs parallel lines, and it is the same construction you use in the harder chase questions.

Example 1The angles of a triangle are 3x°, 4x° and 5x°. Find the three angles and name the type of triangle.
1
Use the angle sum of a triangle. 3x + 4x + 5x = 180
2
Simplify and solve. 12x = 180  →  x = 15
3
Substitute back into each expression. 3x = 45°,   4x = 60°,   5x = 75°
45 + 60 + 75 = 180 ✓
4
All three angles are different and all are less than 90°. scalene and acute-angled
45°, 60° and 75° — a scalene, acute-angled triangle

The exterior angle rule

Extend one side of a triangle. The angle formed outside is the exterior angle, and it equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles — the two you did not touch.

exterior angle = sum of the two opposite interior angles Reason: exterior angle of a triangle

Why it works

Both routes work. Either add the two blue angles, or take the interior angle at B from 180°. The rule follows because ∠ABC + ∠CBD = 180° (angles on a straight line) and ∠ABC = 180° − 50° − 95° (angle sum of a triangle).

Watch out The exterior angle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles — not all three, and never the neighbouring one. In the diagram above, 145 ≠ 35 + 50. Point at the two angles you are adding before you write anything.
Example 2In triangle ABC, side AB is extended to D. ∠CBD = 128° and ∠BAC = 51°. Find ∠ACB and ∠ABC.
1
The exterior angle at B equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles, which are at A and C. 128 = 51 + y  (exterior angle of a triangle)
2
Solve for y. y = 128 − 51 = 77°
3
z and the exterior angle sit on the straight line ABD. z = 180 − 128 = 52°  (angles on a straight line)
4
Check the interior angles. 51 + 77 + 52 = 180 ✓
∠ACB = 77°  and  ∠ABC = 52°

Isosceles reasoning

Half of all geometry marks in Paper 4 come from spotting an isosceles triangle. Two equal sides force two equal angles, and two equal angles force two equal sides — the rule works both ways.

Base angles of an isosceles triangle In a triangle with two equal sides, the angles opposite those sides are equal. The reason to write is “base angles of an isosceles triangle”.

Apex and base angles

Given the apex, find the base angles

base angle = 180° − apex2

Given a base angle, find the apex

apex = 180° − 2 × base angle
Watch out When a question says “isosceles” but does not say which angle is the odd one out, the equal sides in the diagram tell you: the equal angles are the ones opposite the tick-marked sides. Never assume the top angle is the apex — the triangle may be drawn on its side.
Example 3Triangle PQR has PQ = PR and ∠QPR = 34°. Find ∠PQR.
1
PQ = PR, so the angles opposite those sides are equal. Those are the angles at R and Q. ∠PQR = ∠PRQ  (base angles of an isosceles triangle)
2
Call each base angle x and use the angle sum. 34 + x + x = 180
3
Solve. 2x = 146  →  x = 73°
4
Check. 34 + 73 + 73 = 180 ✓
∠PQR = 73°
Exam tip Write the letters of the equal sides down first. It stops you pairing the wrong angles, which is the single commonest error in this topic.

More worked examples

Example 4Triangle ABC has AB = AC and ∠ABC = 68°. BC is extended to D. Find ∠BAC and ∠ACD.
1
AB = AC, so the angles opposite them — at C and at B — are equal. ∠ACB = 68°  (base angles of an isosceles triangle)
2
Use the angle sum for the apex. ∠BAC = 180 − 68 − 68 = 44°  (angle sum of a triangle)
3
∠ACD is the exterior angle at C. Use either route: x = 180 − 68 = 112°  (angles on a straight line)
or x = 44 + 68 = 112°  (exterior angle of a triangle)
∠BAC = 44°  and  ∠ACD = 112°
Exam tip Doing it both ways is a free check. If the two routes disagree, one of your earlier steps is wrong.
Example 5A triangle has an exterior angle of (5x + 10)°. The two opposite interior angles are (2x + 30)° and (x + 40)°. Find all three interior angles.
1
Apply the exterior angle rule. 5x + 10 = (2x + 30) + (x + 40)
2
Simplify the right-hand side. 5x + 10 = 3x + 70
3
Solve. 2x = 60  →  x = 30
4
Find the two opposite interior angles and the exterior angle. 2x + 30 = 90°,   x + 40 = 70°
exterior = 5(30) + 10 = 160°  (and 90 + 70 = 160 ✓)
5
The third interior angle is next to the exterior angle on a straight line. third angle = 180 − 160 = 20°
check: 90 + 70 + 20 = 180 ✓
The interior angles are 90°, 70° and 20° — a right-angled scalene triangle
Example 6AB is parallel to CD. P lies on AB, and Q, R lie on CD. Given ∠APQ = 55° and ∠BPR = 48°, find all three angles of triangle PQR.
1
∠PQR and ∠APQ lie between the parallels on opposite sides of PQ. ∠PQR = 55°  (alternate angles)
2
Likewise for the transversal PR. ∠PRQ = 48°  (alternate angles)
3
Now use the angle sum of the triangle. x = 180 − 55 − 48 = 77°  (angle sum of a triangle)
4
Check by a second route: at P the three angles lie on the straight line AB. 55 + x + 48 = 180  →  x = 77° ✓
∠PQR = 55°,   ∠PRQ = 48°,   ∠QPR = 77°
Why both routes agree This picture is exactly the proof from earlier: the line AB through the apex is parallel to the base, so the angle sum of the triangle and the angles on the straight line are the same three numbers.
Example 7In triangle ABC, ∠BAC = 2x° and ∠ACB = 3x°. AB is extended to D and ∠CBD = 145°. Find x and all three interior angles.
1
The exterior angle at B equals the two opposite interior angles, at A and C. 2x + 3x = 145  (exterior angle of a triangle)
2
Solve. 5x = 145  →  x = 29
3
Find the two given angles. ∠BAC = 2(29) = 58°,   ∠ACB = 3(29) = 87°
4
The interior angle at B is on a straight line with the exterior angle. ∠ABC = 180 − 145 = 35°  (angles on a straight line)
5
Check the angle sum. 58 + 87 + 35 = 180 ✓
x = 29; the angles are 58°, 87° and 35°
Watch out Do not include the angle at B in step 1. The two “opposite” angles are the ones at the far end of the triangle from the exterior angle.

Your turn

Practice set

  1. Find the third angle: (a) 43° and 78°  (b) 90° and 27°  (c) angles 2y°, 3y°, 4y°.
  2. An isosceles triangle has an apex angle of 96°. Find each base angle.
  3. An isosceles triangle has a base angle of 37°. Find the apex angle, and say whether the triangle is acute-angled or obtuse-angled.
  4. A triangle has an exterior angle of 132°. One of the opposite interior angles is 45°. Find the other two interior angles.
  5. A triangle has an exterior angle of (4x − 5)° and opposite interior angles (x + 30)° and (2x + 5)°. Find all three interior angles.
  6. AB is parallel to CD, with P on AB and Q, R on CD. ∠APQ = 62° and ∠BPR = 39°. Find the three angles of triangle PQR, giving a reason for each.

  1. (a) 180 − 43 − 78 = 59°  (b) 180 − 90 − 27 = 63°  (c) 9y = 180 → y = 20, angles 40°, 60°, 80°.
  2. (180 − 96) ÷ 2 = 84 ÷ 2 = 42° each (check 96 + 42 + 42 = 180).
  3. 180 − 2(37) = 180 − 74 = 106°; since 106 > 90 it is obtuse-angled.
  4. Other opposite interior = 132 − 45 = 87°; third angle = 180 − 132 = 48° (check 45 + 87 + 48 = 180).
  5. 4x − 5 = 3x + 35 → x = 40; opposite interiors 70° and 85°, exterior 155°, third angle 180 − 155 = 25° → 70°, 85°, 25°.
  6. ∠PQR = 62° and ∠PRQ = 39° (alternate angles); ∠QPR = 180 − 62 − 39 = 79° (angle sum of a triangle).

Key points to remember

  • The interior angles of any triangle add to 180°; the reason is “angle sum of a triangle”.
  • The proof uses a line through the apex parallel to the base plus alternate angles — the same construction that solves the hard chase questions.
  • Exterior angle = sum of the two opposite interior angles, and exterior + adjacent interior = 180°.
  • Equilateral: three equal sides, three 60° angles, three lines of symmetry.
  • Isosceles: two equal sides and the angles opposite them are equal — write “base angles of an isosceles triangle”.
  • Base angle = (180° − apex) ÷ 2; apex = 180° − 2 × base angle.
  • A triangle can have at most one angle that is 90° or more.
  • Always finish by checking the three angles total 180°, and write a reason for every step.
