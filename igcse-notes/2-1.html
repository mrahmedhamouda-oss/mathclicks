2.1  Using Letters

How letters stand for unknown numbers, how to turn words and diagrams into correct algebraic expressions, and the notation examiners expect.

Why letters Words to algebra Expression or equation From diagrams Examples Your turn Key points

Why we use letters, and how to write them

Arithmetic answers one question at a time. Algebra answers every version of the question at once. If a taxi charges a $3 fixed fee plus $2 for each kilometre, you could work out the fare for 4 km, then for 9 km, then for 12 km — or you could write it once:

C = 3 + 2d Cost in dollars for a journey of d kilometres

That single line replaces an infinite table. Letters let us state a general rule, and they let us write down a quantity we do not yet know so that we can work with it. That is the whole point of Chapter 2.

Definition A variable is a letter standing for a number that can change (d above). A constant is a fixed number (the 3 and the 2). An expression is a collection of numbers and letters joined by +, −, ×, ÷ and powers, with no equals sign.
Exam tip When a question says “let n be the number of…”, write that sentence down yourself before you start. Defining your letter earns method marks and stops you confusing two quantities halfway through the question.

The conventions of algebraic notation

Algebra has a house style. Writing 3 × n is not wrong, but no examiner writes it, and multiplication signs are easily mistaken for the letter x.

Watch out 2n and n² are completely different. 2n means n + n; n² means n × n. When n = 5 they give 10 and 25. Only when n = 2 do they happen to agree.

Terms, coefficients and constants

An expression is built from terms — the chunks separated by + and − signs. The sign in front belongs to the term.

5x²  −  3x  +  7 Three terms: 5x², −3x and 7

Coefficient

The number multiplying the letter part. In 5x² − 3x + 7 the coefficient of x² is 5 and the coefficient of x is −3 — take the minus sign with it.

Constant term

A term with no letter in it. Here the constant is 7. Its value never changes, whatever x is.

More than / less than

Multiples and parts

Consecutive numbers

integers: n,  n+1,  n+2

odd: 2n+1,  2n+3

Order matters

“subtract n from 12” → 12 − n
“n subtract 12” → n − 12

Watch out The two phrases that trip up most candidates are “less than” and “subtract from”, because the words appear in the opposite order to the algebra. “5 less than k” is k − 5, not 5 − k.

Words to algebra translator

Pick a phrase and check your own answer against the translation and the reason.

Example 1Write an expression for each: (a) 7 more than x; (b) subtract y from 20; (c) the product of 3 and (m + 2); (d) half of n, then add 5; (e) 4 less than three times t.
1
(a) “More than” means add, and addition can be written either way round. x + 7
2
(b) “Subtract y from 20” — 20 is the starting amount. 20 − y
3
(c) Product means multiply. The whole bracket is multiplied, so keep the bracket. 3(m + 2)
4
(d) Half of n first, then add 5. Only the n is halved. n2 + 5
5
(e) Three times t is 3t; 4 less than that subtracts 4 from it. 3t − 4
(a) x + 7   (b) 20 − y   (c) 3(m + 2)   (d) n2 + 5   (e) 3t − 4
Exam tip Compare (d) with “half of (n add 5)”, which is n + 52. Read to the end of the sentence before you write.
Example 2For the expression 9y³ − y + 6x − 11, write down (a) the number of terms, (b) the coefficient of y³, (c) the coefficient of y, (d) the constant term.
1
Split at every + or −, keeping each sign with the term that follows it. 9y³  |  −y  |  +6x  |  −11
2
(a) Count them. 4 terms
3
(b) The number in front of y³. 9
4
(c) −y means −1 × y, so the coefficient is −1, not 1. −1
5
(d) The term with no letter, sign included. −11
(a) 4   (b) 9   (c) −1   (d) −11

Expression, equation, formula, identity, inequality

These five words describe different objects and questions use them precisely. “Simplify” belongs to an expression; “solve” belongs to an equation. Mixing them up costs marks.

NameTestExampleWhat you do with it
Expressionno = sign at all4x − 7simplify, expand, factorise, substitute
Equationhas =; true only for particular values4x − 7 = 5solve — here x = 3 only
Formulahas =; a rule linking two or more quantities, with a subjectA = πr²substitute, or rearrange to change the subject
Identitytrue for every value; written with ≡2(x + 3) ≡ 2x + 6prove, or use to rewrite
Inequalityuses < > ≤ ≥4x − 7 > 5solve for a range of values
Note An equation and an identity both contain an equals sign, but 4x − 7 = 5 is only true when x = 3, whereas 2(x+3) = 2x+6 is true for x = 0, x = 1, x = −98.6 — for absolutely everything. That is why it gets the special symbol ≡.

Forming expressions from diagrams

Perimeter is the distance all the way round, so you add every side. If a side is not labelled, work it out from the sides that are.

Two shapes with algebraic sides

Example 3Find, in terms of x, the perimeter of the L-shape in the diagram. Then find the perimeter when x = 2.5 cm.
1
Go round the shape once, listing every side in order and missing none. 5x + 2x + 2x + 3x + 3x + 5x
2
Add the coefficients: 5 + 2 + 2 + 3 + 3 + 5 = 20. P = 20x
3
Substitute x = 2.5. P = 20 × 2.5 = 50 cm
P = 20x   and   P = 50 cm when x = 2.5
Exam tip Mark each side with a small tick as you use it. Candidates lose marks far more often by missing a side than by adding up wrongly.

Total cost

“p pens at 45 cents each and b books at $3 each” — you must use one unit. In cents: 45p + 300b.

Ages

If Sami is s now, then t years ago he was s − t, and in t years he will be s + t. Everyone in the family ages by the same amount.

More worked examples

Example 4Maryam is 4 years younger than her brother Sami. Their mother is three times as old as Sami. Sami is s years old. Write expressions for (a) Maryam's age, (b) their mother's age, (c) the total of the three ages, (d) Maryam's age in 6 years' time.
1
Sami is the letter, so build everything from s. “4 years younger” means subtract 4. (a) Maryam = s − 4
2
“Three times as old as Sami” multiplies Sami's age. (b) Mother = 3s
3
(c) Add all three and collect the s terms. s + (s − 4) + 3s = 5s − 4
4
(d) Add 6 to Maryam's age, then tidy. (s − 4) + 6 = s + 2
(a) s − 4   (b) 3s   (c) 5s − 4   (d) s + 2
Check it Try s = 10: Maryam 6, mother 30, total 10 + 6 + 30 = 46, and 5(10) − 4 = 46. Substituting a number back is the fastest way to test an expression you are unsure of.
Example 5n is an integer. Write down the next two consecutive integers after n, and show that the sum of any three consecutive integers is a multiple of 3.
1
Consecutive integers go up in steps of 1. n,  n + 1,  n + 2
2
Add them, collecting the ns and the numbers separately. n + (n + 1) + (n + 2) = 3n + 3
3
Factorise so the factor of 3 is visible — this is the step that earns the “show that” mark. 3n + 3 = 3(n + 1)
Sum = 3(n + 1), which is 3 × an integer, so it is a multiple of 3
Watch out A “show that” question is never answered by testing a few numbers. 4 + 5 + 6 = 15 is evidence, not proof. You must produce the algebra.
Example 6A shop sells notebooks at $x each and pens at $y each. Rania buys 4 notebooks and 6 pens and pays with a $50 note. (a) Write an expression for her total cost. (b) Write an expression for her change, without brackets. (c) Find the change when x = 3.50 and y = 1.25.
1
(a) Cost of notebooks plus cost of pens. 4x + 6y
2
(b) Change is $50 minus the whole cost, so bracket it first. 50 − (4x + 6y)
3
The minus sign applies to both terms inside. 50 − 4x − 6y
4
(c) Substitute the two values. 50 − 4(3.50) − 6(1.25)
= 50 − 14 − 7.50 = 28.50
(a) 4x + 6y   (b) 50 − 4x − 6y   (c) $28.50
Watch out Writing 50 − 4x + 6y is the classic error. Always put the bracket in first, then remove it carefully.
Example 7State whether each of these is an expression, an equation, a formula, an identity or an inequality: (a) 7 − 2k; (b) v = u + at; (c) 3(x − 1) ≡ 3x − 3; (d) 5m + 2 = 17; (e) 2y − 1 ≤ 9.
1
(a) No relation symbol of any kind. expression
2
(b) A rule connecting four quantities, with v as the subject. formula
3
(c) Expanding the left gives exactly the right, so it holds for every x. identity
4
(d) True only for one value (m = 3), so it can be solved. equation
5
(e) Uses ≤, giving a range of solutions. inequality
(a) expression   (b) formula   (c) identity   (d) equation   (e) inequality

Your turn

Practice set

  1. Write an expression for: (a) 9 more than t; (b) subtract k from 30; (c) the product of 5 and (m − 2); (d) one third of w.
  2. For 7x² − x + 4y − 9 write down (a) the number of terms, (b) the coefficient of x², (c) the coefficient of x, (d) the constant term.
  3. A taxi charges a fixed $3 plus $2 per kilometre. Write a formula for the cost C of a journey of d km, and use it to find the cost of a 7 km journey.
  4. n is an integer. Write down the next two consecutive even numbers after 2n, and show that the sum of the three is a multiple of 6.
  5. Classify each as expression, equation, formula, identity or inequality: (a) 3x − 7; (b) 3x − 7 = 11; (c) A = πr²; (d) 2(x + 3) ≡ 2x + 6; (e) 3x − 7 > 11.
  6. A rectangle has width x cm and length 3 cm more than twice the width. Write and simplify expressions for (a) the perimeter, (b) the area. (c) Find the perimeter when x = 5.

  1. (a) t + 9  (b) 30 − k  (c) 5(m − 2)  (d) w3
  2. Terms are 7x² | −x | +4y | −9 → (a) 4  (b) 7  (c) −1  (d) −9
  3. Fixed part 3, variable part 2 × d → C = 3 + 2d; at d = 7, C = 3 + 14 = $17
  4. Even numbers rise in 2s: 2n + 2 and 2n + 4. Sum = 2n + (2n+2) + (2n+4) = 6n + 6 = 6(n + 1), a multiple of 6.
  5. (a) expression  (b) equation  (c) formula  (d) identity  (e) inequality
  6. Length = 2x + 3. (a) P = 2x + 2(2x + 3) = 6x + 6  (b) A = x(2x + 3) = 2x² + 3x  (c) 6(5) + 6 = 36 cm (check: sides 5 and 13, 2(5 + 13) = 36)

Key points to remember

  • Write 3n not 3 × n or n3; write n4 not n ÷ 4; write n not 1n.
  • 2n is n + n; n² is n × n. They are not the same thing.
  • Terms are separated by + and −, and the sign belongs to the term that follows it — so in 5x² − 3x the coefficient of x is −3.
  • Sum → add, difference → subtract, product → multiply, quotient → divide.
  • “a less than b” and “subtract a from b” both give b − a — the words come in the reverse order.
  • Consecutive integers: n, n+1, n+2. Consecutive even numbers: 2n, 2n+2, 2n+4. Consecutive odd numbers: 2n+1, 2n+3.
  • When a whole expression is subtracted or multiplied, bracket it first, then remove the bracket.
  • Expression (no =) · equation (solve it) · formula (a rule with a subject) · identity (true for all values, ≡) · inequality (< > ≤ ≥).
