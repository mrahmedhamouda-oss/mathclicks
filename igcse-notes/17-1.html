17.1  Earning Money

Salaries, wages, overtime, piecework and commission, then how deductions and banded income tax turn gross pay into net pay.

Basic pay Overtime Commission Deductions Examples Your turn Key points

Salaries, wages and basic pay

Money questions look easy and are answered badly. Almost every mark lost here comes from one of three things: using the wrong number of weeks in a year, forgetting that overtime is paid at a higher rate, or giving money to the wrong number of decimal places. Get those three right and this is the most reliable topic on Paper 2.

SalaryWage
Quoted asAn amount per yearAn amount per hour (or per item)
PaidUsually monthly — 12 equal paymentsUsually weekly — 52 payments
Extra hoursNormally included; no extra payPaid as overtime at a higher rate
Typical wording“earns $46 800 per annum”“paid $12.50 per hour”
Definition Basic pay is the money earned for the normal, agreed hours — before any overtime, bonus or deduction. basic pay = hours worked × hourly rate
monthly pay = annual salary12      weekly pay = annual salary52 12 months in a year, 52 weeks in a year — never 4 weeks in a month
Watch out A month is not four weeks. Twelve lots of four weeks is only 48 weeks. If a question gives a weekly wage and asks for the yearly total, multiply by 52.
Example 1Amara has an annual salary of $46 800, paid monthly. Ben is paid $12.50 per hour and works a basic 38-hour week for 52 weeks. Who earns more in a year, and by how much?
1
Amara’s monthly pay: divide the salary by 12. 46 800 ÷ 12 = $3900.00 per month
2
Ben’s weekly basic pay: hours × rate. 38 × 12.50 = $475.00 per week
3
Ben’s yearly pay: multiply by 52 weeks. 475.00 × 52 = $24 700.00
4
Compare the two yearly amounts. 46 800.00 − 24 700.00 = $22 100.00
Amara earns more, by $22 100.00 per year
Exam tip Compare like with like. Turn both amounts into the same period (both per year, or both per week) before you subtract.

Overtime

Hours worked beyond the basic week are paid at a higher rate. The rate is described as a multiplier of the basic hourly rate.

Time and a quarter

× 1.25 the basic rate

Time and a half

× 1.5 the basic rate — by far the most common in exams

Double time

× 2 the basic rate — usually Sundays or public holidays

total pay = (basic hours × rate) + (overtime hours × rate × multiplier) Work out each block of hours separately, then add
Watch out “Time and a half” means the whole overtime hour is paid at 1.5 × the rate — it is not an extra half-hour on top of normal pay. And if a worker does 44 hours with a basic week of 38, the overtime is 44 − 38 = 6 hours, not 44.
Example 2Ben is paid $12.50 per hour for a basic 38-hour week. One week he also works 6 hours at time and a half and 3 hours at double time. Find his total pay for that week.
1
Basic pay first. 38 × 12.50 = $475.00
2
Find the time-and-a-half rate, then multiply by those hours. 1.5 × 12.50 = $18.75 per hour
6 × 18.75 = $112.50
3
Find the double-time rate, then multiply by those hours. 2 × 12.50 = $25.00 per hour
3 × 25.00 = $75.00
4
Add the three blocks. 475.00 + 112.50 + 75.00 = 662.50
Total pay = $662.50
Exam tip Set the working out in three clear lines like this. If your final answer slips, the method marks are still there for the examiner to award.

Piecework and commission

Not everyone is paid by the hour. Two other systems appear regularly in 0580.

Piecework

Paid a fixed amount for each item produced, however long it takes.

pay = number of items × rate per item

A worker paid $1.80 per item who completes 145 items earns 145 × 1.80 = $261.00.

Commission

Paid a percentage of the value sold. Often added to a small guaranteed basic wage.

commission = rate% × value of sales

3% commission on $8000 of sales is 0.03 × 8000 = $240.00.

Note Questions very often say commission is paid only on sales above a threshold — for example “4% on all sales over $2000”. Subtract the threshold before you take the percentage. Read that sentence twice; it is the single most common error in this topic.
Example 3Selina is paid a basic wage of $320 per week plus 4% commission on all sales over $2000. In one week she sells goods worth $9500. Calculate her total pay for the week.
1
Find the sales that actually earn commission. 9500 − 2000 = $7500
2
Take 4% of that amount. 4% of 7500 = 0.04 × 7500 = $300.00
3
Add the basic wage. 320.00 + 300.00 = 620.00
Total pay = $620.00
Common error Taking 4% of the full $9500 gives $380 and a total of $700 — wrong, because the first $2000 of sales earns nothing.

Gross pay, deductions and net pay

Definition Gross pay is everything earned before anything is taken off. Deductions are the amounts removed — income tax, pension contributions, health insurance, union fees. Net pay (also called take-home pay) is what actually reaches the worker.
net pay = gross pay − total deductions Also worth knowing: net pay = gross pay × (100 − d)% if deductions are d% of gross

Reading a payslip

A payslip lists the earnings on one side and the deductions on the other. You are expected to read values out of it and complete missing entries.

Exam tip Percentage deductions are always a percentage of gross pay, never of the amount left over. Here the pension is 0.05 × 2850 = 142.50.

Income tax with an allowance and bands

Most tax systems in 0580 questions work in two stages.

Stage 1 — the tax-free allowance

A fixed slice of income is taxed at 0%. Subtract it first:

taxable income = gross income − allowance

Stage 2 — the bands

The taxable income is cut into bands, each with its own rate. Tax each band separately, then add. Only the part of the income inside a band is charged at that band’s rate.

How the bands stack up

Example 4Income tax is charged as follows: the first $9000 is tax free; the next $12 000 is taxed at 10%; the next $20 000 at 22%; anything above that at 35%. Marco earns $58 000. Find (a) the tax he pays, (b) his net income, (c) the tax as a percentage of his income.
1
(a) Take off the allowance to get the taxable income. 58 000 − 9000 = $49 000 taxable
2
Fill the first band: $12 000 at 10%. 0.10 × 12 000 = $1200.00
3
Fill the second band: $20 000 at 22%. 0.22 × 20 000 = $4400.00
4
Whatever is left goes in the top band. 49 000 − 12 000 − 20 000 = $17 000
0.35 × 17 000 = $5950.00
5
Add the three band amounts. 1200.00 + 4400.00 + 5950.00 = $11 550.00
6
(b) Net income = gross − tax. 58 000 − 11 550 = $46 450.00
7
(c) Write the tax as a fraction of the income, then multiply by 100. 11 55058 000 × 100 = 19.913… = 19.9%
(a) $11 550.00    (b) $46 450.00    (c) 19.9% (3 s.f.)
Watch out Marco is a 35% taxpayer, but he pays nothing like 35% overall — his average rate is 19.9%. Never multiply the whole income by the top rate.

More worked examples

Example 5A worker’s gross monthly pay is $2850. Income tax of $342.00 and health insurance of $68.50 are deducted, together with a pension contribution of 5% of gross pay. Find (a) her net pay, (b) the total deductions as a percentage of gross pay.
1
(a) Work out the pension — 5% of the gross figure. 0.05 × 2850 = $142.50
2
Add all three deductions. 342.00 + 142.50 + 68.50 = $553.00
3
Subtract from gross pay. 2850.00 − 553.00 = $2297.00
4
(b) Deductions as a percentage of gross pay. 5532850 × 100 = 19.403… = 19.4%
(a) net pay = $2297.00    (b) 19.4% (3 s.f.)
Example 6Deductions take 22% of Priya’s gross pay. Her net pay is $1794.00. Find her gross pay.
1
If 22% is removed, the net pay is the remaining percentage. 100% − 22% = 78%
2
So 78% of the gross pay is $1794. 0.78 × gross = 1794
3
Divide by the multiplier — this is a reverse percentage. gross = 1794 ÷ 0.78 = 2300
4
Check by working forwards. 0.22 × 2300 = 506  and  2300 − 506 = 1794 ✓
Gross pay = $2300.00
Watch out Do not add 22% of $1794. That gives $2188.68, which is wrong, because the 22% was a percentage of the gross figure, not of the net figure.
Example 7Jamal is paid $14.40 per hour for a basic 35-hour week. Overtime is paid at time and a half. One week his gross pay is $655.20. (a) How many hours of overtime did he work? (b) Deductions that week are 15% of gross pay. Find his net pay.
1
(a) Work out what the basic 35 hours are worth. 35 × 14.40 = $504.00
2
The rest of the gross pay must be overtime pay. 655.20 − 504.00 = $151.20
3
Find the overtime rate per hour. 1.5 × 14.40 = $21.60 per hour
4
Divide the overtime pay by the overtime rate. 151.20 ÷ 21.60 = 7 hours
5
(b) Net pay is 85% of gross pay. 0.85 × 655.20 = 556.92
(a) 7 hours of overtime    (b) net pay = $556.92
Exam tip In part (b) the single multiplier × 0.85 is quicker and safer than finding 15% and subtracting — one calculator step instead of two.

Practice set

  1. Kwame is paid $9.60 per hour and works a basic 37-hour week. Find his basic weekly pay.
  2. Lena’s annual salary is $37 440. Find her pay (a) per month, (b) per week.
  3. A worker’s basic rate is $11.20 per hour for a 40-hour week. In one week he also works 5 hours at time and a half and 2 hours at double time. Find his gross pay.
  4. A sales assistant is paid a basic wage of $250 per week plus 6% commission on her sales. One week she sells $4350 of goods. Find her total pay.
  5. Gross monthly pay is $3200. Deductions are income tax at 14% of gross, pension at 6% of gross, and a union fee of $12.50. Find the net pay.
  6. Tax is charged like this: the first $8500 is tax free, the next $15 000 is taxed at 12%, and anything above that at 30%. Adeline earns $46 000. Find (a) the tax paid, (b) her net income, (c) the tax as a percentage of her income, to 3 significant figures.

  1. 37 × 9.60 = $355.20
  2. (a) 37 440 ÷ 12 = $3120.00  (b) 37 440 ÷ 52 = $720.00
  3. Basic 40 × 11.20 = 448.00; overtime rate 1.5 × 11.20 = 16.80 so 5 × 16.80 = 84.00; double time 2 × 11.20 = 22.40 so 2 × 22.40 = 44.80. Total 448.00 + 84.00 + 44.80 = $576.80
  4. 0.06 × 4350 = 261.00, then 250 + 261 = $511.00
  5. Tax and pension together are 20% of gross: 0.20 × 3200 = 640.00. Deductions 640.00 + 12.50 = 652.50, so net 3200 − 652.50 = $2547.50
  6. Taxable 46 000 − 8500 = 37 500. Band 1: 0.12 × 15 000 = 1800.00. Remainder 37 500 − 15 000 = 22 500, so 0.30 × 22 500 = 6750.00. (a) $8550.00  (b) 46 000 − 8550 = $37 450.00  (c) 8550 ÷ 46 000 × 100 = 18.586… = 18.6%

Key points to remember

  • A salary is quoted per year (divide by 12 for monthly, by 52 for weekly); a wage is quoted per hour. There are 52 weeks in a year, not 48.
  • Basic pay = hours × hourly rate. Overtime hours are the hours beyond the basic week.
  • Time and a half means × 1.5 and double time means × 2 — applied to the whole overtime hour.
  • Commission is a percentage of sales; if the question says “on sales over $X”, subtract $X first. Piecework pays a fixed amount per item made.
  • Net pay = gross pay − total deductions. Percentage deductions are always percentages of gross pay.
  • For banded income tax: subtract the allowance, tax each band at its own rate, then add the band amounts. The top rate never applies to the whole income.
  • Going backwards from net pay to gross pay is a reverse percentage: divide by the multiplier, never add the percentage back on.
  • Give every money answer to 2 decimal places unless the question says otherwise.
