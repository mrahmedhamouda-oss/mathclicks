5.4  Standard Form

Writing very large and very small numbers as a × 10 to a power, and calculating with them accurately in the exam.

The form × and ÷ + and − Calculator Examples Your turn Key points

What standard form is

Scientists deal with numbers like 4 500 000 000 and 0.000 000 82. Writing all those zeros is slow and easy to miscount, so we write every number in the same compact shape.

Definition A number is in standard form when it is written as
a × 10n where 1 ≤ a < 10 and n is an integer
The value a must have exactly one non-zero digit before the decimal point.

Large numbers: n is positive

  • 4 500 000 = 4.5 × 106
  • 93 000 = 9.3 × 104
  • 6700 = 6.7 × 103

Count how many places the decimal point moves left; that is n.

Small numbers: n is negative

  • 0.00082 = 8.2 × 10−4
  • 0.0000305 = 3.05 × 10−5
  • 0.0205 = 2.05 × 10−2

Count how many places the point moves right; n is that many, negative.

Note A negative power does not mean the number is negative. 8.2 × 10−4 is a small positive number. Only a minus sign in front of a makes the number negative, as in −8.2 × 10−4.

The scale of powers of ten

Example 1Write in standard form: (a) 4 500 000  (b) 0.00082  (c) 34 × 105.
1
(a) Move the point until there is one non-zero digit in front of it, and count the places. The number is large, so the point travels left. 4 500 000. → 4.5 is 6 places left, so n = 6
2
(b) Same idea, but this number is small, so the point travels right and the power is negative. 0.00082 → 8.2 is 4 places right, so n = −4
3
(c) 34 is not between 1 and 10, so this is not yet in standard form. Move the point one place left in a and add 1 to the power to keep the value the same. 34 × 105 = 3.4 × 101 × 105 = 3.4 × 106
(a) 4.5 × 106    (b) 8.2 × 10−4    (c) 3.4 × 106
Exam trap Part (c) is the single most common lost mark in this topic. An answer of 34 × 105 or 0.6 × 10−4 is not in standard form and scores nothing, however good the working above it.
Example 2Write as ordinary numbers: (a) 3.06 × 105  (b) 9.1 × 10−4.
1
(a) A positive power makes the number bigger, so move the point 5 places right, filling with zeros. 3.06 → 306000.
2
(b) A negative power makes the number smaller, so move the point 4 places left. 9.1 → 0.00091
(a) 306 000     (b) 0.00091
Exam tip Count the zeros carefully: in 9.1 × 10−4 there are three zeros after the decimal point, because the 9 itself occupies the fourth place.

Multiplying and dividing

Standard form is built out of powers of 10, so the index laws do all the work. Deal with the numbers and the powers separately.

(a × 10m) × (b × 10n) = (a × b) × 10m+n
(a × 10m) ÷ (b × 10n) = (a ÷ b) × 10m−n Multiply the fronts, add the powers — divide the fronts, subtract the powers

Worked in one line each

  • (3 × 105) × (4 × 107) = 12 × 1012 = 1.2 × 1013
  • (8.4 × 106) ÷ (2 × 10−3) = 4.2 × 109
  • (2 × 105) ÷ (8 × 102) = 0.25 × 103 = 2.5 × 102

Powers of a number in standard form

Raise both parts to the power:

(3 × 104)3 = 33 × 1012 = 27 × 1012

= 2.7 × 1013

Watch out Subtracting a negative power catches people out every year: 6 − (−3) = 6 + 3 = 9. Write the bracket down rather than trying to do it in your head.
Example 3Work out (6 × 107) × (5 × 10−3), giving your answer in standard form.
1
Separate the fronts from the powers. = (6 × 5) × (107 × 10−3)
2
Multiply the fronts; add the indices. 6 × 5 = 30    7 + (−3) = 4
= 30 × 104
3
30 is not between 1 and 10, so adjust: move the point one place left and add 1 to the index. 30 × 104 = 3 × 101 × 104 = 3 × 105
3 × 105
Check 6 × 107 = 60 000 000 and 5 × 10−3 = 0.005; 60 000 000 × 0.005 = 300 000 = 3 × 105.

Adding and subtracting

There is no index law for adding, so you cannot add the powers. Either write both numbers with the same power of 10 and add the fronts, or convert to ordinary numbers, add, and convert back.

Rewrite the smaller number using the larger power. Moving the point one place left in a raises the power by 1.

4.2 × 104 = 0.42 × 105

3.6 × 105 + 0.42 × 105 = 4.02 × 105

Faster once you trust it, and essential when the numbers are too large for a display.

Write both out in full, add, then convert the answer back.

360 000 + 42 000 = 402 000

402 000 = 4.02 × 105

Safe and perfectly acceptable, but risky when there are many zeros to count.

Watch out 3.6 × 105 + 4.2 × 104 is not 7.8 × 109 and not 7.8 × 105. You may only add the fronts once the powers are identical.
Example 4Work out 3.6 × 105 + 4.2 × 104, giving your answer in standard form.
1
Choose the larger power, 105, and rewrite the other term with it. 4.2 × 104 = 0.42 × 105
2
Now the powers match, so add the fronts. 3.6 + 0.42 = 4.02
= 4.02 × 105
3
Check that a is between 1 and 10. It is, so no adjustment is needed. 1 ≤ 4.02 < 10  ✓
4.02 × 105
Note When the fronts add to 10 or more you must adjust: 9.4 × 106 + 8.1 × 106 = 17.5 × 106 = 1.75 × 107.

Your calculator, and ordering

The ×10x key

Modern Casio calculators have a key marked ×10x; older ones call it EXP or EE. To enter 4.2 × 1016 press

4 · 2   [×10x]   1 6

For a negative index use the (−) key, not the subtraction key.

Two habits that cost marks

  • Do not type × 10 as well — the key already means “times ten to the power”, so 4.2 × 10 [EXP] 16 is ten times too big.
  • The display may read 4.216 or 4.2E16. Never copy that down — write 4.2 × 1016 in full.
Ordering To put numbers in standard form in order, compare the powers first. Only if two powers are equal do you compare the values of a. Remember that 10−5 is smaller than 10−4.
Exam tip If a question says “write your answer in standard form”, that is an instruction, not a suggestion. An answer of 300 000 where 3 × 105 was asked for will not gain the final mark.

More worked examples

Example 5Write these numbers in order, smallest first: 3.2 × 10−3,   4.1 × 10−4,   2.9 × 10−3,   5 × 10−5.
1
Sort by power first. The most negative power gives the smallest number. 10−5 < 10−4 < 10−3
2
Two numbers share the power 10−3, so compare their fronts. 2.9 < 3.2
3
Confirm with ordinary numbers if you are unsure. 0.00005,  0.00041,  0.0029,  0.0032
5 × 10−5,   4.1 × 10−4,   2.9 × 10−3,   3.2 × 10−3
Watch out Do not be fooled by a large front. 5 × 10−5 has the biggest value of a but is by far the smallest number here.
Example 6Work out (2.4 × 10−5) ÷ (6 × 103), giving your answer in standard form.
1
Divide the fronts and subtract the indices. 2.4 ÷ 6 = 0.4    −5 − 3 = −8
2
So far this reads 0.4 × 10−8, which is not standard form because 0.4 < 1. 0.4 × 10−8
3
Move the point one place right in the front, so subtract 1 from the index. = 4 × 10−1 × 10−8 = 4 × 10−9
4 × 10−9
Exam tip Point moves left → power goes up; point moves right → power goes down. The value of the whole number never changes.
Example 7The mass of one grain of sand is 6.5 × 10−5 kg. A beach contains 8.4 × 1012 grains. (a) Find the total mass of sand in kg, in standard form. (b) Write this mass in tonnes, in standard form. (1 tonne = 1000 kg.)
1
(a) Total mass = mass of one grain × number of grains. (6.5 × 10−5) × (8.4 × 1012)
2
Fronts multiply; indices add. 6.5 × 8.4 = 54.6    −5 + 12 = 7
= 54.6 × 107
3
Adjust so that 1 ≤ a < 10. 54.6 × 107 = 5.46 × 101 × 107 = 5.46 × 108
4
(b) Divide by 1000, which is 103, so subtract 3 from the index. 5.46 × 108 ÷ 103 = 5.46 × 105
(a) 5.46 × 108 kg     (b) 5.46 × 105 tonnes
Note Converting units is just another power of 10. Dividing by 1000 lowers the index by 3; multiplying by 1000 raises it by 3. The digits 5 4 6 never change.

Key points to remember

  • Standard form is a × 10n with 1 ≤ a < 10 and n an integer — exactly one non-zero digit before the point.
  • Large numbers give a positive power, numbers below 1 give a negative power. A negative power never means a negative number.
  • Moving the decimal point one place left adds 1 to the power; one place right subtracts 1.
  • To multiply: multiply the fronts and add the indices. To divide: divide the fronts and subtract the indices. Take care with negative indices.
  • To add or subtract, first rewrite both numbers with the same power of 10, then add or subtract the fronts. Indices are never added when you are adding numbers.
  • Always check the front at the end: 34 × 105 and 0.4 × 10−8 are not in standard form — adjust to 3.4 × 106 and 4 × 10−9.
  • Use the ×10x (EXP) key, and never write the calculator display form 4.2E16 as your answer.
  • Order by comparing powers first, then the values of a.
