5.2  Operations on Fractions

Adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing fractions and mixed numbers, plus BIDMAS and reverse “find the original” problems.

Add & subtract Multiply & divide BIDMAS Examples Your turn Key points

Adding and subtracting

Fifths and eighths are different-sized pieces, so they cannot be added directly. Rewrite both fractions over a common denominator first — the LCM of the two denominators keeps the numbers as small as possible — then add or subtract the numerators only.

ab ± cd = ad ± cbbd Correct for every pair, but the LCM usually beats bd for size
Watch out The denominator does not get added. 12 + 13 is 56, not 25. A quick decimal check (0.5 + 0.3̇ = 0.83̇) catches this instantly.
Example 1Work out 38 + 512, giving your answer in its lowest terms.
1
Find the LCM of the denominators. 8 = 2³,  12 = 2² × 3  →  LCM = 2³ × 3 = 24
2
Rewrite each fraction over 24. Whatever multiplies the bottom must multiply the top. 3/8 = 9/24  (×3)
5/12 = 10/24  (×2)
3
Add the numerators; the denominator stays at 24. 9/24 + 10/24 = 19/24
4
Check whether it simplifies. 19 is prime and does not divide 24, so it is already in lowest terms. 19 ÷ 24 = 0.7916̇  =  0.375 + 0.416̇  ✓

Mixed numbers

There are two respectable methods. Method 1 is safer under pressure and is the one to use for subtraction; Method 2 is quicker when the whole numbers are large.

Convert every mixed number to an improper fraction first, do the arithmetic, then convert back at the very end.

314 − 156 = 134 − 116 = 3912 − 2212 = 1712 = 1512

No borrowing, no traps. This is the method to use if you are ever unsure.

Deal with the whole numbers and the fractions separately — but be ready to borrow one whole when the fraction you are subtracting is too big.

3312 − 11012  →  21512 − 11012 = 1512

Here 312 is smaller than 1012, so one whole (twelve twelfths) was carried across: 3 + 312 = 2 + 1512.

Example 2Work out 314 − 156, giving your answer as a mixed number.
1
Turn both mixed numbers into improper fractions. 3 1/4 = (3 × 4 + 1)/4 = 13/4
1 5/6 = (1 × 6 + 5)/6 = 11/6
2
LCM of 4 and 6 is 12. 13/4 = 39/12  (×3)
11/6 = 22/12  (×2)
3
Subtract the numerators. In Paper 4 you must write this line down — it carries the method mark. 39/12 − 22/12 = 17/12
4
Convert back: 17 ÷ 12 = 1 remainder 5. 17/12 = 1 5/12  =  1.416̇
check: 3.25 − 1.83̇ = 1.416̇ ✓

Multiplying and dividing

These are the easy ones: no common denominator is needed. Multiply straight across the top and straight across the bottom.

ab × cd = a × cb × d      ab ÷ cd = ab × dc Multiply across  ·  divide by multiplying by the reciprocal

Cancel before you multiply

Any numerator may be cancelled with any denominator, whichever fraction it comes from — you are just simplifying the single big fraction before you work it out. It keeps the numbers small and means the answer comes out already in lowest terms.

Example 3Work out 815 × 2532.
1
Write it as one fraction so you can see everything. (8 × 25) / (15 × 32)
2
Cancel 8 with 32 (both divide by 8). 8 → 1    32 → 4
3
Cancel 25 with 15 (both divide by 5). 25 → 5    15 → 3
4
Now multiply the small numbers. (1 × 5) / (3 × 4) = 5/12
512   (0.53̇ × 0.78125 = 0.416̇ ✓)
Exam tip Without cancelling you would get 200480 — correct, but you then have to spot that the HCF is 40. Cancelling first is the same work done earlier and more safely.

Dividing: keep – change – flip

Dividing by 16 asks “how many sixths fit inside?”, so dividing by a fraction less than 1 makes the answer bigger. The rule is keep the first fraction, change ÷ to ×, flip the second.

Why 23 ÷ 16 = 4

Example 4Work out 213 ÷ 159.
Non-negotiable You may never keep–change–flip a mixed number. Convert both to improper fractions first.
1
Convert. 2 1/3 = 7/3    1 5/9 = 14/9
2
Keep, change, flip. 7/3 ÷ 14/9 = 7/3 × 9/14
3
Cancel before multiplying: 7 with 14 (÷7), and 9 with 3 (÷3). (1 × 3) / (1 × 2) = 3/2
4
Write as a mixed number. 3/2 = 1 1/2
check: 2.3̇ ÷ 1.5̇ = 1.5 ✓

Fractions inside bigger calculations

Fractions obey BIDMAS exactly like whole numbers: brackets, then indices, then division and multiplication (left to right), then addition and subtraction. The fraction line itself also acts as a bracket — work out the whole top and the whole bottom before dividing.

Order of operations

  1. Brackets
  2. Indices
  3. Division and Multiplication
  4. Addition and Subtraction

Where students lose marks

  • Adding before multiplying in a + b × c
  • Flipping the first fraction when dividing
  • Leaving the answer as 200480 instead of 512
  • Giving 1712 when the question asked for a mixed number

Reverse problems: finding the original amount

A question such as “⅔ of a number is 24” gives you the result and asks for the input. Undo the multiplication by dividing — which, as you now know, means multiplying by the reciprocal.

23 of n = 24  ⇔  n = 24 ÷ 23 = 24 × 32 = 36 Divide by the fraction — or divide by the numerator and multiply by the denominator
Exam tip Always finish by checking forwards: 23 of 36 = 24 ✓. If a worded question says money was spent or a tank was emptied, first work out what fraction the number you are given represents — usually 1 − (the fraction used).

More worked examples

Example 5Work out 112 + 23 × ( 34 − 18 ), giving your answer as a mixed number in its lowest terms.
1
Brackets first. LCM of 4 and 8 is 8. 3/4 − 1/8 = 6/8 − 1/8 = 5/8
2
Multiplication next. Cancel the 2 with the 8 before multiplying. 2/3 × 5/8 = (1 × 5)/(3 × 4) = 5/12
3
Addition last. Write 112 as 32; the LCM of 2 and 12 is 12. 3/2 + 5/12 = 18/12 + 5/12 = 23/12
4
Convert to a mixed number. 23 ÷ 12 = 1 r 11  →  1 11/12
check: 1.5 + 0.416̇ = 1.916̇ ✓
11112
Watch out Adding first would give (112 + 23) × 58 = 11748 — a completely different answer.
Example 6(a) ⅔ of a number is 24. Find the number. (b) Amira spends 38 of her money and has $45 left. How much did she start with?
1
(a) Call the number n and write the sentence as an equation. 2/3 × n = 24
2
Divide both sides by 23, i.e. multiply by 32. n = 24 × 3/2 = 72/2 = 36
3
Check forwards. 36 ÷ 3 = 12,  12 × 2 = 24  ✓
4
(b) The $45 is not 38 of the money — it is what remains. 1 − 3/8 = 5/8 of the money = $45
5
Divide by 58. 45 × 8/5 = 9 × 8 = 72
6
Check: she spends 38 of 72. 72 ÷ 8 × 3 = 27,  72 − 27 = 45  ✓
(a) 36     (b) $72.00
Example 7A tank is 25 full. After 36 litres are poured in, it is 710 full. Find the capacity of the tank.
1
The 36 litres accounts for the change in the fraction, so subtract. LCM of 5 and 10 is 10. 7/10 − 2/5 = 7/10 − 4/10 = 3/10
2
So 310 of the capacity C is 36 litres. 3/10 × C = 36
3
Multiply by the reciprocal 103. C = 36 × 10/3 = 12 × 10 = 120
4
Check both statements. 2/5 of 120 = 48    48 + 36 = 84
7/10 of 120 = 84  ✓
capacity = 120 litres
Method note Every reverse problem has the same shape: find what fraction the given amount represents, then divide by that fraction. Writing that one sentence before calculating is worth a mark on its own in Paper 4.

Key points to remember

  • Add and subtract: common denominator (use the LCM), then combine the numerators only — never the denominators.
  • Multiply: straight across the top and bottom. No common denominator is required.
  • Cancel before you multiply — any numerator against any denominator. The answer then arrives already simplified.
  • Divide: keep – change – flip. Only the second fraction is flipped.
  • Turn every mixed number into an improper fraction first, especially before dividing, then convert back at the end if the question wants a mixed number.
  • Fractions follow BIDMAS; the fraction line acts as a bracket around the top and the bottom.
  • For reverse problems, decide what fraction the given amount is, then divide by that fraction (multiply by its reciprocal) — and check forwards.
  • Always give the final answer in its lowest terms, and in the form the question asked for.
