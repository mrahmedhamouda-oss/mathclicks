13.5  Exchanging Currencies

Reading an exchange rate correctly, converting in both directions, allowing for commission, and comparing prices across two countries.

Exchange rates Converting Commission Comparing Examples Your turn Key points

What an exchange rate tells you

An exchange rate is a conversion rate between two currencies. It is always written as a statement about one unit of one currency, and reading which currency that is decides whether you multiply or divide. Get that wrong and the rest of the question collapses, so slow down for one second and read it.

Definition The exchange rate $1 = €0.85 means one dollar buys 0.85 euros. The 1 belongs to the dollar, so every dollar you hand over comes back as 0.85 euros.
Rate as writtenOne unit of…To go that wayTo come back
$1 = €0.85dollarsdollars → euros: × 0.85euros → dollars: ÷ 0.85
£1 = $1.25poundspounds → dollars: × 1.25dollars → pounds: ÷ 1.25
$1 = 3.75 riyalsdollarsdollars → riyals: × 3.75riyals → dollars: ÷ 3.75

The two directions, in one picture

Exam tip Money answers in 0580 are given to 2 decimal places unless the question says otherwise. Write €425.00, not €425, and $68.40, not $68.4. Do not round part-way through a multi-step question — keep the full figure in your calculator and round only at the end.

Multiplying, dividing, and the sense check

There are only two operations available, so if you are unsure, ask yourself a single question before you touch the calculator:

Should the answer be a bigger number or a smaller number? The sense check that saves the mark

If one dollar is worth less than one euro ($1 = €0.85), then any amount in euros is a smaller number than the same value in dollars. So dollars → euros must shrink the number: multiply by 0.85. Euros → dollars must grow it: divide by 0.85. The number changes, the value does not.

Example 1The exchange rate is $1 = €0.85. Convert $250 into euros.
1
The rate is written for one dollar, and we are starting in dollars, so multiply. 250 × 0.85
2
Sense check: 0.85 is less than 1, so the answer should be a smaller number than 250. It is. = 212.5
3
Write money to 2 decimal places. €212.50
4
Check by converting back. 212.50 ÷ 0.85 = 250  ✓
€212.50
Example 2Using the same rate $1 = €0.85, convert €340 into dollars.
1
This time we are starting in euros, which is not the currency the rate is written for. So we undo the multiplication: divide. 340 ÷ 0.85
2
Sense check: one dollar is worth only 0.85 euros, so a dollar is the weaker unit and it takes more dollars to hold the same value. The answer must therefore be a bigger number than 340. = 400
3
Check by converting back. 400 × 0.85 = 340  ✓
$400.00
Watch out Multiplying by mistake gives 340 × 0.85 = 289, which looks perfectly respectable. Only the sense check catches it, so do the sense check every single time.

Rates given the other way round

The same relationship can always be written from either currency's point of view, and the two rates are reciprocals.

£1 = $1.25  ⇔  $1 = £11.25 = £0.80 1.25 × 0.80 = 1, as it must be
Example 3The rate is £1 = $1.25. (a) Write the rate in the form $1 = £… (b) Convert $90 into pounds, using each version of the rate.
1
(a) Divide both sides by 1.25 so the dollar side becomes 1. $1 = £(1 ÷ 1.25) = £0.80
2
(b) Using $1 = £0.80 we start in the currency the rate is written for, so multiply. 90 × 0.80 = 72
3
Using £1 = $1.25 we are starting in the other currency, so divide. Same answer, as it must be. 90 ÷ 1.25 = 72
4
Check by converting back. 72 × 1.25 = 90  ✓
(a) $1 = £0.80     (b) £72.00
Exam tip If a rate makes you nervous, rewrite it so that the 1 sits on the currency you are starting from. Then you always multiply, and there is nothing left to get wrong.

Commission and bank charges

A bank does not swap your money for free. It takes either a percentage commission, a fixed charge, or both. The charge is normally taken in the currency you are handing over, so the usual order is: subtract the charge first, then convert what is left.

Percentage commission

A commission of 2.5% leaves you with 100% − 2.5% = 97.5% of your money.

amount left = amount × 0.975

Use the multiplier, not two separate steps — it is faster and safer.

Fixed charge

A flat fee such as $3.50 is simply subtracted, whatever the size of the transaction.

amount left = amount − 3.50

Fixed charges hurt small exchanges much more than large ones.

Note For a percentage commission it does not matter whether the bank takes it before or after converting — multiplication is commutative, so (600 × 0.975) × 0.85 and (600 × 0.85) × 0.975 give the same answer. For a fixed charge the order matters a great deal, so read the question carefully to see which currency the fee is charged in.
Example 4A bank changes dollars into euros at $1 = €0.85 and charges 2.5% commission. How many euros does a customer receive for $600?
1
The commission is taken from the dollars, leaving 97.5%. 600 × 0.975 = 585
2
Convert what is left. The rate is written for one dollar and we are starting in dollars, so multiply. 585 × 0.85 = 497.25
3
The other order gives the same result — useful as a check. 600 × 0.85 = 510
510 × 0.975 = 497.25  ✓
4
Check the conversion by reversing it. 497.25 ÷ 0.85 = 585  ✓
€497.25
Watch out Commission of 2.5% does not mean you multiply by 0.25 or by 2.5. Convert the percentage to a multiplier first: 1 − 0.025 = 0.975.

Comparing prices in two countries

“Which is cheaper?” questions look harder than they are. You cannot compare 520 of one currency with 430 of another, so the method is always the same:

The method

  1. Choose one currency to work in — usually the one the question asks the final answer in.
  2. Convert the other price into that currency.
  3. Compare the two numbers and state clearly which is cheaper.
  4. Subtract to find the difference, and give it in the currency the question asked for.
Watch out A question is not finished until you have answered it. Writing “$505.88” scores the calculation mark but not the conclusion mark. Write the sentence: “the camera is cheaper in France, by $14.12”.

More worked examples

Example 5A camera costs $520 in the USA and €430 in France. The exchange rate is $1 = €0.85. In which country is the camera cheaper, and by how much in dollars?
1
The answer is wanted in dollars, so work in dollars. Convert the French price: we are starting in euros, so divide. 430 ÷ 0.85 = 505.882…
2
Keep the full figure for now, but see immediately that it is below $520. €430 = $505.88 (2 d.p.)
3
Subtract to find the difference, using the unrounded value and rounding only at the end. 520 − 505.882… = 14.117…
4
Check the same comparison in euros. 520 × 0.85 = 442.00
442.00 − 430 = €12.00
12 ÷ 0.85 = $14.12  ✓
Cheaper in France, by $14.12
Example 6A bank changes dollars to euros at $1 = €0.85. It charges a fixed fee of $2.00 plus commission of 1.5% of the amount changed. How many euros does a customer receive for $800?
1
Work out the commission in dollars. 1.5% of 800 = 0.015 × 800 = 12.00
2
Add the fixed fee to get the total charge. 12.00 + 2.00 = $14.00
3
Subtract the charge from the amount handed over. 800 − 14 = $786.00
4
Convert the remainder. Starting in dollars, so multiply. 786 × 0.85 = 668.10
5
Check by converting back. 668.10 ÷ 0.85 = 786  ✓
€668.10
Watch out Do not apply the 1.5% to the euros and the $2.00 to the dollars in the same calculation unless the question says so. Deal with both charges in the currency being handed over.
Example 7Aisha changes $1000 into euros at $1 = €0.85 with 2% commission. She spends €233 on holiday. She changes the rest back into dollars at €1 = $1.15, again with 2% commission. How many dollars does she receive?
1
Commission first: 2% taken means 98% remains. 1000 × 0.98 = $980.00
2
Convert to euros — starting in dollars and the rate is per dollar, so multiply. 980 × 0.85 = €833.00
3
Take off what she spends. 833 − 233 = €600.00
4
Commission on the way back, again 98% remaining. 600 × 0.98 = €588.00
5
Convert to dollars. The new rate is written per euro and we are starting in euros, so multiply. 588 × 1.15 = 676.20
6
Check by converting back. 676.20 ÷ 1.15 = 588  ✓
$676.20
Why the two rates differ The rate to buy euros ($1 = €0.85, i.e. €1 = $1.176…) is not the rate to sell them (€1 = $1.15). That gap, plus the commission, is how the bank makes its money — which is why changing money there and back always leaves you worse off.

Your turn

Practice set

  1. The rate is $1 = 3.75 riyals. Convert $340 into riyals.
  2. Using the same rate, convert 2550 riyals into dollars.
  3. The rate is £1 = $1.25. (a) Write it in the form $1 = £… (b) Convert $150 into pounds.
  4. A bank changes dollars into euros at $1 = €0.85 and charges 3% commission. How many euros does a customer get for $400?
  5. A jacket costs $96 in the USA and €78 in Spain, with $1 = €0.85. Which is cheaper, and by how much in dollars?
  6. Omar changes $2000 into rupees at $1 = 82 rupees, paying 1.5% commission. He spends 119540 rupees, then changes the rest back at $1 = 84 rupees with no further charge. How many dollars does he receive?

  1. Starting in dollars, so multiply: 340 × 3.75 = 1275.00 riyals (check: 1275 ÷ 3.75 = 340).
  2. Starting in riyals, so divide: 2550 ÷ 3.75 = $680.00 (check: 680 × 3.75 = 2550).
  3. (a) 1 ÷ 1.25 = $1 = £0.80; (b) 150 × 0.80 = 150 ÷ 1.25 = £120.00 (check: 120 × 1.25 = 150).
  4. 3% commission leaves 97%: 400 × 0.97 = 388, then 388 × 0.85 = €329.80 (check: 329.80 ÷ 0.85 = 388).
  5. 78 ÷ 0.85 = 91.7647… = $91.76, which is less than $96, so cheaper in Spain by $4.24 (check in euros: 96 × 0.85 = 81.60, 81.60 − 78 = €3.60, and 3.60 ÷ 0.85 = 4.24).
  6. 2000 × 0.985 = 1970, then 1970 × 82 = 161540 rupees. 161540 − 119540 = 42000 rupees left. Coming back, divide: 42000 ÷ 84 = $500.00 (check: 500 × 84 = 42000).

Key points to remember

  • Read which currency the 1 belongs to. Starting in that currency you multiply; starting in the other one you divide.
  • Always do the sense check: decide whether the answer should be a bigger or a smaller number before you calculate.
  • Rewriting a rate the other way round means taking the reciprocal: £1 = $1.25 is the same as $1 = £0.80.
  • Check every conversion by converting back — it takes five seconds and catches the multiply/divide error.
  • Turn a commission into a multiplier: 2% commission means × 0.98. A fixed bank charge is simply subtracted, usually before converting.
  • For a percentage commission the order does not matter; for a fixed charge it does, so note which currency the fee is in.
  • To compare prices in two countries, convert both into the same currency, then state which is cheaper and the difference.
  • Give money to 2 decimal places, and do not round until the final line of the working.
