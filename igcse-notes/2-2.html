2.2  Substitution

Replacing letters with numbers — positive, negative and fractional — and evaluating expressions and formulae without losing marks to sign errors.

Substituting Negatives Formulae Examples Your turn Key points

What substitution means

Substitution means replacing each letter in an expression by the number it stands for, then working out the value. It sounds trivial. It is where a large number of Paper 2 and Paper 4 marks are quietly thrown away, almost always through signs and order of operations.

The golden rule Write the substituted number inside a bracket, every single time. It costs you two pen strokes and it protects you from every common sign error.
3a  →  3(a)     a²  →  (a)²     −a²  →  −(a)² Bracket first, then put the number in

Order of operations still applies

Once the numbers are in, you are doing ordinary arithmetic, so BIDMAS governs everything: Brackets, Indices, Division and Multiplication, then Addition and Subtraction.

Powers before multiplying

3x² means 3 × x², so square first. When x = 4: 3(4)² = 3 × 16 = 48, not (3 × 4)² = 144.

A fraction bar is a bracket

2x + 13 means (2x + 1) ÷ 3. When x = 7 it is 153 = 5, not 2(7) + 13.

Example 1Find the value of 4a + 3b when a = 5 and b = −2.
1
Write the expression, then replace each letter with its value in brackets. 4a + 3b = 4(5) + 3(−2)
2
Do the multiplications first (BIDMAS). = 20 + (−6)
3
Adding a negative is the same as subtracting. = 20 − 6
4a + 3b = 14

Substituting negative numbers

This is the single biggest source of lost marks in the whole of Chapter 2. Two rules do all the work.

A negative squared is positive

(−3)² = (−3) × (−3) = 9

(−3)³ = −27 — an odd power keeps the minus sign, an even power removes it.

But −x² is not (−x)²

−x² means “square it, then make it negative”. With x = −3:

−x² = −(−3)² = −9  but  (−x)² = (3)² = 9

Watch out Subtracting a negative adds. In 5 − 2x with x = −4: 5 − 2(−4) = 5 − (−8) = 5 + 8 = 13. Students who skip the bracket typically write 5 − 8 = −3 and lose the lot.
Example 2Given p = −3 and q = 4, find the value of (a) p², (b) −p², (c) 2p − q², (d) (p − q)².
1
(a) Bracket the negative before squaring. p² = (−3)² = 9
2
(b) The minus sign sits outside the squaring, so it survives. −p² = −(−3)² = −(9) = −9
3
(c) Multiply and square first, then subtract. 2(−3) − (4)² = −6 − 16 = −22
4
(d) The bracket comes first here, so subtract before squaring. (−3 − 4)² = (−7)² = 49
(a) 9   (b) −9   (c) −22   (d) 49
Compare (c) and (d) use the same two numbers and give −22 and 49. The brackets are doing all the work — read them carefully.

Fractional values

Fractions substitute in exactly the same way. Keep them as fractions if the numbers are friendly, and remember that a fraction raised to a power raises both the numerator and the denominator.

232 = 2²3² = 49 Square the top and the bottom
Example 3Given x = 12 and y = 23, find (a) 6x + 9y, (b) x²y, (c) x ÷ y.
1
(a) Multiply each fraction by its coefficient and cancel. 6(12) + 9(23) = 3 + 6 = 9
2
(b) Square x first (indices before multiplication). (12)² × 23 = 14 × 23 = 212 = 16
3
(c) Dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal. 12 ÷ 23 = 12 × 32 = 34
(a) 9    (b) 16    (c) 34
Exam tip If the question gives fractions, give a fraction answer. Writing 0.1666… instead of 16 looks like a rounded answer and can be penalised.

Substituting into formulae

A formula is just an expression with a name on the front. The mark scheme almost always wants three lines: the formula, the substitution, the answer with its unit.

Example 4Use c = a² + b² to find c when a = 6 and b = 8. Then find c when a = 3.5 and b = 7.2, correct to 3 significant figures.
1
Substitute, keeping everything under the root. c = (6)² + (8)²
2
Square first, then add, then root. c = 36 + 64 = 100 = 10
3
Second pair: square and add. (3.5)² + (7.2)² = 12.25 + 51.84 = 64.09
4
Take the root and only then round. c = 64.09 = 8.00562…
c = 10   and   c = 8.01 (3 s.f.)
Exam tip Do not round 64.09 or any intermediate value. Keep the full display on your calculator and round once, at the very end.

More worked examples

Example 5The formula C = 5(F − 32)9 converts degrees Fahrenheit to degrees Celsius. Find C when (a) F = 212, (b) F = 5.
1
(a) Substitute and deal with the bracket first. C = 5(212 − 32)9 = 5(180)9
2
Multiply, then divide. C = 9009 = 100
3
(b) Now the bracket gives a negative — keep it in brackets. C = 5(5 − 32)9 = 5(−27)9
4
A negative divided by a positive is negative. C = −1359 = −15
(a) C = 100°C    (b) C = −15°C
Sense check 212°F is boiling water and 100°C is boiling water, so part (a) is right. Always sanity-check a formula answer against something you know.
Example 6A car travels 150 km in 2 hours 30 minutes. (a) Use speed = distancetime to find its average speed in km/h. (b) At that speed, how far would it travel in 3 hours 45 minutes?
1
Convert the time to hours as a decimal. 30 minutes is 3060 of an hour. 2 h 30 min = 2.5 h
2
(a) Substitute into the formula. speed = 1502.5 = 60 km/h
3
(b) Rearranged, distance = speed × time, and 45 min is 0.75 h. distance = 60 × 3.75
4
= 225 km
(a) 60 km/h    (b) 225 km
Watch out Never substitute 2.30 for 2 hours 30 minutes. Minutes are sixtieths: 15 min = 0.25 h, 20 min = 0.3̇ h, 40 min = 0.6̇ h.
Example 7The formula s = ut + 12at² gives the distance s metres travelled. Find s when (a) u = 8, a = −2, t = 3; (b) u = 0, a = 9.8, t = 2.5, giving your answer to 3 significant figures.
1
(a) Substitute all three values, bracketing each one. s = (8)(3) + 12(−2)(3)²
2
Indices first: (3)² = 9. Then the multiplications. s = 24 + 12(−2)(9) = 24 + (−9)
3
Adding a negative subtracts. s = 24 − 9 = 15
4
(b) The first term vanishes because u = 0. s = (0)(2.5) + 12(9.8)(2.5)²
5
(2.5)² = 6.25, so multiply the three numbers. s = 0 + 0.5 × 9.8 × 6.25 = 30.625
6
Round only now, to 3 significant figures. s = 30.6
(a) s = 15 m    (b) s = 30.6 m (3 s.f.)
Watch out In part (a) the t² applies only to t, not to at. Squaring (−2)(3) would give 36 and a completely wrong answer.

  2. x = −5. Find (a) x², (b) −x², (c) 3 − 2x, (d) x³.
  3. p = 23 and q = 14. Find (a) 9p², (b) 8q, (c) p ÷ q.

  5. Use C = 5(F − 32)9 to find C when F = −4.

  2. (a) (−5)² = 25  (b) −(−5)² = −25  (c) 3 − 2(−5) = 3 + 10 = 13  (d) (−5)³ = −125
  3. (a) 9 × 49 = 4  (b) 8 × 14 = 2  (c) 23 × 41 = 83
  4. v = 25 + (−4)(6.5) = 25 − 26 = −1
  5. C = 5(−4 − 32)9 = 5(−36)9 = −1809 = −20°C
  6. A = 4π(6.5)² = 4π(42.25) = 169π = 530.929… = 531 cm² (3 s.f.)

Key points to remember

  • Always bracket the substituted value, especially when it is negative: x² → (−3)².
  • An even power of a negative is positive; an odd power stays negative: (−2)² = 4 but (−2)³ = −8.
  • −x² and (−x)² are different. In −x² only x is squared.
  • BIDMAS still rules after substituting: brackets, indices, then × ÷, then + −.
  • A fraction bar and a root sign both act as brackets — complete everything above the bar or under the root before dividing or rooting.
  • Show the formula, then the substitution, then the answer. The substitution line carries the method mark.
  • Convert times to decimal hours before using speed = distancetime: 2 h 30 min is 2.5 h.
  • Never round part-way through. Round once, at the end, to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.
