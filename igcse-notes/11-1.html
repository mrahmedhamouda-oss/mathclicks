11.1  Pythagoras’ Theorem

The relationship between the three sides of a right-angled triangle, and how to use it to find lengths, test for right angles and solve real problems.

The theorem Finding sides Converse Applications Examples Your turn Key points

The theorem

In any right-angled triangle, the square drawn on the longest side has exactly the same area as the two squares on the shorter sides added together. That single fact — Pythagoras’ theorem — is one of the most heavily used results in the whole 0580 syllabus.

Definition The hypotenuse is the side opposite the right angle. It is always the longest side of a right-angled triangle. Everything in this lesson depends on identifying it correctly first.
a² + b² = c² where c is the hypotenuse and a, b are the two shorter sides

Why it is true — the squares picture

Finding the hypotenuse first

The right angle can be drawn anywhere on the page. Do not assume the hypotenuse is the bottom or the sloping side — find the right angle, then look straight across from it.

Same rule, three orientations

Watch out Pythagoras works only in right-angled triangles. If a triangle has no right angle you need the sine or cosine rule instead, not a² + b² = c².

Finding a missing side

Finding the hypotenuse — you add

Square both short sides, add, then square root.

c = a² + b²

Finding a shorter side — you subtract

Square the hypotenuse, subtract the square of the known short side, then square root.

a = c² − b²

Exam tip Decide before you touch the calculator: is the missing side the longest one? If yes, add. If no, subtract. Half of all lost marks on this topic come from adding when you should have subtracted.
Example 1A right-angled triangle has shorter sides of 8 cm and 15 cm. Find the length of the hypotenuse.
1
The unknown side is opposite the right angle, so it is the hypotenuse. That means add. a² + b² = c²
2
Substitute the two shorter sides. 8² + 15² = x²
3
Square each number, then add. 64 + 225 = x²
x² = 289
4
Square root. Because 289 is a perfect square the answer is exact. x = 289 = 17
hypotenuse = 17 cm
Exam tip Check for sense: the hypotenuse must be longer than 15 and shorter than 8 + 15 = 23. 17 passes both tests.
Example 2In triangle PQR, angle Q = 90°, PR = 9.4 cm and QR = 6.1 cm. Find PQ.
1
The right angle is at Q, so the hypotenuse is the side opposite it: PR = 9.4 cm. The missing side is a shorter side, so subtract. x² + 6.1² = 9.4²
2
Rearrange so the unknown is on its own. x² = 9.4² − 6.1²
3
Square first, then subtract — never subtract the lengths themselves. x² = 88.36 − 37.21 = 51.15
4
Square root and round to 3 significant figures. x = 51.15 = 7.15192…
PQ = 7.15 cm (3 s.f.)
Watch out 9.4 − 6.1 = 3.3 is not the answer. The subtraction happens between the squares, not the lengths.

Exact answers or 3 significant figures

If the square root is not exact, 0580 expects 3 significant figures unless the question says otherwise. If the question says “give your answer in exact form” or “in surd form”, leave the root and simplify it instead.

diagonal of a 6 cm square = 6² + 6² = 72 = 62 exact form — or 8.49 cm to 3 s.f.

Testing for a right angle

Pythagoras also works backwards. This reversed version is called the converse, and questions that say “show that triangle ABC is right-angled” are testing exactly it.

The converse If the square on the longest side equals the sum of the squares on the other two sides, the triangle is right-angled — and the right angle is the one opposite the longest side.
ComparisonConclusion
a² + b² = c²right-angled
a² + b² > c²all angles acute
a² + b² < c²the largest angle is obtuse
Example 3Decide whether each triangle is right-angled: (a) sides 9, 40, 41  (b) sides 5, 7, 9.
1
(a) Pick out the longest side, 41. Square the other two and add. 9² + 40² = 81 + 1600 = 1681
2
Square the longest side and compare. 41² = 1681   ✓ equal
3
(b) Longest side is 9. 5² + 7² = 25 + 49 = 74
9² = 81
4
Here 74 < 81, so the two shorter sides fall short — the angle opposite the 9 has opened past 90°.
(a) right-angled, with the right angle opposite the side of 41    (b) not right-angled (obtuse)
Exam tip Write both lines out separately and finish with a sentence: “since 9² + 40² = 41², the triangle is right-angled”. The conclusion carries a mark of its own.

Pythagorean triples

A Pythagorean triple is a set of three whole numbers that fits the theorem exactly. Recognising them saves you time, and any multiple of a triple is also a triple.

Using Pythagoras in real problems

Most exam questions hide the triangle inside a picture. Your first job is always the same: draw the right-angled triangle on its own, mark the right angle and label the three sides.

Diagonal of a rectangle

Where the triangle hides

  • Ladders and ramps — the ladder is the hypotenuse.
  • Rectangles and cuboids — the diagonal is the hypotenuse.
  • Coordinates — go across, then up.
  • Isosceles triangles — drop a perpendicular to the base and it splits the base exactly in half.
  • Circles — the perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord, giving a radius–half-chord triangle.
Example 4A 5.2 m ladder leans against a vertical wall. Its foot is 1.8 m from the base of the wall. How far up the wall does the ladder reach?
1
The wall is vertical and the ground horizontal, so the triangle is right-angled. The ladder leans across the right angle, so the ladder is the hypotenuse. h² + 1.8² = 5.2²
2
Subtract, because we want a shorter side. h² = 27.04 − 3.24 = 23.8
3
Square root. h = 23.8 = 4.87852…
the ladder reaches 4.88 m up the wall (3 s.f.)
Note Keep the full value in your calculator if the question continues — for example if it then asks for the angle. Round only at the very end.

More worked examples

Example 5Find the distance between the points A(−3, 2) and B(5, 8).
1
Find the horizontal step. Subtract the x-coordinates. 5 − (−3) = 8
2
Find the vertical step. Subtract the y-coordinates. 8 − 2 = 6
3
Those two steps meet at a right angle, so AB is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle with legs 8 and 6. d² = 8² + 6² = 64 + 36 = 100
4
d = 100 = 10
AB = 10 units
Note Because both steps are squared, a negative step does no harm. That is why the distance formula d = (x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)² is just Pythagoras in disguise.
Example 6An isosceles triangle has two sides of 13 cm and a base of 10 cm. Find its perpendicular height and hence its area.
1
Drop the perpendicular from the apex. In an isosceles triangle it lands on the midpoint of the base, creating two identical right-angled triangles with base 10 ÷ 2 = 5 cm.
2
In one of those, the 13 cm side is the hypotenuse. h² + 5² = 13²
3
Subtract. h² = 169 − 25 = 144  →  h = 12
4
Now use ½ × base × height. area = ½ × 10 × 12 = 60
height = 12 cm, area = 60 cm²
Watch out Halve the base, not the sloping side. Splitting the 13 is the most common error here.
Example 7A circle has centre O and radius 10 cm. AB is a chord of length 16 cm. Find (a) the distance from O to the chord, (b) the shortest distance from the midpoint of AB to the circle.
1
The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects it, so each half is 16 ÷ 2 = 8 cm. Join O to B — that line is a radius, 10 cm, and it is the hypotenuse.
2
Apply the theorem to triangle OMB. d² + 8² = 10²
3
Subtract and square root. d² = 100 − 64 = 36  →  d = 6
4
(b) The shortest route from the midpoint to the circle runs straight on along the same line, and the full distance from O to the circle is the radius. 10 − 6 = 4
(a) 6 cm    (b) 4 cm
Exam tip Whenever a chord appears, draw the radius to one end and the perpendicular to its midpoint. That triangle solves almost every chord question in Paper 4.

Your turn

Practice set

  1. A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 9 cm and 12 cm. Find the hypotenuse.
  2. The hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle is 8.5 cm and one shorter side is 4 cm. Find the other shorter side.
  3. Is a triangle with sides 12 cm, 35 cm and 37 cm right-angled? Show your working.
  4. Find the distance between P(1, −4) and Q(7, 4).
  5. A rectangle measures 11 cm by 7 cm. Find the length of a diagonal, correct to 3 significant figures.
  6. (a) An isosceles triangle has equal sides 10 cm and base 12 cm. Find its area. (b) A chord of a circle of radius 8 cm is 6 cm from the centre. Find the length of the chord.

  1. 9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225 → 15 cm
  2. 8.5² − 4² = 72.25 − 16 = 56.25 → 7.5 cm (exact)
  3. 12² + 35² = 144 + 1225 = 1369 and 37² = 1369 → yes, right-angled (right angle opposite the 37)
  4. Steps 7 − 1 = 6 and 4 − (−4) = 8; 36 + 64 = 100 → 10 units
  5. 11² + 7² = 121 + 49 = 170; 170 = 13.038… → 13.0 cm
  6. (a) half base 6, h² = 100 − 36 = 64, h = 8, area = ½ × 12 × 8 → 48 cm²  ·  (b) half chord = 64 − 36 = 28 = 5.2915… → chord = 2 × 5.2915 = 10.6 cm (3 s.f.)

Key points to remember

  • Find the hypotenuse first — it is the side opposite the right angle, and it is always the longest.
  • a² + b² = c²: add to find the hypotenuse, subtract to find a shorter side.
  • Square the lengths before you add or subtract — never subtract the lengths themselves.
  • Give answers to 3 significant figures unless an exact or surd answer is asked for; do not round part-way through.
  • The converse: if a² + b² = c² the triangle is right-angled. Compare the two totals and write a conclusion.
  • Learn the triples 3,4,5  5,12,13  8,15,17  7,24,25 and their multiples.
  • In word problems, redraw the right-angled triangle on its own before calculating.
  • Two standard set-ups: the height of an isosceles triangle halves the base; the perpendicular from a centre bisects a chord.
