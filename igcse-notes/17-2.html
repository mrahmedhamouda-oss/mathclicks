Simple and compound interest, depreciation, hire purchase, loans and mortgages — and how to tell instantly which formula a question wants.

Simple interest

With simple interest the interest is worked out on the original amount every single year. The money invested (or borrowed) is called the principal, and it never changes, so the interest is the same amount each year.

I = P r t100 I = interest, P = principal, r = rate per year (%), t = time in years
Key idea The formula gives you the interest only. If a question asks for the total amount or the value of the investment, you must add the principal back on: A = P + I.

Because the formula has four letters, any one of them can be the unknown. Rearranged:

UnknownRearrangementIn words
Interest II = Prt ÷ 100multiply all three, divide by 100
Principal PP = 100I ÷ (rt)how much was invested
Rate rr = 100I ÷ (Pt)what percentage per year
Time tt = 100I ÷ (Pr)how many years
Watch out t must be in years. Nine months is t = 0.75; 18 months is t = 1.5. And r goes into the formula as the plain number 3.5, not as 0.035 — the ÷ 100 is already there.
Example 1$4500 is invested at 3.5% per year simple interest for 4 years. Find (a) the interest earned, (b) the value of the investment at the end.
1
Write down what each letter is worth. P = 4500,   r = 3.5,   t = 4
2
(a) Substitute into I = Prt ÷ 100. I = (4500 × 3.5 × 4) ÷ 100
I = 63 000 ÷ 100 = 630
3
(b) The value is principal plus interest. 4500.00 + 630.00 = 5130.00
(a) interest = $630.00    (b) value = $5130.00
Exam tip Sense-check it: one year’s interest is 0.035 × 4500 = 157.50, and 4 × 157.50 = 630. Same answer, and it shows you understand what simple interest means.

Compound interest

With compound interest the interest earned is added to the account, and next year’s interest is worked out on the new, larger amount. You earn interest on your interest, so the growth speeds up year after year.

V = P(1 + r100)n V = final value, P = principal, r = rate per year (%), n = number of years
Key idea This formula gives the final value, not the interest. To find the interest earned: interest = V − P. Read the question carefully — “how much is in the account” wants V; “how much interest” wants V − P.

The multiplier

An increase of r% is a multiplier of 1 + r100:

  • 3.5% → × 1.035
  • 7% → × 1.07
  • 12.5% → × 1.125

Compounding for n years just means using that multiplier n times, which is the power.

Simple or compound?

  • Simple: interest is a fixed amount every year; the graph is a straight line.
  • Compound: interest grows each year; the graph curves upwards.
  • Over 1 year they give exactly the same answer.
  • After that, compound always gives more — and the gap widens.
Example 2$4500 is invested at 3.5% per year compound interest for 4 years. Find the interest earned, and state how much better this is than the simple interest in Example 1.
1
Write the multiplier for a 3.5% increase. 1 + 3.5 ÷ 100 = 1.035
2
Substitute into V = P(1 + r/100)n with n = 4. V = 4500 × 1.0354
V = 4500 × 1.147523… = 5163.8535…
3
Round the value to 2 decimal places. V = $5163.85
4
Interest earned is the value minus the principal. 5163.85 − 4500.00 = $663.85
5
Compare with the simple interest of $630.00. 663.85 − 630.00 = $33.85 more
Interest = $663.85 — that is $33.85 more than simple interest
Watch out Keep the full value in your calculator until the very last step. If you round 1.0354 to 1.15 first, you get $675 and lose the accuracy mark.

Depreciation

Cars, machinery, computers and phones lose a percentage of their value each year. This is depreciation — compound interest running backwards.

V = P(1 − r100)n Exactly the same structure — the plus sign becomes a minus sign
Note A 15% loss means a multiplier of 1 − 0.15 = 0.85. Because the multiplier is less than 1, the value falls quickly at first and then more slowly — it never actually reaches zero.
Example 3A car is bought for $18 000. It depreciates by 15% each year. Find its value after 5 years, and the total loss in value.
1
Write the multiplier for a 15% decrease. 1 − 15 ÷ 100 = 0.85
2
Substitute into V = P(1 − r/100)n. V = 18 000 × 0.855
V = 18 000 × 0.443705… = 7986.6956…
3
Round to 2 decimal places. V = $7986.70
4
Loss in value = original − final. 18 000.00 − 7986.70 = $10 013.30
Value after 5 years = $7986.70; loss = $10 013.30
Common error Losing 15% a year for 5 years is not losing 75%. Repeated percentage change is never the same as adding the percentages up.

Hire purchase, loans and mortgages

Definition Hire purchase (HP) lets you take the goods now and pay in instalments. You pay a deposit first, then a fixed number of equal payments. The total paid is always more than the cash price — the difference is the cost of borrowing.
total HP price = deposit + (number of instalments × instalment) extra cost = total HP price − cash price

Deposits

Usually given as a percentage of the cash price. A 20% deposit on $1250 is 0.20 × 1250 = $250.00.

Loans

Money borrowed and repaid with interest, often monthly. Total repaid = monthly payment × number of months; the interest is that total minus the amount borrowed.

Mortgages

A very large loan to buy property, repaid over 20–30 years. Small differences in rate become enormous over that length of time.

Example: $150 000 repaid at $890 per month for 25 years costs 890 × 300 = $267 000 — that is $117 000 of interest.

Example 4A washing machine has a cash price of $1250. On hire purchase a customer pays a deposit of 20% plus 24 monthly instalments of $47.50. Find (a) the total hire purchase price, (b) the extra amount paid compared with the cash price, (c) that extra as a percentage of the cash price.
1
(a) Find the deposit — 20% of the cash price. 0.20 × 1250 = $250.00
2
Find the total of the instalments. 24 × 47.50 = $1140.00
3
Add them for the total HP price. 250.00 + 1140.00 = $1390.00
4
(b) Compare with the cash price. 1390.00 − 1250.00 = $140.00
5
(c) Write the extra as a percentage of the cash price. 1401250 × 100 = 11.2%
(a) $1390.00    (b) $140.00 extra    (c) 11.2%
Exam tip The percentage is always taken from the cash price — that is the amount the customer would have paid without borrowing.

More worked examples

Example 5$8000 is invested at 4.2% per year compound interest. Find the least number of complete years before the investment is worth more than $10 000.
1
Write the condition using the compound formula. 8000 × 1.042n > 10 000
2
There is no algebra needed — try values of n on your calculator and make a short table. n = 4:  8000 × 1.0424 = 9431.07
n = 5:  8000 × 1.0425 = 9827.17
3
Keep going until you pass $10 000. n = 6:  8000 × 1.0426 = 10 239.91  ✓
4
After 5 years it is still below $10 000, so 6 whole years are needed. n = 6
6 years
Exam tip Show at least the two values either side of the target — the one that fails and the one that works. That is what earns the method mark on this style of question.
Example 6A painting bought for $12 000 is worth $15 972.00 three years later. Its value has increased by the same percentage each year. Find that percentage.
1
Put the values into the compound formula, calling the multiplier k. 12 000 × k3 = 15 972
2
Divide both sides by 12 000. k3 = 15 972 ÷ 12 000 = 1.331
3
Take the cube root (the 3√ key). k = 3√1.331 = 1.1
4
A multiplier of 1.1 is an increase of 0.1, which is 10%. r = (1.1 − 1) × 100 = 10
10% per year
Note The same method works for depreciation: you would get a multiplier below 1, say 0.88, and the rate of depreciation would be (1 − 0.88) × 100 = 12% per year.
Example 7A machine costs $24 000 new. It loses 20% of its value in the first year and 12% of its value in each year after that. Find its value after 4 years, and express this as a percentage of the original cost.
1
Deal with the first year on its own — multiplier 0.8. 24 000 × 0.8 = $19 200.00
2
Years 2, 3 and 4 use a multiplier of 0.88, three times. 19 200 × 0.883
3
Evaluate, keeping the full accuracy. 0.883 = 0.681472
19 200 × 0.681472 = 13 084.2624
4
Round the money answer to 2 decimal places. V = $13 084.26
5
As a percentage of the original cost: 13 084.2624 000 × 100 = 54.517… = 54.5%
Value = $13 084.26, which is 54.5% (3 s.f.) of the original cost
Watch out The power is 3, not 4 — the first year has already been dealt with separately. Count the years the multiplier is actually applied.

Key points to remember

  • Simple interest: I = Prt ÷ 100 gives the interest only; add P for the total value.
  • Compound interest: V = P(1 + r/100)n gives the final value; subtract P for the interest earned.
  • Depreciation: V = P(1 − r/100)n — same formula, minus sign, multiplier below 1.
  • Compound beats simple over more than one year, and the gap grows with time. Over exactly one year they are identical.
  • Repeated percentage change never equals the percentages added together: 15% off five times is not 75% off.
  • To find n, try values on your calculator and show the value either side of the target. To find r, find the multiplier first, then use nth roots.
  • Hire purchase: total = deposit + instalments; the extra cost is measured against the cash price.
  • Keep full accuracy in the calculator and round only at the end — money answers to 2 decimal places.
