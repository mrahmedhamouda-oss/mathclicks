19.3  Symmetry Properties of Circles

The three symmetry results for chords and tangents, and how each one hands you a right-angled triangle to use with Pythagoras' theorem.

Chords Tangents Two tangents Examples Your turn Key points

The perpendicular from the centre to a chord

A circle has infinitely many lines of symmetry, and every one of them passes through the centre. Fold a circle along a diameter and the two halves match exactly. All three results in this lesson are consequences of that single fact.

Property 1 The perpendicular drawn from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord. Equivalently: the line from the centre to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to it, and the perpendicular bisector of any chord passes through the centre.

The right-angled triangle this creates

r² = d² + c2 2 radius r, distance from centre d, chord length c
Exam tip The instant you see a chord, draw the radius to one end of it and the perpendicular from the centre. That gives a right-angled triangle with sides r, d and c ÷ 2 — and Pythagoras finishes the job.
Watch out The hypotenuse is always the radius, because it faces the right angle. Writing d² = r² + (c÷2)² is the single most common error in this topic. Also, never put the full chord length into the triangle — only half of it.
Example 1A chord of length 24 cm is drawn in a circle of radius 13 cm. Find the distance of the chord from the centre.
1
Draw the perpendicular from the centre O to the chord AB, meeting it at M. It bisects the chord. AM = MB = 24 ÷ 2 = 12 cm
2
Triangle OMA is right-angled at M, with the radius as the hypotenuse. OM² + 12² = 13²
3
Rearrange and take the square root. OM² = 169 − 144 = 25
OM = √25 = 5
The chord is 5 cm from the centre
Check 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169 = 13² ✓ The 5, 12, 13 triple appears constantly in circle questions — along with 3, 4, 5 and 8, 15, 17.

Equal chords are equidistant from the centre

In a circle of radius 10 cm, both chords are 16 cm long, and both sit √(100 − 64) = 6 cm from the centre. The result works both ways: equal chords are equidistant from the centre, and chords equidistant from the centre are equal. The nearer a chord is to the centre, the longer it is — the longest chord of all is the diameter, at distance 0.

Example 2A chord of a circle is 12 cm long and 8 cm from the centre. Find (a) the radius, (b) the length of a chord that is 5 cm from the centre of the same circle.
1
(a) Half the chord is 12 ÷ 2 = 6 cm. Use Pythagoras with the radius as the hypotenuse. r² = 8² + 6² = 64 + 36 = 100
r = 10 cm
2
(b) Now the radius is known and the distance is 5 cm, so the unknown is the half-chord. (half)² = 10² − 5² = 100 − 25 = 75
half = √75 = 8.6602…
3
Double it for the full chord, and only round at the end. chord = 2 × 8.6602… = 17.3205…
(a) r = 10 cm     (b) chord = 17.3 cm (3 s.f.)
Watch out Do not round √75 to 8.66 and then double it — keep the full value in your calculator until the final line. Rounding early is worth a lost accuracy mark on Paper 2 and Paper 4 alike.

The tangent and the radius

A tangent is a straight line that touches a circle at exactly one point, called the point of contact. By symmetry, the tangent must sit square-on to the radius drawn to that point — if it leaned either way it would cut the circle twice.

Property 2 A tangent is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of contact. angle between tangent and radius = 90°

Tangent, radius, right angle

Exam tip When a diagram shows a tangent, mark the right angle at the point of contact before you do anything else. Nine times out of ten that right angle is the whole question: it turns the figure into a right-angled triangle, so Pythagoras or trigonometry can be used.
Example 3A circle has centre O and radius 8 cm. P is a point 17 cm from O, and PT is a tangent touching the circle at T. Find the length of PT.
1
Draw the radius OT. Because PT is a tangent, angle OTP = 90°, so triangle OTP is right-angled at T.
2
The hypotenuse is OP — it is the side opposite the right angle, not the radius this time. OT² + PT² = OP²
8² + PT² = 17²
3
Solve. PT² = 289 − 64 = 225
PT = √225 = 15
PT = 15 cm
Check 8² + 15² = 64 + 225 = 289 = 17² ✓

Two tangents from an external point

From a point outside a circle you can draw exactly two tangents. The whole figure is symmetrical about the line joining the external point to the centre — and that one line of symmetry gives you three results at once.

Property 3 If PA and PB are tangents from an external point P touching a circle, centre O, at A and B, then PA = PB, OP bisects angle APB and angle AOB, and triangles OAP and OBP are congruent right-angled triangles.

The tangent kite

Why the triangles are congruent

  • Right angle: OAP = OBP = 90° (tangent ⊥ radius)
  • Hypotenuse: OP is common to both
  • Side: OA = OB = r (both radii)

RHS congruence, so PA = PB and the two angles at P are equal.

The angle fact you will need

OAPB is a quadrilateral, so its angles add to 360°. Two of them are right angles, which leaves

angle AOB + angle APB = 180°

Know this and most tangent-angle questions are one line of arithmetic.

Example 4Two tangents from P touch a circle, centre O, at A and B. Angle APB = 48°. Find angle AOB, angle APO and angle AOP.
1
Angles of quadrilateral OAPB add to 360°, and the angles at A and B are right angles. angle AOB = 360° − 90° − 90° − 48° = 132°
2
OP bisects angle APB, by symmetry. angle APO = 48° ÷ 2 = 24°
3
In triangle OAP the angles add to 180°. angle AOP = 180° − 90° − 24° = 66°
angle AOB = 132°, angle APO = 24°, angle AOP = 66°
Check OP also bisects angle AOB: 132° ÷ 2 = 66° ✓ The two results agree, which is a good sign that the working is right.

More worked examples

Example 5A circle has radius 6 cm. A chord of the circle has length 9 cm. Find the distance of the chord from the centre, correct to 3 significant figures.
1
Halve the chord. 9 ÷ 2 = 4.5 cm
2
Right-angled triangle, radius as hypotenuse. d² + 4.5² = 6²
d² = 36 − 20.25 = 15.75
3
Square root, then round at the very end. d = √15.75 = 3.96862…
d = 3.97 cm (3 s.f.)
Exam tip Unless the question says otherwise, give a non-exact answer to 3 significant figures. If the question asks for an exact answer, leave it in surd form: √15.75 = 3√72.
Example 6In a circle of radius 10 cm, two parallel chords have lengths 12 cm and 16 cm. Find the distance between the chords when they are (a) on opposite sides of the centre, (b) on the same side of the centre.
1
Distance of the 12 cm chord: half of it is 6 cm. d₁² = 10² − 6² = 100 − 36 = 64  →  d₁ = 8 cm
2
Distance of the 16 cm chord: half of it is 8 cm. d₂² = 10² − 8² = 100 − 64 = 36  →  d₂ = 6 cm
3
(a) On opposite sides, the centre lies between them, so add the distances. 8 + 6 = 14 cm
4
(b) On the same side, both are measured from the centre in the same direction, so subtract. 8 − 6 = 2 cm
(a) 14 cm     (b) 2 cm
Watch out A question that just says “two parallel chords” has two answers. Read carefully, and if the diagram is given, use it to decide which case applies. Notice also that the longer chord (16 cm) is the one nearer the centre.
Example 7Tangents from a point P touch a circle of radius 7 cm, centre O, at A and B. OP = 25 cm. Find (a) the length PA, (b) the perimeter of quadrilateral OAPB, (c) its area, (d) angle APB.
1
(a) Angle OAP = 90°, so triangle OAP is right-angled with OP as hypotenuse. PA² = 25² − 7² = 625 − 49 = 576
PA = √576 = 24 cm
2
(b) PB = PA = 24 cm and OB = OA = 7 cm, so OAPB is a kite. perimeter = 2(7 + 24) = 62 cm
3
(c) The kite is made of two congruent right-angled triangles with legs 7 cm and 24 cm. area = 2 × ½ × 7 × 24 = 168 cm²
4
(d) In triangle OAP, the side opposite angle APO is the radius, and the hypotenuse is OP. sin(APO) = 7 ÷ 25 = 0.28
angle APO = 16.2602…°
5
OP bisects angle APB, so double it. angle APB = 2 × 16.2602… = 32.520…°
(a) 24 cm   (b) 62 cm   (c) 168 cm²   (d) 32.5° (3 s.f.)
Check 7² + 24² = 49 + 576 = 625 = 25² ✓ The 7, 24, 25 triple joins 3, 4, 5 · 5, 12, 13 · 8, 15, 17 as one worth recognising instantly.

Your turn

Practice set

  1. A chord of length 30 cm is drawn in a circle of radius 17 cm. How far is it from the centre?
  2. A chord of length 24 cm is 9 cm from the centre of a circle. Find the radius.
  3. PT is a tangent to a circle of radius 5 cm with centre O, and OP = 13 cm. Find PT.
  4. Two tangents from P touch a circle, centre O, at A and B, and angle AOB = 116°. Find angle APB and angle OPA.
  5. A circle has radius 9 cm and P is 14 cm from the centre. Find the length of a tangent from P, correct to 3 significant figures.
  6. AB is a chord of length 16 cm in a circle of radius 10 cm, with midpoint M. Find (a) OM, (b) the shortest distance from M to the circumference.

  1. Half-chord 15; d² = 17² − 15² = 289 − 225 = 64 → d = 8 cm
  2. Half-chord 12; r² = 9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225 → r = 15 cm
  3. Right angle at T; PT² = 13² − 5² = 169 − 25 = 144 → PT = 12 cm
  4. angle APB = 360° − 90° − 90° − 116° = 64°; OP bisects it → angle OPA = 32°
  5. t² = 14² − 9² = 196 − 81 = 115; t = √115 = 10.7238… → 10.7 cm (3 s.f.)
  6. (a) half-chord 8, OM² = 100 − 64 = 36 → OM = 6 cm  (b) continue OM to the circumference: 10 − 6 = 4 cm

Key points to remember

  • The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord: use r² = d² + (c ÷ 2)² with the radius as hypotenuse.
  • The perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre — two chords locate the centre of any circle.
  • Equal chords are equidistant from the centre, and the nearer a chord is to the centre the longer it is; the diameter is the longest chord.
  • A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact. Mark that right angle immediately.
  • The two tangents from an external point are equal, and the line from the centre to that point bisects both the angle between the tangents and the angle at the centre.
  • Triangles OAP and OBP are congruent by RHS, so OAPB is a kite with area r × (tangent length).
  • In that kite, angle AOB + angle APB = 180°.
  • Keep full accuracy through the working and round only the final answer — 3 significant figures unless the question says otherwise.
