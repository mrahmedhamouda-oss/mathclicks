Question 1  [2 marks]
(a)Write down the number of lines of symmetry of this trapezium.
  1. A line of symmetry is a mirror line: folding along it makes the two halves match exactly.
  2. Only the vertical line through the midpoints of the two parallel sides does this; a horizontal fold does not, because the parallel sides have different lengths.
  3. Number of lines of symmetry = 1
(b)Write down the order of rotational symmetry of this trapezium.
  1. Turn the trapezium about its centre through a full turn and count the positions in which it looks the same.
  2. Only the starting position works, because a half turn swaps the long and short parallel sides.
  3. Order of rotational symmetry = 1 — every shape maps onto itself after a full turn

Question 2  [3 marks]
(a)Write down the number of lines of symmetry of a rhombus.
  1. A rhombus has four equal sides, and its two diagonals are its mirror lines.
  2. A fold along either diagonal makes the two halves match. — the sides of the rhombus are all equal
  3. Number of lines of symmetry = 2 — the sides themselves are not mirror lines
(b)Write down the order of rotational symmetry of a parallelogram.
  1. A parallelogram (not a rectangle or rhombus) has no line of symmetry, but it does have point symmetry about the point where the diagonals cross.
  2. A half turn about that point maps each side onto the opposite side.
  3. Order of rotational symmetry = 2 — it looks the same after 180° and after 360°
(c)Write down the name of a quadrilateral that has exactly one line of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order 1.
  1. A kite has one pair of equal opposite angles and one mirror line — the long diagonal.
  2. Turning a kite through a half turn does not map it onto itself.
  3. A kite — an isosceles trapezium is also acceptable

Question 3  [3 marks]
(a)Write down the number of lines of symmetry of a regular hexagon.
  1. A regular polygon with n sides has n lines of symmetry.
  2. For the hexagon these are the 3 lines through opposite vertices and the 3 lines through the midpoints of opposite sides.
  3. Number of lines of symmetry = 6 — 3 + 3
(b)Write down the order of rotational symmetry of a regular hexagon.
  1. Each turn of 360 ÷ 6 = 60° maps the hexagon onto itself.
  2. Order of rotational symmetry = 6
(c)A different regular polygon has 9 lines of symmetry.
Write down its order of rotational symmetry.
  1. For any regular polygon the number of lines of symmetry and the order of rotational symmetry are both equal to the number of sides.
  2. 9 lines of symmetry means the polygon is a regular 9‑sided polygon.
  3. Order of rotational symmetry = 9

Question 4  [4 marks]
(a)Write down the number of lines of symmetry of the shape.
  1. The shape folds onto itself about a vertical mirror and about a horizontal mirror.
  2. A fold along either diagonal does not work, because the shape is not as tall as it is wide in the same way.
  3. Number of lines of symmetry = 2
(b)Write down the equation of each line of symmetry.
  1. The shape stretches from x = 1 to x = 7, so the vertical mirror is halfway, at x = (1 + 7) ÷ 2 = 4.
  2. It stretches from y = 0 to y = 6, so the horizontal mirror is at y = (0 + 6) ÷ 2 = 3.
  3. The lines of symmetry are x = 4 and y = 3
(c)Write down the order of rotational symmetry of the shape.
  1. The two mirror lines cross at (4, 3), and a half turn about that point maps the shape onto itself.
  2. A quarter turn does not, because the shape is 6 wide and 6 high but the arms point up and down only.
  3. Order of rotational symmetry = 2

Question 5  [4 marks]
(a)Write down the number of lines of symmetry of the pentagon.
  1. Each line of symmetry passes through one vertex and the midpoint of the opposite side.
  2. A regular polygon with n sides has n lines of symmetry.
  3. Number of lines of symmetry = 5
(b)Work out angle AOB.
  1. The five angles at the centre are equal, because the pentagon has rotational symmetry of order 5.
  2. Angle AOB = 360 ÷ 5 = 72°
(c)Work out the size of one interior angle of the pentagon.
  1. Triangle OAB is isosceles with OA = OB. — both join the centre to a vertex
  2. Angle OAB = (180 − 72) ÷ 2 = 54° — angle sum of a triangle
  3. The interior angle at A is made of two of these equal angles, one from triangle OAB and one from triangle OAE. — by the line of symmetry through A
  4. Interior angle = 2 × 54 = 108° — check: (5 − 2) × 180 ÷ 5 = 540 ÷ 5 = 108 ✓

Question 6  [4 marks]
(a)Write down the equation of the line of symmetry of the curve.
  1. Complete the square: x2 − 6x + 5 = (x − 3)2 − 9 + 5 — half of −6 is −3
  2. y = (x − 3)2 − 4
  3. The squared bracket is unchanged when x is the same distance either side of 3.
  4. Line of symmetry: x = 3
  5. Check: the curve cuts the x‑axis where (x − 1)(x − 5) = 0, at x = 1 and x = 5, and 3 is halfway between them ✓
(b)Find the coordinates of the minimum point of the curve.
  1. From y = (x − 3)2 − 4, the smallest value of (x − 3)2 is 0. — a square can never be negative
  2. This happens when x = 3, giving y = −4. — on the line of symmetry
  3. Minimum point = (3, −4)

Question 7  [4 marks]
(a)Write down the number of planes of symmetry of this cuboid.
  1. Three planes cut the cuboid in half parallel to a pair of faces. — one for each pair of opposite faces
  2. Because the cross‑section is a square, there are 2 more planes through the diagonals of the square faces. — these would not exist if all three lengths were different
  3. Number of planes of symmetry = 3 + 2 = 5
(b)Write down the order of rotational symmetry of this cuboid about the axis through the centres of the two square faces.
  1. The square face maps onto itself after each quarter turn.
  2. Turning the cuboid through 90°, 180°, 270° and 360° about this axis gives the same solid each time.
  3. Order of rotational symmetry = 4
(c)Write down the number of planes of symmetry of a square‑based pyramid.
  1. Each plane of symmetry must contain the apex.
  2. Two planes pass through the midpoints of opposite edges of the square base and two pass through opposite corners of the base.
  3. Number of planes of symmetry = 4 — the same as the number of lines of symmetry of the square base
(d)A prism has a cross‑section that is an equilateral triangle.
Write down the number of planes of symmetry of this prism.
  1. The equilateral triangle cross‑section has 3 lines of symmetry, and each gives a vertical plane of symmetry of the prism.
  2. There is also one horizontal plane halfway along the prism, parallel to the triangular ends.
  3. Number of planes of symmetry = 3 + 1 = 4

Question 8  [4 marks]
(a)Write down the coordinates of the image of P after a rotation of 90° anticlockwise about O.
  1. A 90° anticlockwise rotation about the origin sends (x, y) to (−y, x).
  2. (3, 1) → (−1, 3)
  3. Image = (−1, 3) — check: both points are √10 from O ✓
(b)Write down the coordinates of the other two vertices of the shape that correspond to P.
  1. Rotational symmetry of order 4 means the vertex repeats every 90°.
  2. A further 90°: (−1, 3) → (−3, −1) — this is the half turn of P
  3. A further 90°: (−3, −1) → (1, −3)
  4. The other two vertices are (−3, −1) and (1, −3)
(c)The shape also has the line y = x as a line of symmetry.
Write down the coordinates of the image of P after reflection in y = x.
  1. Reflection in y = x swaps the coordinates: (x, y) → (y, x).
  2. (3, 1) → (1, 3)

Question 9  [5 marks]
(a)Show that OM = 8 cm.
  1. The circle is symmetrical about the line OMC, so this line bisects the chord AB at right angles. — the perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord
  2. AM = 12 ÷ 2 = 6 cm and angle OMA = 90°.
  3. In right‑angled triangle OMA: OM2 + 62 = 102 — Pythagoras’ theorem, with OA = 10 cm a radius
  4. OM2 = 100 − 36 = 64, so OM = √64 = 8 cm
(b)Calculate the area of triangle OAB.
  1. Take AB as the base of triangle OAB; the perpendicular height is OM.
  2. Area = 1⁄2 × base × height = 1⁄2 × 12 × 8
  3. Area = 48 cm2
(c)Write down the length of MC.
  1. OC is a radius, so OC = 10 cm.
  2. MC = OC − OM = 10 − 8 = 2 cm — M lies between O and C

Question 10  [6 marks]
(a)Write down the order of rotational symmetry of this polygon.
  1. For a regular polygon, the number of lines of symmetry equals the number of sides, and equals the order of rotational symmetry.
  2. 15 lines of symmetry means 15 sides.
  3. Order of rotational symmetry = 15
(b)Calculate the size of one interior angle of the polygon.
  1. The exterior angles add up to 360°, so each exterior angle is 360 ÷ 15 = 24°.
  2. Interior angle = 180 − 24 = 156° — interior and exterior angles are on a straight line
  3. Check: (15 − 2) × 180 ÷ 15 = 2340 ÷ 15 = 156 ✓
(c)Calculate angle OAB.
  1. Joining O to every vertex makes 15 congruent isosceles triangles, so angle AOB = 360 ÷ 15 = 24°. — by the rotational symmetry of order 15
  2. Triangle OAB is isosceles with OA = OB.
  3. Angle OAB = (180 − 24) ÷ 2 = 78° — angle sum of a triangle; it is also half of the interior angle, 156 ÷ 2 = 78 ✓
(d)A prism has this polygon as its cross‑section.
Write down the number of planes of symmetry of the prism.
  1. Each of the 15 lines of symmetry of the cross‑section gives a plane of symmetry at right angles to the ends.
  2. There is one more plane, halfway along the prism and parallel to its ends.
  3. Number of planes of symmetry = 15 + 1 = 16
