Question 2  [3 marks]
(a)Write down the number of lines of symmetry of a rhombus.
  1. A rhombus has four equal sides, and its two diagonals are its mirror lines.
  2. A fold along either diagonal makes the two halves match. — the sides of the rhombus are all equal
  3. Number of lines of symmetry = 2 — the sides themselves are not mirror lines
(b)Write down the order of rotational symmetry of a parallelogram.
  1. A parallelogram (not a rectangle or rhombus) has no line of symmetry, but it does have point symmetry about the point where the diagonals cross.
  2. A half turn about that point maps each side onto the opposite side.
  3. Order of rotational symmetry = 2 — it looks the same after 180° and after 360°
(c)Write down the name of a quadrilateral that has exactly one line of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order 1.
  1. A kite has one pair of equal opposite angles and one mirror line — the long diagonal.
  2. Turning a kite through a half turn does not map it onto itself.
  3. A kite — an isosceles trapezium is also acceptable

Question 3  [3 marks]
(a)Write down the number of lines of symmetry of a regular hexagon.
  1. A regular polygon with n sides has n lines of symmetry.

(b)Write down the order of rotational symmetry of a regular hexagon.

  1. For any regular polygon the number of lines of symmetry and the order of rotational symmetry are both equal to the number of sides.
  2. 9 lines of symmetry means the polygon is a regular 9‑sided polygon.

  1. The circle is symmetrical about the line OMC, so this line bisects the chord AB at right angles. — the perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord

Question 10  [6 marks]
(a)Write down the order of rotational symmetry of this polygon.
  1. For a regular polygon, the number of lines of symmetry equals the number of sides, and equals the order of rotational symmetry.
  2. 15 lines of symmetry means 15 sides.
  3. Order of rotational symmetry = 15
(b)Calculate the size of one interior angle of the polygon.
  1. The exterior angles add up to 360°, so each exterior angle is 360 ÷ 15 = 24°.
  2. Interior angle = 180 − 24 = 156° — interior and exterior angles are on a straight line
  3. Check: (15 − 2) × 180 ÷ 15 = 2340 ÷ 15 = 156 ✓

  1. Each of the 15 lines of symmetry of the cross‑section gives a plane of symmetry at right angles to the ends.

Question 25  [6 marks]
(a)Write down the equation of the line of symmetry.
  1. The line of symmetry is the perpendicular bisector of AB (and of DC).
  2. The midpoint of AB has x = (−3 + 7) ÷ 2 = 2, and the line is vertical.
  3. Line of symmetry: x = 2
(b)Find the coordinates of D.
  1. D is the reflection of C in the line x = 2. — the mirror swaps A, B and swaps C, D
  2. C is 5 − 2 = 3 units to the right of the line, so D is 3 units to the left: x = −1.
  3. D = (−1, 4) — check: AD = BC = √(22 + 42) ✓
(c)Calculate the area of the trapezium.
  1. Parallel sides: AB = 7 − (−3) = 10 and DC = 5 − (−1) = 6; the height is 4.
  2. Area = 1⁄2(10 + 6) × 4 — area of a trapezium = ½(a + b)h
  3. Area = 32 units2
(d)Calculate the length of BC. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. From B(7, 0) to C(5, 4) is 2 across and 4 up.
  2. BC = √(22 + 42) = √20 = 4.47 units (3 s.f.) — Pythagoras’ theorem
