Question 1  [2 marks]

Question 2  [3 marks]
(a)Write 108 as a product of its prime factors.
  1. Divide by prime numbers, smallest first.
  2. 108 ÷ 2 = 54, 54 ÷ 2 = 27, 27 ÷ 3 = 9, 9 ÷ 3 = 3, 3 ÷ 3 = 1
  3. 108 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 22 × 33 — collect equal primes as powers
  4. Check: 4 × 27 = 108 ✓
(b)Write down the square number that is a factor of 108 and is greater than 30.
  1. The factors of 108 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 27, 36, 54, 108.
  2. The square numbers among them are 1, 4, 9 and 36. — 1 = 12, 4 = 22, 9 = 32, 36 = 62
  3. The only one greater than 30 is 36.

Question 3  [3 marks]
(a)Find the highest common factor (HCF) of 24 and 36.
  1. For the HCF take the lower power of each prime that appears in both.
  2. 24 = 23 × 3 and 36 = 22 × 32, so use 22 and 31.
  3. HCF = 22 × 3 = 12 — 12 divides both 24 and 36
(b)Find the lowest common multiple (LCM) of 24 and 36.
  1. For the LCM take the higher power of every prime that appears.
  2. LCM = 23 × 32 = 8 × 9
  3. LCM = 72 — 72 = 3 × 24 = 2 × 36, so it is a multiple of both
  4. Check: HCF × LCM = 12 × 72 = 864 = 24 × 36 ✓

Question 4  [3 marks]
(a)Find the next time that a bus to Marina and a bus to Riverside leave the station at the same time.
  1. The buses leave together after a number of minutes that is a multiple of both 12 and 18.
  2. Multiples of 12: 12, 24, 36, 48, …  Multiples of 18: 18, 36, 54, …
  3. LCM(12, 18) = 36, so they next leave together 36 minutes after 08 00. — the lowest common multiple is the first time the two patterns meet
  4. The time is 08 36.
(b)Work out how many more times before 10 00 the two buses leave the station at the same time.
  1. They leave together every 36 minutes: 08 36, 09 12, 09 48, 10 24, …
  2. From 08 00 to 10 00 is 120 minutes, and 36, 72 and 108 are the multiples of 36 that are less than 120.
  3. This happens 3 more times. — 10 24 is after 10 00

Question 5  [4 marks]
(a)Write 300 as a product of its prime factors.
  1. Divide 300 by prime numbers, smallest first.
  2. 300 ÷ 2 = 150, 150 ÷ 2 = 75, 75 ÷ 3 = 25, 25 ÷ 5 = 5, 5 ÷ 5 = 1
  3. 300 = 22 × 3 × 52
  4. Check: 4 × 3 × 25 = 300 ✓
(b)Find the smallest positive integer n such that 540n is a cube number.
  1. A number is a cube number exactly when every index in its prime factorisation is a multiple of 3.
  2. 540 = 22 × 33 × 51
  3. The index of 2 must rise from 2 to 3, and the index of 5 from 1 to 3, so one more 2 and two more 5s are needed. — the index of 3 is already 3, a multiple of 3
  4. n = 2 × 52 = 50
  5. Check: 540 × 50 = 27 000 = 303 ✓

Question 6  [4 marks]
(a)Find the highest common factor (HCF) of P and Q.
  1. For the HCF, use only the primes that appear in both, each to the lower power.
  2. 2: lower of 23 and 22 is 22.  3: lower of 32 and 31 is 3.  5: lower of 51 and 52 is 5.  7 appears in Q only, so it is not used.
  3. HCF = 22 × 3 × 5 = 60
(b)Find the lowest common multiple (LCM) of P and Q. Give your answer as a product of prime factors.

  2. 23, 32, 52 and 7.

Question 8  [4 marks]
(a)Two numbers have a highest common factor of 8 and a lowest common multiple of 120. One of the numbers is 24.
Find the other number.
  1. HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers.
  2. 8 × 120 = 24 × (the other number)
  3. 960 = 24 × (the other number), so the other number = 960 ÷ 24 = 40
  4. Check: HCF(24, 40) = 8 and LCM(24, 40) = 120 ✓
(b)Two different numbers have a highest common factor of 6 and a lowest common multiple of 90.
Neither number is 6 and neither number is 90.
Find the two numbers.
  1. Both numbers are multiples of 6, so write them as 6a and 6b with a and b having no common factor. — 6 is the highest common factor
  2. 6a × 6b = 6 × 90 = 540, so ab = 15. — HCF × LCM = product of the numbers
  3. ab = 15 gives a = 1, b = 15 (the numbers 6 and 90, not allowed) or a = 3, b = 5.
  4. The two numbers are 18 and 30.
  5. Check: HCF(18, 30) = 6 and LCM(18, 30) = 90 ✓

Question 9  [5 marks]
(a)Find the largest possible length of the side of a tile. Give your answer in centimetres.
  1. Work in centimetres: the floor is 450 cm by 360 cm. — 4.5 m = 450 cm
  2. A tile of side d cm fits exactly only if d divides both 450 and 360, so the largest tile has side HCF(450, 360).
  3. 450 = 2 × 32 × 52 and 360 = 23 × 32 × 5
  4. HCF = 2 × 32 × 5 = 90 cm
(b)Work out the number of tiles needed.
  1. Along the 450 cm side: 450 ÷ 90 = 5 tiles.
  2. Along the 360 cm side: 360 ÷ 90 = 4 tiles.
  3. Number of tiles = 5 × 4 = 20
  4. Check: 20 × 90 × 90 = 162 000 cm2 = 450 × 360 ✓
(c)These tiles cost $7.25 each.
Work out the total cost of the tiles.
  1. Total cost = 20 × $7.25
  2. Total cost = $145

Question 10  [6 marks]
(a)Write 1176 as a product of its prime factors.
  1. 1176 = 8 × 147, and 8 = 23, 147 = 3 × 49 = 3 × 72.
  2. 1176 = 23 × 3 × 72
  3. Check: 8 × 3 × 49 = 1176 ✓
(b)Find the smallest positive integer m such that 1176m is a square number.
  1. A number is a square number exactly when every index in its prime factorisation is even.
  2. In 23 × 31 × 72 the indices of 2 and of 3 are odd; the index of 7 is already even.
  3. One more 2 and one more 3 are needed, so m = 2 × 3 = 6
  4. Check: 1176 × 6 = 7056 = 842 ✓
(c)Find the smallest positive integer k such that 1176k is a cube number.
  1. A number is a cube number exactly when every index is a multiple of 3.
  2. The index of 2 is 3 ✓; the index of 3 must rise from 1 to 3; the index of 7 must rise from 2 to 3.
  3. k = 32 × 7 = 63
  4. Check: 1176 × 63 = 74 088 = 423 ✓
