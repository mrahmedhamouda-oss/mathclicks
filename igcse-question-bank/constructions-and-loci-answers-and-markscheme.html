Question 2  [3 marks]
(a)Using a straight edge and compasses only, construct the locus of the points that are equidistant from A and from B. Leave in all your construction arcs.
  1. Open the compasses to more than half of AB and keep that radius fixed. — the radius must be more than 4 cm so that the arcs cross
  2. With the compass point on A, draw arcs above and below AB.
  3. With the same radius and the compass point on B, draw two more arcs crossing the first pair.
  4. Join the two crossing points with a ruler — this is the required locus — every point of that line is the same distance from A as from B
(b)Write down the mathematical name of this locus.
  1. The line passes through the midpoint of AB and meets AB at 90°.
  2. The locus is the perpendicular bisector of AB

Question 3  [3 marks]
(a)Using a straight edge and compasses only, construct the bisector of angle PQR. Leave in all your construction arcs.
  1. Put the compass point on Q and draw one arc that crosses both QP and QR. Call these crossings X and Y.
  2. QX = QY, because they are radii of the same arc.
  3. With the compasses open to a fixed radius, draw an arc from X and an equal arc from Y, meeting at a point Z inside the angle.
  4. Join Q to Z with a ruler — this is the bisector of angle PQR — triangles QXZ and QYZ are congruent (SSS), so the two angles at Q are equal
(b)Write down the size of the angle between QP and this bisector.
  1. The bisector cuts the 76° angle into two equal parts.
  2. Angle between QP and the bisector = 76 ÷ 2 = 38°

Question 4  [4 marks]
(a)Show that the perimeter of this boundary is (24 + 10π) m.
  1. The ring slides along the whole fence, so the boundary has two straight parts, each parallel to AB and 5 m from it.
  2. Straight parts: 2 × 12 = 24 m — each has the same length as the fence
  3. At each end the goat sweeps a semicircle of radius 5 m, centred on A and on B.
  4. Curved parts: 2 × 1⁄2 × 2 × π × 5 = 10π m — two semicircles make one whole circle of radius 5 m
  5. Perimeter = (24 + 10π) m as required — this is 55.4 m correct to 3 significant figures
(b)Calculate the total area of the field the goat can reach. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. The region is a rectangle 12 m by 10 m together with two semicircles of radius 5 m. — the rectangle is 2 × 5 = 10 m wide
  2. Rectangle: 12 × 10 = 120 m2
  3. Two semicircles = one circle: π × 52 = 25π = 78.539… m2
  4. Total = 120 + 25π = 198.539…
  5. Area = 199 m2 (3 s.f.) — exact value (120 + 25π) m2

Question 5  [4 marks]
(a)Using a ruler and compasses only, construct triangle ABC accurately in the space below. Start by drawing the line AB of length 8 cm. Leave in all your construction arcs.
  1. Draw AB = 8 cm accurately with a ruler.
  2. Open the compasses to 7 cm, put the point on A and draw an arc above AB. — every point of that arc is 7 cm from A
  3. Open the compasses to 6 cm, put the point on B and draw a second arc crossing the first. — every point of that arc is 6 cm from B
  4. The crossing point is C, because it is 7 cm from A and 6 cm from B at the same time.
  5. Join AC and BC to complete the required triangle, leaving both arcs on the diagram.
(b)Calculate angle ACB. Give your answer correct to 1 decimal place.
  1. Angle ACB is between the sides CA = 7 and CB = 6, opposite the side AB = 8. — so use the cosine rule in the ‘find an angle’ form
  2. cos C = 72 + 62 − 82⁄2 × 7 × 6
  3. cos C = 49 + 36 − 64⁄84 = 21⁄84 = 0.25
  4. C = cos−1(0.25) = 75.522…
  5. Angle ACB = 75.5° (1 d.p.) — it is the largest angle, opposite the longest side, so a value below 90° is reasonable here

Question 6  [4 marks]
(a)Describe fully the locus of the points inside the garden that are equidistant from AB and from AD.
  1. AB and AD are two straight lines meeting at the corner A.
  2. Points equidistant from two intersecting lines lie on the bisector of the angle between them.
  3. Angle DAB = 90°, so the bisector makes an angle of 45° with AB. — half of the right angle at A
  4. The locus is the bisector of angle DAB — the straight line from A at 45° to AB, ending where it meets DC.
(b)Calculate the length of this locus. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. Take A as the origin, with AB along the horizontal.
  2. The locus is the line at 45°, so it rises 1 m for every 1 m across.
  3. It reaches the height of DC (18 m) after travelling 18 m across, and 18 < 24, so it leaves the garden through DC, not through BC. — this is why the length uses 18 twice
  4. Length = √(182 + 182) = √648 = 18√2 = 25.455… — Pythagoras' theorem on a right-angled isosceles triangle
  5. Length = 25.5 m (3 s.f.) — exact value 18√2 m

Question 7  [4 marks]
(a)Using a straight edge and compasses only, construct the locus of the points that are equidistant from PQ and from PR. Leave in all your construction arcs.
  1. ‘The same distance from PQ as from PR’ means equidistant from two straight lines, so the locus is an angle bisector.
  2. Put the compass point on P and draw one arc crossing PQ and PR.
  3. From each crossing point, draw two arcs of equal radius that meet inside the triangle.
  4. Rule the line from P through that meeting point — this is the bisector of angle QPR
(b)Using a straight edge and compasses only, construct the locus of the points that are equidistant from Q and from R, and mark the position of the well with the letter W.
  1. ‘The same distance from Q as from R’ means equidistant from two points, so the locus is a perpendicular bisector.
  2. With the compasses open to more than half of QR, draw arcs from Q and equal arcs from R, above and below QR.
  3. Rule the line through the two crossing points — the perpendicular bisector of QR.
  4. W is where the two loci cross, since only there are both conditions true at the same time.

Question 8  [4 marks]
(a)A path is built inside the park so that every point on it is the same distance from AB as it is from DC. Write down the distance of the path from AB, and the length of the path.
  1. AB and DC are the two long sides, 100 m apart. — BC = 100 m is the distance between them
  2. Points the same distance from both lie halfway between them.
  3. Distance from AB = 100 ÷ 2 = 50 m
  4. The path runs the whole way across the park, parallel to AB, so its length = 160 m — it is the same length as AB
(b)A dog is attached to the corner A by a lead of length 40 m. Calculate the area of the park that the dog can reach. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. The dog can reach every point within 40 m of A, so the boundary is an arc of a circle of radius 40 m, centre A.
  2. The two sides at A meet at 90°, and 40 < 100 and 40 < 160, so the region is exactly a quarter circle inside the park. — the arc does not reach either B or D
  3. Area = 1⁄4 × π × 402 = 400π = 1256.6…
  4. Area = 1260 m2 (3 s.f.) — exact value 400π m2

Question 10  [6 marks]
(a)Calculate the area of the hall that the camera at A can see. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. The camera sees every point within 24 m of the corner A.
  2. The walls AB and AD meet at 90°, and AD = BC = 24 m, so the arc just reaches D and the region is a quarter circle. — 24 ≤ 24 and 24 < 40, so the quarter circle fits inside the hall
  3. Area = 1⁄4 × π × 242 = 144π = 452.389…
  4. Area = 452 m2 (3 s.f.) — exact value 144π m2
(b)Calculate the area of the hall that the camera at B can see. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. The same argument applies at B, with radius 15 m.
  2. 15 < 24 and 15 < 40, so this region is also a quarter circle.
  3. Area = 1⁄4 × π × 152 = 56.25π = 176.714…
  4. Area = 177 m2 (3 s.f.) — exact value 56.25π m2
(c)Show that the two regions do not overlap.
  1. A point seen by the camera at A is less than 24 m from A; a point seen by the camera at B is less than 15 m from B.
  2. If a point were in both regions, its distance from A plus its distance from B would be less than 24 + 15 = 39 m.
  3. But AB = 40 m, and by the triangle inequality that sum is at least 40 m. — the shortest route from A to B is the straight line AB
  4. 39 < 40, so no point can lie in both regions — they do not overlap.
(d)Calculate the area of the hall that neither camera can see. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. Area of the hall = 40 × 24 = 960 m2
  2. Because the regions do not overlap, the covered area is simply 144π + 56.25π = 200.25π = 629.103… m2
  3. Uncovered area = 960 − 629.103… = 330.896…
  4. Area = 331 m2 (3 s.f.) — exact value (960 − 200.25π) m2
