Question 3  [3 marks]
(a)Using a straight edge and compasses only, construct the bisector of angle PQR. Leave in all your construction arcs.
  1. Put the compass point on Q and draw one arc that crosses both QP and QR. Call these crossings X and Y.
  2. QX = QY, because they are radii of the same arc.
  3. With the compasses open to a fixed radius, draw an arc from X and an equal arc from Y, meeting at a point Z inside the angle.
  4. Join Q to Z with a ruler — this is the bisector of angle PQR — triangles QXZ and QYZ are congruent (SSS), so the two angles at Q are equal
(b)Write down the size of the angle between QP and this bisector.
  1. The bisector cuts the 76° angle into two equal parts.
  2. Angle between QP and the bisector = 76 ÷ 2 = 38°

Question 6  [4 marks]
(a)Describe fully the locus of the points inside the garden that are equidistant from AB and from AD.
  1. AB and AD are two straight lines meeting at the corner A.
  2. Points equidistant from two intersecting lines lie on the bisector of the angle between them.
  3. Angle DAB = 90°, so the bisector makes an angle of 45° with AB. — half of the right angle at A
  4. The locus is the bisector of angle DAB — the straight line from A at 45° to AB, ending where it meets DC.

  5. Length = 25.5 m (3 s.f.) — exact value 18√2 m

Question 10  [6 marks]
(a)Calculate the area of the hall that the camera at A can see. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. The camera sees every point within 24 m of the corner A.
  2. The walls AB and AD meet at 90°, and AD = BC = 24 m, so the arc just reaches D and the region is a quarter circle. — 24 ≤ 24 and 24 < 40, so the quarter circle fits inside the hall
  3. Area = 1⁄4 × π × 242 = 144π = 452.389…
  4. Area = 452 m2 (3 s.f.) — exact value 144π m2
(b)Calculate the area of the hall that the camera at B can see. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. The same argument applies at B, with radius 15 m.
  2. 15 < 24 and 15 < 40, so this region is also a quarter circle.
  3. Area = 1⁄4 × π × 152 = 56.25π = 176.714…
  4. Area = 177 m2 (3 s.f.) — exact value 56.25π m2
(c)Show that the two regions do not overlap.
  1. A point seen by the camera at A is less than 24 m from A; a point seen by the camera at B is less than 15 m from B.
  2. If a point were in both regions, its distance from A plus its distance from B would be less than 24 + 15 = 39 m.
  3. But AB = 40 m, and by the triangle inequality that sum is at least 40 m. — the shortest route from A to B is the straight line AB
  4. 39 < 40, so no point can lie in both regions — they do not overlap.
(d)Calculate the area of the hall that neither camera can see. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. Area of the hall = 40 × 24 = 960 m2
  2. Because the regions do not overlap, the covered area is simply 144π + 56.25π = 200.25π = 629.103… m2
  3. Uncovered area = 960 − 629.103… = 330.896…
  4. Area = 331 m2 (3 s.f.) — exact value (960 − 200.25π) m2

Question 23  [5 marks]
(a)Calculate the area of the part of the region that is a three-quarter circle. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. From A the goat can swing round through every direction except the right angle occupied by the shed — that is 270° out of 360°, three-quarters of a circle of radius 6 m.
  2. Area = 3⁄4 × π × 62 = 27π = 84.82…
  3. Area = 84.8 m2 (3 s.f.) — exact value 27π m2
(b)When the rope is pulled tight along the wall AB and round the corner B, and when it is pulled along AD and round D, the goat can reach two more parts of the field. Calculate the total area the goat can reach. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. Along AB the rope uses 4 m, leaving 6 − 4 = 2 m to swing round B: a quarter circle of radius 2 m. — 2 m < 3 m = BC, so it cannot reach round the next corner C
  2. Along AD the rope uses 3 m, leaving 6 − 3 = 3 m to swing round D: a quarter circle of radius 3 m. — 3 m < 4 m = DC
  3. Extra areas: 1⁄4π(2)2 + 1⁄4π(3)2 = π + 2.25π = 3.25π — these two pieces are on different sides of the shed, so they do not overlap
  4. Total = 27π + 3.25π = 30.25π = 95.033…
  5. Total area = 95.0 m2 (3 s.f.) — exact value 30.25π m2

Question 24  [6 marks]
(a)Using a straight edge and compasses only, construct the locus of the points equidistant from A and B, and draw the locus of the points 6 cm from A.
  1. ‘Nearer to A than to B’ needs the perpendicular bisector of AB: equal arcs from A and B, and rule the line through their crossings.
  2. ‘Not more than 6 cm from A’ needs the circle of radius 6 cm centred on A.
  3. Both loci drawn; the circle cuts the bisector because 6 > 4.
(b)Shade the region in which P can lie.
  1. Shade the part of the circle on the A side of the bisector — the circle of radius 6 with the segment beyond the bisector cut off
(c)Calculate the area of this region. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. The bisector is 4 cm from A. Where it meets the circle, the radius makes an angle α with AB, where cos α = 4⁄6, so α = 48.19° and the chord subtends 2α = 96.38° at A.
  2. Segment cut off = sector − triangle = 96.38⁄360 × π × 62 − 1⁄2 × 62 × sin 96.38° = 30.278 − 17.889 = 12.389
  3. Whole circle = π × 62 = 113.097
  4. Region = 113.097 − 12.389 = 100.708…
  5. Area = 101 cm2 (3 s.f.) — check: it is a little less than the whole circle (113) and much more than a semicircle (56.5) ✓

Question 25  [5 marks]
(a)Write down angle LPX, the angle between PL and the ship's path.
  1. Both bearings are measured clockwise from north at P.
  2. Angle LPX = 110 − 50 = 60°
(b)Calculate the shortest distance from the lighthouse to the ship's path. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. The shortest distance from L to the path is the perpendicular from L to the path.
  2. In the right-angled triangle formed, the hypotenuse is PL = 12 and the angle at P is 60°.
  3. Distance = 12 sin 60° = 6√3 = 10.392…
  4. Shortest distance = 10.4 km (3 s.f.) — exact value 6√3 km
(c)Explain why the ship never comes too close to the lighthouse.
  1. The points less than 8 km from L form a circle of radius 8 km about L.
  2. The path never comes closer than 10.4 km, and 10.4 > 8, so the path never enters the circle
(d)At the point X on its path the ship is the same distance from P as from L. Work out PX.
  1. X is equidistant from P and L, so X lies on the perpendicular bisector of PL, and triangle PXL is isosceles with XP = XL.
  2. The base angles at P and L are equal, so both are 60°, and the angle at X is 60° too — the triangle is equilateral.
  3. PX = PL = 12 km — check: PX = 6 ÷ cos 60° = 12 ✓
