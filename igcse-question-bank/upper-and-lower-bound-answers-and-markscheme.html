Question 1  [2 marks]
(a)Write down the lower bound of the length.
  1. The measurement is correct to the nearest 1 cm, so the true length is within ½ × 1 = 0.5 cm of 47 cm. — half of the unit of accuracy
  2. Lower bound = 47 − 0.5 = 46.5 cm
(b)Write down the upper bound of the length.
  1. Upper bound = 47 + 0.5 = 47.5 cm — any length below 47.5 cm rounds down to 47 cm

Question 2  [3 marks]
(a)Write down the lower bound of the mass.
  1. 1 decimal place means correct to the nearest 0.1 kg, so the half‑interval is 0.05 kg.
  2. Lower bound = 3.6 − 0.05 = 3.55 kg
(b)Write down the upper bound of the mass.
  1. Upper bound = 3.6 + 0.05 = 3.65 kg
(c)A number x is 8.40, correct to 2 decimal places.
Complete the error interval for x.
…………… ≤ x < ……………
  1. 2 decimal places means correct to the nearest 0.01, so the half‑interval is 0.005.
  2. 8.40 − 0.005 = 8.395 and 8.40 + 0.005 = 8.405
  3. 8.395 ≤ x < 8.405 — the lower bound is included, the upper bound is not

Question 5  [4 marks]
(a)Calculate the upper bound of the length of wood that is left.
  1. Plank: 2.35 ≤ length < 2.45. Piece cut off: 0.845 ≤ length < 0.855. — half‑intervals of 0.05 m and 0.005 m
  2. What is left is largest when the plank is longest and the piece cut off is shortest. — for a subtraction, use the upper bound minus the lower bound

(b)Calculate the lower bound of the length of wood that is left.
  1. What is left is smallest when the plank is shortest and the piece cut off is longest.

Question 6  [4 marks]
(a)Calculate the upper bound of the runner's average speed.
Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. Distance: 475 ≤ d < 485. Time: 32.45 ≤ t < 32.55. — half‑intervals of 5 m and 0.05 s
  2. Speed = distance ÷ time, so the speed is greatest when the distance is greatest and the time is least. — dividing by a smaller number gives a larger answer
  3. Upper bound = 485 ÷ 32.45 = 14.946…
  4. Upper bound = 14.9 m/s (3 s.f.)
(b)Calculate the lower bound of the runner's average speed.
Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. The speed is least when the distance is least and the time is greatest.
  2. Lower bound = 475 ÷ 32.55 = 14.592…
  3. Lower bound = 14.6 m/s (3 s.f.)

Question 10  [6 marks]
(a)Calculate the upper bound of v.
Give your answer correct to 4 significant figures.
  1. Distance: 1475 ≤ d < 1485. Time: 123.95 ≤ t < 124.05. — half‑intervals of 5 m and 0.05 s
  2. The speed is greatest for the greatest distance and the least time.
  3. Upper bound = 1485 ÷ 123.95 = 11.98063…
  4. Upper bound = 11.98 m/s (4 s.f.)
(b)Calculate the lower bound of v.
Give your answer correct to 4 significant figures.
  1. The speed is least for the least distance and the greatest time.
  2. Lower bound = 1475 ÷ 124.05 = 11.89036…
  3. Lower bound = 11.89 m/s (4 s.f.)
(c)By comparing your answers to parts (a) and (b), write down the value of v to a suitable degree of accuracy.
Give a reason for your answer.
  1. Compare the two bounds digit by digit: 11.98… and 11.89…
  2. To 3 significant figures they are 12.0 and 11.9 — these do not agree.
  3. To 2 significant figures they are both 12.
  4. v = 12 m/s — the upper and lower bounds agree to 2 significant figures, so only 2 significant figures of the answer can be relied on
