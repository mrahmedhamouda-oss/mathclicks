Question 3  [3 marks]
(a)Write down the coordinates of the point in R with the largest possible value of x.
  1. x is largest when y is as small as possible, so take y = 2. — y ≥ 2
  2. x + 2 ≤ 9 gives x ≤ 7. — substitute y = 2 into x + y ≤ 9
  3. The point is (7, 2)
(b)Find the largest possible value of x + 2y for a point in R.
  1. x + 2y grows fastest with y, so make y as large as possible. — the coefficient of y is the larger one
  2. x ≥ 1, so y ≤ 9 − 1 = 8, giving the point (1, 8).
  3. x + 2y = 1 + 2 × 8 = 17
  4. Check the neighbouring corner points: (7, 2) gives 11 and (1, 2) gives 5, both smaller. ✓

Question 5  [4 marks]
(a)Find the largest possible value of x for a point in R.
  1. To make x as large as possible, make y as small as possible: y = 1. — y ≥ 1
  2. 2x + 3 ≤ 18 — substitute y = 1 into 2x + 3y ≤ 18
  3. 2x ≤ 15, so x ≤ 7.5
  4. x is an integer, so the largest value is 7
  5. Check: (7, 1) gives 2 × 7 + 3 = 17 ≤ 18 ✓, but (8, 1) gives 19 > 18 ✗
(b)P = 5x + 4y.
Find the maximum value of P for a point in R.
  1. Test the integer points near the corners of R.
  2. (7, 1): P = 35 + 4 = 39
  3. (1, 5): P = 5 + 20 = 25
  4. (6, 2): P = 30 + 8 = 38
  5. The maximum is P = 39, at (7, 1)

Question 6  [4 marks]
(a)Write down an inequality for the wood.
  1. x tables use 5x units of wood and y chairs use 2y units.
  2. 5x + 2y ≤ 60 — “at most 60 units”
(b)Write down an inequality for the time.
  1. x tables take 2x hours and y chairs take y hours.
  2. 2x + y ≤ 26 — “at most 26 hours”
(c)The profit is $35 on each table and $15 on each chair.
Calculate the greatest possible profit.
  1. The best point is where the two resource lines cross, so solve them together.
  2. 5x + 2y = 60 and 2x + y = 26, so y = 26 − 2x.
  3. 5x + 2(26 − 2x) = 60 gives x + 52 = 60, so x = 8 and y = 10. — substitution
  4. This satisfies x ≥ 4, so 8 tables and 10 chairs are allowed.
  5. Profit = 35 × 8 + 15 × 10 = 280 + 150 = $430
  6. Check nearby points: (7, 12) gives $425 and (9, 7) gives $420, both smaller. ✓

Question 7  [4 marks]
(a)Write down the inequality given by the line y = 2x.
  1. R lies below the line y = 2x.
  2. Test (4, 3), inside R: 3 ≤ 2 × 4 = 8 is true. — substitute a point
  3. y ≤ 2x
(b)Write down the inequality given by the line x + y = 9.
  1. R lies below and to the left of the line x + y = 9.
  2. Test (4, 3): 4 + 3 = 7 ≤ 9 is true.
  3. x + y ≤ 9
(c)T = 2x + 5y.
Find the maximum value of T for a point in R.
  1. T = 2x + 5y grows fastest with y, so look for the point of R with the largest y.
  2. That is where y = 2x meets x + y = 9: x + 2x = 9, so x = 3 and y = 6. — solve the two boundary equations together
  3. T = 2 × 3 + 5 × 6 = 6 + 30 = 36
  4. Check other corners: (8, 1) gives 21 and (4, 5) gives 33, both smaller. ✓

  2. x ≥ 2y — the number of small buses is at least this
(d)A small bus costs $80 to hire and a large bus costs $150.

  1. Only two combinations satisfy all three inequalities: (8, 4) and (9, 3). — e.g. (9, 3): 12 ≤ 12 ✓, 135 + 120 = 255 ≥ 250 ✓, 9 ≥ 6 ✓

  4. The least possible total cost is $1170

Question 9  [5 marks]
(a)Write down an inequality for the number of fruit cakes.
  1. “At least 10 fruit cakes” means 10 or more.
  2. x ≥ 10
(b)Write down an inequality connecting x and y for the number of sponge cakes.
  1. The number of sponge cakes, y, is at least the number of fruit cakes, x.
  2. y ≥ x
(c)Show that the flour available gives the inequality 8x + 5y ≤ 200.
  1. Flour used = 400x + 250y grams.
  2. 10 kg = 10 × 1000 = 10 000 g — convert to the same units
  3. 400x + 250y ≤ 10 000
  4. Divide every term by 50: 8x + 5y ≤ 200, as required — 50 is the HCF of 400, 250 and 10 000
(d)Find the greatest number of sponge cakes he can make in one day.
  1. y is largest when x is smallest, so take x = 10. — x ≥ 10
  2. 8 × 10 + 5y ≤ 200 gives 5y ≤ 120, so y ≤ 24.
  3. Also y ≥ x = 10, so y = 24 is allowed. — check the other inequality
  4. The greatest number of sponge cakes is 24
  5. Check: 400 × 10 + 250 × 24 = 4000 + 6000 = 10 000 g = 10 kg exactly ✓

Question 10  [6 marks]
(a)Write down the inequality given by the line x = 2.
  1. R lies on or to the right of the line x = 2.
  2. The point (1, 5) is outside R. — check a point
  3. x ≥ 2
(b)Write down the inequality given by the line y = 2.
  1. R lies on or above the line y = 2.
  2. The point (5, 1) is outside R.
  3. y ≥ 2
(c)Write down the inequality given by the line x + y = 11.
  1. R lies on or below the line x + y = 11.
  2. The point (6, 6) has 6 + 6 = 12 > 11 and is outside R.
  3. x + y ≤ 11
(d)The shop makes a profit of $9 on each rucksack and $5 on each sports bag.
Calculate the greatest possible profit.
  1. Profit P = 9x + 5y. The coefficient of x is larger, so make x as large as possible.
  2. The vertices of R are (2, 2), (9, 2) and (2, 9).
  3. (2, 2): P = 18 + 10 = $28
  4. (2, 9): P = 18 + 45 = $63
  5. (9, 2): P = 81 + 10 = $91 — this is the greatest
(e)The shop now also orders at least as many sports bags as rucksacks.
Write down the greatest possible profit now.
  1. The extra condition is y ≥ x, so (9, 2) is no longer allowed.
  2. With y ≥ x and x + y ≤ 11, the largest x is 5, giving y = 6. — if x = 6 then y ≥ 6 and x + y ≥ 12
  3. P = 9 × 5 + 5 × 6 = 45 + 30 = $75
  4. Check (4, 7): P = 36 + 35 = $71, which is smaller. ✓
