Question 1  [2 marks]
(a)Write down the probability that the counter is red.
  1. Total number of counters = 5 + 3 + 4 = 12
  2. P(red) = number of red counters⁄total = 5⁄12 — all 12 counters are equally likely
(b)Write down the probability that the counter is not blue.
  1. Counters that are not blue: 5 + 4 = 9
  2. P(not blue) = 9⁄12 = 3⁄4 — or 1 − 3⁄12, since P(not A) = 1 − P(A)

Question 7  [4 marks]
(a)Find the probability that both sweets are orange.
  1. For the first sweet, P(orange) = 4⁄7.
  2. Only 6 sweets are left and 3 of them are orange, so the second probability is 3⁄6. — there is no replacement
  3. P(orange, orange) = 4⁄7 × 3⁄6 = 12⁄42 = 2⁄7
(b)Find the probability that the two sweets have different flavours.
  1. Different flavours happens two ways: orange then lemon, or lemon then orange.
  2. P(orange, lemon) = 4⁄7 × 3⁄6 = 12⁄42
  3. P(lemon, orange) = 3⁄7 × 4⁄6 = 12⁄42
  4. Total = 12⁄42 + 12⁄42 = 24⁄42 = 4⁄7 — add the probabilities of the separate routes
  5. Check: P(both lemon) = 3⁄7 × 2⁄6 = 1⁄7, and 2⁄7 + 1⁄7 + 4⁄7 = 1 ✓

Question 9  [5 marks]
(a)Find the probability that Leila wins both games.
  1. Multiply along the ‘wins’ then ‘wins’ branches.
  2. P(wins both) = 0.6 × 0.7
  3. = 0.42
(b)Find the probability that Leila wins exactly one of the two games.
  1. Exactly one win happens two ways: win then lose, or lose then win.

Question 10  [6 marks]
(a)Show that n² − 5n − 6 = 0.
  1. There are n + 4 balls altogether, so P(first is red) = n⁄n + 4.
  2. After a red ball is removed, n − 1 red balls remain out of n + 3, so P(second is red) = n − 1⁄n + 3. — the balls are not replaced
  3. n⁄n + 4 × n − 1⁄n + 3 = 1⁄3
  4. 3n(n − 1) = (n + 4)(n + 3) — cross-multiply
  5. 3n² − 3n = n² + 7n + 12 — expand both sides
  6. 2n² − 10n − 12 = 0 — collect all terms on one side
  7. n² − 5n − 6 = 0 — divide every term by 2
(b)Solve the equation n² − 5n − 6 = 0 to find the number of red balls in the bag.
  1. n² − 5n − 6 = 0
  2. (n − 6)(n + 1) = 0 — two numbers with product −6 and sum −5 are −6 and +1
  3. n = 6 or n = −1
  4. A number of balls cannot be negative, so n = 6
  5. Check: 6⁄10 × 5⁄9 = 30⁄90 = 1⁄3 ✓
(c)Find the probability that both balls are blue.
  1. The bag holds 6 red and 4 blue balls, 10 in total.
  2. P(blue, blue) = 4⁄10 × 3⁄9 = 12⁄90 = 2⁄15
