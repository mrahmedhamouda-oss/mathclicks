Question 6  [4 marks]
(a)Write down the size of each interior angle of the hexagon.
  1. Sum of the interior angles = (6 − 2) × 180 = 720°
  2. Each interior angle = 720⁄6 = 120° — all six angles are equal because the hexagon is regular
(b)Find angle BAC.
  1. AB = BC, so triangle ABC is isosceles. — all sides of a regular hexagon are equal
  2. Angle ABC = 120°, so the two base angles share 180 − 120 = 60°. — angle sum of a triangle
  3. Angle BAC = 60⁄2 = 30° — base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal
(c)Find angle ACD.
  1. Angle BCD = 120° is the interior angle at C, and the diagonal CA splits it.
  2. Angle BCA = 30°, so angle ACD = 120 − 30 = 90° — angle BCA = angle BAC in the isosceles triangle

Question 9  [5 marks]
(a)Show that each interior angle of a regular octagon is 135°.
  1. Sum of the interior angles = (8 − 2) × 180 = 1080° — the octagon splits into 6 triangles
  2. All eight angles are equal because the octagon is regular.
  3. Each interior angle = 1080⁄8 = 135° — alternatively 180 − 360⁄8 = 180 − 45
(b)Find the value of x.
  1. The angles at the point A are the octagon angle, the square angle and x°.
  2. 135 + 90 + x = 360 — angles at a point add up to 360°
  3. 225 + x = 360
  4. x = 135
(c)Explain why regular octagons do not tessellate.
  1. For a shape to tessellate, a whole number of its interior angles must fit round a point.
  2. 360⁄135 = 2.67…, which is not a whole number.
  3. Two octagons leave a gap of 90° and three would overlap, so regular octagons do not tessellate.

Question 10  [6 marks]
(a)Write down the size of each interior angle of the pentagon.
  1. Sum of the interior angles = (5 − 2) × 180 = 540°
  2. Each interior angle = 540⁄5 = 108° — all five angles are equal because the pentagon is regular
(b)Find angle ACB.
  1. BA = BC, so triangle ABC is isosceles. — all sides of a regular pentagon are equal
  2. Angle ABC = 108°, so angle ACB = (180 − 108) ÷ 2 — angle sum of a triangle, base angles equal
  3. = 72⁄2 = 36°
(c)Find angle ACD.
  1. Angle BCD = 108° is the interior angle at C, and CA divides it into angle BCA and angle ACD.
  2. Angle ACD = 108 − 36 = 72°
(d)Find angle APB.
  1. In the same way, triangle BCD is isosceles, so angle DBC = 36°.
  2. Angle ABD = 108 − 36 = 72° — the diagonal BD splits the interior angle at B
  3. In triangle ABP: angle PAB = 36° and angle ABP = 72°. — angle PAB is angle CAB, which equals angle ACB = 36°
  4. Angle APB = 180 − 36 − 72 = 72° — angle sum of a triangle
