Question 1  [2 marks]
Calculate the area of the trapezium.
  1. Area of a trapezium = 1⁄2 × (sum of the parallel sides) × the perpendicular height — the two parallel sides are 8 cm and 14 cm
  2. = 1⁄2 × (8 + 14) × 6
  3. = 1⁄2 × 22 × 6
  4. Area = 66 cm2

Question 2  [3 marks]
Calculate the total area of the shape. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. Split the shape into a rectangle and a semicircle.
  2. Area of the rectangle = 12 × 7 = 84 cm2
  3. The diameter of the semicircle is 7 cm, so its radius is 3.5 cm. — radius = diameter ÷ 2
  4. Area of the semicircle = 1⁄2 × π × 3.52 = 19.2422… cm2
  5. Total = 84 + 19.2422… = 103.2422…
  6. Area = 103 cm2 (3 s.f.)

Question 3  [3 marks]

  1. Volume of a cuboid = length × width × height
  2. = 12 × 5 × 8 = 480 cm3

Question 5  [4 marks]
(a)Show that the perpendicular height of the cone is 12 cm.
  1. The radius, the perpendicular height and the slant height form a right-angled triangle, with the slant height as the hypotenuse.
  2. h2 + 52 = 132 — Pythagoras' theorem
  3. h2 = 169 − 25 = 144
  4. h = √144 = 12 cm as required.
(b)Calculate the volume of the cone. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
[The volume, V, of a cone with radius r and height h is V = 1⁄3πr2h.]
  1. V = 1⁄3πr2h = 1⁄3 × π × 52 × 12
  2. = 1⁄3 × π × 300 = 100π = 314.159…
  3. Volume = 314 cm3 (3 s.f.)

Question 6  [4 marks]
(a)Calculate the volume of the hemisphere. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. A hemisphere is half a sphere, so halve the sphere formula.
  2. V = 1⁄2 × 4⁄3 × π × 63 = 2⁄3 × π × 216
  3. = 144π = 452.389…
  4. Volume = 452 cm3 (3 s.f.)
(b)Calculate the total surface area of the hemisphere.
  1. The surface is made of a curved part and a flat circular face. — "total surface area" includes the flat face
  2. Curved surface = 1⁄2 × 4π × 62 = 2π × 36 = 72π
  3. Flat circle = π × 62 = 36π
  4. Total = 72π + 36π = 108π = 339.292…
  5. Total surface area = 339 cm2 (3 s.f.)

Question 7  [4 marks]
(a)Calculate the volume of the prism.
  1. Volume of a prism = area of cross-section × length
  2. Area of the triangle = 1⁄2 × 6 × 8 = 24 cm2 — the two shorter sides of a right-angled triangle are the base and the height
  3. Volume = 24 × 15 = 360 cm3
(b)Calculate the total surface area of the prism.
  1. The hypotenuse of the cross-section is √(62 + 82) = √100 = 10 cm. — Pythagoras' theorem
  2. Two triangular ends: 2 × 24 = 48 cm2
  3. Three rectangles: perimeter × length = (6 + 8 + 10) × 15 = 24 × 15 = 360 cm2
  4. Total surface area = 48 + 360 = 408 cm2

Question 8  [4 marks]
(a)Calculate the height of the tank. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. Volume of a cylinder = πr2h
  2. 2.4 = π × 0.62 × h — substitute the known values
  3. 2.4 = 1.13097… × h — π × 0.36 = 1.13097…
  4. h = 2.4 ÷ 1.13097… = 2.12206… — divide both sides
  5. Height = 2.12 m (3 s.f.)

(b)Write down the capacity of the tank in litres.

  2. 2.4 × 1000 = 2400 litres

Question 9  [5 marks]
(a)Calculate the total volume of the solid. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. Volume of the cylinder = π × 72 × 20 = 980π = 3078.76… cm3
  2. Volume of the hemisphere = 1⁄2 × 4⁄3 × π × 73 = 686⁄3π = 718.377… cm3
  3. Total = 3078.76… + 718.377… = 3797.13…
  4. Volume = 3800 cm3 (3 s.f.)
(b)The solid is made of a material of density 2.7 g/cm3.
Calculate the mass of the solid, in kilograms.
  1. Mass = density × volume
  2. = 2.7 × 3797.13… = 10 252.2… g — use the unrounded volume to avoid a rounding error
  3. 10 252.2… ÷ 1000 = 10.2522… kg — 1000 g = 1 kg
  4. Mass = 10.3 kg (3 s.f.)

Question 10  [6 marks]
(a)Calculate the volume of the cone. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. V = 1⁄3πr2h = 1⁄3 × π × 62 × 15
  2. = 1⁄3 × π × 540 = 180π = 565.486…
  3. Volume of the cone = 565 cm3 (3 s.f.)
(b)Calculate the volume of one sphere. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. V = 4⁄3πr3 = 4⁄3 × π × 1.63
  2. 1.63 = 4.096, so V = 4⁄3 × π × 4.096 = 17.1573…
  3. Volume of one sphere = 17.2 cm3 (3 s.f.)
(c)Work out the greatest number of complete spheres that can be made.
  1. Number of spheres = volume of the cone⁄volume of one sphere
  2. = 565.486…⁄17.1573… = 32.959… — use the unrounded volumes
  3. Only complete spheres can be made, so round down. — 33 spheres would need 566.19 cm3, which is more metal than there is
  4. Greatest number = 32
