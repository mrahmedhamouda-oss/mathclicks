Question 1  [2 marks]
(a)Write down an irrational number from the list.
  1. A rational number can be written as a fraction a⁄b with a and b integers; an irrational number cannot.
  2. √16 = 4 is an integer, 3.75 = 15⁄4 and 22⁄7 are fractions, so those three are rational.
  3. 20 is not a perfect square and 10 is not a perfect cube, so √20 and 3√10 do not simplify to fractions; π is also irrational.
  4. An irrational number from the list is √20 — π or 3√10 also earn the mark
(b)Write down a number from the list that is rational but is not an integer.
  1. The rational numbers in the list are √16, 3.75 and 22⁄7.
  2. √16 = 4, which is an integer, so it is not allowed here.
  3. A rational number that is not an integer is 3.75 — 22⁄7 = 3.142857… also earns the mark

Question 2  [3 marks]
(a)√50
  1. Look for the largest square factor of 50: 50 = 25 × 2.
  2. √50 = √25 × √2 = 5√2 — √(mn) = √m × √n
(b)√12 + √27
  1. √12 = √4 × √3 = 2√3 — 4 is the largest square factor of 12
  2. √27 = √9 × √3 = 3√3 — 9 is the largest square factor of 27
  3. 2√3 + 3√3 = 5√3 — the surd parts are the same, so add the numbers in front
  4. Check: √12 + √27 = 3.464… + 5.196… = 8.660… = 5 × 1.732… ✓

Question 3  [3 marks]
(a)√8 × √6
  1. √8 × √6 = √(8 × 6) = √48 — √m × √n = √(mn)
  2. 48 = 16 × 3, and 16 is a perfect square.
  3. √48 = √16 × √3 = 4√3
  4. Check: 2.828… × 2.449… = 6.928… = 4 × 1.732… ✓
(b)√45 ÷ √5
  1. √45 ÷ √5 = √(45 ÷ 5) = √9 — √m ÷ √n = √(m⁄n)
  2. √9 = 3 — this answer is a rational number

Question 4  [4 marks]
(a)Show that the recurring decimal 0.272727… = 3⁄11.
  1. Let x = 0.272727…
  2. Two digits recur, so multiply by 100: 100x = 27.272727…
  3. 100x − x = 27.272727… − 0.272727… = 27 — the recurring parts cancel exactly
  4. 99x = 27, so x = 27⁄99
  5. 27⁄99 = 3⁄11 — divide numerator and denominator by 9
  6. Check: 3 ÷ 11 = 0.272727… ✓
(b)Write the recurring decimal 0.444… as a fraction.
  1. One digit recurs, so use 9 as the denominator. — 10x − x = 4 gives 9x = 4
  2. 0.444… = 4⁄9

  2. 3 × 2 = 6, 3 × (−√5) = −3√5, √5 × 2 = 2√5, √5 × (−√5) = −5 — √5 × √5 = 5

Question 6  [4 marks]
(a)6⁄√3
  1. Multiply the numerator and the denominator by √3. — this leaves the value unchanged, since √3⁄√3 = 1
  2. (6 × √3) ÷ (√3 × √3) = 6√3 ÷ 3
  3. = 2√3
(b)8⁄(3 + √5)
  1. Multiply the numerator and the denominator by the conjugate 3 − √5.
  2. Denominator: (3 + √5)(3 − √5) = 9 − 5 = 4 — difference of two squares, so the surd disappears
  3. Numerator: 8(3 − √5) = 24 − 8√5
  4. (24 − 8√5) ÷ 4 = 6 − 2√5
  5. Check: 8 ÷ (3 + 2.2360…) = 1.5278… and 6 − 2 × 2.2360… = 1.5278… ✓

Question 7  [4 marks]
(a)Show that pq is a rational number.
  1. pq = (2 + √3)(2 − √3)
  2. = 4 − 2√3 + 2√3 − 3 — expand; the two surd terms cancel
  3. pq = 1, which is an integer and therefore rational. — difference of two squares: 22 − (√3)2 = 4 − 3
(b)Write down the value of p + q.
  1. p + q = (2 + √3) + (2 − √3)
  2. The +√3 and −√3 cancel, giving 4
(c)Explain why p − q is irrational.
  1. p − q = (2 + √3) − (2 − √3) = 2√3
  2. 2√3 is irrational, because √3 is irrational and multiplying an irrational number by the non-zero rational number 2 cannot make it rational.

Question 9  [5 marks]
(a)Show that the area of the rectangle is a rational number.
  1. Area = (5 + √3)(5 − √3)
  2. = 25 − 5√3 + 5√3 − 3 — expand; the surd terms cancel
  3. Area = 25 − 3 = 22 cm2, which is an integer and so is rational. — difference of two squares: 52 − (√3)2
(b)Write down the perimeter of the rectangle.
  1. Perimeter = 2 × [(5 + √3) + (5 − √3)] = 2 × 10
  2. Perimeter = 20 cm — the surds cancel again
(c)Calculate the length of a diagonal of the rectangle, correct to 3 significant figures.
  1. The diagonal d satisfies d2 = length2 + width2 — Pythagoras' theorem
  2. (5 + √3)2 = 28 + 10√3 and (5 − √3)2 = 28 − 10√3
  3. d2 = 28 + 10√3 + 28 − 10√3 = 56
  4. d = √56 = 2√14 = 7.4833… = 7.48 cm (3 s.f.)

Question 10  [6 marks]
(a)Show that x = 7⁄33.
  1. x = 0.212121… and two digits recur, so multiply by 100.
  2. 100x = 21.212121…
  3. 100x − x = 21.212121… − 0.212121… = 21 — subtracting removes the recurring tail exactly
  4. 99x = 21, so x = 21⁄99
  5. 21⁄99 = 7⁄33 — divide numerator and denominator by 3
  6. Check: 7 ÷ 33 = 0.212121… ✓
(b)Hence write x + 1⁄3 as a single fraction in its simplest form.
  1. 7⁄33 + 1⁄3, and 33 is a multiple of 3.
  2. 1⁄3 = 11⁄33 — common denominator 33
  3. 7⁄33 + 11⁄33 = 18⁄33
  4. 18⁄33 = 6⁄11 — divide both parts by 3
  5. Check: 6 ÷ 11 = 0.545454… = 0.212121… + 0.333333… ✓
(c)Write down whether πx is rational or irrational. Give a reason for your answer.
  1. x = 7⁄33 is rational and π is irrational.
  2. πx is irrational, because a non-zero rational number multiplied by an irrational number is always irrational.
