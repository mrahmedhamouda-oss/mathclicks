Question 1  [2 marks]
(a)Factorise 7x + 21.
  1. Look for the highest common factor of 7x and 21. — 7 divides both terms; there is no common letter
  2. 7x + 21 = 7(x + 3) — take out the common factor 7
  3. Check by expanding: 7 × x + 7 × 3 = 7x + 21 ✓
(b)Factorise 10y2 − 4y.
  1. The HCF of 10y2 and 4y is 2y. — 2 is the HCF of 10 and 4; y is the highest power of y in both terms
  2. 10y2 − 4y = 2y(5y − 2)
  3. Check: 2y × 5y − 2y × 2 = 10y2 − 4y ✓

Question 2  [3 marks]
(a)Factorise 12ab − 18b2.
  1. The HCF of 12ab and 18b2 is 6b. — 6 is the HCF of 12 and 18; b appears in both terms but a does not
  2. 12ab − 18b2 = 6b(2a − 3b)
  3. Check: 6b × 2a − 6b × 3b = 12ab − 18b2 ✓
(b)Factorise x2 + 9x + 20.
  1. Find two numbers whose product is +20 and whose sum is +9. — for x2 + bx + c the numbers multiply to c and add to b
  2. 4 × 5 = 20 and 4 + 5 = 9.
  3. x2 + 9x + 20 = (x + 4)(x + 5)
  4. Check: x2 + 5x + 4x + 20 = x2 + 9x + 20 ✓

Question 3  [3 marks]
(a)Factorise x2 − 49.
  1. x2 − 49 = x2 − 72 — both terms are perfect squares and they are subtracted
  2. x2 − 49 = (x + 7)(x − 7) — difference of two squares: a2 − b2 = (a + b)(a − b)
  3. Check: x2 − 7x + 7x − 49 = x2 − 49 ✓
(b)Factorise x2 − 3x − 40.
  1. Find two numbers whose product is −40 and whose sum is −3. — the product is negative, so the two numbers have opposite signs
  2. −8 × 5 = −40 and −8 + 5 = −3.
  3. x2 − 3x − 40 = (x − 8)(x + 5)
  4. Check: x2 + 5x − 8x − 40 = x2 − 3x − 40 ✓

Question 4  [4 marks]
(a)Factorise 3x + 3y + ax + ay.
  1. Group the four terms in pairs: (3x + 3y) + (ax + ay). — the first pair has a common factor 3, the second has a common factor a
  2. = 3(x + y) + a(x + y) — factorise each pair
  3. Both terms now contain the bracket (x + y).
  4. 3x + 3y + ax + ay = (3 + a)(x + y) — take out the common bracket
  5. Check: 3x + 3y + ax + ay ✓ — expanding gives back all four terms
(b)Factorise completely 2m2 − 50.
  1. Take out the common factor 2 first. — “completely” means the numerical factor must come out too
  2. 2m2 − 50 = 2(m2 − 25)
  3. m2 − 25 = (m + 5)(m − 5) — difference of two squares
  4. 2m2 − 50 = 2(m + 5)(m − 5)

Question 5  [4 marks]
(a)Factorise 2x2 + 7x + 3.
  1. Multiply the coefficient of x2 by the constant: 2 × 3 = 6. — the “ac” method
  2. Find two numbers with product 6 and sum 7: these are 6 and 1.
  3. 2x2 + 7x + 3 = 2x2 + 6x + x + 3 — split the middle term
  4. = 2x(x + 3) + 1(x + 3) — factorise in pairs
  5. = (2x + 1)(x + 3) — take out the common bracket
(b)Factorise 6x2 − 11x − 10.
  1. Multiply the coefficient of x2 by the constant: 6 × (−10) = −60.
  2. Find two numbers with product −60 and sum −11: these are 4 and −15.
  3. 6x2 − 11x − 10 = 6x2 + 4x − 15x − 10 — split the middle term
  4. = 2x(3x + 2) − 5(3x + 2) — factorise in pairs
  5. = (3x + 2)(2x − 5) — take out the common bracket

Question 6  [4 marks]
(a)Factorise 9x2 − 25y2.
  1. 9x2 = (3x)2 and 25y2 = (5y)2. — both terms are perfect squares
  2. 9x2 − 25y2 = (3x + 5y)(3x − 5y) — difference of two squares
(b)Factorise completely 3x2 − 12.
  1. First take out the common factor 3: 3x2 − 12 = 3(x2 − 4). — always remove the common factor before looking for a pattern
  2. x2 − 4 = (x + 2)(x − 2) — difference of two squares
  3. 3x2 − 12 = 3(x + 2)(x − 2)
(c)Factorise 4 − 25t2.
  1. 4 = 22 and 25t2 = (5t)2.
  2. 4 − 25t2 = (2 − 5t)(2 + 5t) — difference of two squares, with the square number written first

Question 7  [4 marks]
(a)Factorise x2 − 5x − 24.
  1. Find two numbers with product −24 and sum −5.
  2. −8 × 3 = −24 and −8 + 3 = −5.
  3. x2 − 5x − 24 = (x − 8)(x + 3)
(b)Hence solve x2 − 5x − 24 = 0.
  1. (x − 8)(x + 3) = 0 — use the factorisation from part (a)
  2. So x − 8 = 0 or x + 3 = 0. — if a product of two factors is zero, at least one factor must be zero
  3. x = 8 or x = −3
  4. Check: 82 − 5(8) − 24 = 64 − 40 − 24 = 0 ✓ and (−3)2 − 5(−3) − 24 = 9 + 15 − 24 = 0 ✓

Question 8  [4 marks]
(a)Simplify (x2 − 9)⁄(x2 + 7x + 12), giving your answer in its simplest form.
  1. Factorise the numerator: x2 − 9 = (x + 3)(x − 3). — difference of two squares
  2. Factorise the denominator: x2 + 7x + 12 = (x + 3)(x + 4). — 3 × 4 = 12 and 3 + 4 = 7
  3. (x + 3)(x − 3)⁄(x + 3)(x + 4) — rewrite the fraction in factorised form
  4. = (x − 3)⁄(x + 4) — cancel the common factor (x + 3)
(b)Hence find the value of the expression when x = 7. Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form.
  1. Substitute x = 7 into (x − 3)⁄(x + 4).
  2. = (7 − 3)⁄(7 + 4) = 4⁄11 — = 0.364 to 3 significant figures
  3. Check with the original: (49 − 9)⁄(49 + 49 + 12) = 40⁄110 = 4⁄11 ✓

Question 9  [5 marks]
(a)Factorise 4x2 − 20x + 25.
  1. Note 4x2 = (2x)2 and 25 = 52, so try a perfect square.
  2. (2x − 5)2 = 4x2 − 10x − 10x + 25 = 4x2 − 20x + 25 ✓ — the middle term matches
  3. 4x2 − 20x + 25 = (2x − 5)2
(b)Factorise completely 3x3 − 27x.
  1. Take out the common factor 3x: 3x3 − 27x = 3x(x2 − 9). — 3 is the HCF of 3 and 27, and x is in both terms
  2. x2 − 9 = (x + 3)(x − 3) — difference of two squares
  3. 3x3 − 27x = 3x(x + 3)(x − 3)
(c)Show that 512 − 492 = 200 without using a calculator.
  1. 512 − 492 = (51 − 49)(51 + 49) — difference of two squares with a = 51 and b = 49
  2. = 2 × 100 = 200 — no long multiplication is needed

Question 10  [6 marks]
(a)Simplify (2x2 + 5x − 3)⁄(4x2 − 1), giving your answer in its simplest form.
  1. Factorise the numerator: 2 × (−3) = −6, and 6 − 1 = 5, so split 5x into 6x − x.
  2. 2x2 + 6x − x − 3 = 2x(x + 3) − 1(x + 3) = (2x − 1)(x + 3)
  3. Factorise the denominator: 4x2 − 1 = (2x)2 − 12 = (2x − 1)(2x + 1) — difference of two squares
  4. (2x − 1)(x + 3)⁄(2x − 1)(2x + 1) = (x + 3)⁄(2x + 1) — cancel the common factor (2x − 1)
(b)Factorise 6x2 + 7x − 20.
  1. 6 × (−20) = −120. Two numbers with product −120 and sum 7 are 15 and −8.
  2. 6x2 + 15x − 8x − 20 — split the middle term
  3. = 3x(2x + 5) − 4(2x + 5) — factorise in pairs
  4. = (3x − 4)(2x + 5)
(c)Hence solve 6x2 + 7x − 20 = 0.
  1. (3x − 4)(2x + 5) = 0, so 3x − 4 = 0 or 2x + 5 = 0. — one of the two factors must be zero
  2. x = 4⁄3 or x = −5⁄2 — oe 1.33 and −2.5
