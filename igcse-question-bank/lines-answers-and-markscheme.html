Question 2  [3 marks]
(a)Write down the gradient of the line.
  1. The equation is already in the form y = mx + c, so the gradient is the coefficient of x.
  2. Gradient = 3
(b)Write down the coordinates of the point where the line crosses the y-axis.
  1. On the y-axis, x = 0, so y = 3 × 0 − 12 = −12.
  2. The line crosses the y-axis at (0, −12) — the constant c is always the y-intercept
(c)Find the coordinates of the point where the line crosses the x-axis.
  1. On the x-axis, y = 0, so 0 = 3x − 12.
  2. 3x = 12, so x = 4. — add 12 to both sides, then divide by 3
  3. The line crosses the x-axis at (4, 0)

Question 3  [3 marks]

Question 5  [4 marks]
(a)Find the equation of the line parallel to y = 2x + 7 that passes through the point (3, 1).
  1. Parallel lines have equal gradients, so the gradient is 2.
  2. y = 2x + c, and (3, 1) lies on it: 1 = 2 × 3 + c.
  3. c = 1 − 6 = −5
  4. y = 2x − 5
(b)Find the equation of the line perpendicular to y = 2x + 7 that passes through the point (4, 5).
  1. For perpendicular lines the product of the gradients is −1.
  2. Gradient = −1⁄2 — the negative reciprocal of 2; check 2 × −1⁄2 = −1
  3. y = −1⁄2x + c, and (4, 5) lies on it: 5 = −1⁄2 × 4 + c.
  4. c = 5 + 2 = 7
  5. y = −1⁄2x + 7

Question 6  [4 marks]

  1. Rearrange 3x + 4y = 24 into the form y = mx + c.
  2. 4y = −3x + 24 — subtract 3x from both sides
  3. y = −3⁄4x + 6 — divide every term by 4

Question 7  [4 marks]
(a)Write down the coordinates of M, the midpoint of AB.
  1. M = ( −1 + 7⁄2 , 2 + 8⁄2 )
  2. M = (3, 5)
(b)Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of AB. Give your answer in the form y = mx + c.
  1. Gradient of AB = 8 − 2⁄7 − (−1) = 6⁄8 = 3⁄4
  2. Gradient of the perpendicular bisector = −4⁄3 — the negative reciprocal, since the product of perpendicular gradients is −1
  3. It passes through the midpoint M(3, 5): 5 = −4⁄3 × 3 + c — a perpendicular bisector cuts the segment in half at right angles
  4. 5 = −4 + c, so c = 9.
  5. y = −4⁄3x + 9 (or 4x + 3y = 27)

Question 8  [4 marks]
(a)Show that the equation of L is y = 2x + 3.
  1. Gradient = 11 − (−1)⁄4 − (−2) = 12⁄6 = 2 — be careful with the double negatives
  2. y = 2x + c, and B(4, 11) lies on L: 11 = 2 × 4 + c.
  3. c = 11 − 8 = 3, so y = 2x + 3 as required.
  4. Check with A(−2, −1): 2 × (−2) + 3 = −1 ✓
(b)The point C(k, 17) lies on L. Find the value of k.
  1. C lies on L, so its coordinates satisfy y = 2x + 3.
  2. 17 = 2k + 3 — substitute x = k and y = 17
  3. 2k = 14, so k = 7
(c)Write down the equation of the line that is parallel to L and passes through the origin.
  1. A parallel line has the same gradient, 2, and through the origin c = 0.
  2. y = 2x

Question 9  [5 marks]
(a)Find the coordinates of C.
  1. AC : CB = 3 : 1, so C is 3⁄4 of the way from A to B. — the ratio splits AB into 3 + 1 = 4 equal parts
  2. From A to B the change is (9 − 1, 6 − 2) = (8, 4).
  3. x = 1 + 3⁄4 × 8 = 1 + 6 = 7
  4. y = 2 + 3⁄4 × 4 = 2 + 3 = 5
  5. C = (7, 5)
  6. Check: AC = √(62 + 32) = √45 and CB = √(22 + 12) = √5, and √45 = 3√5 ✓
(b)Find the equation of the line through C that is perpendicular to AB. Give your answer in the form y = mx + c.
  1. Gradient of AB = 6 − 2⁄9 − 1 = 4⁄8 = 1⁄2
  2. Perpendicular gradient = −2 — negative reciprocal of 1⁄2
  3. Through C(7, 5): 5 = −2 × 7 + c, so c = 19.
  4. y = −2x + 19

Question 10  [6 marks]
(a)Find the coordinates of P.
  1. At P the two equations are satisfied at the same time.
  2. x + y = 10 gives y = 10 − x. — rearrange the second line
  3. 2x + 1 = 10 − x — equate the two expressions for y
  4. 3x = 9, so x = 3. — add x to both sides and subtract 1
  5. y = 2 × 3 + 1 = 7
  6. P = (3, 7)
  7. Check in the second equation: 3 + 7 = 10 ✓
(b)Calculate the area of triangle ABP.
  1. A is (0, 1) and B is (0, 10), so AB = 10 − 1 = 9. — the y-intercepts are the constant terms
  2. Take AB as the base; it lies along the y-axis.
  3. The perpendicular height is the x-coordinate of P, which is 3. — the distance of P from the y-axis
  4. Area = 1⁄2 × 9 × 3 = 13.5 units2
