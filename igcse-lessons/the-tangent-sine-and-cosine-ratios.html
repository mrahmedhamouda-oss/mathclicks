1 ·  Labelling the sides — relative to the angle

Trigonometry only works in a right-angled triangle. Before you can choose a ratio you must name the three sides. This is the step that costs more marks than any other in the whole topic, because two of the three names change depending on which angle you are working with.

Definition
  • The hypotenuse (H) is the side opposite the right angle. It is always the longest side, and it never changes, whatever angle you use.
  • The opposite (O) is the side facing the angle you are using — it does not touch it.
  • The adjacent (A) is the side that touches the angle you are using and is not the hypotenuse.

The same triangle, two different angles

AC is the hypotenuse in both pictures. But BC is the opposite for angle A and the adjacent for angle C — they swap over.

The three-step labelling routine

  1. Find the right angle. Label the side facing it H.
  2. Find the angle you are using (the one you know or the one you want). Label the side facing it O.
  3. The remaining side is A.
Watch out Never label the sides before you have decided which angle you are using. In a two-part question you may have to re-label the same triangle half way through.

2 ·  SOHCAHTOA: choosing and using a ratio

In every right-angled triangle with the same angle θ, the three possible pairs of sides are always in the same proportion. Those three fixed proportions are called the sine, cosine and tangent of θ.

sin θ = OH     cos θ = AH     tan θ = OA SOH · CAH · TOA

Reading SOHCAHTOA

Sin = Opposite over Hypotenuse
Cos = Adjacent over Hypotenuse
Tan = Opposite over Adjacent

Write the letters down the side of your working every single time. It costs four seconds and saves whole questions.

Choosing the ratio

Mark the side you know and the side you want. Ignore the third side completely — you do not need it.

Know + wantUse
O and Hsin
A and Hcos
O and Atan
Unknown on top or bottom? Once the ratio is chosen the equation always has the unknown either on the top of the fraction — then multiply, e.g. x = 12 × sin θ — or on the bottom — then swap and divide, e.g. x = 7 ÷ tan θ.
Example 1In a right-angled triangle the hypotenuse is 12 cm and one angle is 35°. Find the side opposite that angle.
1
Label relative to the 35° angle: the 12 cm side faces the right angle so it is H; the side we want faces 35° so it is O. know H = 12, want O
2
O and H together → SOH, so use sine. sin 35° = x / 12
3
The unknown is on the top, so multiply both sides by 12. x = 12 × sin 35°
x = 12 × 0.573576… = 6.8829…
x = 6.88 cm (3 s.f.)
Exam tip Keep the full display value in your calculator. Rounding sin 35° to 0.57 first gives 6.84 — a wrong answer for an accuracy mark.
Example 2A right-angled triangle has an angle of 40° and the side opposite it is 7 cm. Find the side adjacent to that angle.
1
Relative to 40°: 7 cm is O, the side we want is A. know O = 7, want A
2
O and A together → TOA, so use tangent. tan 40° = 7 / x
3
The unknown is on the bottom. Multiply both sides by x, then divide by tan 40°. x × tan 40° = 7
x = 7 ÷ tan 40°
4
Evaluate in one go. x = 7 ÷ 0.8390996… = 8.3422…
x = 8.34 cm (3 s.f.)
Watch out A quick sense check: 40° is less than 45°, so the opposite must be shorter than the adjacent. 8.34 > 7, so the answer is believable. Had you multiplied by mistake you would have got 5.87, which is too small.

3 ·  Finding a missing angle

If you know two sides and want the angle, work out the ratio as a number first, then undo the sine, cosine or tangent with the inverse function — the second function on your calculator keys, written sin−1, cos−1, tan−1.

sin θ = 0.625  ⇔  θ = sin−1(0.625) The inverse function answers “which angle has this ratio?”
Watch out sin−1x does not mean 1sin x. It is the inverse operation, like √ undoing a square. Also check your calculator is in degree mode — a D or DEG on the display. In radian mode every answer in this topic is wrong.
Example 3A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 8 cm and one side of 5 cm. Find the angle between the hypotenuse and the third side.
1
The 5 cm side faces θ, so it is O; 8 cm faces the right angle, so it is H. O = 5, H = 8 → SOH → sine
2
Write the ratio as a decimal. sin θ = 5 / 8 = 0.625
3
Apply the inverse sine. θ = sin−1(0.625) = 38.6821…°
θ = 38.7° (1 d.p.)
Exam tip Angles are normally asked to 1 decimal place; lengths to 3 significant figures. If the question says nothing, 3 s.f. is always accepted.

If a question says “give your answer in exact form” or “in surd form”, it is telling you the angle is 30°, 45° or 60° and that no calculator rounding is allowed. For example, with an adjacent of 6 cm and an angle of 60°: cos 60° = 6h, so h = 6 ÷ ½ = 12 cm exactly, and the opposite is 6 tan 60° = 6√3 cm.

4 ·  Angles of elevation and depression

Definition The angle of elevation is measured upwards from the horizontal to your line of sight. The angle of depression is measured downwards from the horizontal to your line of sight. Both are measured from the horizontal, never from a vertical wall or mast.
Exam tip The angle of depression is outside the triangle you want to use. Because the horizontal at the top is parallel to the ground, the angle down at the far point equals the angle of depression (alternate angles). Drop it into the triangle and carry on as normal.
Example 4Amira stands 47 m from the foot of a vertical tower. The angle of elevation of the top of the tower is 32°. Find (a) the height of the tower, (b) the distance from Amira to the top.
1
(a) Relative to 32°: 47 m is A, the height is O. O and A → tangent. tan 32° = h / 47
2
Unknown on top, so multiply. h = 47 × tan 32° = 47 × 0.624869… = 29.3688…
3
(b) Now the 47 m is still A but we want H → cosine. cos 32° = 47 / d
4
Unknown on the bottom, so divide. d = 47 ÷ cos 32° = 47 ÷ 0.848048… = 55.4213…
(a) h = 29.4 m    (b) d = 55.4 m (3 s.f.)
Check Pythagoras on the unrounded values: √(47² + 29.3688²) = √3071.5 = 55.42 ✓. Notice the hypotenuse is the longest side, as it must be.

Trigonometry or Pythagoras?

What the question gives youWhat to use
Two sides, want the third sidePythagoras a² + b² = c²
Two sides, want an angleInverse trig: sin−1, cos−1, tan−1
One side and an angle, want another sidesin, cos or tan

In short: no angle mentioned anywhere → Pythagoras. As soon as an angle appears, trigonometry is faster and usually the only route.

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Labelling before choosing the angle Opposite and adjacent swap over when you switch angles. Decide which angle you are using first, then label — and re-label for each new part of the question. Only the hypotenuse never moves.
Calculator in radian mode Check for D or DEG on the display before you start. In radian mode every trig answer is wrong, and the error is almost impossible to spot from the working. Also remember sin−1x is the inverse function, not 1 ÷ sin x.
Rounding part-way through Never round the ratio before the final line. Rounding sin 35° to 0.57 first turns 6.88 into 6.84 and loses the accuracy mark. Keep the full calculator value until the end.
Exam tip Give lengths to 3 significant figures and angles to 1 decimal place, then sense-check: the hypotenuse must be the longest side, and against an angle under 45° the opposite is shorter than the adjacent.
