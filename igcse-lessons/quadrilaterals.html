1 ·  The angle sum of a quadrilateral

a + b + c + d = 360° The four interior angles of any quadrilateral

Why it is 360°

Draw one diagonal. It cuts the quadrilateral into two triangles, and every angle of those two triangles is part of an angle of the quadrilateral. Two lots of 180° gives 360°.

Example 1The angles of a quadrilateral are x°, 2x°, 3x° and 4x°. Find all four angles.
1
The four interior angles of a quadrilateral add to 360°. x + 2x + 3x + 4x = 360
2
Collect and solve. 10x = 360  →  x = 36
3
Substitute back and check the total. 36°, 72°, 108°, 144°
36 + 72 + 108 + 144 = 360 ✓
36°, 72°, 108° and 144°

2 ·  The special quadrilaterals

Each shape is defined by its sides; the angle, diagonal and symmetry properties then follow. Learn them as a family: a square is a rectangle and a rhombus, and both of those are parallelograms.

Parallelogram

Opposite sides equal and parallel, opposite angles equal, adjacent angles add to 180°. Diagonals bisect each other.

Rhombus

A parallelogram with 4 equal sides. Diagonals bisect each other at 90° and bisect the corner angles.

Trapezium

One pair of parallel sides. The two angles on each slanted side are co-interior, so they add to 180°.

Kite

Two pairs of adjacent equal sides. One pair of opposite angles equal. Diagonals cross at 90°; the long one bisects the short one.

Isosceles trapezium A trapezium whose two non-parallel sides are equal. It gains a line of symmetry, its base angles come in equal pairs, and its diagonals are equal. Exam diagrams often mark it only with two tick marks — spot them and you gain a whole set of facts.
Exam tip “Write down the name of this quadrilateral” is decided by the most specific name. A shape with four equal sides and four right angles is a square, not a rhombus — even though it is also a rhombus.

3 ·  Using the properties to find angles

Because a parallelogram and a trapezium contain parallel sides, every one of them hides a pair of co-interior angles. That is the fact that turns one given angle into all four.

Example 2In parallelogram ABCD, ∠DAB = 62°. Find the other three angles with reasons.
1
AB is parallel to DC, and AD crosses them both. z = 180 − 62 = 118°  (co-interior angles)
2
Opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal. y = 62°  and  x = 118°  (opposite angles of a parallelogram)
3
Check with the angle sum of a quadrilateral. 62 + 118 + 62 + 118 = 360 ✓
x = 118°,   y = 62°,   z = 118°
The short version In a parallelogram the four angles are always a, 180 − a, a, 180 − a as you go round. One given angle is enough.
Example 3A kite has one angle of 116° and the opposite angle 68°. Find the other two angles.
1
The two angles between the pairs of unequal sides are equal — the kite’s line of symmetry maps one onto the other. Call each x.
2
Use the angle sum of a quadrilateral. 116 + 68 + x + x = 360
3
Solve and check. 2x = 360 − 184 = 176  →  x = 88°
116 + 68 + 88 + 88 = 360 ✓
The other two angles are both 88°

4 ·  A diagonal makes an isosceles triangle

A diagonal of a rhombus, square or kite joins two equal sides, so it creates an isosceles triangle — usually the fastest route to a missing angle.

Example 4ABCD is a rhombus with ∠ABC = 116°. Find ∠BAC, giving your reasons.
1
All four sides of a rhombus are equal, so in triangle ABC we have AB = BC. triangle ABC is isosceles
2
The base angles opposite those equal sides are equal. ∠BAC = ∠BCA = x  (base angles of an isosceles triangle)
3
Use the angle sum of triangle ABC. 116 + x + x = 180  →  2x = 64  →  x = 32°
4
Check: adjacent angles of a rhombus are co-interior, so ∠DAB = 180 − 116 = 64°, and the diagonal AC bisects it. 64 ÷ 2 = 32° ✓
∠BAC = 32°

Practice Now you try

Do every question on paper first, then open the solution and mark your own working.

Practice 1Three angles of a quadrilateral are 104°, 39° and 151°. Find the fourth.
1
Subtract the known angles from 360°. 104 + 39 + 151 = 294  →  360 − 294 = 66°
66°
Practice 2One angle of a parallelogram is 47°. Write down the other three, with a reason for each.
1
The opposite angle equals it. 47°  (opposite angles of a parallelogram)
2
The two neighbouring angles are co-interior with it. 180 − 47 = 133° each  (co-interior angles)
check: 47 + 133 + 47 + 133 = 360 ✓
47°, 133° and 133°
Practice 3A kite has angles 124° and 36° at the two ends of its line of symmetry. Find the other two angles.
1
The remaining two angles are the equal pair. 360 − 124 − 36 = 200
2
Split equally. 200 ÷ 2 = 100° each
100° and 100°
Practice 4Name the quadrilateral: (a) diagonals equal and bisect each other at 90°; (b) diagonals bisect each other but are not equal and not perpendicular; (c) exactly one line of symmetry and one pair of parallel sides.
1
Match each clue against the properties table, choosing the most specific name.
(a) square  (b) parallelogram  (c) isosceles trapezium
Practice 5The angles of a quadrilateral are (3x − 15)°, (2x + 25)°, 4x° and (x + 30)°. Find x and the largest angle.
1
Use the 360° sum. 3x − 15 + 2x + 25 + 4x + x + 30 = 360  →  10x + 40 = 360
2
Solve. 10x = 320  →  x = 32
3
Substitute back. 81°, 89°, 128°, 62°  (total 360 ✓)
x = 32; the largest angle is 128°

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Assuming both pairs of opposite angles in a kite are equal Only one pair is equal — the pair the line of symmetry does not pass through. The angles at the two ends of the symmetry line are usually different.
Giving a correct but not specific enough name Four equal sides and four right angles is a square, not a rhombus. Naming questions are decided by the most specific name that fits.
Forgetting reflex angles still count A quadrilateral may contain a reflex angle (an arrowhead shape). The 360° rule still holds — you just use the reflex value in the sum.
Exam tip When a question asks “could it be a parallelogram / trapezium?”, test the angle pairs: a parallelogram needs two equal pairs, a trapezium needs one pair summing to 180°. State the test you used.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1Three angles of a quadrilateral are 90°, 85° and 95°. Find the fourth.
360 − 270 = 90°.
Exit 2One angle of a parallelogram is 110°. What is each neighbouring angle?
180 − 110 = 70° — adjacent angles are co-interior.
Exit 3Which quadrilateral has diagonals that are equal and bisect each other, but no right angle between them?
A rectangle.
