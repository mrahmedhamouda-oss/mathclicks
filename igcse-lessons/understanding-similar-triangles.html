Understanding Similar Triangles

Spot similar triangles, name them in the right order, and use the scale factor to calculate missing sides.

1 ·  What “similar” means

Two shapes are similar if one is an enlargement of the other: same shape, possibly different size. In mathematics that has a precise meaning.

Definition Two figures are similar if (i) their corresponding angles are equal, and (ii) their corresponding sides are in the same ratio. That common ratio is the scale factor, usually written k.

The same shape at two sizes

9.6 ÷ 6 = 7.2 ÷ 4.5 = 12 ÷ 7.5 = 1.6 every pair of corresponding sides gives the same scale factor
For triangles, angles alone are enough If two pairs of angles are equal, the third pair follows from the 180° angle sum — and equiangular triangles are automatically similar. So a complete similarity proof in the exam is two pairs of equal angles, each with a reason. (SSS and SAS ratio checks also work.)
Similar is not congruent Congruent triangles are identical in shape and size — the special case k = 1. Similar triangles need only the same shape.

2 ·  Naming in the correct order

When you write triangle ABC is similar to triangle PQR, you are stating that A corresponds to P, B to Q and C to R. Match the equal angles first, write the triangles one above the other so matching vertices line up, then read the sides off pair by pair.

PQAB = QRBC = PRAC = k corresponding sides, in matching order
Example 1In triangles ABC and DEF, angle A = angle D = 55° and angle B = angle E = 68°. AB = 8 cm, DE = 12 cm and BC = 10 cm. Find EF.
1
Two pairs of angles are equal, so the third pair must be equal too and the triangles are similar in the order given. triangle ABC is similar to triangle DEF
2
Find the scale factor from the pair of sides you know completely. AB corresponds to DE. k = 12 ÷ 8 = 1.5
3
BC corresponds to EF, so multiply by the scale factor. EF = 10 × 1.5 = 15
EF = 15 cm
Exam tip Before you calculate, decide whether the answer should be bigger or smaller. Here DEF is the larger triangle, so EF must exceed 10 cm.

3 ·  Where similar triangles hide

Nested triangles — a line parallel to one side

Because DE is parallel to BC: angle ADE = angle ABC and angle AED = angle ACB (corresponding angles), and angle A is shared. So triangle ADE is similar to triangle ABC.

Watch out The ratio uses whole sides from the apex: AD / AB, never AD / DB. If the question gives you DB, add it to AD first to get AB.

The bow tie — two lines crossing between parallel lines

With AB parallel to DC, the alternate angles at A and C are equal, those at B and D are equal, and the angles at X are vertically opposite. So triangle ABX is similar to triangle CDX.

Example 2In triangle ABC, D lies on AB and E on AC with DE parallel to BC. AD = 6 cm, DB = 4 cm and DE = 7.5 cm. Find BC.
1
DE is parallel to BC, so triangle ADE is similar to triangle ABC (corresponding angles, common angle A).
2
Work out the whole side AB, not just AD. AB = AD + DB = 6 + 4 = 10 cm
3
Scale factor from small to large. k = AB ÷ AD = 10 ÷ 6 = 5/3
4
DE corresponds to BC. BC = 7.5 × 5/3 = 12.5
BC = 12.5 cm
Watch out Using 6/4 as the scale factor is the single most common error here. That ratio compares a part with a part, not a triangle with a triangle.
Example 3AB is parallel to DC. The diagonals of trapezium ABCD meet at X. AB = 5 cm, DC = 12 cm and AX = 3.5 cm. Find XC.
1
This is the bow tie. Alternate angles give angle BAX = angle DCX and angle ABX = angle CDX. triangle ABX is similar to triangle CDX
2
AB corresponds to CD, so the scale factor is k = 12 ÷ 5 = 2.4
3
AX corresponds to CX. XC = 3.5 × 2.4 = 8.4
XC = 8.4 cm
Example 4A vertical stick 1.5 m tall casts a shadow 2.4 m long. At the same moment a tower casts a shadow 36 m long. How tall is the tower?
1
The sun's rays make the same angle with the ground in both cases, and both objects are vertical, so the two right-angled triangles are equiangular and therefore similar.
2
Scale factor from the shadows. k = 36 ÷ 2.4 = 15
3
Apply it to the heights. height = 1.5 × 15 = 22.5
The tower is 22.5 m tall

Practice Now you try

Do every question on paper first, then open the solution and mark your own working.

Practice 1Triangle ABC is similar to triangle PQR with AB = 5 cm, PQ = 15 cm and BC = 7 cm. Find QR.
1
AB corresponds to PQ. k = 15 ÷ 5 = 3  →  QR = 7 × 3 = 21
QR = 21 cm
Practice 2In triangle ABC, DE is parallel to BC with D on AB and E on AC. AD = 8 cm, AB = 20 cm and BC = 15 cm. Find DE.
1
Going from the large triangle to the small one, the scale factor is k = AD ÷ AB = 8 ÷ 20 = 0.4
2
BC corresponds to DE. DE = 15 × 0.4 = 6
DE = 6 cm
Practice 3Two triangles have sides 3, 5, 7 and 9, 15, 20. Are they similar? Justify your answer.
1
Pair smallest with smallest and check all three ratios. 9/3 = 3,   15/5 = 3,   20/7 = 2.857…
Not similar — the third ratio differs from the other two.
Practice 4AB is parallel to DC and the diagonals of trapezium ABCD meet at X. AB = 6 cm, DC = 9 cm and DX = 7.2 cm. Find BX.
1
Bow tie: triangle ABX is similar to triangle CDX, so BX ÷ DX = AB ÷ DC = 6/9
2
Solve for BX. BX = 7.2 × 6/9 = 4.8
BX = 4.8 cm
Practice 5A photograph 15 cm wide and 10 cm high is enlarged so that its width becomes 24 cm. Find the new height.
1
Scale factor from the widths. k = 24 ÷ 15 = 1.6  →  height = 10 × 1.6 = 16
16 cm

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Part-to-part ratios With a line parallel to one side, the scale factor is AD / AB (whole side over whole side), never AD / DB. Add the parts to get the whole side before you divide.
Letters out of order triangle ABC similar to triangle PQR pins A to P, B to Q, C to R. Match the equal angles first, then write the letters — every ratio you build depends on the order.
Multiplying when you should divide Check the direction of the scale factor: multiply by k to go small → large, divide to go large → small. A one-second sense check — should the answer be bigger or smaller? — catches this every time.
Exam tip To prove two triangles are similar, list two pairs of equal angles with a reason for each (corresponding angles, alternate angles, common angle, vertically opposite…). That is a complete proof — the third pair follows automatically from the angle sum.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1How many pairs of equal angles do you need to show two triangles are similar?
Two — the third pair follows from the 180° angle sum.
Exit 2Triangles are similar with scale factor 2.5. One side of the smaller is 6 cm. What is the corresponding side of the larger?
6 × 2.5 = 15 cm
Exit 3In triangle ABC, DE is parallel to BC with AD = 4 cm and DB = 8 cm. What is the scale factor from triangle ADE to triangle ABC?
AB = 12, so k = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 (not 8 ÷ 4 = 2).
