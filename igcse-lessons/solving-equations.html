Solving Equations

The balance method from start to finish — brackets, unknowns on both sides, fractions, and forming your own equations from words and diagrams.

Learning objectives What you will be able to do

  • Solve linear equations using the balance method, undoing operations with inverses in reverse order.
  • Deal with brackets and with the unknown on both sides, keeping every sign under control.
  • Clear fractions by multiplying every term by the LCM of the denominators, and cross-multiply when each side is a single fraction.
  • Form an equation from a worded or geometric situation, solve it, and answer the question actually asked.
  • Check every solution by substituting it back into the original equation.

1 ·  The balance principle

An equation says two expressions have the same value. Think of the equals sign as the pivot of a set of scales: as long as you do exactly the same thing to both sides, the scales stay level and the equation stays true.

Definition To solve an equation is to find the value of the unknown letter that makes the two sides equal. That value is the solution (or root) of the equation.

Keeping the scales level

To get the letter on its own, undo what has been done to it using the inverse operation — and undo in reverse order: deal with adding and subtracting before multiplying and dividing.

Operation on the unknownInverse you apply to both sides
add 7subtract 7
subtract 7add 7
multiply by 4divide by 4
divide by 4multiply by 4
Example 1Solve 5x − 7 = 23.
1
The x was multiplied by 5, then 7 was subtracted. Undo in reverse order: add 7 to both sides first. 5x − 7 + 7 = 23 + 7
5x = 30
2
Now undo the multiplication: divide both sides by 5. x = 30 ÷ 5 = 6
3
Check in the original equation. 5 × 6 − 7 = 30 − 7 = 23  ✓
x = 6

2 ·  Brackets, and the unknown on both sides

Brackets

Expand first. Multiply every term inside by the number outside, then collect like terms. Take care with a negative multiplier: −3(x − 4) = −3x + 12.

Unknown on both sides

Letters on one side, numbers on the other. Move the smaller x term, so the coefficient you are left with is positive.

Example 2Solve 3(2x − 5) = 4(x + 1).
1
Expand both brackets. 6x − 15 = 4x + 4
2
The smaller x term is 4x, so subtract 4x from both sides. 2x − 15 = 4
3
Add 15, then divide by 2. A fraction or decimal answer is perfectly acceptable. 2x = 19
x = 192 = 9.5
4
Check. LHS = 3(19 − 5) = 3 × 14 = 42
RHS = 4(10.5) = 42  ✓
x = 9.5
Note When an x term is negative, move that one. In 7 − 2x = 5x − 14, add 2x to both sides: 7 = 7x − 14, then 21 = 7x, so x = 3. It does not matter that the letter ended up on the right — 3 = x and x = 3 say the same thing.

3 ·  Equations containing fractions

Fractions make an equation look worse than it is. Clear them in one move: multiply every term on both sides by the LCM of the denominators. Each denominator cancels and an ordinary linear equation is left.

x3 + x4 = 7   →   12 × x3 + 12 × x4 = 12 × 7   →   4x + 3x = 84 LCM of 3 and 4 is 12 — multiply every term, including the one with no fraction
Example 3Solve x + 33 + x − 24 = 4.
1
The LCM of 3 and 4 is 12. Multiply every term by 12 — and put brackets round any numerator with more than one term. 4(x + 3) + 3(x − 2) = 48
2
Expand and collect like terms. 4x + 12 + 3x − 6 = 48
7x + 6 = 48
3
Subtract 6, then divide by 7. 7x = 42
x = 6
4
Check. 93 + 44 = 3 + 1 = 4  ✓
x = 6
Watch out The brackets in step 1 are not optional. Writing 4x + 3 + 3x − 2 = 48 loses marks and gives the wrong answer.

If x sits underneath, multiply both sides by that denominator to lift it up. When each side is a single fraction, go straight to cross-multiplication:

pq = rs   ⇔   ps = qr Valid provided q ≠ 0 and s ≠ 0
Example 4Solve 52x − 3 = 2x + 1.
1
One fraction equals one fraction, so cross-multiply. 5(x + 1) = 2(2x − 3)
2
Expand both sides. 5x + 5 = 4x − 6
3
Subtract 4x, then subtract 5. x + 5 = −6
x = −11
4
Check — and confirm no denominator is zero. LHS = 5 ÷ (−25) = −0.2
RHS = 2 ÷ (−10) = −0.2  ✓
x = −11

4 ·  Forming an equation

Many marks come from questions that give you a situation rather than an equation. The routine is always the same.

1. Name the unknown

Let x be the quantity asked for — write down what it stands for, with units.

2. Write each fact in algebra

Translate every sentence or label into an expression in x.

3. Find the equals

Something must equal something: a total, a perimeter, an angle sum, two equal sides.

4. Solve, then answer

Solve, then answer the actual question — often a length or angle, not x itself.

Example 5The three angles of a triangle are (2x + 10)°, (3x − 25)° and (x + 15)°. Find the size of the largest angle.
1
The linking fact: angles in a triangle add up to 180°. (2x + 10) + (3x − 25) + (x + 15) = 180
2
Collect like terms — here the numbers cancel: 10 − 25 + 15 = 0. 6x = 180  →  x = 30
3
Answer the question — substitute back to get the angles. 2(30) + 10 = 70°
3(30) − 25 = 65°
30 + 15 = 45°
4
Check. 70 + 65 + 45 = 180  ✓
largest angle = 70°

Practice Now you try

Do every question on paper first, then open the solution and mark your own working.

Practice 1Solve 3 − 4x = 19.
1
Subtract 3 from both sides. −4x = 16
2
Divide by −4, and check. x = −4
3 − 4(−4) = 3 + 16 = 19  ✓
x = −4
Practice 2Solve 5(x − 2) = 3x + 4.
1
Expand, then subtract 3x from both sides. 5x − 10 = 3x + 4
2x − 10 = 4
2
Add 10, divide by 2. 2x = 14  →  x = 7
3
Check. 5(7 − 2) = 25  and  3(7) + 4 = 25  ✓
x = 7
Practice 3Solve x + 52 + x3 = 9.
1
Multiply every term by 6, the LCM of 2 and 3, bracketing the numerator. 3(x + 5) + 2x = 54
2
Expand and solve. 3x + 15 + 2x = 54
5x = 39  →  x = 7.8
3
Check. 12.8 ÷ 2 + 7.8 ÷ 3 = 6.4 + 2.6 = 9  ✓
x = 7.8
Practice 4Solve 6x − 2 = 4x + 1.
1
Cross-multiply. 6(x + 1) = 4(x − 2)
6x + 6 = 4x − 8
2
Subtract 4x, subtract 6, divide by 2. 2x = −14  →  x = −7
3
Check — both sides give the same value, and no denominator is zero. 6 ÷ (−9) = −23  and  4 ÷ (−6) = −23  ✓
x = −7
Practice 5Three angles on a straight line are (x + 20)°, (2x)° and (3x − 20)°. Find the largest of the three angles.
1
Angles on a straight line sum to 180°. (x + 20) + 2x + (3x − 20) = 180
6x = 180  →  x = 30
2
Substitute back to get the three angles. 50°,  60°,  70°  —  total 180° ✓
largest angle = 70°

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Sign slips crossing the equals sign A term being subtracted on one side becomes added on the other: 7 − 2x = 5x − 14 → 7 + 14 = 5x + 2x. Write the operation you are doing beside each line and you will not lose track.
Forgetting brackets when clearing fractions A numerator with more than one term must be bracketed before you multiply through. 12 × (x + 3)/3 = 4(x + 3), never 4x + 3.
Forcing a whole-number answer Solutions are allowed to be negative, fractional or decimal — x = 9.5 is a perfectly good answer. Do not "fix" a correct fraction into a wrong integer.
Exam tip Write one line per step with the equals signs lined up underneath each other, and finish with a ten-second substitution check. Method marks are given for the intermediate lines — a bare wrong answer with no working scores nothing.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1Solve 4x + 9 = 33.
4x = 24, so x = 6.
Exit 2What is the first move when solving x5 + x2 = 7?
Multiply every term by 10, the LCM of 5 and 2: 2x + 5x = 70, so x = 10.
Exit 3Why must x = 2 be rejected as a solution of 6x − 2 = 3?
It would make the denominator zero — dividing by zero is undefined. (The actual solution is x = 4.)
