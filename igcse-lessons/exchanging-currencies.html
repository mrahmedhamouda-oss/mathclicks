Exchanging Currencies

Read an exchange rate correctly, convert money in both directions, allow for commission and bank charges, and compare prices across two countries.

Learning objectives What you will be able to do

  • Read an exchange rate and decide whether to multiply or divide when converting.
  • Rewrite a rate the other way round using its reciprocal.
  • Allow for a percentage commission or a fixed bank charge in a conversion.
  • Compare the price of the same item in two countries and state the difference in the currency asked for.
  • Sense-check every answer and give money to 2 decimal places.

1 ·  Reading an exchange rate

An exchange rate is a conversion rate between two currencies. It is always a statement about one unit of one currency — and spotting which currency the 1 belongs to decides whether you multiply or divide. Get that wrong and the whole question collapses, so read the rate slowly.

Definition The rate $1 = €0.85 means one dollar buys 0.85 euros. The 1 belongs to the dollar, so every dollar handed over comes back as 0.85 euros.
Rate as writtenOne unit of…To go that wayTo come back
$1 = €0.85dollarsdollars → euros: × 0.85euros → dollars: ÷ 0.85
£1 = $1.25poundspounds → dollars: × 1.25dollars → pounds: ÷ 1.25
$1 = 3.75 riyalsdollarsdollars → riyals: × 3.75riyals → dollars: ÷ 3.75

The two directions, in one picture

Should the answer be a bigger number or a smaller number? The sense check that saves the mark — ask it before you calculate

If $1 = €0.85, a dollar is worth less than a euro, so an amount written in euros is a smaller number than the same value in dollars. Dollars → euros must shrink the number (multiply by 0.85); euros → dollars must grow it (divide by 0.85). The number changes — the value does not.

Example 1The exchange rate is $1 = €0.85. (a) Convert $250 into euros. (b) Convert €340 into dollars.
1
(a) The rate is written for one dollar and we start in dollars, so multiply. 250 × 0.85 = 212.5  →  €212.50
2
Sense check: 0.85 < 1, so the answer should be smaller than 250. It is.
3
(b) Starting in euros — the other currency — so divide. 340 ÷ 0.85 = 400
4
Check both by converting back. 212.50 ÷ 0.85 = 250 ✓    400 × 0.85 = 340 ✓
(a) €212.50     (b) $400.00

2 ·  Rates written the other way round

The same relationship can be written from either currency's point of view, and the two versions are reciprocals of each other.

£1 = $1.25  ⇔  $1 = £11.25 = £0.80 1.25 × 0.80 = 1, as it must be
Exam tip If a rate makes you nervous, rewrite it so the 1 sits on the currency you are starting from. Then you always multiply, and there is nothing left to get wrong.
Example 2The rate is £1 = $1.25. (a) Write the rate in the form $1 = £… (b) Convert $90 into pounds using each version of the rate.
1
(a) Divide both sides by 1.25 so the dollar side becomes 1. $1 = £(1 ÷ 1.25) = £0.80
2
(b) Using $1 = £0.80: we start in the currency the rate is written for, so multiply. 90 × 0.80 = 72
3
Using £1 = $1.25: we start in the other currency, so divide. Same answer, as it must be. 90 ÷ 1.25 = 72
(a) $1 = £0.80     (b) £72.00

3 ·  Commission and bank charges

A bank does not swap your money for free. It takes a percentage commission, a fixed charge, or both — normally in the currency you are handing over. The usual order is: subtract the charge first, then convert what is left.

Percentage commission

Commission of 2.5% leaves you with 100% − 2.5% = 97.5% of your money — one multiplication: × 0.975.

Fixed charge

A flat fee such as $3.50 is simply subtracted, whatever the size of the transaction. Fixed charges hurt small exchanges far more than large ones.

Note For a percentage commission the order does not matter — (600 × 0.975) × 0.85 equals (600 × 0.85) × 0.975. For a fixed charge the order matters a great deal, so read carefully which currency the fee is taken in.
Example 3A bank changes dollars into euros at $1 = €0.85 and charges 2.5% commission. How many euros does a customer receive for $600?
1
Commission is taken from the dollars, leaving 97.5%. 600 × 0.975 = 585
2
Convert what is left. Starting in dollars, so multiply. 585 × 0.85 = 497.25
3
The other order gives the same result — useful as a check. 600 × 0.85 = 510  →  510 × 0.975 = 497.25 ✓
€497.25
Watch out 2.5% commission does not mean × 0.25 or × 2.5. Convert the percentage to a multiplier first: 1 − 0.025 = 0.975.

4 ·  Comparing prices in two countries

“Which is cheaper?” questions look harder than they are. You cannot compare 520 of one currency with 430 of another, so:

The method

  1. Choose one currency to work in — usually the one the answer is asked for in.
  2. Convert the other price into that currency.
  3. Compare the two numbers and state clearly which is cheaper.
  4. Subtract to find the difference, in the currency the question asked for.
Example 4A camera costs $520 in the USA and €430 in France. The exchange rate is $1 = €0.85. In which country is the camera cheaper, and by how much in dollars?
1
The answer is wanted in dollars, so work in dollars. Convert the French price — starting in euros, so divide. 430 ÷ 0.85 = 505.882…  →  $505.88 (2 d.p.)
2
Compare: $505.88 < $520, so France is cheaper. Subtract using the unrounded value. 520 − 505.882… = 14.117…
3
Check the same comparison in euros. 520 × 0.85 = 442.00  →  442 − 430 = €12.00
12 ÷ 0.85 = $14.12 ✓
Cheaper in France, by $14.12
Watch out Writing “$505.88” scores the calculation mark but not the conclusion mark. Finish with the sentence: the camera is cheaper in France, by $14.12.

1
(a) Starting in dollars, so multiply. 340 × 3.75 = 1275
2
(b) Starting in riyals, so divide. 2550 ÷ 3.75 = 680
(a) 1275.00 riyals     (b) $680.00
Practice 2The rate is £1 = $1.25. (a) Write it in the form $1 = £… (b) Convert $150 into pounds.
1
(a) Take the reciprocal. $1 = £(1 ÷ 1.25) = £0.80
2
(b) Either multiply by 0.80 or divide by 1.25. 150 × 0.80 = 120    (150 ÷ 1.25 = 120 ✓)
(a) $1 = £0.80     (b) £120.00
Practice 3A bank changes dollars into euros at $1 = €0.85 and charges 3% commission. How many euros does a customer get for $400?
1
3% commission leaves 97%. 400 × 0.97 = 388
2
Convert what is left — starting in dollars, so multiply. 388 × 0.85 = 329.80
€329.80
Practice 4A jacket costs $96 in the USA and €78 in Spain, with $1 = €0.85. Which is cheaper, and by how much in dollars?
1
Convert the Spanish price into dollars — starting in euros, so divide. 78 ÷ 0.85 = 91.7647…  →  $91.76
2
Compare and subtract, rounding only at the end. 96 − 91.7647… = 4.235…  →  $4.24
Cheaper in Spain, by $4.24
Practice 5Omar changes $2000 into rupees at $1 = 82 rupees, paying 1.5% commission. He spends 119540 rupees, then changes the rest back at $1 = 84 rupees with no further charge. How many dollars does he receive?
1
Commission first: 1.5% taken means 98.5% remains. 2000 × 0.985 = 1970
2
Convert to rupees — starting in dollars, so multiply. 1970 × 82 = 161540 rupees
3
Take off what he spends. 161540 − 119540 = 42000 rupees
4
Coming back at the new rate, divide. 42000 ÷ 84 = 500
$500.00

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Multiplying when you should divide Converting €340 at $1 = €0.85 by multiplying gives 289 — which looks perfectly respectable. Only the sense check catches it: a dollar is the weaker unit here, so the dollar amount must be a bigger number. Do the sense check every single time.
Turning commission into the wrong multiplier 2.5% commission is not × 0.25, × 2.5 or × 0.75. Take it from 100% first: 100% − 2.5% = 97.5%, so multiply by 0.975.
Not answering the question In a “which country is cheaper?” question, a bare number is not a conclusion. State which country, and give the difference in the currency the question asked for.
Exam tip Check every conversion by converting back — it takes five seconds and catches the multiply/divide error. Give money to 2 decimal places, and never round part-way through a multi-step question: keep the full figure in the calculator and round only at the end.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1The rate is $1 = €0.90. Convert $200 into euros.
200 × 0.90 = €180.00
Exit 2With the same rate, which operation converts euros into dollars?
Divide by 0.90 — you are starting in the currency the 1 does not belong to.
Exit 3What single multiplier applies a 2% commission?
× 0.98 — because 100% − 2% = 98% remains.
