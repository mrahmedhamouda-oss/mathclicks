Rearranging Formulae

Changing the subject of a formula — from one-step rearrangements to powers, roots, fractions, and the case where the new subject appears twice.

Learning objectives What you will be able to do

  • Explain what it means for a letter to be the subject of a formula, and change the subject using the balance method.
  • Undo squares with square roots and roots with squares, squaring every factor outside a root.
  • Rearrange formulae where the subject sits on top of, or underneath, a fraction.
  • Use the clear–expand–collect–factorise routine when the new subject appears twice.
  • Check any rearrangement by substituting simple numbers into both versions.

1 ·  What “make x the subject” means

In v = u + at the letter v is the subject: it stands alone on one side and appears nowhere else. Rearranging a formula means rewriting it so a different letter becomes the subject. The method is exactly the balance method you use to solve equations — the same operation on both sides — but with letters, so you cannot simplify as you go.

Definition A letter is the subject of a formula when it appears once only, on its own, on one side of the equals sign. Making x the subject means finishing with x = … where the right-hand side contains no x.

The reverse flowchart: building y = 5x + 3, and undoing it

y = 5x + 3   →   x = y − 35 Undo the + 3 first, then the × 5
Example 1Make a the subject of v = u + at.
1
Get the term containing a on its own: subtract u from both sides. v − u = at
2
a is multiplied by t, so divide both sides by t. The whole of v − u goes over the fraction line. a = v − ut
3
Check with numbers. Take u = 5, a = 3, t = 4; then v = 17. (17 − 5) ÷ 4 = 3  ✓
a = v − ut
Watch out v − ut is not the same as v − ut. Dividing by t divides every term on that side.

2 ·  Powers and roots

Squaring and square-rooting are inverses, so they undo each other — applied to both sides.

To undo a square

Take the square root of both sides. In a formula from a real context (a length, a time, a radius) take the positive root only; in a pure algebra question write ±.

To undo a square root

Square both sides — and square everything on each side, so (2√k)² = 4k: the 2 gets squared too.

Example 2The area of a circle is A = πr². Make r the subject, and find the radius of a circle of area 50 cm².
1
Divide both sides by π to isolate r². r² = Aπ
2
Square root both sides. A radius is a length, so only the positive root makes sense. r = √(A/π)
3
Substitute A = 50, keeping the full calculator value until the end. r = √(50 ÷ π) = √15.9154… = 3.9894…
r = √(A/π)     r = 3.99 cm (3 s.f.)
Exam tip Unless the question says otherwise, give a numerical answer to 3 significant figures and do not round any intermediate value.

3 ·  When the subject sits in a fraction

Two situations, one idea: multiply both sides by the denominator to lift everything back onto the line.

Subject on top

y = x + 14 → multiply by 4 → 4y = x + 1. The fraction line already acts as a bracket round x + 1.

Subject underneath

y = 6x → multiply by x → xy = 6 → divide by y → x = 6y.

Example 3Make x the subject of (a) y = 4x − 53  (b) y = 12x − 4.
1
(a) Multiply both sides by 3 — the whole numerator comes down. 3y = 4x − 5
2
Add 5, then divide by 4. 3y + 5 = 4x  →  x = 3y + 54
3
(b) Here x is in the denominator, so multiply both sides by (x − 4) first. y(x − 4) = 12
4
Divide by y, then add 4. Do not expand the bracket — there is only one x, so dividing is quicker. x − 4 = 12y  →  x = 12y + 4
5
Check (b) with x = 10: y = 12 ÷ 6 = 2. 12 ÷ 2 + 4 = 10  ✓
(a) x = 3y + 54     (b) x = 12y + 4

4 ·  When the new subject appears twice

This is the Extended-tier case, worth learning as a fixed routine — the marks are almost always awarded for the same four moves.

The routine

  1. Clear all fractions — multiply by every denominator.
  2. Expand any brackets.
  3. Collect every term containing the new subject on one side, everything else on the other.
  4. Factorise the new subject out, then divide by the bracket.
Example 4Make x the subject of y = 2x + 3x − 1.
1
Multiply both sides by (x − 1) to clear the fraction. y(x − 1) = 2x + 3
2
Expand — x now appears twice, once on each side. xy − y = 2x + 3
3
Collect the x terms on the left, everything else on the right. xy − 2x = 3 + y
4
Factorise x out — the step examiners look for — then divide by the whole bracket. x(y − 2) = y + 3
x = y + 3y − 2
5
Check with x = 4: y = 11/3. (11/3 + 3) ÷ (11/3 − 2) = (20/3) ÷ (5/3) = 4  ✓
x = y + 3y − 2
Watch out You cannot cancel the ys in that answer. Only factors cancel, and y is a term here, not a factor.
Example 5Make a the subject of s = ut + ½at².
1
Only the second term contains a, so subtract ut from both sides. s − ut = ½at²
2
Multiply both sides by 2, then divide by t². 2(s − ut) = at²
a = 2(s − ut)t²
3
Check with u = 3, t = 4, a = 2, which give s = 12 + ½(2)(16) = 28. 2(28 − 12) ÷ 16 = 32 ÷ 16 = 2  ✓
a = 2(s − ut)t²
Note 2s − 2utt² is the same answer written differently, and equally acceptable. What is not acceptable is cancelling one t from 2ut and t² while the 2s is still there.

Practice 2Make r the subject of V = πr²h.
1
Divide both sides by πh to isolate r². r² = Vπh
2
Square root — positive root only, since r is a length. r = √(V/(πh))
r = √(V/(πh))
Practice 3Make x the subject of y = x − 47 + 2.
1
Subtract 2 first, then multiply by 7. y − 2 = x − 47
7(y − 2) = x − 4
2
Add 4 and tidy up. x = 7y − 14 + 4 = 7y − 10
3
Check with x = 11: y = 1 + 2 = 3. 7(3) − 10 = 11  ✓
x = 7y − 10
Practice 4Make t the subject of s = 6t + 1.
1
Multiply both sides by (t + 1), then divide by s. s(t + 1) = 6  →  t + 1 = 6s
2
Subtract 1. t = 6s − 1
t = 6s − 1
Practice 5Make x the subject of y = 3x − 1x + 2.
1
Clear the fraction and expand. y(x + 2) = 3x − 1
xy + 2y = 3x − 1
2
Collect the x terms on one side, the rest on the other. 2y + 1 = 3x − xy
3
Factorise and divide by the bracket. 2y + 1 = x(3 − y)
x = 2y + 13 − y
4
Check with x = 1: y = 2/3. (4/3 + 1) ÷ (3 − 2/3) = (7/3) ÷ (7/3) = 1  ✓
x = 2y + 13 − y

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Dividing only part of a side Dividing by a letter divides every term on that side. v − ut is not v − ut — keep the numerator together with a bracket or the fraction line.
Squaring only part of a side Squaring 2π√k gives 4π²k, not 2π²k. Every factor outside the root is squared too.
Cancelling terms instead of factors In x = y + 3y − 2 the ys do not cancel. Only factors cancel in a fraction, never individual terms.
Exam tip Check any rearrangement by putting simple numbers into the original and into your new version — they must hand back exactly the value you started with. It takes seconds and exposes almost every slip.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1Make c the subject of P = a + b + c.
c = P − a − b
Exit 2What is the first move when making x the subject of y = 6x?
Multiply both sides by x (the denominator): xy = 6, so x = 6/y.
Exit 3In the subject-appears-twice routine, which step do examiners award the key mark for?
Factorising the new subject out of the collected terms — x(y − 2) = y + 3 — before dividing by the bracket.
