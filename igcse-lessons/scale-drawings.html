Scale Drawings

Read a scale, convert between a drawing and real life in both directions, keep your units straight, and remember that areas scale with the factor squared.

← All lessons Objectives Starter Learn Practice Mistakes Exit check

Learning objectives What you will be able to do

  • Interpret a scale written as a ratio 1 : n or in words such as “1 cm represents 2 km”, and convert between the two forms.
  • Convert a length from the drawing to real life (multiply by n) and from real life to the drawing (divide by n).
  • Use the squared factor k² when converting areas between a map and real life.
  • Choose a suitable, friendly scale so that a drawing is as large as possible while still fitting the space.

Learn Teach · example · together

1 ·  What a scale means

A scale drawing is a picture of something real, drawn so that every length is reduced (or enlarged) by the same factor. A map, a floor plan and a model aeroplane are all scale drawings, and the scale tells you how to move between the drawing and real life.

Definition A scale is the ratio drawing length : real length, with both lengths measured in the same unit. It is written with the drawing side reduced to 1, for example 1 : 50 000. The number n in 1 : n is the scale factor.

As a ratio: 1 : n

1 : 50 000 means 1 unit on the drawing represents 50 000 of the same units in real life. No units are written, because it works for any unit: 1 cm represents 50 000 cm, and 1 inch represents 50 000 inches.

In words: “1 cm represents 2 km”

Here the units differ on the two sides — friendlier for a map. To turn it back into a ratio, make the units match first: 2 km = 200 000 cm, so this is the scale 1 : 200 000.

The same house, drawn to a scale of 1 cm to 2 m

Watch out A ratio scale has no units. Writing “1 cm : 50 000 km” is wrong, and so is “the scale is 50 000”. Write either 1 : 50 000 or “1 cm represents 0.5 km”.
Example 1A map has a scale of 1 : 25 000. Write this scale in the form “1 cm represents …”, in metres and in kilometres.
1
Start with the ratio. Both sides are in the same unit. 1 cm on the map → 25 000 cm in real life
2
Convert centimetres to metres by dividing by 100. 25 000 ÷ 100 = 250 m
3
Convert metres to kilometres by dividing by 1000. 250 ÷ 1000 = 0.25 km
1 cm represents 250 m, that is 0.25 km
Exam tip Learn the two common ones by heart: 1 : 50 000 is “1 cm to 0.5 km” and 1 : 100 000 is “1 cm to 1 km”. Everything else you can build from ÷100 then ÷1000.

2 ·  Converting in both directions

Only two operations exist, and choosing the right one is the whole battle.

Drawing → real

You are making things bigger, so multiply.

real = drawing × n

Real → drawing

You are making things smaller, so divide.

drawing = real ÷ n
Exam tip Before you press a single calculator button, ask: “should my answer be bigger or smaller than the number I started with?” On a 1 : 50 000 map a real distance is always a much bigger number than the map distance. That one sentence catches nearly every ×/÷ slip.
Example 2A map is drawn to a scale of 1 : 50 000. (a) Two villages are 7.4 cm apart on the map. Find the real distance in km. (b) A straight road is 12 km long. How long is it on the map?
1
(a) Map to real, so multiply by the scale factor. Keep everything in centimetres first. 7.4 × 50 000 = 370 000 cm
2
Now convert to kilometres: divide by 100, then by 1000. 370 000 cm = 3700 m = 3.7 km
3
(b) Real to map, so divide. Convert the real length to centimetres first so the units match the scale. 12 km = 12 000 m = 1 200 000 cm
4
Divide by the scale factor. 1 200 000 ÷ 50 000 = 24 cm
(a) 3.7 km     (b) 24 cm
Watch out Never mix units inside one calculation. Get both sides into centimetres, do the multiplication or division, and only then convert the answer to the unit the question asks for.

3 ·  Maps and area — the factor gets squared

Distances on a map behave exactly as you expect: multiply by the scale factor. Areas do not. An area is a length multiplied by a length, so both lengths get multiplied by the scale factor.

Key idea If lengths are multiplied by k, then areas are multiplied by k² (and volumes by k³).

Why the factor is squared

In practice the safest route is to work out what 1 cm represents in a sensible unit first, then square that number to get what 1 cm² represents.

1 cm → 0.25 km   so   1 cm² → 0.25² = 0.0625 km² Square the “1 cm represents” number, not the ratio
Example 3A map has a scale of 1 : 25 000. A forest covers 12 cm² on the map. Find the real area of the forest in km².
1
Work out what 1 cm represents, in kilometres. 25 000 cm = 250 m = 0.25 km
2
Square it to find what 1 cm² represents. 0.25² = 0.0625, so 1 cm² → 0.0625 km²
3
Multiply by the map area. 12 × 0.0625 = 0.75 km²
real area = 0.75 km²
Check it the long way 12 × 25 000² = 7.5 × 109 cm², and 1 km² = 1010 cm², so the area is 7.5 × 109 ÷ 1010 = 0.75 km². Same answer, many more chances to slip.

4 ·  Choosing a scale and drawing accurately

If a question says “choose a suitable scale”, you get the marks for a scale that makes the drawing as large as possible while still fitting the space, and that is easy to work with.

How to choose a scale in three moves

  1. Find the largest real length you must draw.
  2. Divide it by the space available: this is the smallest number of real units each centimetre must carry.
  3. Round that up to a friendly number — 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500, …
Note Friendly scales matter because you will have to convert several lengths by hand. “1 cm to 5 m” is pleasant; “1 cm to 4.7 m” is a guaranteed arithmetic slip. For a drawn answer, accuracy marks are usually within ±2 mm and ±2°: write the scale on your drawing, label every vertex, and leave your construction lines showing.
Example 4A triangular field ABC has AB = 40 m, AC = 30 m and angle BAC = 55°. (a) Choose a suitable scale for a drawing that fits inside a space 10 cm wide. (b) Make the scale drawing and use it to find the length of BC.
1
(a) The longest side is 40 m and it must fit in 10 cm. 40 ÷ 10 = 4, so 1 cm must carry at least 4 m
2
Round up to a friendly value: use 1 cm to 5 m. As a ratio, 5 m = 500 cm. scale 1 : 500
3
Convert the two given sides by dividing by 5. AB: 40 ÷ 5 = 8 cm   AC: 30 ÷ 5 = 6 cm
4
Draw AB = 8 cm. At A measure 55° with a protractor, draw a ray, and mark C 6 cm along it. Join CB.
5
Measure BC on the drawing and convert back by multiplying by 5. BC = 6.7 cm → 6.7 × 5 = 33.5 m
Scale 1 : 500; BC ≈ 33.5 m
Exam tip A measured answer is never exact, so anything from about 33 m to 34 m would score. State the scale you used — without it the examiner cannot follow your conversion.

1
Length: convert then divide by 20. 3.6 m = 360 cm → 360 ÷ 20 = 18 cm

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Putting units on a ratio scale “1 cm : 50 000 km” is meaningless — a ratio scale compares two lengths in the same unit, so it carries no units at all. Write 1 : 50 000, or switch to words: “1 cm represents 0.5 km”.
Mixing units inside one calculation Multiplying 12 km by a scale factor that is written in centimetres gives nonsense. Convert everything to centimetres, multiply or divide, and only then convert the answer to the unit the question wants.
Forgetting to square the factor for areas On a 1 : 25 000 map, 1 cm² does not represent 25 000 cm² — it represents 25 000² cm². And remember the unit conversions square too: 1 m² = 10 000 cm², not 100.
Exam tip Before calculating, decide whether the answer should be bigger (drawing → real: multiply) or smaller (real → drawing: divide) than the number you started with. That single sanity check catches nearly every ×/÷ slip on this topic.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1On a 1 : 100 000 map, what does 1 cm represent in km?
100 000 cm = 1000 m = 1 km, so 1 cm represents 1 km.
Exit 2A path is 5.2 cm long on a 1 : 500 plan. How long is it in real life?
5.2 × 500 = 2600 cm = 26 m.
Exit 3Lengths on a plan are multiplied by k to get real lengths. What are areas multiplied by?
k² — both lengths in an area get scaled (and volumes go by k³).
