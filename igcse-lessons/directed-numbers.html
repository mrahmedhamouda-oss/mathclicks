Directed Numbers

Reading, ordering and calculating with positive and negative numbers — the sign rules, the powers trap, and how to key it all correctly into a calculator.

1 ·  The number line and ordering

A directed number carries a direction as well as a size: −7°C is 7 degrees below freezing, a submarine at −180 m is 180 m below the surface, and a bank balance of −$45.20 means you owe $45.20.

Key idea On the number line, numbers get larger as you move right and smaller as you move left. a < b simply means a lies to the left of b.
Watch out The bigger the digits, the smaller a negative number is: −9 < −2 and −0.5 > −0.8 are both true. Never compare the digits on their own — picture the line every time.
Example 1Six midday temperatures are −12, 5, −3, 0, −8, 2 (in °C). (a) Write them in order, coldest first. (b) Find the difference between the highest and the lowest.
1
(a) Deal with the negatives first: the one with the largest digits is furthest left, so it is the smallest. negatives, coldest first: −12, −8, −3
then 0, then the positives: 2, 5
2
(b) “Difference” means highest minus lowest, and subtracting a negative adds. 5 − (−12) = 5 + 12 = 17
3
Sense-check on the line: from −12 up to 0 is 12 degrees, then 0 up to 5 is another 5. 12 + 5 = 17 ✓
(a) −12, −8, −3, 0, 2, 5    (b) 17°C

2 ·  Adding and subtracting

Every addition or subtraction is a journey along the number line: start at the first number; the second number tells you how far and which way. When an operation sign lands next to the sign of a number, replace the pair with a single sign first.

You seeReplace withExampleAnswer
+ (+a)+ a7 + (+3)7 + 3 = 10
+ (−a)− a7 + (−3)7 − 3 = 4
− (+a)− a7 − (+3)7 − 3 = 4
− (−a)+ a7 − (−3)7 + 3 = 10
Watch out The rule applies only to two signs standing next to each other. In −5 + 3 nothing combines: the answer is −2, not −8. Chanting “two minuses make a plus” without checking loses marks every single year.
Example 2Work out 6 − (−4) + (−9).
1
Simplify each pair of adjacent signs before doing any arithmetic. − (−4) → + 4    + (−9) → − 9
2
Rewrite the whole expression on one clean line, then work left to right. 6 + 4 − 9 = 10 − 9 = 1
6 − (−4) + (−9) = 1
Exam tip Write out the simplified line 6 + 4 − 9. It is a method mark, and it stops you losing track of the signs.

3 ·  Multiplying, dividing and powers

For × and ÷ the rule is simpler: work out the answer ignoring the signs, then decide the sign at the end.

same signs → positive      different signs → negative Applies to multiplication and division only
Exam tip For a long string of multiplications and divisions, just count the negative signs: an odd number of negatives gives a negative answer, an even number gives a positive one. So −2 × (−3) × (−5) = −30.

(−3)² = 9

The brackets say the whole of −3 is squared: (−3) × (−3) = 9.

−3² = −9

Without brackets the power applies to the 3 only; the minus is applied afterwards: −(3 × 3) = −9.

Key idea A negative number raised to an even power is positive; raised to an odd power it is negative: (−2)² = 4, (−2)³ = −8, (−2)4 = 16, (−2)5 = −32.
Example 3Evaluate (a) −20 ÷ (−4) × (−3)  (b) (−2)³ × (−5)².
1
(a) Multiplication and division have equal priority — work strictly left to right. −20 ÷ (−4) = 5  (same signs → positive)
5 × (−3) = −15  (different signs → negative)
2
(b) Odd power → negative; even power → positive. (−2)³ = −8    (−5)² = 25
3
One negative factor, so the product is negative. −8 × 25 = −200
(a) −15    (b) −200

4 ·  Substituting negative values

Substitution is where the powers trap costs real marks. Build one habit: put every negative value in brackets when you substitute — on paper and on the calculator.

Using your calculator without losing marks

  • Use the (−) key for a negative number and the − key for the operation of subtracting — they are different keys.
  • Bracket a negative number before a power: key ( (−) 3 ) x² to get 9. Keying (−) 3 x² gives −9.
  • Put brackets round the whole numerator and the whole denominator of any fraction before dividing.
  • Do not round part-way through — round only the final answer.
Example 4Find the value of b² − 4ac when a = 2, b = −7 and c = 3.
1
Substitute, putting every negative value in brackets. (−7)² − 4 × 2 × 3
2
Deal with the power first. (−7)² = 49
3
Then the multiplication, then the subtraction. 4 × 2 × 3 = 24
49 − 24 = 25
b² − 4ac = 25
Exam tip You will meet b² − 4ac again in the quadratic formula. Writing −7² = −49 there turns a correct pair of solutions into no solutions at all — build the bracket habit now.

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Combining signs that are not adjacent In −5 + 3 there is nothing to combine: the answer is −2, not −8. The two-signs rule only applies when an operation sign stands directly next to a number’s sign.
Confusing −3² with (−3)² (−3)² = 9 but −3² = −9. The brackets decide what is being squared — on paper and on the calculator.
Dropping the sign from the answer A balance of −$13.70 is not the same as $13.70, and an answer of −235 m means a depth of 235 m. The sign is part of the answer.
Exam tip Substitute every negative value in brackets, use the calculator’s (−) key for negative numbers, and bracket whole numerators and denominators before dividing.
