Substitution

Replace letters with numbers — positive, negative and fractional — and evaluate expressions and formulae without losing marks to sign errors.

1 ·  The golden rule: bracket every value

Substitution means replacing each letter in an expression by the number it stands for, then working out the value. It sounds trivial — and it is where a large number of exam marks are quietly thrown away, almost always through signs and order of operations.

The golden rule Write the substituted number inside a bracket, every single time. It costs you two pen strokes and it protects you from every common sign error.
3a  →  3(a)     a²  →  (a)²     −a²  →  −(a)² Bracket first, then put the number in

Once the numbers are in, you are doing ordinary arithmetic, so BIDMAS governs everything: Brackets, Indices, Division and Multiplication, then Addition and Subtraction.

Powers before multiplying

3x² means 3 × x², so square first. When x = 4: 3(4)² = 3 × 16 = 48, not (3 × 4)² = 144.

A fraction bar is a bracket

2x + 13 means (2x + 1) ÷ 3. When x = 7 it is 153 = 5.

Example 1Find the value of 4a + 3b when a = 5 and b = −2.
1
Write the expression, then replace each letter with its value in brackets. 4a + 3b = 4(5) + 3(−2)
2
Do the multiplications first (BIDMAS). = 20 + (−6)
3
Adding a negative is the same as subtracting. = 20 − 6 = 14
4a + 3b = 14
Exam tip Show the substitution line. If your arithmetic slips, a correct substitution line still scores the method mark.

2 ·  Substituting negative numbers

This is the single biggest source of lost marks in algebra. Two rules do all the work.

A negative squared is positive

(−3)² = (−3) × (−3) = 9

(−3)³ = −27 — an odd power keeps the minus sign, an even power removes it.

But −x² is not (−x)²

−x² means “square it, then make it negative”. With x = −3:

−x² = −(−3)² = −9  but  (−x)² = (3)² = 9

Watch out Subtracting a negative adds. In 5 − 2x with x = −4: 5 − 2(−4) = 5 − (−8) = 5 + 8 = 13. Students who skip the bracket typically write 5 − 8 = −3 and lose the lot.
Example 2Given p = −3 and q = 4, find the value of (a) p², (b) −p², (c) 2p − q², (d) (p − q)².
1
(a) Bracket the negative before squaring. p² = (−3)² = 9
2
(b) The minus sign sits outside the squaring, so it survives. −p² = −(−3)² = −(9) = −9
3
(c) Multiply and square first, then subtract. 2(−3) − (4)² = −6 − 16 = −22
4
(d) The bracket comes first here, so subtract before squaring. (−3 − 4)² = (−7)² = 49
(a) 9   (b) −9   (c) −22   (d) 49
Compare (c) and (d) use the same two numbers and give −22 and 49. The brackets are doing all the work — read them carefully.

3 ·  Fractional values

Fractions substitute in exactly the same way. Keep them as fractions if the numbers are friendly, and remember that a fraction raised to a power raises both the numerator and the denominator.

(23)² = 2²3² = 49 Square the top and the bottom
Example 3Given x = 12 and y = 23, find (a) 6x + 9y, (b) x²y, (c) x ÷ y.
1
(a) Multiply each fraction by its coefficient and cancel. 6(12) + 9(23) = 3 + 6 = 9
2
(b) Square x first (indices before multiplication). (12)² × 23 = 14 × 23 = 212 = 16
3
(c) Dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal. 12 ÷ 23 = 12 × 32 = 34
(a) 9    (b) 16    (c) 34
Exam tip If the question gives fractions, give a fraction answer. Writing 0.1666… instead of 16 looks like a rounded answer and can be penalised.

4 ·  Substituting into formulae

A formula is just an expression with a name on the front. The mark scheme almost always wants three lines: the formula, the substitution, the answer with its unit.

FormulaWhat it givesCareful with…
A = πr²area of a circlesquare r, then multiply by π
speed = distancetimeaverage speedtime in hours for km/h — 2 h 30 min is 2.5, not 2.30
v = u + atfinal velocitya is often negative
s = ut + 12at²distance travelledsquare t before multiplying
C = 5(F − 32)9°F converted to °Cthe bracket must be done first
c = √(a² + b²)hypotenuse (Pythagoras)add before taking the root
Watch out √(a² + b²) is not a + b. The root sign is a bracket: everything under it is worked out first. With a = 6, b = 8 the answer is 10, not 14.
Example 4Use c = √(a² + b²) to find c when a = 6 and b = 8. Then find c when a = 3.5 and b = 7.2, correct to 3 significant figures.
1
Substitute, keeping everything under the root, then square, add, root. c = √((6)² + (8)²) = √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10
2
Second pair: square and add. (3.5)² + (7.2)² = 12.25 + 51.84 = 64.09
3
Take the root and only then round. c = √64.09 = 8.00562… = 8.01 (3 s.f.)
c = 10, and c = 8.01 (3 s.f.)
Exam tip Do not round 64.09 or any intermediate value. Keep the full display on your calculator and round once, at the very end.
Example 5The formula s = ut + 12at² gives the distance s metres travelled. Find s when u = 8, a = −2 and t = 3.
1
Substitute all three values, bracketing each one. s = (8)(3) + 12(−2)(3)²
2
Indices first: (3)² = 9. Then the multiplications. s = 24 + 12(−2)(9) = 24 + (−9)
3
Adding a negative subtracts. s = 24 − 9 = 15
s = 15 m
Watch out The t² applies only to t, not to at. Squaring (−2)(3) would give 36 and a completely wrong answer.

Practice Now you try

Do every question on paper first, then open the solution and mark your own working.

Practice 1a = 4 and b = −3. Find (a) 2a + 5b, (b) ab, (c) a² + b², (d) (a + b)².
1
Bracket every value. (a) 2(4) + 5(−3) = 8 − 15 = −7
(b) (4)(−3) = −12
(c) (4)² + (−3)² = 16 + 9 = 25
(d) (4 + (−3))² = (1)² = 1
(a) −7   (b) −12   (c) 25   (d) 1
Practice 2x = −5. Find (a) x², (b) −x², (c) 3 − 2x, (d) x³.
1
Even powers remove the sign; odd powers keep it; subtracting a negative adds. (a) (−5)² = 25
(b) −(−5)² = −25
(c) 3 − 2(−5) = 3 + 10 = 13
(d) (−5)³ = −125
(a) 25   (b) −25   (c) 13   (d) −125
Practice 3p = 23 and q = 14. Find (a) 9p², (b) 8q, (c) p ÷ q.
1
(a) Square first: (23)² = 49. 9 × 49 = 4
2
(b) 8 × 14 = 2
3
(c) Multiply by the reciprocal of q. 23 × 41 = 83
(a) 4   (b) 2   (c) 83
Practice 4Use v = u + at to find v when u = 25, a = −4 and t = 6.5.
1
Substitute with brackets, multiply first. v = 25 + (−4)(6.5) = 25 − 26
v = −1
Practice 5Use C = 5(F − 32)9 to find C when F = −4.
1
Bracket first — it gives a negative. C = 5(−4 − 32)9 = 5(−36)9
2
A negative divided by a positive is negative. C = −1809 = −20

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Skipping the bracket on a negative In 5 − 2x with x = −4, the bracket gives 5 − 2(−4) = 5 + 8 = 13. Without it, students write 5 − 8 = −3 and lose every mark on the line.
Mixing up −x² and (−x)² In −x² only x is squared, so with x = −3 it equals −9, while (−x)² = 9. The bracket decides.
Substituting 2.30 for 2 hours 30 minutes Minutes are sixtieths of an hour: 2 h 30 min = 2.5 h, 15 min = 0.25 h, 45 min = 0.75 h. Convert the time before it goes anywhere near speed = distancetime.
Exam tip Always show three lines: the formula, the substitution (this line carries the method mark), and the answer with its unit. Never round part-way through — round once, at the end, to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.

Exit 1What is (−2)³?
