Understanding Units

Rebuild any metric conversion from three prefixes, square and cube the factor for areas and volumes, and read compound units — speed, density, pressure — as the formulas they spell out.

Learning objectives What you will be able to do

  • Convert between metric units of length, mass and capacity using the prefixes kilo, centi and milli.
  • Convert areas and volumes by squaring or cubing the linear factor.
  • Use 1 ml = 1 cm³ and 1 litre = 1000 cm³ to treat capacity as volume.
  • Read compound units — speed, density, pressure — and write the formula the unit spells out.
  • Convert between m/s and km/h with the factor 3.6.

1 ·  The metric system in three prefixes

Every metric unit is a base unit plus a prefix that says how many of the base unit you have. Know the prefixes and you can rebuild any conversion table in the margin of the exam paper in ten seconds.

PrefixSymbolMeaningExample
kilok× 10001 km = 1000 m
centic× 1100100 cm = 1 m
millim× 110001000 mm = 1 m

Length

  • 1 cm = 10 mm
  • 1 m = 100 cm = 1000 mm
  • 1 km = 1000 m

Mass

  • 1 g = 1000 mg
  • 1 kg = 1000 g
  • 1 tonne = 1000 kg

Capacity

  • 1 litre = 1000 ml
  • 1 litre = 100 cl
  • 1 cl = 10 ml
The only rule you need Going to a smaller unit, the number gets bigger, so you multiply. Going to a bigger unit, the number gets smaller, so you divide. A distance of 3 m is 300 cm (bigger number, smaller unit) but only 0.003 km.

2 ·  Converting areas and volumes

This is where most marks are lost. Students who happily write 1 m = 100 cm then write “1 m² = 100 cm²” — wrong by a factor of one hundred. An area is a length times a length, so the conversion factor gets used twice; a volume uses it three times.

1 cm² = (10 mm) × (10 mm) = 100 mm²      1 cm³ = (10 mm)³ = 1000 mm³ Square the linear factor for area, cube it for volume

Why the factor is squared and cubed

Capacity and volume are the same thing 1 ml = 1 cm³  ·  1 litre = 1000 cm³  ·  1 m³ = 1000 litres. A “capacity” question is a volume question in disguise.
Example 1Convert (a) 3.7 m² to cm², (b) 45 000 mm² to cm², (c) 2.4 m³ to cm³.
1
(a) The linear factor is 1 m = 100 cm. Square it. 100² = 10 000  →  3.7 × 10 000 = 37 000 cm²
2
(b) mm² to cm² is a bigger unit, so divide by 10² = 100. 45 000 ÷ 100 = 450 cm²
3
(c) Volume, so cube the linear factor. 100³ = 1 000 000  →  2.4 × 1 000 000 = 2 400 000 cm³
(a) 37 000 cm²   (b) 450 cm²   (c) 2 400 000 cm³
Exam tip Check the direction with a sentence: “a centimetre square is small, so I need lots of them” → the number must go up.
Example 2A tank is a cuboid measuring 0.8 m by 45 cm by 300 mm. Find its capacity in litres.
1
Put every length in the same unit first — centimetres, because 1 litre = 1000 cm³. 0.8 m = 80 cm  ·  45 cm  ·  300 mm = 30 cm
2
Volume of a cuboid = l × w × h. 80 × 45 × 30 = 108 000 cm³
3
Convert to litres by dividing by 1000. 108 000 ÷ 1000 = 108
Capacity = 108 litres
Watch out Never multiply lengths that are in different units. Convert first, multiply second.

3 ·  Compound units: per means divide

A compound unit is built from two other units, and the word “per” (or the slash) always means divide. If you can read the unit, you can write the formula.

Speed

speed = distancetime

Units: m/s, km/h.

Density

density = massvolume

Units: g/cm³, kg/m³; rearrange to mass = density × volume.

Pressure

pressure = forcearea

Units: N/m² (the pascal), N/cm².

1 m/s = 3.6 km/h Multiply by 3.6 to go m/s → km/h (÷1000 for km, ×3600 for hours); divide by 3.6 to come back
Exam tip The unit tells you the operation. If an answer must be in g/cm³, the mass must be in grams and the volume in cm³ before you divide. Convert first, then divide.
Example 3(a) A cheetah runs at 28 m/s. Write this in km/h. (b) A speed limit is 90 km/h. Write this in m/s.
1
(a) In one second it covers 28 m, so in one hour (3600 s) it covers 28 × 3600 = 100 800 m
2
Change metres to kilometres. 100 800 ÷ 1000 = 100.8 km/h
3
(b) Reverse it: divide by 3.6. 90 ÷ 3.6 = 25 m/s
(a) 100.8 km/h    (b) 25 m/s
Sense check A number of km/h is always larger than the same speed in m/s, because a kilometre per hour is a much smaller speed than a metre per second.
Example 4A crate has weight 250 N and its base is a rectangle 80 cm by 50 cm. Find the pressure on the floor in N/m².
1
Area of the base, in cm² first. A = 80 × 50 = 4000 cm²
2
The answer must be in N/m², so the area must be in m². Divide by 10 000, not by 100. 4000 ÷ 10 000 = 0.4 m²
3
Now divide force by area. pressure = 250 ÷ 0.4 = 625
Pressure = 625 N/m²
Exam trap Dividing 4000 by 100 gives 40 m² and a “pressure” of 6.25 N/m² — a hundred times too small. Area conversions use the squared factor.

Practice Now you try

Do every question on paper first, then open the solution and mark your own working.

Practice 1Convert: (a) 2.6 km to m  (b) 750 g to kg  (c) 4.5 litres to ml  (d) 85 mm to cm.
1
Smaller unit → multiply; bigger unit → divide. (a) 2.6 × 1000 = 2600 m
(b) 750 ÷ 1000 = 0.75 kg
(c) 4.5 × 1000 = 4500 ml
(d) 85 ÷ 10 = 8.5 cm
(a) 2600 m  (b) 0.75 kg  (c) 4500 ml  (d) 8.5 cm
Practice 2Convert: (a) 6.2 cm² to mm²  (b) 0.75 m² to cm²  (c) 3 200 000 cm³ to m³.
1
(a) Square the factor 10. 6.2 × 100 = 620 mm²
2
(b) Square the factor 100. 0.75 × 10 000 = 7500 cm²
3
(c) Cube the factor 100 and divide. 3 200 000 ÷ 1 000 000 = 3.2 m³
(a) 620 mm²  (b) 7500 cm²  (c) 3.2 m³
Practice 3A fish tank measures 60 cm by 35 cm by 40 cm. Find its capacity in litres.
1
Volume in cm³. 60 × 35 × 40 = 84 000 cm³
2
1 litre = 1000 cm³. 84 000 ÷ 1000 = 84
84 litres
Practice 4Change 12.5 m/s into km/h, and 126 km/h into m/s.
1
m/s → km/h: multiply by 3.6. 12.5 × 3.6 = 45 km/h
2
km/h → m/s: divide by 3.6. 126 ÷ 3.6 = 35 m/s
45 km/h and 35 m/s
Practice 5A piece of oak of volume 2500 cm³ has mass 1.8 kg. Find its density in g/cm³.
1
The answer is wanted in g/cm³, so the mass must be in grams first. 1.8 kg = 1800 g
2
Density = mass ÷ volume. 1800 ÷ 2500 = 0.72
0.72 g/cm³

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Using the linear factor for areas and volumes 1 m² is not 100 cm². Picture a square metre: it is 100 cm along each edge, so it holds 100 × 100 = 10 000 centimetre squares — and a cubic metre holds 1 000 000 cm³.
Multiplying lengths in different units 0.8 m × 45 cm × 300 mm means nothing until every length is in the same unit. Convert first, multiply second.
Dividing before the units match the answer If the answer must be in g/cm³ or N/m², convert the mass, volume or area into those exact units before dividing — the unit in the question tells you the operation and the units to use.
Exam tip Check every conversion with a sentence about size: going to a smaller unit needs a bigger number, and a speed in km/h is always a bigger number than the same speed in m/s. If the direction feels wrong, it probably is.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1How many cm² are there in 1 m²?
10 000 — the factor 100 is squared.
Exit 2How many litres are there in 1 m³?
1000 litres (1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³ and 1 litre = 1000 cm³).
Exit 3Convert 18 m/s into km/h.
18 × 3.6 = 64.8 km/h.
