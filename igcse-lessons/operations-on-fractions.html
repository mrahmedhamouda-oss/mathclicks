Operations on Fractions

Add and subtract with a common denominator, multiply and divide with cancelling and keep–change–flip, then use fractions inside BIDMAS and reverse “find the original” problems.

Learning objectives What you will be able to do

  • Add and subtract fractions and mixed numbers using the LCM as the common denominator.
  • Multiply fractions, cancelling before multiplying so answers arrive already simplified.
  • Divide fractions with keep–change–flip, converting mixed numbers to improper fractions first.
  • Apply BIDMAS to calculations that mix fractions, brackets and different operations.
  • Solve reverse problems: find the original amount when a fraction of it is given.

1 ·  Adding and subtracting

Fifths and eighths are different-sized pieces, so they cannot be added directly. Rewrite both fractions over a common denominator — the LCM of the denominators keeps the numbers as small as possible — then add or subtract the numerators only.

Watch out The denominator does not get added: 12 + 13 = 56, not 25. A quick decimal check (0.5 + 0.3̇ = 0.83̇) catches this instantly.
Example 1Work out 38 + 512, giving your answer in its lowest terms.
1
Find the LCM of the denominators. 8 = 2³,  12 = 2² × 3  →  LCM = 24
2
Rewrite each fraction over 24 — whatever multiplies the bottom must multiply the top. 3/8 = 9/24  (×3)
5/12 = 10/24  (×2)
3
Add the numerators; the denominator stays at 24. 9/24 + 10/24 = 19/24
4
19 is prime and does not divide 24, so it is already in lowest terms.
1924

For mixed numbers, the safest method — and the one to use for subtraction — is to convert everything to improper fractions first, do the arithmetic, and convert back at the very end.

Example 2Work out 314 − 156, giving your answer as a mixed number.
1
Turn both mixed numbers into improper fractions. 3 1/4 = 13/4    1 5/6 = 11/6
2
LCM of 4 and 6 is 12. 13/4 = 39/12  (×3)
11/6 = 22/12  (×2)
3
Subtract the numerators — write this line down, it carries the method mark. 39/12 − 22/12 = 17/12
4
Convert back: 17 ÷ 12 = 1 remainder 5. 17/12 = 1 5/12    check: 3.25 − 1.83̇ = 1.416̇ ✓
1512

2 ·  Multiplying — cancel first

Multiplying needs no common denominator: multiply straight across the top and straight across the bottom. Before you do, cancel any numerator with any denominator, whichever fraction it comes from — you are just simplifying the single big fraction early, and the answer comes out already in lowest terms.

ab × cd = a × cb × d      ab ÷ cd = ab × dc Multiply across  ·  divide by multiplying by the reciprocal
Example 3Work out 815 × 2532.
1
Write it as one fraction so you can see everything. (8 × 25) / (15 × 32)
2
Cancel 8 with 32 (both divide by 8), and 25 with 15 (both divide by 5). 8 → 1,  32 → 4    25 → 5,  15 → 3
3
Now multiply the small numbers. (1 × 5) / (3 × 4) = 5/12
512
Exam tip Without cancelling you would get 200480 — correct, but you then have to spot that the HCF is 40. Cancelling first is the same work done earlier and more safely.

3 ·  Dividing: keep – change – flip

Dividing by 16 asks “how many sixths fit inside?”, so dividing by a fraction less than 1 makes the answer bigger. The rule: keep the first fraction, change ÷ to ×, flip the second.

Why 23 ÷ 16 = 4

Example 4Work out 213 ÷ 159.
Non-negotiable You may never keep–change–flip a mixed number. Convert both to improper fractions first.
1
Convert. 2 1/3 = 7/3    1 5/9 = 14/9
2
Keep, change, flip. 7/3 ÷ 14/9 = 7/3 × 9/14
3
Cancel before multiplying: 7 with 14 (÷7), and 9 with 3 (÷3). (1 × 3) / (1 × 2) = 3/2
4
Write as a mixed number. 3/2 = 1 1/2    check: 2.3̇ ÷ 1.5̇ = 1.5 ✓
112

4 ·  BIDMAS and reverse problems

Fractions obey BIDMAS exactly like whole numbers: brackets, indices, division and multiplication (left to right), then addition and subtraction. And a question such as “23 of a number is 24” gives the result and asks for the input — undo the multiplication by dividing, which means multiplying by the reciprocal.

23 of n = 24  ⇔  n = 24 × 32 = 36 Divide by the fraction — multiply by its reciprocal
Example 5Work out 112 + 23 × ( 34 − 18 ), giving your answer as a mixed number in its lowest terms.
1
Brackets first. LCM of 4 and 8 is 8. 3/4 − 1/8 = 6/8 − 1/8 = 5/8
2
Multiplication next — cancel the 2 with the 8. 2/3 × 5/8 = (1 × 5)/(3 × 4) = 5/12
3
Addition last. Write 1½ as 32; LCM of 2 and 12 is 12. 3/2 + 5/12 = 18/12 + 5/12 = 23/12
4
Convert to a mixed number. 23 ÷ 12 = 1 r 11  →  1 11/12
11112
Watch out Adding first would give a completely different answer — the multiplication outranks the addition.
Example 6Amira spends 38 of her money and has $45 left. How much did she start with?
1
The $45 is not 38 of the money — it is what remains. 1 − 3/8 = 5/8 of the money = $45
2
Divide by 58 — multiply by the reciprocal. 45 × 8/5 = 9 × 8 = 72
3
Check forwards: she spends 38 of 72. 72 ÷ 8 × 3 = 27  →  72 − 27 = 45 ✓
$72.00
Method note Every reverse problem has the same shape: find what fraction the given amount represents, then divide by that fraction. Writing that one sentence before calculating is worth a mark on its own.

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Adding the denominators 12 + 13 is 56, never 25. Only the numerators combine — and only once the denominators match.
Flipping the wrong fraction — or a mixed number In keep–change–flip, only the second fraction is flipped, and a mixed number must be converted to an improper fraction before any flipping happens.
Wrong form of the final answer Leaving 200480 instead of 512, or giving 1712 when the question asked for a mixed number, throws away the last mark of a correct calculation.
Exam tip Check every fraction answer with decimals: the fraction line is a division sign, so 39/12 − 22/12 = 17/12 can be verified as 3.25 − 1.83̇ = 1.416̇ in seconds on a calculator — and the written common-denominator line is where the method mark lives.
