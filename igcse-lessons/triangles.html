Triangles

The angle sum, the exterior angle rule and isosceles reasoning — the three tools that unlock almost every geometry question.

Learning objectives What you will be able to do

  • Name a triangle by its sides (equilateral, isosceles, scalene) and by its angles (acute-, right- or obtuse-angled).
  • Use the angle sum of a triangle, a + b + c = 180°, with a correct reason.
  • Apply the exterior angle rule: exterior angle = sum of the two opposite interior angles.
  • Spot isosceles triangles and use “base angles of an isosceles triangle” in angle-chase questions.
  • Set up and solve equations when angles are given algebraically.

1 ·  Naming triangles

A triangle is named twice: once by its sides and once by its angles. A question may say “the triangle is isosceles” and expect you to supply the equal angles yourself, so learn both lists properly.

Equilateral

All three sides equal, all three angles 60°.

Isosceles

Two sides equal. The base angles (opposite the equal sides) are equal.

Scalene

All three sides different, all three angles different.

Right-angled

One angle is 90°. The longest side, opposite it, is the hypotenuse.

By angles as well All angles below 90°: acute-angled. One angle above 90°: obtuse-angled. A triangle can have at most one angle of 90° or more — two would already use up the whole 180°.

2 ·  The angle sum is always 180°

a + b + c = 180° The three interior angles of any triangle

Why it is 180°

Draw a line through the top vertex parallel to the base. The two outer angles at the top are alternate angles with the base angles, so the three angles at the top vertex are just a, c and b rearranged — and they lie on a straight line.

Example 1The angles of a triangle are 3x°, 4x° and 5x°. Find the three angles and name the type of triangle.
1
Use the angle sum of a triangle. 3x + 4x + 5x = 180
2
Simplify and solve. 12x = 180  →  x = 15
3
Substitute back into each expression. 3x = 45°,   4x = 60°,   5x = 75°
45 + 60 + 75 = 180 ✓
4
All three angles are different and all are less than 90°. scalene and acute-angled
45°, 60° and 75° — a scalene, acute-angled triangle

3 ·  The exterior angle rule

Extend one side of a triangle. The angle formed outside is the exterior angle, and it equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles — the two you did not touch.

exterior angle = sum of the two opposite interior angles Reason: exterior angle of a triangle

Both routes work: add the two far angles, or take the neighbouring interior angle from 180° (angles on a straight line). If the two routes disagree, an earlier step is wrong — that is a free check.

Example 2In triangle ABC, side AB is extended to D. ∠CBD = 128° and ∠BAC = 51°. Find ∠ACB and ∠ABC.
1
The exterior angle at B equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles, at A and C. 128 = 51 + ∠ACB  (exterior angle of a triangle)
2
Solve. ∠ACB = 128 − 51 = 77°
3
∠ABC and the exterior angle sit on the straight line ABD. ∠ABC = 180 − 128 = 52°  (angles on a straight line)
4
Check the angle sum. 51 + 77 + 52 = 180 ✓
∠ACB = 77°  and  ∠ABC = 52°

4 ·  Isosceles reasoning

A large share of geometry marks comes from spotting an isosceles triangle. Two equal sides force two equal angles, and two equal angles force two equal sides — the rule works both ways.

Base angles of an isosceles triangle In a triangle with two equal sides, the angles opposite those sides are equal. The reason to write is “base angles of an isosceles triangle”.

Given the apex, find the base angles

base angle = 180° − apex2

Given a base angle, find the apex

apex = 180° − 2 × base angle
Example 3Triangle PQR has PQ = PR and ∠QPR = 34°. Find ∠PQR.
1
PQ = PR, so the angles opposite those sides are equal — the angles at R and Q. ∠PQR = ∠PRQ  (base angles of an isosceles triangle)
2
Call each base angle x and use the angle sum. 34 + x + x = 180
3
Solve and check. 2x = 146  →  x = 73°
34 + 73 + 73 = 180 ✓
∠PQR = 73°
Exam tip Write the letters of the equal sides down first. It stops you pairing the wrong angles, which is the single commonest error in this topic.
Example 4Triangle ABC has AB = AC and ∠ABC = 68°. BC is extended to D. Find ∠BAC and ∠ACD.
1
AB = AC, so the angles opposite them — at C and at B — are equal. ∠ACB = 68°  (base angles of an isosceles triangle)
2
Use the angle sum for the apex. ∠BAC = 180 − 68 − 68 = 44°  (angle sum of a triangle)
3
∠ACD is the exterior angle at C. Use either route: ∠ACD = 180 − 68 = 112°  (angles on a straight line)
or ∠ACD = 44 + 68 = 112°  (exterior angle of a triangle)
∠BAC = 44°  and  ∠ACD = 112°

Practice Now you try

Do every question on paper first, then open the solution and mark your own working.

Practice 1Find the third angle of a triangle whose other angles are (a) 43° and 78°  (b) 90° and 27°. (c) A triangle has angles 2y°, 3y°, 4y° — find them.
1
(a) and (b): subtract from 180. (a) 180 − 43 − 78 = 59°    (b) 180 − 90 − 27 = 63°
2
(c) Use the angle sum to build an equation. 9y = 180  →  y = 20  →  40°, 60°, 80°
(a) 59°  (b) 63°  (c) 40°, 60° and 80°
Practice 2An isosceles triangle has an apex angle of 96°. Find each base angle. Another isosceles triangle has a base angle of 37° — find its apex angle and say whether it is acute- or obtuse-angled.
1
Base angle from the apex. (180 − 96) ÷ 2 = 84 ÷ 2 = 42°  (check: 96 + 42 + 42 = 180 ✓)
2
Apex from a base angle. 180 − 2 × 37 = 180 − 74 = 106°
3
106 > 90, so the triangle is obtuse-angled.
Base angles 42° each; apex 106°, an obtuse-angled triangle
Practice 3A triangle has an exterior angle of 132°. One of the opposite interior angles is 45°. Find the other two interior angles.
1
Exterior = sum of the two opposite interior angles. other opposite interior = 132 − 45 = 87°
2
The third interior angle is on a straight line with the exterior angle. 180 − 132 = 48°  (check: 45 + 87 + 48 = 180 ✓)
87° and 48°
Practice 4A triangle has an exterior angle of (4x − 5)° and opposite interior angles (x + 30)° and (2x + 5)°. Find all three interior angles.
1
Apply the exterior angle rule. 4x − 5 = (x + 30) + (2x + 5) = 3x + 35
2
Solve. x = 40
3
Find the angles. x + 30 = 70°,   2x + 5 = 85°,   exterior = 155°
third angle = 180 − 155 = 25°  (check: 70 + 85 + 25 = 180 ✓)
70°, 85° and 25°
Practice 5AB is parallel to CD, with P on AB and Q, R on CD. ∠APQ = 62° and ∠BPR = 39°. Find the three angles of triangle PQR, giving a reason for each.
1
Use the parallels for each transversal. ∠PQR = 62°  and  ∠PRQ = 39°  (alternate angles)
2
Then the angle sum — or the straight line at P; both give the same number. ∠QPR = 180 − 62 − 39 = 79°  (angle sum of a triangle)
∠PQR = 62°,   ∠PRQ = 39°,   ∠QPR = 79°

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Adding the wrong angles in the exterior angle rule The exterior angle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles — not all three, and never the neighbouring one. Point at the two angles you are adding before you write anything.
Pairing the wrong angles in an isosceles triangle The equal angles sit opposite the tick-marked equal sides. Never assume the top angle is the apex — the triangle may be drawn on its side.
No reasons written “Angle sum of a triangle”, “exterior angle of a triangle”, “base angles of an isosceles triangle” — the reason is worth a mark of its own in angle-chase questions.
Exam tip Always finish by checking that your three interior angles total 180°. Where two routes exist (exterior angle rule vs straight line), do both — agreement is a free check on all your earlier steps.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1Two angles of a triangle are 64° and 71°. Find the third.
180 − 64 − 71 = 45°.
Exit 2An isosceles triangle has apex 40°. What are its base angles?
(180 − 40) ÷ 2 = 70° each.
Exit 3An exterior angle of a triangle is 110°. What do the two opposite interior angles add up to?
110° — exterior angle = sum of the two opposite interior angles.
