Lines and Angles

The angle facts every geometry question is built from — straight lines, points, crossings and parallel lines — and how to write the reason the examiner is looking for.

Learning objectives What you will be able to do

1 ·  Naming and describing angles

An angle measures turn. Two rays meeting at a point create an angle, and the whole of geometry in this course is built on knowing what to call each one and how big it can be.

NameSizeHow to spot it
Acute0° < a < 90°Sharp — smaller than a corner of a page
Righta = 90°Marked with a small square, never with an arc
Obtuse90° < a < 180°Open, but not yet a straight line
Straighta = 180°A half turn — the two rays form one straight line
Reflex180° < a < 360°The angle measured the “long way round”

The four angle types

Naming with three letters The angle written ∠ABC is the angle at B, between the arms BA and BC. The middle letter is always the vertex. If only one angle sits at a vertex you may write ∠B.

2 ·  The four facts you build everything on

Angles on a straight line

Angles that sit side by side on a straight line add up to 180°.

Reason: angles on a straight line

Angles at a point

Angles that fill a complete turn about a point add up to 360°.

Reason: angles at a point

Vertically opposite angles

When two straight lines cross, the angles opposite each other are equal.

Reason: vertically opposite angles

Complementary / supplementary

Two angles are complementary if they add to 90° and supplementary if they add to 180°.

Example 1Three angles (x + 30)°, 3x° and (2x + 30)° lie on a straight line. Find x and all three angles.
1
Write the fact as an equation. The three angles sit on a straight line, so they total 180°. (x + 30) + 3x + (2x + 30) = 180
2
Collect like terms and solve. 6x + 60 = 180  →  6x = 120  →  x = 20
3
Substitute back into each expression — and check the total. x + 30 = 50°,   3x = 60°,   2x + 30 = 70°
50 + 60 + 70 = 180 ✓
x = 20, and the angles are 50°, 60° and 70°
Exam tip Always substitute back. It costs ten seconds and catches almost every arithmetic slip before the examiner does.
Example 2Four angles meet at a point. They are a right angle, 2x°, 3x° and (x + 30)°. Find x and all four angles.
1
The four angles fill a complete turn, so they add to 360°. The small square means that angle is exactly 90°. 90 + 2x + 3x + (x + 30) = 360
2
Collect like terms and solve. 6x + 120 = 360  →  6x = 240  →  x = 40
3
Substitute back and check the total is 360°. 2x = 80°,   3x = 120°,   x + 30 = 70°
90 + 80 + 120 + 70 = 360 ✓
x = 40; the angles are 90°, 80°, 120° and 70°
Watch out A right angle is marked with a square, never with a number and never with an arc. If you see the square you may use 90° without being told.

3 ·  Parallel lines and a transversal

A line that cuts across two parallel lines is called a transversal. It creates eight angles — but only two different sizes, and they always add to 180°. Every parallel-line question is just a matter of deciding which of the two sizes each angle is.

The eight angles

Here b = c = f = g and a = d = e = h, and any orange plus any blue makes 180°.

Corresponding (F)

Corresponding angles are equal. Same corner at each crossing; the highlighted lines make an F.

Alternate (Z)

Alternate angles are equal. Between the parallels, opposite sides of the transversal; the path makes a Z.

Co-interior (C)

Co-interior angles add to 180°. Same side, between the parallels; the path makes a C. Here 62 + 118 = 180.

Watch out F, Z and C are memory aids, not reasons. In the exam you must write the words “corresponding angles”, “alternate angles” or “co-interior angles”. And every one of them needs the lines to be parallel — if the diagram has no arrows, you cannot use these facts at all.
Example 3Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, making an angle of 68° at the top crossing. Find p (vertically opposite the 68°), q (between the parallels, same side of the transversal as p) and r (vertically opposite q), giving a reason for each.
1
p is directly opposite the 68° angle at the same crossing. p = 68°  (vertically opposite angles)
2
q and p both lie between the parallels, on the same side of the transversal. q = 180 − 68 = 112°  (co-interior angles)
3
r is opposite q at the lower crossing. r = 112°  (vertically opposite angles)
p = 68°,   q = 112°,   r = 112°
Exam tip There is usually more than one valid route. Marks are for a correct value with a correct reason, so pick whichever single step you can justify most cleanly.

4 ·  Multi-step angle chases

A “chase” is a chain of small steps. Examiners award marks for each value with its reason, so never write a bare number.

The construction-line trick If a diagram has a bend or a zig-zag between two parallel lines, draw a third line through the corner, parallel to the other two. It turns one hard angle into two easy alternate angles. Draw it dashed and label it “parallel to AB” — the construction itself earns credit.
Example 4In the diagram AB is parallel to CD, ∠APX = 42° and ∠CQX = 35°. Find ∠PXQ.
1
Draw a dashed line through X parallel to AB (and therefore parallel to CD as well). construction line through X, parallel to AB and CD
2
The upper part of ∠PXQ and ∠APX are alternate angles between AB and the dashed line. upper part = 42°  (alternate angles)
3
The lower part and ∠CQX are alternate angles between the dashed line and CD. lower part = 35°  (alternate angles)
4
Add the two parts. ∠PXQ = 42 + 35 = 77°
∠PXQ = 77°
The pattern For a single zig-zag between parallel lines, the bend angle is simply the sum of the two outer angles — but still show the construction line, because the reason marks live there.

Practice Now you try

Do every question on paper first, then open the solution and mark your own working.

Practice 1Three angles on a straight line are 4x°, (3x + 10)° and (2x − 10)°. Find x and the three angles.
1
Angles on a straight line total 180°. 4x + 3x + 10 + 2x − 10 = 180  →  9x = 180  →  x = 20
2
Substitute back and check. 80°, 70°, 30°  —  80 + 70 + 30 = 180 ✓
x = 20; the angles are 80°, 70° and 30°
Practice 2Four angles at a point are 6y°, 5y°, 4y° and 3y°. Find y and the largest angle.
1
Angles at a point total 360°. 6y + 5y + 4y + 3y = 360  →  18y = 360  →  y = 20
2
The largest angle is 6y. 6 × 20 = 120°
y = 20; the largest angle is 120°
Practice 3Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, and one angle is 73°. Write down, with reasons, the size of (a) the corresponding angle, (b) the alternate angle, (c) the co-interior angle.
1
Corresponding and alternate angles are equal; co-interior angles add to 180°. (a) 73°  (b) 73°  (c) 180 − 73 = 107°
(a) 73° (corresponding angles)  (b) 73° (alternate angles)  (c) 107° (co-interior angles)
Practice 4AB is parallel to CD. A zig-zag runs from P on AB to X to Q on CD, with ∠APX = 28° and ∠CQX = 47°. Find ∠PXQ.
1
Draw a line through X parallel to AB. The two parts of the bend are alternate angles with the given ones. upper part = 28°, lower part = 47°  (alternate angles)
2
Add the parts. ∠PXQ = 28 + 47 = 75°
∠PXQ = 75°
Practice 5Two co-interior angles between parallel lines are (2x + 15)° and (3x − 20)°. Find x and both angles, and state which is obtuse.
1
Co-interior angles add to 180°. 2x + 15 + 3x − 20 = 180  →  5x − 5 = 180  →  x = 37
2
Substitute back and check the total. 2(37) + 15 = 89°,   3(37) − 20 = 91°  —  89 + 91 = 180 ✓
x = 37; the angles are 89° and 91°, and the obtuse one is 91°

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Setting co-interior angles equal Same side between the parallels always means add to 180°. Co-interior angles are equal only in the special case of 90° and 90°. “Alternate” means set them equal; “co-interior” means make them add to 180°.
Using parallel-line reasons without parallel lines Corresponding, alternate and co-interior facts all need the lines to be parallel. If the diagram has no matching arrows, none of those reasons is available.
Writing a bare number with no reason Every step in an angle chase needs a value and a reason, in the exam wording: “angles on a straight line”, “vertically opposite angles”, “alternate angles”… F, Z and C are memory aids, not acceptable reasons.
Exam tip For a zig-zag between parallels, draw a dashed line through the bend parallel to the others and label it. The bend angle becomes the sum of two alternate angles — and the construction itself earns credit.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1Angles at a point add up to…?
360° — a complete turn.
Exit 2One of the angles where a transversal crosses parallel lines is 73°. What is the alternate angle?
73° — alternate angles are equal.
Exit 3An angle is co-interior with a 62° angle. How big is it?
180 − 62 = 118° — co-interior angles sum to 180°.
