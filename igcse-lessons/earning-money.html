Earning Money

Salaries, wages, overtime, piecework and commission — then how deductions and banded income tax turn gross pay into net pay.

  • Calculate overtime pay using multipliers such as time and a half and double time.

1 ·  Salaries, wages and basic pay

Money questions look easy and are answered badly. Almost every mark lost comes from one of three things: using the wrong number of weeks in a year, forgetting that overtime is paid at a higher rate, or giving money to the wrong number of decimal places.

SalaryWage
Quoted asAn amount per yearAn amount per hour (or per item)
PaidUsually monthly — 12 equal paymentsUsually weekly — 52 payments
Extra hoursNormally included; no extra payPaid as overtime at a higher rate
Typical wording“earns $46 800 per annum”“paid $12.50 per hour”
Definition Basic pay is the money earned for the normal, agreed hours — before any overtime, bonus or deduction: basic pay = hours worked × hourly rate.
monthly pay = annual salary12      weekly pay = annual salary52 12 months in a year, 52 weeks in a year — never 4 weeks in a month
Example 1Amara has an annual salary of $46 800, paid monthly. Ben is paid $12.50 per hour and works a basic 38-hour week for 52 weeks. Who earns more in a year, and by how much?
1
Amara’s monthly pay: divide the salary by 12. 46 800 ÷ 12 = $3900.00 per month
2
Ben’s weekly basic pay: hours × rate, then multiply by 52 weeks. 38 × 12.50 = $475.00 per week
475.00 × 52 = $24 700.00 per year
3
Compare the two yearly amounts. 46 800.00 − 24 700.00 = $22 100.00
Amara earns more, by $22 100.00 per year
Exam tip Compare like with like — turn both amounts into the same period (both per year, or both per week) before you subtract.

2 ·  Overtime, piecework and commission

Hours beyond the basic week are paid at a higher rate, described as a multiplier of the basic hourly rate.

Time and a quarter

× 1.25 the basic rate

Time and a half

× 1.5 the basic rate — by far the most common in exams

Double time

× 2 the basic rate — usually Sundays or public holidays

total pay = (basic hours × rate) + (overtime hours × rate × multiplier) Work out each block of hours separately, then add

Not everyone is paid by the hour. Two other systems appear regularly:

Piecework

Paid a fixed amount for each item produced: pay = items × rate per item. A worker paid $1.80 per item who completes 145 items earns 145 × 1.80 = $261.00.

Commission

Paid a percentage of the value sold, often on top of a small basic wage: commission = rate% × value of sales. 3% on $8000 of sales is 0.03 × 8000 = $240.00.

Note Questions very often say commission is paid only on sales above a threshold — “4% on all sales over $2000”. Subtract the threshold before you take the percentage. Read that sentence twice; it is the single most common error in this topic.
Example 2Ben is paid $12.50 per hour for a basic 38-hour week. One week he also works 6 hours at time and a half and 3 hours at double time. Find his total pay for that week.
1
Basic pay first. 38 × 12.50 = $475.00
2
Time-and-a-half rate, then those hours. 1.5 × 12.50 = $18.75 per hour
6 × 18.75 = $112.50
3
Double-time rate, then those hours. 2 × 12.50 = $25.00 per hour
3 × 25.00 = $75.00
4
Add the three blocks. 475.00 + 112.50 + 75.00 = 662.50
Total pay = $662.50
Exam tip Set the working out in three clear lines like this — if the final answer slips, the method marks are still there to be awarded.
Example 3Selina is paid a basic wage of $320 per week plus 4% commission on all sales over $2000. In one week she sells goods worth $9500. Calculate her total pay for the week.
1
Find the sales that actually earn commission. 9500 − 2000 = $7500
2
Take 4% of that amount. 0.04 × 7500 = $300.00
3
Add the basic wage. 320.00 + 300.00 = 620.00
Total pay = $620.00
Common error Taking 4% of the full $9500 gives $380 and a total of $700 — wrong, because the first $2000 of sales earns nothing.

3 ·  Gross pay, deductions and net pay

Definition Gross pay is everything earned before anything is taken off. Deductions are the amounts removed — income tax, pension contributions, health insurance, union fees. Net pay (take-home pay) is what actually reaches the worker.
net pay = gross pay − total deductions Also worth knowing: net pay = gross pay × (100 − d)% if deductions are d% of gross
Exam tip Percentage deductions are always a percentage of gross pay, never of the amount left over. A 5% pension on gross pay of $2850 is 0.05 × 2850 = $142.50.

4 ·  Income tax with an allowance and bands

Most tax systems in exam questions work in two stages: subtract a tax-free allowance first (taxable income = gross income − allowance), then cut the taxable income into bands, each with its own rate. Only the part of the income inside a band is charged at that band’s rate.

How the bands stack up

Example 4Income tax is charged as follows: the first $9000 is tax free; the next $12 000 is taxed at 10%; the next $20 000 at 22%; anything above that at 35%. Marco earns $58 000. Find (a) the tax he pays, (b) his net income, (c) the tax as a percentage of his income.
1
(a) Take off the allowance to get the taxable income. 58 000 − 9000 = $49 000 taxable
2
Tax each band separately. 0.10 × 12 000 = $1200.00
0.22 × 20 000 = $4400.00
3
Whatever is left goes in the top band. 49 000 − 12 000 − 20 000 = $17 000
0.35 × 17 000 = $5950.00
4
Add the three band amounts. 1200.00 + 4400.00 + 5950.00 = $11 550.00
5
(b) Net income = gross − tax. 58 000 − 11 550 = $46 450.00
6
(c) Write the tax as a fraction of the income, then multiply by 100. 11 550 ÷ 58 000 × 100 = 19.913… = 19.9%
(a) $11 550.00    (b) $46 450.00    (c) 19.9% (3 sf)
Watch out Marco is a 35% taxpayer, but his average rate is only 19.9%. Never multiply the whole income by the top rate.

Practice Now you try

Do every question on paper first, then open the solution and mark your own working.

Practice 1Lena’s annual salary is $37 440. Find her pay (a) per month, (b) per week.
1
Divide by 12 for monthly, 52 for weekly. 37 440 ÷ 12 = 3120.00
37 440 ÷ 52 = 720.00
(a) $3120.00 per month    (b) $720.00 per week
Practice 2A worker’s basic rate is $11.20 per hour for a 40-hour week. In one week he also works 5 hours at time and a half and 2 hours at double time. Find his gross pay.
1
Basic pay. 40 × 11.20 = $448.00
2
Overtime blocks, each at its own rate. 1.5 × 11.20 = 16.80  →  5 × 16.80 = $84.00
2 × 11.20 = 22.40  →  2 × 22.40 = $44.80
3
Add the three blocks. 448.00 + 84.00 + 44.80 = 576.80
Gross pay = $576.80
Practice 3A sales assistant is paid a basic wage of $250 per week plus 6% commission on her sales. One week she sells $4350 of goods. Find her total pay.
1
Commission first — no threshold this time, so take 6% of everything. 0.06 × 4350 = $261.00
2
Add the basic wage. 250.00 + 261.00 = 511.00
Total pay = $511.00
Practice 4Gross monthly pay is $3200. Deductions are income tax at 14% of gross, pension at 6% of gross, and a union fee of $12.50. Find the net pay.
1
Tax and pension together are 20% of gross. 0.20 × 3200 = $640.00
2
Add the union fee, then subtract everything from gross. 640.00 + 12.50 = $652.50
3200.00 − 652.50 = 2547.50
Net pay = $2547.50
Practice 5Tax is charged like this: the first $8500 is tax free, the next $15 000 is taxed at 12%, and anything above that at 30%. Adeline earns $46 000. Find (a) the tax paid, (b) her net income, (c) the tax as a percentage of her income, to 3 significant figures.
1
Taxable income first. 46 000 − 8500 = $37 500
2
Tax each band. 0.12 × 15 000 = $1800.00
37 500 − 15 000 = 22 500  →  0.30 × 22 500 = $6750.00
3
Add the bands, then find net income and the average rate. 1800 + 6750 = $8550.00
46 000 − 8550 = $37 450.00
8550 ÷ 46 000 × 100 = 18.586…
(a) $8550.00    (b) $37 450.00    (c) 18.6% (3 sf)

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Treating a month as four weeks Twelve lots of four weeks is only 48 weeks. Yearly ↔ monthly uses 12; yearly ↔ weekly uses 52 — never mix the two.
Commission on the whole sales figure “4% on all sales over $2000” means you subtract $2000 first. Taking the percentage of the full amount is the single most common error in this topic.
Taxing the whole income at the top rate Banded tax charges each slice at its own rate. Subtract the allowance, fill the bands one by one, and only the final slice pays the top rate.
Exam tip When deductions are d% of gross, the single multiplier × (100 − d)/100 gets net pay in one calculator step — 15% deductions means net = 0.85 × gross. And give every money answer to 2 decimal places unless told otherwise.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1Kwame is paid $9.60 per hour and works a basic 37-hour week. Find his basic weekly pay.
37 × 9.60 = $355.20
Exit 2An hourly rate is $14.40. What is the double-time rate, and the time-and-a-half rate?
Double time $28.80; time and a half 1.5 × 14.40 = $21.60.
Exit 3Gross pay is $2850 and total deductions are $553. Write down the net pay.
2850 − 553 = $2297.00
