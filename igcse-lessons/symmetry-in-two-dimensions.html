Symmetry in Two Dimensions

Lines of symmetry and order of rotational symmetry — how to count them, the standard-shape results you must know by heart, and how to complete a shape point by point.

Learning objectives What you will be able to do

  • Identify and count the lines of symmetry of a shape using the folding or mirror test.
  • Find the order of rotational symmetry, and the angle of the smallest rotation 360° ÷ order.
  • Recall the lines of symmetry and rotational order of every standard triangle, quadrilateral and regular polygon.
  • Complete a shape or pattern so that it has a given line or rotational symmetry, working point by point.

1 ·  Line symmetry

A shape has line symmetry if one half is the mirror image of the other. The mirror is called a line of symmetry (also an axis of symmetry or a mirror line).

Definition A line of symmetry is a line about which a shape can be reflected onto itself. Every point of the shape has a matching point the same perpendicular distance on the other side of the line.

Test 1 — the folding test

Imagine folding the shape along the line. If the two halves land exactly on top of each other, with no overhang, the fold line is a line of symmetry. This is the test to use in the exam room: trace the shape, fold, look.

Test 2 — the mirror test

Put a mirror along the line. If what you see (half the shape plus its reflection) looks identical to the whole original shape, it is a line of symmetry. Tracing paper does the same job: trace one half, flip it over the line, check it fits.

Counting the mirror lines

Watch out A diagonal of a rectangle is not a line of symmetry. Fold a non-square rectangle along its diagonal and the two halves do not fit — they are congruent, but one is a rotation of the other, not a reflection. The same trap appears with the diagonal of a parallelogram.
Example 1Write down the number of lines of symmetry of (a) an isosceles trapezium, (b) a parallelogram that is not a rectangle, (c) a regular pentagon, (d) a kite.
1
(a) An isosceles trapezium has one pair of equal sloping sides, so folding down the middle matches them. 1 line — the vertical line through the midpoints of the parallel sides
2
(b) Try both diagonals and both centre lines of a parallelogram — none of them fold onto themselves. 0 lines
3
(c) A regular polygon with n sides has n lines of symmetry. 5 lines
4
(d) A kite folds along the diagonal joining the two vertices where unequal sides meet. 1 line — the long diagonal only
(a) 1    (b) 0    (c) 5    (d) 1

2 ·  Rotational symmetry

Turn a shape about its centre. If it fits exactly onto its own outline before you have gone all the way round, it has rotational symmetry. The order counts how many times it fits in one complete turn.

Definition The order of rotational symmetry is the number of positions, in a full turn of 360°, in which the shape looks exactly the same as it did at the start. The fixed point it turns about is the centre of rotation.
angle of the smallest rotation = 360°order Order 4 → it fits every 90°. Order 3 → every 120°.

Order 2 and order 3

Order 1 means none Every shape fits onto itself after a full 360° turn, so the smallest possible order is 1. An answer of “order 1” means the shape has no rotational symmetry. Never write order 0.
Example 2Write down the order of rotational symmetry of (a) a rectangle, (b) an equilateral triangle, (c) a regular octagon, (d) a scalene triangle. For (c), state the angle of the smallest rotation.
1
(a) A rectangle turned 180° about its centre fits its outline; a quarter turn does not (unless it is a square). order 2
2
(b) and (c) A regular n-sided polygon has order n. order 3  and  order 8
3
(d) A scalene triangle has no matching sides, so only a full turn works. order 1 (no rotational symmetry)
4
Smallest rotation for the octagon: 360° ÷ 8 = 45°
(a) 2    (b) 3    (c) 8, rotating 45°    (d) 1

Exam tip For a regular polygon the two numbers are always the same as the number of sides. So a regular polygon with 12 lines of symmetry has order 12, turns through 360° ÷ 12 = 30°, has exterior angle 30° and interior angle 150°. One fact unlocks four answers.
Example 3A regular polygon has rotational symmetry of order 12. Find (a) the number of lines of symmetry, (b) the angle of the smallest rotation that maps it onto itself, (c) the size of one interior angle.
1
(a) For a regular polygon the order equals the number of sides, and the number of lines of symmetry also equals the number of sides. 12 sides → 12 lines of symmetry
2
(b) Divide a full turn by the order. 360° ÷ 12 = 30°
3
(c) The exterior angle of a regular 12-gon is also 360° ÷ 12 = 30°, and interior + exterior = 180°. interior angle = 180° − 30° = 150°
(a) 12    (b) 30°    (c) 150°

4 ·  Completing a shape

A very common question gives you part of a shape and the symmetry it must have, and asks you to finish it. Work point by point — never try to sketch the whole thing by eye.

Completing for line symmetry

For each marked point, count the squares perpendicular to the mirror line, then count the same number across on the other side and mark the image. Join the images in the same order.

Completing for rotational symmetry

Put tracing paper over the shape, mark the centre, trace, then turn the paper through 360° ÷ order and copy the shape into its new position. Repeat until you are back at the start.

Example 4A triangle has vertices at (1, 1), (3, 1) and (1, 2). A second triangle is drawn so that the completed shape has line symmetry about x = 4. Find the coordinates of the second triangle’s vertices.
1
The mirror is vertical, so the y-coordinates do not change. Only x moves.
2
Find how far each point is from the mirror line, then go that far past it: (1, 1) is 3 to the left of x = 4, so its image is 3 to the right. x’ = 4 + 3 = 7  →  (7, 1)
3
Repeat. A quick rule for a vertical mirror x = a is x’ = 2a − x, here x’ = 8 − x. (3, 1) → (5, 1)    (1, 2) → (7, 2)
(7, 1),  (5, 1)  and  (7, 2)

Practice 4A triangle has vertices (1, 1), (4, 1) and (1, 3). The completed shape must have line symmetry about y = 4. Write down the coordinates of the three image vertices.
1
The mirror is horizontal, so x stays and y’ = 2(4) − y = 8 − y. (1, 1) → (1, 7)   (4, 1) → (4, 7)   (1, 3) → (1, 5)

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Folding along a diagonal that does not work The diagonal of a rectangle or parallelogram is not a line of symmetry — the two halves are congruent but one is a rotation of the other, not a reflection. Test every candidate line by folding, never by instinct.
Confusing the two kinds of symmetry N, S and Z have no line of symmetry at all, yet a half turn maps each onto itself, so their order is 2. A parallelogram is the shape version of the same trap: 0 lines, order 2. Always answer both parts separately.
Writing order 0 The smallest possible order is 1, because every shape fits itself after a full turn. “No rotational symmetry” is written as order 1, never order 0.
Exam tip When completing a pattern, a half turn about the centre sends the square in row r, column c of an n × n grid to row n + 1 − r, column n + 1 − c. Rotating is not the same as reflecting — shading the mirror-image squares instead is the classic lost mark.

Exit 1How many lines of symmetry does an equilateral triangle have, and what is its order?

Exit 2A shape has rotational symmetry of order 5. What is the smallest angle of rotation that maps it onto itself?
360° ÷ 5 = 72°.
Exit 3Give a shape with rotational symmetry of order 2 but no line of symmetry.
A parallelogram (that is not a rectangle or rhombus) — or the letters N, S, Z.
