Prime Numbers

What makes a number prime, how to test one quickly, and how writing a number as a product of primes unlocks roots, squares, cubes, HCFs and LCMs.

1 ·  What a prime number is

Definition A prime number is a positive integer with exactly two distinct factors: 1 and itself. A positive integer with more than two factors is composite.
Why 1 is not prime 1 has only one factor (itself), not two, so it fails the definition. This is not an arbitrary rule: if 1 counted as prime, then 12 = 2² × 3 = 1 × 2² × 3 = 1² × 2² × 3 would all be different prime factorisations. Excluding 1 keeps every factorisation unique.
Note 2 is the only even prime — every other even number has 2 as a third factor. “Write down the only even prime number” is a real exam mark.

2 ·  Testing whether a number is prime

You do not have to try every divisor. If n had a factor larger than √n, its partner would be smaller than √n — so you would already have found it.

Test only the primes up to √n If none of them divides n, then n is prime
Example 1Show that 397 is a prime number.
1
Find the square root and round up — that is how far you must test. √397 = 19.92… → test primes up to 19
2
Clear the easy ones with divisibility tests. 2: 397 is odd ✗    3: 3+9+7 = 19 ✗
5: does not end in 0 or 5 ✗    11: 3 − 9 + 7 = 1 ✗
3
Divide by the remaining primes 7, 13, 17, 19. 397 ÷ 7 = 56.71…    397 ÷ 13 = 30.54…
397 ÷ 17 = 23.35…    397 ÷ 19 = 20.89…
4
Why stop at 19? The next prime is 23, and 23² = 529 > 397 — any factor above 19 would need a partner below 19, already ruled out.
397 has exactly two factors, 1 and 397, so it is prime

3 ·  Writing a number as a product of primes

Key idea Every integer greater than 1 can be written as a product of primes in exactly one way (apart from the order). Different starting splits of a factor tree always give the same final answer.

Split the number into any factor pair, then keep splitting until every branch ends on a prime. Finish by writing the answer in index form, smallest prime first.

Exam tip “Write 360 as a product of its prime factors” means the answer must be a product: 2³ × 3² × 5. The list “2, 3, 5” scores nothing — the multiplication signs are part of the answer.
Example 2Use the division ladder to write 1176 as a product of primes in index form.
1
Keep halving while the number is even. 1176 ÷ 2 = 588    588 ÷ 2 = 294    294 ÷ 2 = 147
2
147 is odd; digit sum 1+4+7 = 12, so divide by 3. 147 ÷ 3 = 49
3
49 is not divisible by 2, 3 or 5. Try 7. 49 ÷ 7 = 7    7 ÷ 7 = 1 — stop
4
Read the divisors down the ladder, group them, and check. 1176 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 7 × 7 = 2³ × 3 × 7²
8 × 3 × 49 = 24 × 49 = 1176 ✓
1176 = 2³ × 3 × 7²

4 ·  What prime factorisation is for

Prime factorisation is not an end in itself — it is the tool behind four different exam questions.

Square roots — halve the indices

A number is a perfect square exactly when every index is even. Halve each index to get the root.

1764 = 2² × 3² × 7² → √1764 = 2 × 3 × 7 = 42

Cube roots — divide the indices by 3

A perfect cube has every index a multiple of 3.

3375 = 3³ × 5³ → ∛3375 = 3 × 5 = 15

HCF — lowest powers

Only the primes common to both, each to its lowest power.

126 = 2 × 3² × 7
168 = 2³ × 3 × 7
HCF = 2 × 3 × 7 = 42

LCM — highest powers

Every prime that appears, each to its highest power.

LCM = 2³ × 3² × 7 = 504

Check For the pair above, 42 × 504 = 21 168 = 126 × 168 — the HCF × LCM product rule agrees, as it must for two numbers.
Example 3Find the smallest positive integer k such that 720k is a perfect square.
1
Factorise 720. 720 = 72 × 10 = (2³ × 3²) × (2 × 5) = 24 × 3² × 5
2
A perfect square needs every index even. Inspect them. 24 → even ✓    3² → even ✓    51 → odd ✗
3
Only the 5 is short — supply one more 5 to lift its index from 1 to 2. k = 5
4
Verify. 720 × 5 = 3600 = 24 × 3² × 5²
√3600 = 2² × 3 × 5 = 60 ✓
k = 5, and 720k = 3600 = 60²
Watch out The question says smallest. k = 20 also gives a square (14 400 = 120²), but it is not the smallest, so it scores nothing.

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Calling 1 (or 51, 57, 87, 91) prime 1 has only one factor, so it is not prime — and the classic traps 51 = 3 × 17, 57 = 3 × 19, 87 = 3 × 29, 91 = 7 × 13 are all composite.
Writing a list instead of a product “Product of prime factors” means 2³ × 3² × 5, with the multiplication signs. The bare list “2, 3, 5” scores nothing.
Guessing the multiplier for a square or cube Work prime by prime: top up each index to the next multiple of 2 (square) or 3 (cube). Guessing almost always misses a prime, and there are no marks for a nearly-right answer.
Exam tip When testing for primes, run the instant divisibility tests for 2, 3, 5 and 11 first — they remove most candidates before you touch the calculator, and you only ever need primes up to √n.
