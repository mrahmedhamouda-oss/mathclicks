Borrowing and Investing Money

Simple and compound interest, depreciation and hire purchase — and how to tell instantly which formula a question wants.

1 ·  Simple interest

With simple interest the interest is worked out on the original amount every single year. The money invested (or borrowed) is called the principal, and it never changes — so the interest is the same amount each year.

I = P r t100 I = interest, P = principal, r = rate per year (%), t = time in years
Key idea The formula gives you the interest only. If a question asks for the total amount or the value of the investment, add the principal back on: A = P + I.
Watch out t must be in years — nine months is t = 0.75. And r goes into the formula as the plain number 3.5, not as 0.035 — the ÷ 100 is already there.
Example 1$4500 is invested at 3.5% per year simple interest for 4 years. Find (a) the interest earned, (b) the value of the investment at the end.
1
Write down what each letter is worth. P = 4500,   r = 3.5,   t = 4
2
(a) Substitute into I = Prt ÷ 100. I = (4500 × 3.5 × 4) ÷ 100 = 63 000 ÷ 100 = 630
3
(b) The value is principal plus interest. 4500.00 + 630.00 = 5130.00
(a) interest = $630.00    (b) value = $5130.00
Exam tip Sense-check it: one year’s interest is 0.035 × 4500 = 157.50, and 4 × 157.50 = 630. Same answer — and it shows you understand what simple interest means.

2 ·  Compound interest

With compound interest the interest earned is added to the account, and next year’s interest is worked out on the new, larger amount. You earn interest on your interest, so the growth speeds up year after year.

V = P(1 + r100)n V = final value, P = principal, r = rate per year (%), n = number of years

The multiplier

An increase of r% is a multiplier of 1 + r/100: 3.5% → × 1.035, 7% → × 1.07, 12.5% → × 1.125. Compounding for n years just means using that multiplier n times — which is the power.

Simple or compound?

  • Simple: a fixed amount every year; the graph is a straight line.
  • Compound: interest grows each year; the graph curves upwards.
  • Over 1 year they give exactly the same answer; after that compound always gives more.
Key idea This formula gives the final value, not the interest. “How much is in the account” wants V; “how much interest” wants V − P.
Example 2$4500 is invested at 3.5% per year compound interest for 4 years. Find the interest earned, and how much better this is than the simple interest in the last example.
1
Write the multiplier for a 3.5% increase. 1 + 3.5 ÷ 100 = 1.035
2
Substitute with n = 4. V = 4500 × 1.0354 = 4500 × 1.147523… = 5163.8535…
3
Round the value to 2 decimal places, then subtract the principal. V = $5163.85
5163.85 − 4500.00 = $663.85
4
Compare with the simple interest of $630.00. 663.85 − 630.00 = $33.85 more
Interest = $663.85 — $33.85 more than simple interest
Watch out Keep the full value in your calculator until the very last step. Rounding 1.0354 to 1.15 first gives $675 and loses the accuracy mark.

3 ·  Depreciation

Cars, machinery, computers and phones lose a percentage of their value each year. This is depreciation — compound interest running backwards.

V = P(1 − r100)n Exactly the same structure — the plus sign becomes a minus sign
Note A 15% loss means a multiplier of 1 − 0.15 = 0.85. Because the multiplier is less than 1, the value falls quickly at first and then more slowly — it never actually reaches zero.
Example 3A car is bought for $18 000. It depreciates by 15% each year. Find its value after 5 years, and the total loss in value.
1
Write the multiplier for a 15% decrease. 1 − 15 ÷ 100 = 0.85
2
Substitute into V = P(1 − r/100)n. V = 18 000 × 0.855 = 18 000 × 0.443705… = 7986.6956…
3
Round to 2 decimal places, then find the loss. V = $7986.70
18 000.00 − 7986.70 = $10 013.30
Value after 5 years = $7986.70; loss = $10 013.30
Common error Losing 15% a year for 5 years is not losing 75%. Repeated percentage change is never the same as adding the percentages up.

4 ·  Hire purchase

Definition Hire purchase (HP) lets you take the goods now and pay in instalments: a deposit first (usually a percentage of the cash price), then a fixed number of equal payments. The total paid is always more than the cash price — the difference is the cost of borrowing.
total HP price = deposit + (number of instalments × instalment) extra cost = total HP price − cash price
Example 4A washing machine has a cash price of $1250. On hire purchase a customer pays a deposit of 20% plus 24 monthly instalments of $47.50. Find (a) the total hire purchase price, (b) the extra amount paid, (c) that extra as a percentage of the cash price.
1
(a) Deposit is 20% of the cash price. 0.20 × 1250 = $250.00
2
Total of the instalments, then the HP total. 24 × 47.50 = $1140.00
250.00 + 1140.00 = $1390.00
3
(b) Compare with the cash price. 1390.00 − 1250.00 = $140.00
4
(c) Write the extra as a percentage of the cash price. 140 ÷ 1250 × 100 = 11.2%
(a) $1390.00    (b) $140.00 extra    (c) 11.2%
Exam tip The percentage is always taken from the cash price — the amount the customer would have paid without borrowing.

Practice Now you try

Do every question on paper first, then open the solution and mark your own working.

Practice 1$3200 is invested for 3 years at 4.5% per year simple interest. Find the interest earned and the total value.
1
Substitute into I = Prt ÷ 100. I = (3200 × 4.5 × 3) ÷ 100 = $432.00
2
Add the principal for the total. 3200.00 + 432.00 = 3632.00
Interest = $432.00; total value = $3632.00
Practice 2$3200 is invested for 3 years at 4.5% per year compound interest. Find the value, and how much more interest this earns than Practice 1.
1
Multiplier 1.045, power 3. V = 3200 × 1.0453 = 3651.7316… = $3651.73
2
Compare the two amounts of interest. interest = 3651.73 − 3200 = $451.73
451.73 − 432.00 = $19.73
Value = $3651.73 — $19.73 more interest than simple
Practice 3A motorbike costing $6400 depreciates by 18% per year. Find its value after 3 years.
1
Multiplier for an 18% decrease is 0.82. V = 6400 × 0.823 = 6400 × 0.551368 = 3528.7552
Value after 3 years = $3528.76
Practice 4$5000 earns $900 simple interest in 4 years. Find the rate of interest per year.
1
Rearrange I = Prt ÷ 100 for r. r = 100I ÷ (Pt) = (100 × 900) ÷ (5000 × 4)
2
Evaluate. r = 90 000 ÷ 20 000 = 4.5
Rate = 4.5% per year
Practice 5A laptop has a cash price of $960. On hire purchase you pay a 15% deposit and 18 monthly instalments of $52.75. Find the extra amount paid, and express it as a percentage of the cash price.
1
Deposit, instalments, then the HP total. 0.15 × 960 = $144.00
18 × 52.75 = $949.50
144.00 + 949.50 = $1093.50
2
Extra cost, then as a percentage of the cash price. 1093.50 − 960.00 = $133.50
133.50 ÷ 960 × 100 = 13.90625
Extra = $133.50, which is 13.9% (3 sf) of the cash price

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Answering with the wrong quantity I = Prt ÷ 100 gives the interest only; V = P(1 + r/100)n gives the final value. Read the question: “how much interest” and “how much in the account” need different answers.
Adding percentages for repeated change Losing 15% a year for 5 years is not losing 75% — repeated percentage change multiplies, it never adds. Use the multiplier raised to a power.
Rounding too early Keep 1.0354 at full calculator accuracy and round only the final answer — money to 2 decimal places. Rounding the multiplier first destroys the accuracy mark.
Exam tip “Least number of complete years” questions need no algebra: try values of n on your calculator and show the value either side of the target — the one that fails and the one that works. That comparison is what earns the method mark.

Exit check Prove it before you leave
