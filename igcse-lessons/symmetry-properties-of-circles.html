Symmetry Properties of Circles

Chords, tangents and the tangent kite — three symmetry results that each hand you a right-angled triangle, ready for Pythagoras' theorem.

Learning objectives What you will be able to do

  • Use the fact that the perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord, and apply r² = d² + (c ÷ 2)².
  • Recognise that equal chords are equidistant from the centre, and vice versa.
  • Mark the right angle between a tangent and the radius at the point of contact, and use it in calculations.
  • Use the equal-tangent and angle-bisecting properties of two tangents drawn from an external point.

Learn Teach · example · together

1 ·  The perpendicular from the centre to a chord

A circle has infinitely many lines of symmetry, and every one passes through the centre. Fold a circle along a diameter and the two halves match exactly. Every result in this lesson is a consequence of that single fact.

Property 1 The perpendicular drawn from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord. Equivalently, the perpendicular bisector of any chord passes through the centre.

The right-angled triangle this creates

r² = d² + (c ÷ 2)² radius r · distance from centre d · chord length c
Bonus fact Equal chords are equidistant from the centre, and chords equidistant from the centre are equal. The nearer a chord is to the centre, the longer it is — the longest chord of all is the diameter, at distance 0.
Example 1A chord of length 24 cm is drawn in a circle of radius 13 cm. Find the distance of the chord from the centre.
1
Draw the perpendicular from the centre O to the chord AB, meeting it at M. It bisects the chord. AM = MB = 24 ÷ 2 = 12 cm
2
Triangle OMA is right-angled at M, with the radius as the hypotenuse. OM² + 12² = 13²
3
Rearrange and take the square root. OM² = 169 − 144 = 25  →  OM = 5
The chord is 5 cm from the centre
Check 5² + 12² = 169 = 13² ✓ The 5, 12, 13 triple appears constantly in circle questions, along with 3, 4, 5 and 8, 15, 17.

2 ·  The tangent and the radius

A tangent is a straight line that touches the circle at exactly one point — the point of contact. By symmetry it must sit square-on to the radius drawn to that point: if it leaned either way, it would cut the circle twice.

Property 2 A tangent is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of contact: angle between tangent and radius = 90°.

Tangent, radius, right angle

Exam tip When a diagram shows a tangent, mark the right angle at the point of contact before you do anything else. Nine times out of ten that right angle is the whole question — it turns the figure into a right-angled triangle, ready for Pythagoras or trigonometry.
Example 2A circle has centre O and radius 8 cm. P is a point 17 cm from O, and PT is a tangent touching the circle at T. Find PT.
1
Draw the radius OT. Because PT is a tangent, angle OTP = 90°, so triangle OTP is right-angled at T.
2
This time the hypotenuse is OP — the side opposite the right angle, not the radius. OT² + PT² = OP²  →  8² + PT² = 17²
3
Solve. PT² = 289 − 64 = 225  →  PT = 15
PT = 15 cm

3 ·  Two tangents from an external point

From a point outside a circle you can draw exactly two tangents. The whole figure is symmetrical about the line joining the external point to the centre — and that one line of symmetry gives three results at once.

Property 3 If tangents from an external point P touch the circle, centre O, at A and B, then PA = PB, OP bisects both angle APB and angle AOB, and triangles OAP and OBP are congruent right-angled triangles.

The tangent kite

Why the triangles are congruent

  • Right angle: OAP = OBP = 90° (tangent ⊥ radius)
  • Hypotenuse: OP is common to both
  • Side: OA = OB = r (both radii)

RHS congruence, so PA = PB and the two angles at P are equal.

The angle fact you will need

OAPB is a quadrilateral, so its angles add to 360°. Two of them are right angles, which leaves

angle AOB + angle APB = 180°

Know this and most tangent-angle questions are one line of arithmetic.

Example 3Two tangents from P touch a circle, centre O, at A and B. Angle APB = 48°. Find angle AOB, angle APO and angle AOP.
1
Angles of quadrilateral OAPB add to 360°, and the angles at A and B are right angles. angle AOB = 360° − 90° − 90° − 48° = 132°
2
OP bisects angle APB, by symmetry. angle APO = 48° ÷ 2 = 24°
3
In triangle OAP the angles add to 180°. angle AOP = 180° − 90° − 24° = 66°
angle AOB = 132°, angle APO = 24°, angle AOP = 66°
Check OP also bisects angle AOB: 132° ÷ 2 = 66° ✓
Example 4In a circle of radius 10 cm, two parallel chords have lengths 12 cm and 16 cm. Find the distance between them when they are (a) on opposite sides of the centre, (b) on the same side.
1
Distance of the 12 cm chord: half of it is 6 cm. d₁² = 10² − 6² = 64  →  d₁ = 8 cm
2
Distance of the 16 cm chord: half of it is 8 cm. d₂² = 10² − 8² = 36  →  d₂ = 6 cm
3
(a) On opposite sides, the centre lies between the chords, so add. (b) On the same side, subtract. (a) 8 + 6 = 14 cm    (b) 8 − 6 = 2 cm
(a) 14 cm     (b) 2 cm
Watch out A question that just says “two parallel chords” has two answers. Notice also that the longer chord (16 cm) is the one nearer the centre.

Do every question on paper first, then open the solution and mark your own working.

Practice 1A chord of length 30 cm is drawn in a circle of radius 17 cm. How far is the chord from the centre?
1
Half the chord is 15 cm; the radius is the hypotenuse. d² = 17² − 15² = 289 − 225 = 64
d = 8 cm
Practice 2A chord of length 24 cm is 9 cm from the centre of a circle. Find the radius.
1
Half the chord is 12 cm. r² = 9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225
r = 15 cm
Practice 3PT is a tangent to a circle of radius 5 cm with centre O, and OP = 13 cm. Find PT.
1
Right angle at T; OP is the hypotenuse. PT² = 13² − 5² = 169 − 25 = 144
PT = 12 cm
Practice 4Two tangents from P touch a circle, centre O, at A and B, and angle AOB = 116°. Find angle APB and angle OPA.
1
In quadrilateral OAPB the angles at A and B are 90°. angle APB = 360° − 90° − 90° − 116° = 64°
2
OP bisects angle APB. angle OPA = 64° ÷ 2 = 32°
angle APB = 64°, angle OPA = 32°
Practice 5A circle has radius 9 cm and P is 14 cm from the centre. Find the length of a tangent from P, correct to 3 significant figures.
1
Right angle at the point of contact. t² = 14² − 9² = 196 − 81 = 115
2
Square root, round only at the end. t = √115 = 10.7238…
10.7 cm (3 s.f.)

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Putting the radius on the wrong side of Pythagoras In a chord triangle the hypotenuse is always the radius, because it faces the right angle. Writing d² = r² + (c ÷ 2)² is the single most common error in this topic. In a tangent triangle, though, the hypotenuse is OP — check which side faces the right angle every time.
Using the full chord instead of half The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, so only half the chord length goes into the right-angled triangle. Halve first, then square.
Rounding too early Do not round √75 to 8.66 and then double it. Keep the full calculator value until the final line — rounding early costs an accuracy mark.
Exam tip The instant you see a chord, draw the radius to one end and the perpendicular from the centre; the instant you see a tangent, mark the 90° at the point of contact. Learn the triples 3, 4, 5 · 5, 12, 13 · 8, 15, 17 · 7, 24, 25 — they cover most circle questions without a calculator.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1The perpendicular from the centre meets a chord of length 18 cm. How long is each half of the chord?
9 cm — the perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord.
Exit 2What angle does a tangent make with the radius at the point of contact?
90° — tangent and radius are perpendicular.
Exit 3Two tangents from a point make an angle of 40° with each other. What is the angle at the centre between the two radii to the points of contact?
180° − 40° = 140°
