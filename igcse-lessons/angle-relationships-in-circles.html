Angle Relationships in Circles

The circle theorems — learn each one with the exact wording the examiner expects, because half the marks are in the reason.

Starter 1A triangle has two sides that are radii of the same circle. What type of triangle is it, and what does that tell you about its angles?
Isosceles — the two base angles (opposite the equal radii) are equal.
Starter 2Angles on a straight line and angles in a quadrilateral add up to what?
180° on a straight line; 360° in a quadrilateral.
Starter 3What angle does a tangent make with the radius drawn to the point of contact?

Learn Teach · example · together

1 ·  Angles at the centre and at the circumference

An angle is at the centre if its vertex is at O, and at the circumference if its vertex lies on the circle. When both angles stand on the same arc, they are locked together.

The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference

angle AOB = 2 × angle APB Reason: the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference
Two theorems come free with it Angle in a semicircle: if AB is a diameter, the centre angle is 180°, so angle ACB = 90° for any C on the circle. Angles in the same segment are equal: two circumference angles on the same arc are each half of the same centre angle, so they equal each other — look for the bow-tie shape.
Example 1A, B and C lie on a circle centre O. Angle OBC = 28°. Find angle BAC.
1
OB and OC are both radii, so triangle OBC is isosceles. angle OCB = angle OBC = 28°
2
Angle sum of triangle OBC. angle BOC = 180° − 28° − 28° = 124°
3
Angle BOC is at the centre and angle BAC at the circumference, both on arc BC. angle BAC = 124° ÷ 2 = 62°
angle BAC = 62°
Exam tip Two radii always make an isosceles triangle. That single observation unlocks a large fraction of circle questions.
Example 2PQ is a diameter of a circle and R lies on the circle. Angle RPQ = 27°. Find angle PQR.
1
PQ is a diameter, so the angle at R is a right angle. angle PRQ = 90°  (angle in a semicircle)
2
Use the angle sum of triangle PQR. angle PQR = 180° − 90° − 27° = 63°
angle PQR = 63°
Exam tip Whenever a question mentions a diameter, write “90° — angle in a semicircle” on the diagram before you do anything else.

2 ·  The cyclic quadrilateral

A cyclic quadrilateral has all four vertices on the circle. Its opposite angles are supplementary — they add to 180°.

Opposite angles add to 180°

angle A + angle C = 180°   and   angle B + angle D = 180° Reason: opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary

It follows that the exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral equals the interior opposite angle — useful, and worth quoting by name.

Watch out All four vertices must lie on the circle. A quadrilateral with one vertex at the centre, or inside or outside the circle, is not cyclic and the theorem does not apply.
Example 3ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral. Angle ABC = 96° and angle BCD = 71°. Find angle ADC and angle DAB.
1
ABC and ADC are opposite angles. angle ADC = 180° − 96° = 84°
2
BCD and DAB are opposite angles. angle DAB = 180° − 71° = 109°
3
Check: the four angles of any quadrilateral add to 360°. 96 + 71 + 84 + 109 = 360  ✓
angle ADC = 84°, angle DAB = 109°

3 ·  The alternate segment theorem

When a tangent meets a chord at the point of contact, the angle between them equals the angle the chord subtends in the segment on the other side of the chord — the alternate segment.

Tangent–chord angle = angle in the alternate segment

angle between tangent and chord = angle in the alternate segment Reason: alternate segment theorem
Example 4TA is a tangent to a circle at A. B and C lie on the circle, the angle between TA and chord AB is 63°, and angle BAC = 44°. Find angle ACB and angle ABC.
1
The tangent–chord angle equals the angle subtended by AB at C. angle ACB = 63°  (alternate segment theorem)
2
Angle sum of triangle ABC. angle ABC = 180° − 63° − 44° = 73°
angle ACB = 63°, angle ABC = 73°
Watch out The alternate segment is the one on the other side of the chord from the angle you are given. Shade it lightly on the diagram if you are unsure.

4 ·  A method that always works

Four steps for any circle-angle problem

  1. Mark what you are given — the centre, any diameter, any tangent, equal radii.
  2. Hunt for the shapes: arrowhead (centre theorem), bow tie (same segment), semicircle (right angle), four points on the circle (cyclic quadrilateral), tangent touching (alternate segment or perpendicular radius).
  3. Fill in every angle you can, one at a time, writing the reason next to each.
  4. Remember the ordinary facts too: angle sums of triangles and straight lines, and that any triangle with two radii as sides is isosceles.
Exam tip One mark is for the angle, one is for the reason. Write the reason in full — “angles in the same segment” scores; “circle theorem” does not.

Practice 1P, Q and R lie on a circle centre O. Angle POQ = 146°. Find angle PRQ.
1
The angle at the circumference is half the angle at the centre on the same arc. angle PRQ = 146° ÷ 2 = 73°
73° (angle at centre is twice the angle at the circumference)
Practice 2AB is a diameter and C is on the circle. Angle ABC = 41°. Find angle BAC.
1
Angle ACB = 90° (angle in a semicircle).
2
Angle sum of the triangle. angle BAC = 180° − 90° − 41° = 49°
49°
Practice 3WXYZ is a cyclic quadrilateral with angle WXY = 103°. Find angle WZY.
1
Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°. angle WZY = 180° − 103° = 77°
77°
Practice 4A, B, C and D lie on a circle. Angle ACB = 25°. Write down angle ADB, with a reason.
1
Both angles stand on chord AB, with C and D on the same side of it — the bow-tie shape.
25° — angles in the same segment are equal.
Practice 5BD is a diameter of a circle through A, B, C, D. Angle ADB = 32°. Find angle ABD and angle BCD, giving a reason for each.
1
BD is a diameter, so the angle at A is a right angle. angle BAD = 90°  (angle in a semicircle)
2
Angle sum of triangle ABD. angle ABD = 180° − 90° − 32° = 58°
3
BD is also a diameter for triangle BCD. angle BCD = 90°  (angle in a semicircle)
angle ABD = 58°, angle BCD = 90°

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Pairing angles on different arcs The centre theorem only links two angles standing on the same arc. Trace the arrowhead from A round to B for both angles before you double or halve anything.
Calling any quadrilateral cyclic Opposite angles add to 180° only when all four vertices lie on the circle. A quadrilateral with one vertex at the centre (like OABC) is not cyclic — use the centre theorem or radii triangles instead.
Giving the angle without the reason “x = 63°” alone can lose half the marks. Every angle needs its theorem quoted in full, in the expected wording.
Exam tip Fill in every angle you can find, one at a time, writing the reason beside each — even angles the question did not ask for. Circle questions are chains: the angle you need is usually two or three easy steps away from the one you were given.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1The angle at the centre on arc AB is 84°. What is the angle at the circumference on the same arc?
42° — the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.
Exit 2One angle of a cyclic quadrilateral is 112°. What is the opposite angle?
180° − 112° = 68°
Exit 3A tangent makes an angle of 58° with a chord. What angle does that chord subtend in the alternate segment?
58° — alternate segment theorem.
