Revisiting Fractions

Equivalent fractions, lowest terms and mixed numbers — then use a common denominator to order any set of fractions, and decide whether a fraction terminates or recurs as a decimal.

Learning objectives What you will be able to do

  • Find a fraction of a quantity, including “of the remainder” problems.

1 ·  What a fraction actually means

A fraction is a division waiting to happen. The line between the two numbers is a division sign, so 34 = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75. That single idea explains everything else in this lesson.

Definition In ab the numerator a counts how many parts you have, and the denominator b says how many equal parts the whole was cut into. The denominator can never be 0.

Multiplying (or dividing) the numerator and denominator by the same number does not change the value — you are just cutting the same amount into more, smaller pieces.

34 = 68 = 912 = 75100 = 0.75 Whatever you do to the top, do to the bottom

The same amount cut three different ways

Example 1Write 84126 in its lowest terms.
1
Break both numbers into prime factors. 84 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7
126 = 2 × 3 × 3 × 7
2
The HCF is the product of the shared factors: one 2, one 3, one 7. HCF = 2 × 3 × 7 = 42
3
Divide top and bottom by 42. 84 ÷ 42 = 2    126 ÷ 42 = 3
84126 = 23  (both equal 0.6̇ — a good check)
Exam tip If you cannot spot the HCF, keep halving or dividing by 3, 7, 11… until nothing works. Repeated simplifying reaches the same place; it costs only time, not marks.

2 ·  Mixed numbers and improper fractions

Proper fraction

Numerator smaller than denominator — value less than 1, e.g. 38.

Improper fraction

Numerator at least as big as the denominator — top-heavy, e.g. 358.

Mixed number

A whole number and a proper fraction, added: 438 = 4 + 38.

mixed → improper:   abc = (a × c) + bc Multiply the whole by the denominator, add the numerator, keep the denominator
Example 2(a) Write 438 as an improper fraction. (b) Write 476 as a mixed number.
1
(a) There are 8 eighths in every whole, so 4 wholes are 32 eighths. Add the 3 you already have. (4 × 8) + 3 = 35  →  35/8
2
Check with decimals. 35 ÷ 8 = 4.375 = 4 + 0.375  ✓
3
(b) Going back is a division with a remainder: the quotient is the whole number, the remainder is the new numerator. 47 ÷ 6 = 7 remainder 5  →  7 5/6
(a) 358     (b) 756
Watch out 438 means 4 plus 38, not 4 times it — which is exactly why examiners never use mixed numbers inside algebraic expressions.

3 ·  Comparing and ordering fractions

You cannot compare fifths with eighths any more than metres with pounds. Rewrite every fraction over the same denominator — the LCM of the denominators is the smallest one that works — then simply compare the numerators. For just two fractions, cross-multiplying (a × d against c × b) does the same job faster.

Example 3Write 58, 712, 35 and 0.62 in order, smallest first.
1
Find the LCM of 8, 12 and 5. 8 = 2³,  12 = 2² × 3,  5 = 5
LCM = 2³ × 3 × 5 = 120
2
Rewrite each fraction over 120. 5/8 = 75/120  (×15)
7/12 = 70/120  (×10)
3/5 = 72/120  (×24)
3
Turn the decimal into 120ths too. 0.62 × 120 = 74.4  →  0.62 = 74.4/120
4
Order the numerators. 70 < 72 < 74.4 < 75
712, 35, 0.62, 58
Watch out The question asked for the original numbers in order. Writing 70/120, 72/120, … as your final answer loses the accuracy mark — convert back.

4 ·  Decimals and fractions of a quantity

To convert a fraction to a decimal, divide the top by the bottom. To predict what kind of decimal you will get, write the fraction in lowest terms first and factorise the denominator.

Terminating

The denominator's only prime factors are 2 and/or 5 — the primes that build 10.

740, 40 = 2³ × 5 → 0.175

Recurring

The denominator contains any other prime (3, 7, 11, 13…).

512, 12 = 2² × 3 → 0.416̇

To find a fraction of a quantity, remember that of means multiply: divide by the denominator, then multiply by the numerator — dividing first keeps the numbers small.

38 of 240 = 240 ÷ 8 × 3 = 30 × 3 = 90 Divide by the bottom, multiply by the top
Example 4A school has 1260 students. 49 walk to school. Of the remainder, 35 come by bus. How many students come by bus?
1
Find the number who walk: divide by 9, multiply by 4. 1260 ÷ 9 = 140  →  140 × 4 = 560 walk
2
The remainder is everyone else. 1260 − 560 = 700
3
Take 35 of the remainder, not of 1260. 700 ÷ 5 = 140  →  140 × 3 = 420
420 students come by bus
Exam trap “Of the remainder” is the single most common way this question is made harder. Taking 35 of 1260 gives 756 — wrong, and worth no marks.

Practice 4Write each as a decimal and say whether it terminates or recurs: (a) 940  (b) 415
1
(a) 40 = 2³ × 5 — only 2s and 5s, so it terminates. 9 ÷ 40 = 0.225
2
(b) 15 = 3 × 5 contains a 3, so it must recur. 4 ÷ 15 = 0.2666… = 0.26̇
(a) 0.225, terminating   (b) 0.26̇, recurring
Practice 5A bag of ingredients has mass 7.2 kg. 59 of it is flour. Find the mass of the flour and the mass of everything else.
1
Divide by the denominator, multiply by the numerator. 7.2 ÷ 9 = 0.8  →  0.8 × 5 = 4
2
The rest is what is left of the bag. 7.2 − 4 = 3.2
Flour 4 kg; everything else 3.2 kg

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Leaving answers unsimplified Final answers must be in lowest terms, and ordering answers must use the original numbers — writing 70/120, 72/120, … as the final line loses the accuracy mark even when the order is right.
Taking “of the remainder” from the original total When a second fraction is taken “of the remainder”, subtract first. Taking it straight from the original total is the classic trap and scores nothing.
Reading a mixed number as a product 438 means 4 + 38. Treating it as 4 × 38 ruins every calculation that follows.
Exam tip The fraction line is a division sign, so any fraction answer can be checked in seconds: 2/3 = 0.6̇ and 84/126 = 0.6̇ — if the two decimals agree, your simplification is right.
