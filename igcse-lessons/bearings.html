Bearings

From north, clockwise, three figures — master the three rules, find back bearings with a reason, and solve journey problems by accurate drawing or by calculation.

Learning objectives What you will be able to do

  • State and apply the three rules of a bearing: measured from north, clockwise, and written with three figures.
  • Convert compass directions such as south-west into three-figure bearings.
  • Find a back bearing by adding or subtracting 180°, and justify it using co-interior angles between parallel north lines.
  • Find the angle between two directions taken from the same point.
  • Solve a two-leg journey problem by accurate scale drawing, and by calculation when a right angle appears.

1 ·  The three rules of a bearing

A bearing is how navigators, pilots and examiners describe a direction. Everyday words such as “north-east” are not precise enough, so a bearing gives the direction as an angle.

Definition A bearing is an angle that is (1) measured from the north line, (2) measured clockwise, and (3) written with three figures.

All three rules earn marks. A direction of 65 degrees must be written 065°, and a direction of 8 degrees must be written 008°. Bearings run from 000° round to 360°, so a bearing is never negative and never more than 360°.

Two bearings, measured the only legal way

Watch out “The bearing of B from A” means you stand at A, draw the north line at A, and turn clockwise until you face B. The order of the letters decides where the north line goes, and swapping them changes the answer completely.
Example 1Write each of these directions as a three-figure bearing: north, north-east, east, south-east, south, south-west, west, north-west. Also write “a direction of 8 degrees east of north” correctly.
1
Start at north, which is 0°, and add 45° for each step clockwise round the compass. N 000°  NE 045°  E 090°  SE 135°
2
Keep going clockwise past south. S 180°  SW 225°  W 270°  NW 315°
3
For the last part, 8° is only one figure, so pad it to three with leading zeros. 8° → 008°
N 000°, NE 045°, E 090°, SE 135°, S 180°, SW 225°, W 270°, NW 315° — and 008°
Exam tip North can be written 000° or 360°; both are accepted. Everything else must have exactly three figures before the degree sign.

2 ·  Drawing a bearing over 180°

A protractor only measures up to 180°, so bearings split into two cases.

Bearing less than 180°

Put the protractor centre on the point, the zero line along north, and read the clockwise scale directly.

Bearing more than 180°

Measure the smaller angle anticlockwise from north instead, then subtract from 360°.

anticlockwise angle = 360° − bearing
Example 2The bearing of Y from X is 250°. Explain how to draw this accurately with a protractor.
1
Mark X and draw a north line straight up from it. Label it N.
2
250° > 180°, so a single protractor reading clockwise is impossible. Work out the anticlockwise angle instead. 360 − 250 = 110°
3
Place the protractor centre on X with its zero line along the north line, and measure 110° anticlockwise. Mark the point and draw the ray XY.
4
Sanity check the picture: 250° lies between 180° (south) and 270° (west), so the arrow must point into the south-west quarter. It does.
Measure 110° anticlockwise from north at X

3 ·  Back bearings

If you know the bearing of B from A, the bearing of A from B is called the back bearing. It is the direction of the return journey.

back bearing = bearing + 180°   (if the bearing is less than 180°)
back bearing = bearing − 180°   (if the bearing is 180° or more) Choose whichever keeps the answer between 000° and 360°

Why it works: north lines are parallel, and the line joining the two points is a transversal across them. The angle at A between north and AB, and the angle at B between north and BA, are co-interior (allied) angles, so they add up to 180°.

Two parallel north lines and one transversal

Exam tip If a question says “give a reason”, the reason is co-interior angles between parallel lines add to 180° (some books call them allied angles). Naming the angle fact is worth a mark on its own.
Example 3(a) The bearing of B from A is 062°. Find the bearing of A from B. (b) The bearing of Q from P is 205°. Find the bearing of P from Q.
1
(a) 062° is less than 180°, so add 180°. 62 + 180 = 242°
2
Check with a sketch: B is north-east of A, so A must be south-west of B, and 242° is south-west. ✓
3
(b) 205° is more than 180°, so subtract 180° — adding would give 385°, which is not a bearing. 205 − 180 = 25° → 025°
(a) 242°     (b) 025°
Watch out Do not forget the third figure. Writing “25°” instead of “025°” can cost the accuracy mark.

4 ·  Journeys: drawing and calculating

Most bearing questions are really journey questions: go so far on one bearing, then so far on another, and describe where you end up. If the question says by accurate drawing: choose a scale and write it down, draw a north line at the start, measure the first bearing and leg, then draw a fresh north line at the next point — the step candidates forget — before measuring the second bearing. Finally join start to finish, measure, and convert back with the scale.

When it says “calculate” Find the angle inside the triangle from the bearings first — usually via a back bearing — then use Pythagoras and SOHCAHTOA if there is a right angle, or the sine and cosine rules if not. Two bearings taken from the same point simply subtract to give the angle between the directions.
Example 4A ship sails from port P for 8 km on a bearing of 070° to a buoy Q, then 6 km on a bearing of 160° to a lighthouse R. Calculate the distance PR and the bearing of R from P.
1
Find the angle inside the triangle at Q. Start with the back bearing of P from Q. 70 + 180 = 250°
2
Both directions at Q are now measured from the same north line, so subtract. angle PQR = 250 − 160 = 90°
3
A right angle, so use Pythagoras for the distance. PR² = 8² + 6² = 64 + 36 = 100
PR = 10 km
4
For the bearing, first find angle QPR using tan (opposite 6, adjacent 8). tan(QPR) = 6 ÷ 8 = 0.75
QPR = 36.87°
5
R is further clockwise from north at P than Q is, so add. bearing of R from P = 70 + 36.87 = 106.87°
PR = 10 km on a bearing of 106.9° (3 s.f.)
Exam tip Keep the full value in your calculator until the very last line; rounding 36.87° to 37° early can shift the final answer. Measured (drawn) answers are accepted within about ±0.1 cm and ±2°; calculated ones follow the usual 3-significant-figure rule.

Practice Now you try

Do every question on paper first, then open the solution and mark your own working.

Practice 1Write as three-figure bearings: (a) south-west  (b) a turn of 8° clockwise from north  (c) due north.
1
South-west is three 45° steps past east; pad short answers to three figures.
(a) 225°  (b) 008°  (c) 000° (or 360°)
Practice 2Find the back bearing of (a) 073°  (b) 310°  (c) 180°.
1
Add 180° if the bearing is under 180°, subtract otherwise. 73 + 180 = 253  ·  310 − 180 = 130  ·  180 − 180 = 0
(a) 253°  (b) 130°  (c) 000°
Practice 3The bearing of X from Y is 148°. Find the bearing of Y from X.
1
148 < 180, so add. 148 + 180 = 328°
328°
Practice 4From A the bearing of B is 035° and the bearing of C is 290°. Find angle BAC.
1
Both bearings are from the same north line at A. Going clockwise from B round to C is 290 − 35 = 255°
2
That is the reflex side; the angle inside is 360 − 255 = 105°
angle BAC = 105°
Practice 5A boat sails 12 km due east, then 5 km due south. Calculate its distance and bearing from the start.
1
East then south makes a right angle, so use Pythagoras. d = √(12² + 5²) = √169 = 13 km
2
The angle south of east: tan θ = 5 ÷ 12 → θ = 22.6°
3
East is 090°, and the boat has turned further clockwise. bearing = 90 + 22.6 = 112.6°
13 km on a bearing of 112.6° (1 d.p.)

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Swapping “B from A” and “A from B” The letter after “from” is where you stand and where the north line goes. Reversing the letters changes the answer by exactly 180° — a completely different direction.
Dropping the leading zeros A bearing must have three figures: 65° east of north is 065°, and 8° is 008°. Writing two figures can cost the accuracy mark even when the angle is right.
Forgetting the new north line On a two-leg journey, the second bearing is measured from a fresh north line at the second point, parallel to the first. Measuring it from the first north line, or from the previous leg, bends the whole drawing out of shape.
Exam tip Always sketch the compass quarters before finalising an answer: 000–090 is north-east, 090–180 south-east, 180–270 south-west, 270–360 north-west. If your calculated bearing lands in the wrong quarter of your sketch, you added when you should have subtracted.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1Write “due west” as a three-figure bearing.
270° — three quarter-turns clockwise from north.
Exit 2The bearing of B from A is 100°. What is the bearing of A from B?
100 + 180 = 280°.
Exit 3Why does the back-bearing rule “±180°” work?
North lines are parallel, so the two angles at the ends of the line are co-interior and add to 180°.
