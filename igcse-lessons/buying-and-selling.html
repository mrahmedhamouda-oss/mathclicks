1 ·  Profit, loss and percentage profit

Definition The cost price (CP) is what the seller paid. The selling price (SP) is what the customer pays. If SP > CP there is a profit; if SP < CP there is a loss.

A profit of $48 means very different things on a $60 item and on a $6000 item, so profit is almost always converted into a percentage — measured against the cost price unless the question says otherwise.

percentage profit = profitcost price × 100     percentage loss = losscost price × 100 The cost price is the denominator — it is the 100% the change is measured from
Watch out Dividing by the selling price is the most frequent error in this whole topic. You only divide by something else when the question explicitly says so — “as a percentage of the selling price”. Underline that word as you read.
Example 1A shop buys a jacket for $240 and sells it for $288. Find the profit and the percentage profit.
1
Identify the two prices, then subtract for the profit. CP = $240.00, SP = $288.00
288.00 − 240.00 = $48.00
2
Divide by the cost price and multiply by 100. 48 ÷ 240 × 100 = 20
Profit = $48.00, percentage profit = 20%
Exam tip Check with a multiplier: a 20% profit means SP = 1.2 × 240 = 288 ✓. One line, and it confirms both answers.
Example 2A trader buys a scooter for $1450 and later sells it for $1218. Find the percentage loss.
1
The selling price is lower, so this is a loss. 1450 − 1218 = $232.00
2
Divide the loss by the cost price and multiply by 100. 232 ÷ 1450 = 0.16  →  16
Percentage loss = 16%
Note Never write a percentage loss as a negative number — write the size of the change and label it “loss”.

2 ·  Discounts and sale prices

A discount is a reduction from the marked (or list) price. You can find the discount and subtract it, but the multiplier method is one calculator step: subtract the percentage from 100 first, then multiply once. A 30% reduction leaves 70%, so sale price = 0.70 × marked price.

25% off× 0.7515% on× 1.15

Example 3A coat is marked at $85. In a sale it is reduced by 30%. (a) Find the sale price. (b) A second coat has a sale price of $59.50 after the same 30% reduction — find its marked price.
1
(a) A 30% reduction leaves 70%, so the multiplier is 0.7. 0.70 × 85.00 = 59.50
2
(b) Going backwards, divide by the same multiplier. marked price = 59.50 ÷ 0.70 = 85.00
3
Check forwards. 0.70 × 85.00 = 59.50 ✓
(a) $59.50    (b) $85.00
Watch out To reverse a 30% discount you divide by 0.7. Adding 30% back on gives 1.3 × 59.50 = 77.35 — wrong, because the 30% was a percentage of the larger, original price.

3 ·  Sales tax

Definition Sales tax (VAT in many countries) is a percentage added to the price of goods and services. A price is either excluding tax (pre-tax) or including tax — and the tax is always a percentage of the pre-tax price.
price including tax = pre-tax price × (1 + rate100) Multiply going forwards — divide by the same number coming back

Forwards and backwards with 15% tax

Example 4Sales tax is charged at 15%. (a) A table is priced at $240 before tax. Find the price a customer pays. (b) A chair costs $471.50 including tax. Find its pre-tax price and the tax charged.
1
(a) Adding 15% means multiplying by 1.15. 240.00 × 1.15 = 276.00
2
(b) The $471.50 already contains the tax, so it is 115% of the pre-tax price. Divide. 471.50 ÷ 1.15 = 410.00
3
The tax is the difference between the two prices. 471.50 − 410.00 = 61.50
(a) $276.00    (b) pre-tax $410.00, tax $61.50
Watch out Do not take 15% of $471.50 — that gives $70.73 and it is wrong, because the tax is 15% of the pre-tax price, not of the total.

4 ·  Choosing the better value

To compare two packs of different sizes you must compare like with like — reduce both to the same unit.

Price per unit

price ÷ quantity — the smaller answer is the better value.

Quantity per dollar

quantity ÷ price — the larger answer is the better value.

OfferWorkingPrice per litre
1.5 litres for $2.852.85 ÷ 1.5$1.90
2.5 litres for $4.604.60 ÷ 2.5$1.84
Exam tip State the comparison in a full sentence: “the 2.5 litre bottle is better value because it costs $1.84 per litre compared with $1.90 per litre”. The bigger pack is usually better value but not always — do the arithmetic, never assume.

Practice 2A van bought for $2400 is sold for $1872. Find the percentage loss.
1
Loss, then as a percentage of the cost price. 2400 − 1872 = $528.00
528 ÷ 2400 × 100 = 22
Percentage loss = 22%
Practice 3A coat marked at $180 is reduced by 35% in a sale. Find the sale price.
1
A 35% reduction leaves 65%. 0.65 × 180 = 117.00
Sale price = $117.00
Practice 4A restaurant bill is $46 before a 12% service tax is added. Find the total to pay.
1
Adding 12% is a multiplier of 1.12. 1.12 × 46 = 51.52
Total to pay = $51.52
Practice 5Which is better value: 400 g of coffee for $3.60, or 750 g for $6.60? Show your comparison.
1
Bring both to the same unit — price per 100 g. 3.60 ÷ 400 × 100 = $0.90 per 100 g
6.60 ÷ 750 × 100 = $0.88 per 100 g
The 750 g pack is better value — $0.88 per 100 g against $0.90 per 100 g.

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Dividing by the selling price Percentage profit and loss are measured against the cost price — the price the seller paid. Dividing by the selling price is the most frequent error in this whole topic.
Adding a percentage back on to reverse it To undo a percentage change, divide by the multiplier. A sale price of $59.50 after 30% off comes from 59.50 ÷ 0.7, not 1.3 × 59.50.
Taking the tax from the tax-inclusive price Sales tax is a percentage of the pre-tax price. If a price already includes 15% tax, it is 115% of the pre-tax price — divide by 1.15 to strip the tax out.
Exam tip Successive changes multiply: a 20% reduction then 10% tax is × 0.8 × 1.1 = × 0.88, not a 10% reduction overall. And check every percentage answer with its multiplier — one forward multiplication confirms the whole calculation.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1Bought for $320, sold for $448. What is the percentage profit?
128 ÷ 320 × 100 = 40%
Exit 2A price of $644 includes 15% sales tax. What single division finds the pre-tax price?
644 ÷ 1.15 = $560.00
Exit 3Two packs give unit prices of $1.90 and $1.84 per litre. Which is better value?
The $1.84 one — a smaller price per unit is the better value.
