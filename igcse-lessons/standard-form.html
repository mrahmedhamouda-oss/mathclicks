Cambridge IGCSE® Mathematics 0580 Extended Number Lesson

Standard Form

Write very large and very small numbers as a × 10n, then multiply, divide, add and subtract them without miscounting a single zero.

← All lessons Objectives Starter Learn Practice Mistakes Exit check

Learning objectives What you will be able to do

  • Write any number in standard form a × 10n with 1 ≤ a < 10, and convert back to an ordinary number.
  • Multiply and divide numbers in standard form using the index laws, adjusting the front when needed.
  • Add and subtract numbers in standard form by first matching the powers of 10.
  • Order a list of numbers written in standard form by comparing powers first.
  • Enter standard form correctly on a calculator and never copy the display form 4.2E16 as an answer.

Learn Teach · example · together

1 ·  The form a × 10n

Scientists deal with numbers like 4 500 000 000 and 0.000 000 82. Writing all those zeros is slow and easy to miscount, so every number is written in the same compact shape.

Definition A number is in standard form when it is written as a × 10n, where 1 ≤ a < 10 and n is an integer. The front a must have exactly one non-zero digit before the decimal point.

Large numbers: n is positive

  • 4 500 000 = 4.5 × 106
  • 93 000 = 9.3 × 104
  • 6700 = 6.7 × 103

Count how many places the decimal point moves left; that is n.

Small numbers: n is negative

  • 0.00082 = 8.2 × 10−4
  • 0.0000305 = 3.05 × 10−5
  • 0.0205 = 2.05 × 10−2

Count how many places the point moves right; n is that many, negative.

Note A negative power does not mean the number is negative. 8.2 × 10−4 is a small positive number. Only a minus sign in front of a makes the number negative, as in −8.2 × 10−4.
Example 1Write in standard form: (a) 4 500 000  (b) 0.00082  (c) 34 × 105.
1
(a) Move the point until there is one non-zero digit in front of it, and count the places. The number is large, so the point travels left. 4 500 000. → 4.5 is 6 places left, so n = 6
2
(b) Same idea, but this number is small, so the point travels right and the power is negative. 0.00082 → 8.2 is 4 places right, so n = −4
3
(c) 34 is not between 1 and 10, so this is not yet in standard form. Move the point one place left in the front and add 1 to the power to keep the value the same. 34 × 105 = 3.4 × 101 × 105 = 3.4 × 106
(a) 4.5 × 106    (b) 8.2 × 10−4    (c) 3.4 × 106
Exam trap Part (c) is the single most common lost mark in this topic. An answer of 34 × 105 or 0.6 × 10−4 is not in standard form and scores nothing, however good the working above it.

2 ·  Multiplying and dividing

Standard form is built out of powers of 10, so the index laws do all the work. Deal with the fronts and the powers separately.

(a × 10m) × (b × 10n) = (a × b) × 10m+n
(a × 10m) ÷ (b × 10n) = (a ÷ b) × 10m−n Multiply the fronts, add the powers — divide the fronts, subtract the powers
Watch out Subtracting a negative power catches people out every year: 6 − (−3) = 6 + 3 = 9. Write the bracket down rather than trying to do it in your head.
Example 2Work out (6 × 107) × (5 × 10−3), giving your answer in standard form.
1
Separate the fronts from the powers. = (6 × 5) × (107 × 10−3)
2
Multiply the fronts; add the indices. 6 × 5 = 30    7 + (−3) = 4
= 30 × 104
3
30 is not between 1 and 10, so adjust: move the point one place left and add 1 to the index. 30 × 104 = 3 × 101 × 104 = 3 × 105
3 × 105
Check 6 × 107 = 60 000 000 and 5 × 10−3 = 0.005; 60 000 000 × 0.005 = 300 000 = 3 × 105.
Example 3Work out (2.4 × 10−5) ÷ (6 × 103), giving your answer in standard form.
1
Divide the fronts and subtract the indices. 2.4 ÷ 6 = 0.4    −5 − 3 = −8
2
So far this reads 0.4 × 10−8, which is not standard form because 0.4 < 1. 0.4 × 10−8
3
Move the point one place right in the front, so subtract 1 from the index. = 4 × 10−1 × 10−8 = 4 × 10−9
4 × 10−9
Exam tip Point moves left → power goes up; point moves right → power goes down. The value of the whole number never changes.

3 ·  Adding and subtracting

There is no index law for adding, so you cannot add the powers. Either write both numbers with the same power of 10 and add the fronts, or convert to ordinary numbers, add, and convert back.

Watch out 3.6 × 105 + 4.2 × 104 is not 7.8 × 109 and not 7.8 × 105. You may only add the fronts once the powers are identical.
Example 4Work out 3.6 × 105 + 4.2 × 104, giving your answer in standard form.
1
Choose the larger power, 105, and rewrite the other term with it. Moving the point one place left in the front raises the power by 1. 4.2 × 104 = 0.42 × 105
2
Now the powers match, so add the fronts. 3.6 + 0.42 = 4.02
= 4.02 × 105
3
Check that the front is between 1 and 10. It is, so no adjustment is needed. 1 ≤ 4.02 < 10  ✓
4.02 × 105
Note When the fronts add to 10 or more you must adjust: 9.4 × 106 + 8.1 × 106 = 17.5 × 106 = 1.75 × 107.

4 ·  Ordering, and your calculator

Ordering To put numbers in standard form in order, compare the powers first. Only if two powers are equal do you compare the fronts. Remember that 10−5 is smaller than 10−4.

The ×10x key

Modern Casio calculators have a key marked ×10x; older ones call it EXP or EE. To enter 4.2 × 1016 press

4 · 2   [×10x]   1 6

For a negative index use the (−) key, not the subtraction key.

Two habits that cost marks

  • Do not type × 10 as well — the key already means “times ten to the power”, so the answer would be ten times too big.
  • The display may read 4.2E16. Never copy that down — write 4.2 × 1016 in full.
Example 5Write these numbers in order, smallest first: 3.2 × 10−3,   4.1 × 10−4,   2.9 × 10−3,   5 × 10−5.
1
Sort by power first. The most negative power gives the smallest number. 10−5 < 10−4 < 10−3
2
Two numbers share the power 10−3, so compare their fronts. 2.9 < 3.2
3
Confirm with ordinary numbers if you are unsure. 0.00005,  0.00041,  0.0029,  0.0032
5 × 10−5,   4.1 × 10−4,   2.9 × 10−3,   3.2 × 10−3
Watch out Do not be fooled by a large front. 5 × 10−5 has the biggest front here but is by far the smallest number.

Practice Now you try

Do every question on paper first, then open the solution and mark your own working.

Practice 1Write in standard form: (a) 62 000  (b) 0.00509. Then write as ordinary numbers: (c) 8.03 × 104  (d) 2.5 × 10−6.
1
(a) The point moves 4 places left. (b) The first non-zero digit is 5, and the point moves 3 places right. 62 000 = 6.2 × 104    0.00509 = 5.09 × 10−3
2
(c) Move the point 4 places right; (d) move it 6 places left. 8.03 × 104 = 80 300    2.5 × 10−6 = 0.0000025
(a) 6.2 × 104  (b) 5.09 × 10−3  (c) 80 300  (d) 0.0000025
Practice 2Work out (4 × 109) × (7 × 10−4) in standard form.
1
Multiply the fronts; add the indices. 4 × 7 = 28    9 + (−4) = 5  →  28 × 105
2
Adjust the front: 28 is not between 1 and 10. 28 × 105 = 2.8 × 106
2.8 × 106
Practice 3Work out (9.6 × 105) ÷ (3.2 × 10−2) in standard form.
1
Divide the fronts; subtract the indices — write the bracket for the negative. 9.6 ÷ 3.2 = 3    5 − (−2) = 7
3 × 107
Practice 4Work out 5.2 × 10−4 + 6 × 10−5 in standard form.
1
Rewrite the smaller number with the larger power, 10−4. 6 × 10−5 = 0.6 × 10−4
2
Add the fronts now the powers match. 5.2 + 0.6 = 5.8
5.8 × 10−4
Practice 5A rectangle measures 2.5 × 103 m by 4 × 10−1 m. Find its area in standard form.
1
Area = length × width; fronts multiply, indices add. 2.5 × 4 = 10    3 + (−1) = 2  →  10 × 102
2
10 is not between 1 and 10, so adjust. 10 × 102 = 1 × 103

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Leaving the front outside 1 to 10 34 × 105 and 0.4 × 10−8 are not in standard form. Always check the front at the end and adjust: point left → power up, point right → power down.
Adding the powers when adding numbers The index laws only apply to multiplying and dividing. To add or subtract, rewrite both numbers with the same power of 10 first, then combine the fronts.
Reading a negative power as a negative number 8.2 × 10−4 is a small positive number. Only a minus sign in front of the whole thing makes it negative.
Exam tip If a question says “write your answer in standard form”, that is an instruction, not a suggestion — an answer of 300 000 where 3 × 105 was asked for will not gain the final mark. And never copy the calculator display 4.2E16; write it out in full.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1Write 93 000 in standard form.
9.3 × 104
Exit 2Is 0.6 × 104 in standard form? If not, fix it.
No — 0.6 < 1. It equals 6 × 103.
Exit 3Which is larger: 9.9 × 10−4 or 1.1 × 10−3?
1.1 × 10−3 — compare powers first: 10−3 > 10−4 (0.0011 > 0.00099).
