Simplifying Expressions

Recognise like terms, collect them safely, and multiply, divide and cancel algebraic terms to leave an expression in its simplest form.

Learning objectives What you will be able to do

  • Decide whether two terms are like terms — same letters, same powers.
  • Collect like terms, taking the sign in front of each term with it.
  • Multiply terms by multiplying coefficients and adding indices.
  • Divide terms by writing a fraction, cancelling numbers and subtracting indices.
  • Form and simplify perimeter and area expressions from a diagram.

1 ·  Like and unlike terms

Simplifying means writing the same expression with fewer terms. You can only do that when terms are like each other — and the test is stricter than most students think.

Definition Two terms are like terms if they have exactly the same letters raised to exactly the same powers. The coefficients may be different; nothing else may be.
Pair of termsLike?Reason
3x and 7xYessame letter, same power
2ab and 5baYesorder does not matter: ba = ab
4x²y and −x²yYesidentical letter part x²y
3x and 3x²Nosame letter, different power
4x²y and 4xy²Nothe powers are on different letters
6 and 6xNoa constant is not like a term containing a letter
Watch out x²y and xy² look almost identical and are not like terms. Test them with numbers: at x = 3, y = 2 they give 9 × 2 = 18 and 3 × 4 = 12.

2 ·  Collecting like terms

To collect like terms, add or subtract the coefficients and leave the letter part completely unchanged. Think of 3x + 5x as 3 boxes plus 5 boxes: you get 8 boxes, not 8 double-boxes.

3x + 5x = 8x      3x × 5x = 15x² Adding keeps the power; multiplying increases it
Example 1Simplify 7x + 3y − 2x + 5y − 4.
1
Identify the families, keeping each sign with its term. x terms: +7x, −2x    y terms: +3y, +5y    constants: −4
2
Combine the coefficients of each family. 7 − 2 = 5  →  5x     3 + 5 = 8  →  8y
3
Write the answer, constant last. 5x + 8y − 4
5x + 8y − 4
Check it Put x = 1, y = 1 into both: 7 + 3 − 2 + 5 − 4 = 9 and 5 + 8 − 4 = 9. A quick substitution check catches almost every slip.
Example 2Simplify 5a²b − 3ab² + 2a²b + ab² − a²b.
1
Only two families here: a²b and ab². They are not like each other. a²b: +5, +2, −1    ab²: −3, +1
2
ab² on its own means 1 × ab² and −a²b means −1 × a²b. 5 + 2 − 1 = 6     −3 + 1 = −2
6a²b − 2ab²
Check it With a = 2, b = 1: a²b = 4 and ab² = 2, so the original is 20 − 6 + 8 + 2 − 4 = 20, and the answer is 6(4) − 2(2) = 20.

3 ·  Multiplying, dividing and cancelling

Multiplying is a different operation from collecting, and it obeys different rules. Deal with the numbers first, then each letter in turn.

Multiplying

  • Multiply the coefficients.
  • For each letter, add the indices: xa × xb = xa+b.
  • Write letters in alphabetical order.

3a × 4b = 12ab
2x × 5x² = 10x³
−4p × 3pq = −12p²q

Dividing

  • Write it as a fraction.
  • Cancel the numbers using their HCF.
  • For each letter, subtract the indices: xa ÷ xb = xa−b.

10x³2x = 5x²    6ab9b = 2a3

Watch out A power outside a bracket applies to everything inside, the number included. (2x)³ = 2³ × x³ = 8x³, not 2x³. Likewise (3y)² = 9y².
Example 3Simplify (a) 3a × 4b, (b) 2x × 5x², (c) −6m × 4mn, (d) (4t)².
1
(a) Numbers: 3 × 4 = 12. Letters: a × b = ab. 12ab
2
(b) Numbers: 2 × 5 = 10. Letters: x × x² = x³. 10x³
3
(c) Numbers: −6 × 4 = −24. Letters: m × mn = m²n. −24m²n
4
(d) Square the 4 as well as the t. (4t)² = 4²t² = 16t²
(a) 12ab   (b) 10x³   (c) −24m²n   (d) 16t²
Watch out 3a + 4a = 7a but 3a × 4a = 12a². Adding never changes the power; multiplying always does.

An algebraic fraction is simplified by cancelling factors that appear on the top and the bottom. Take the number and each letter separately.

12x³y8xy = 128 × x³x × yy = 32 × x² × 1 = 3x²2 Split the fraction, cancel each part, then recombine
Example 4Simplify fully (a) 15a²b5ab², (b) 4x²y × 3xy³ ÷ 6x²y².
1
(a) Numbers: 15 ÷ 5 = 3. Letters: a² ÷ a = a; for b the larger power is on the bottom, so a b is left underneath. 3ab
2
(b) Do the multiplication first: numbers together, then add the indices for each letter. 4x²y × 3xy³ = 12x³y4
3
Now write the division as a fraction and cancel: 12 ÷ 6 = 2, x³ ÷ x² = x, y4 ÷ y² = y². 12x³y46x²y² = 2xy²
(a) 3ab    (b) 2xy²
Exam tip “Simplify fully” means nothing may be left to cancel. Always check the numbers and every letter before you stop.

4 ·  Perimeter and area expressions

Shape questions are the commonest place simplifying appears in the exam. Add the sides for a perimeter, multiply the sides for an area — then simplify.

A rectangle with a corner square removed

Example 5The diagram shows a rectangle measuring 5x by 2x with a square of side x cut from one corner. Find simplified expressions for (a) the area of the shape, (b) its perimeter.
1
(a) Area of the whole rectangle first — multiply the two sides. 5x × 2x = 10x²
2
Subtract the area of the square, x × x. These are like terms, so they collect. 10x² − x² = 9x²
3
(b) Go round the shape adding all six sides. 4x + x + x + x + 5x + 2x
4
Collect the coefficients: 4 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 5 + 2 = 14. P = 14x
(a) Area = 9x²    (b) Perimeter = 14x
Interesting The perimeter of the original rectangle was 2(5x) + 2(2x) = 14x as well. Cutting a square from a corner removes two sides and replaces them with two equal ones, so the perimeter does not change — but the area certainly does.

Do every question on paper first, then open the solution and mark your own working.

Practice 1Simplify: (a) 8x + 3x − 5x, (b) 4a + 7b − a + 2b, (c) 5m − 3n − 8m + n.
1
Collect each family, signs attached. (a) 8 + 3 − 5 = 6 → 6x
(b) 4a − a = 3a, 7b + 2b = 9b → 3a + 9b
(c) 5m − 8m = −3m, −3n + n = −2n → −3m − 2n
(a) 6x   (b) 3a + 9b   (c) −3m − 2n
Practice 2Simplify 6p² + 4p − 2p² − 9p + 5.
1
p² and p are different powers — separate families. 6p² − 2p² = 4p²    4p − 9p = −5p
4p² − 5p + 5

1
Numbers first, then add indices letter by letter. (a) 20ab
(b) 3 × 2 = 6, x × x³ = x4 → 6x4
(c) 4²t² = 16t²
(a) 20ab   (b) 6x4   (c) 16t²
Practice 4Simplify fully: (a) 12x³y8xy, (b) 18a4b²6a²b5.
1
(a) Numbers 12/8 = 3/2; x³/x = x²; y/y = 1. 3x²2
2
(b) Numbers 18/6 = 3; a4/a² = a²; b²/b5 leaves b³ below. 3a²b³
(a) 3x²2    (b) 3a²b³
Practice 5A rectangle has length 4x + 3 and width 2x − 1. (a) Write a simplified expression for its perimeter. (b) A square has the same perimeter. Write a simplified expression for the length of one side of the square.
1
(a) Two of each side, then collect. P = 2(4x + 3) + 2(2x − 1) = 8x + 6 + 4x − 2 = 12x + 4
2
(b) A square has four equal sides, so divide by 4. (12x + 4) ÷ 4 = 3x + 1
3
Check at x = 2: sides 11 and 3 give P = 28, and 28 ÷ 4 = 7 = 3(2) + 1. ✓
(a) P = 12x + 4    (b) side = 3x + 1

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Collecting unlike terms x²y and xy² are not like terms, and neither are 3x and 3x². Like terms need exactly the same letters to exactly the same powers — only then may the coefficients combine.
Confusing adding with multiplying 3a + 4a = 7a but 3a × 4a = 12a². Adding never changes the power; multiplying always does — add the indices when you multiply, subtract them when you divide.
Forgetting the number inside a bracket power (2x)³ = 8x³, not 2x³ — the power applies to everything inside. And a power of a power multiplies: (b²)³ = b6, not b5.
Exam tip Check any simplification by substituting a small value such as x = 2 into the original and into your answer — they must agree. And when the question says “simplify fully”, check the numbers and every letter before you stop: nothing may be left to cancel.
