1 ·  The method: hunt the triangle, then draw it flat

There is no new mathematics in this topic. Every 3D question reduces to a right-angled triangle you already know how to solve — the only difficulty is seeing it inside the solid and getting its side lengths right.

The routine
  1. Find a right-angled triangle inside the solid that contains what you know and what you want.
  2. Redraw it flat, on its own, as a plain 2D triangle. Mark the right angle.
  3. Fill in every length you can. You will often need a first triangle just to supply a length for the second.
  4. Solve it with Pythagoras (no angles) or SOHCAHTOA (an angle involved).
Watch out Do not try to do the trigonometry on the 3D picture. Lengths in a 3D sketch are distorted, and angles that look like right angles often are not. Once the triangle is on its own, the picture is honest again.

2 ·  The space diagonal of a cuboid

Label a cuboid ABCDEFGH with ABCD the base and E, F, G, H directly above A, B, C, D. The longest straight line inside it runs corner to opposite corner — here AG. Finding it takes two applications of Pythagoras: first the base diagonal, then the vertical triangle standing on it.

AC² = l² + w²     then     AG² = AC² + h²   so   AG = √(l² + w² + h²) Keep AC in squared form so you never round it
Example 1A cuboid measures 8 cm by 6 cm by 5 cm. Find the length of the space diagonal AG.
1
Draw the base triangle ABC flat. It is right angled at B because the base is a rectangle. AC² = 8² + 6² = 64 + 36 = 100
2
Here AC = 10 exactly, but leave it as AC² = 100 to carry forward.
3
Draw triangle ACG flat. The vertical edge CG is perpendicular to the base, so the right angle is at C. AG² = AC² + CG² = 100 + 5² = 125
4
Square root. AG = √125 = 11.1803…
AG = 11.2 cm (3 s.f.)
One-line check √(8² + 6² + 5²) = √125 = 11.18 ✓. The space diagonal must be longer than every edge and every face diagonal.

3 ·  The angle between a line and a plane

Definition Drop a perpendicular from the far end of the line onto the plane. Join the foot of that perpendicular back to where the line meets the plane: that new line is the projection (the shadow) of the original line. The angle you want is between the line and its projection.

For the space diagonal AG and the base, the perpendicular is the vertical edge GC, so the projection is the base diagonal AC — and the angle is found in the same flat triangle ACG you used for the length.

Example 2For the same 8 cm by 6 cm by 5 cm cuboid, find the angle that AG makes with the base ABCD.
1
The angle between AG and the base is the angle inside triangle ACG at A, because AC is the projection of AG. required angle = angle GAC
2
Label relative to θ: CG = 5 is O, AC = 10 is A. O and A → tangent. tan θ = 5 / 10 = 0.5
3
Inverse tangent. θ = tan−1(0.5) = 26.5650…°
θ = 26.6° (1 d.p.)
Exam tip Use tangent with the base diagonal rather than sine with the space diagonal — tangent uses two exact numbers, while sine would need the rounded √125.
Watch out The angle is never measured to an edge of the solid unless that edge happens to be the projection. For AG and the base, the projection is the base diagonal AC — not the edge AB.

4 ·  The angle between two planes: pyramids and cones

Definition Two planes meet in a straight line. From one point on that line, draw a line in each plane perpendicular to the line of intersection. The angle between those two lines is the angle between the planes.

For a square-based pyramid VABCD with apex V above the centre M, keep these two triangles apart:

Slant edge

VA, apex to a corner. Its flat triangle uses half the base diagonal, AM.

Slant height

VN, apex to the midpoint of an edge. Its flat triangle uses half the base side, MN. This is the one the face–base angle lives in.

Example 3A pyramid has a square base of side 10 cm and a vertical height of 12 cm. Find the angle between a sloping face and the base.
1
The face VBC meets the base along BC. Take N, the midpoint of BC. Then NV lies in the face and NM lies in the base, both perpendicular to BC. required angle = angle VNM
2
M is the centre of the square, so MN is half the side. MN = 10 ÷ 2 = 5 cm,   VM = 12 cm
3
Draw triangle VMN flat, right angled at M. VM = 12 is O, MN = 5 is A → tangent. tan θ = 12 / 5 = 2.4
θ = tan−1(2.4) = 67.3801…°
θ = 67.4° (1 d.p.)
Exam tip The slant height comes free from the same triangle: VN = √(12² + 5²) = √169 = 13 cm — the number you need for the surface area of the pyramid.
Example 4A cone has base radius 6 cm and slant height 14 cm. Find (a) its vertical height, (b) the angle the slant height makes with the base.
1
Cut the cone vertically through the apex. The half cross-section is a right-angled triangle: radius along the base, height up the axis, slant height as the hypotenuse. r = 6,  l = 14,  right angle at the centre
2
(a) Two sides known, no angle needed → Pythagoras. The slant height is the longest side, so subtract. h² = 14² − 6² = 196 − 36 = 160
h = √160 = 12.6491…
3
(b) At the base rim the 6 cm radius is A and the 14 cm slant is H → cosine. cos θ = 6 / 14 = 0.428571…
θ = cos−1(0.428571…) = 64.6230…°
(a) h = 12.6 cm    (b) θ = 64.6°

Practice Now you try

Do every question on paper first — draw the flat triangle every time — then open the solution and mark your own working.

Practice 1A cuboid measures 9 cm by 12 cm by 8 cm. Find the length of its space diagonal.
1
Base diagonal first. AC² = 9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225
2
Then the vertical triangle. AG² = 225 + 8² = 289  →  AG = √289 = 17
The space diagonal is exactly 17 cm
Practice 2For that cuboid, find the angle the space diagonal makes with the base.
1
The projection is the base diagonal, AC = √225 = 15 cm.
2
Height 8 is O, AC = 15 is A → tangent. tan θ = 8 / 15  →  θ = tan−1(0.5333…) = 28.0724…°
θ = 28.1° (1 d.p.)
Practice 3A pyramid has a square base of side 8 cm and a vertical height of 15 cm. Find the slant height (apex to the midpoint of a base edge) and the angle between a sloping face and the base.
1
Half the base side is MN = 4 cm. Triangle VMN is right angled at M. VN² = 15² + 4² = 225 + 16 = 241
VN = √241 = 15.5241…
2
The face–base angle sits at N: VM = 15 is O, MN = 4 is A. tan θ = 15 / 4 = 3.75  →  θ = 75.0685…°
slant height = 15.5 cm (3 s.f.);   θ = 75.1°
Practice 4A cone has base radius 9 cm and vertical height 12 cm. Find the angle between a slant height and the base.
1
In the half cross-section the height 12 is O and the radius 9 is A. tan θ = 12 / 9 = 1.3333…  →  θ = 53.1301…°
θ = 53.1° (1 d.p.)
Practice 5For the pyramid in Practice 3, find the length of a slant edge and the angle it makes with the base.
1
The projection of a slant edge is half the base diagonal. AC² = 8² + 8² = 128  →  AM = √128 ÷ 2 = 5.6568…  (AM² = 32)
2
Pythagoras in triangle AMV. VA² = 32 + 15² = 257  →  VA = √257 = 16.0312…
3
Angle at A: VM = 15 is O, AM is A. tan θ = 15 ÷ 5.6568… = 2.6516…  →  θ = 69.3352…°
slant edge = 16.0 cm (3 s.f.);   θ = 69.3°
Compare Slant edge 16.0 cm at 69.3° versus slant height 15.5 cm at 75.1° — edge means corner, face means edge midpoint. Read the question carefully.

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Doing trigonometry on the 3D sketch Lengths in a 3D drawing are distorted and its “right angles” often are not. Always redraw the triangle flat before you calculate — the flat sketch also earns method marks on its own.
Rounding the first triangle’s answer Carry the base diagonal forward in squared form (AC² = 100), or store the full calculator value. Rounding to 3 s.f. mid-way loses accuracy marks in the final answer.
Measuring the angle to the wrong line The angle between a line and a plane uses the line’s projection — for a space diagonal that is the base diagonal, never a base edge. In a pyramid, mixing up the slant edge (to a corner) and the slant height (to an edge midpoint) changes both the length and the angle.
Exam tip Every 3D answer can be sanity-checked: a space diagonal must beat every edge and face diagonal, and an angle of elevation must be between 0° and 90°. Ten seconds of checking catches most slips.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1Write down the one-line formula for the space diagonal of an l × w × h cuboid.
√(l² + w² + h²)
Exit 2What is the projection of the space diagonal AG onto the base ABCD?
The base diagonal AC — and the required angle is angle GAC.
Exit 3A cube has edge 5 cm. How long is its space diagonal?
√(25 + 25 + 25) = √75 = 8.66 cm (3 s.f.)
