Polygons

Naming polygons, the interior and exterior angle rules, finding the number of sides from a single angle, and why only some shapes tessellate.

1 ·  Naming polygons

A polygon is a closed flat shape made only of straight sides. You are expected to know the names up to twelve sides — “the interior angles of a nonagon” must tell you n = 9 immediately.

Regular polygon A polygon is regular when both conditions hold: all sides are the same length and all interior angles are equal. A rhombus has equal sides but unequal angles, and a rectangle has equal angles but unequal sides — neither is regular.

2 ·  The interior angle sum

Pick one vertex of a polygon and join it to every vertex it is not already joined to. The polygon splits into triangles — and because each triangle contributes 180°, that is where the whole rule comes from.

Why the formula works

Sum of interior angles = (n − 2) × 180° n is the number of sides
Example 1Four angles of a pentagon are 100°, 115°, 130° and 90°. Find the fifth angle.
1
Work out the angle sum for n = 5. (5 − 2) × 180° = 3 × 180° = 540°
2
Add the four angles you are given. 100 + 115 + 130 + 90 = 435°
3
Subtract from the total — show this line, it carries the method mark. x = 540 − 435 = 105°
The fifth angle is 105°
Example 2The interior angles of a polygon add up to 2340°. How many sides has it?
1
Set the formula equal to the sum you are given. (n − 2) × 180 = 2340
2
Divide both sides by 180, then add 2. n − 2 = 2340 ÷ 180 = 13  →  n = 15
3
Check by substituting back. (15 − 2) × 180 = 13 × 180 = 2340 ✓
The polygon has 15 sides

3 ·  Exterior angles and regular polygons

Extend one side of a polygon past a vertex. The angle between that extension and the next side is the exterior angle. Interior and exterior angle at the same vertex sit on a straight line, so they add to 180° — and walking once round the shape you turn through a full 360°.

Walk once round the shape and you turn through 360°

Sum of exterior angles = 360° True for every convex polygon, however many sides

Regular polygon: one exterior angle

exterior angle = 360°n

Regular polygon: one interior angle

interior angle = 180° − 360°n
Exam tip Nearly every “how many sides?” question is fastest through the exterior angle, because n = 360 ÷ exterior is a single division. Convert an interior angle to its exterior angle first by subtracting from 180°.
Example 3Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 156°. How many sides does it have?
1
Turn the interior angle into an exterior angle using the straight line. exterior = 180 − 156 = 24°
2
The exterior angles share out 360°. n = 360 ÷ 24 = 15
3
Check. 180 − (360 ÷ 15) = 180 − 24 = 156° ✓
15 sides
Watch out If the division does not give a whole number, no such regular polygon exists — say so. An interior angle of 155° gives 360 ÷ 25 = 14.4, which is impossible.

4 ·  Fitting polygons round a point

Shapes tessellate when copies of them cover the plane with no gaps and no overlaps. At every meeting point the angles must total exactly 360° — that one condition decides everything.

Only three regular polygons tessellate alone The equilateral triangle (6 × 60 = 360), the square (4 × 90 = 360) and the regular hexagon (3 × 120 = 360) — the only ones whose interior angle divides exactly into 360°. But every triangle and every quadrilateral tessellates, even irregular ones, because their angles total 180° and 360°.
Example 4Two regular octagons and one square meet at a point. Show that they fit exactly.
1
Interior angle of a regular octagon. exterior = 360 ÷ 8 = 45°  →  interior = 180 − 45 = 135°
2
A square has interior angle 90°. Add the three angles at the point. 135 + 135 + 90 = 360°
3
The angles total exactly 360°, so there is no gap and no overlap.
135° + 135° + 90° = 360°, so the three shapes tessellate together
Example 5A regular pentagon and a regular hexagon share an edge. Find the angle x between their outer edges at a shared vertex.
1
Interior angle of the regular pentagon. 180 − (360 ÷ 5) = 180 − 72 = 108°
2
Interior angle of the regular hexagon. 180 − (360 ÷ 6) = 180 − 60 = 120°
3
The three angles at the shared vertex go all the way round a point. x + 108 + 120 = 360  →  x = 360 − 228 = 132°
x = 132°
Watch out The angles at that vertex make a full turn (360°), not a straight line. Writing x + 108 + 120 = 180 gives a negative angle — an instant sign that something is wrong.

1
The exterior angles share out 360°. n = 360 ÷ 15 = 24 sides
2
Hexagon sum is 720°; the five given angles total 600°. 720 − 600 = 120°
24 sides; the sixth angle is 120°
Practice 4In a regular polygon each interior angle is 8 times the size of each exterior angle. Find the number of sides.
1
Let the exterior angle be e. Interior and exterior lie on a straight line. e + 8e = 180  →  9e = 180  →  e = 20°
2
Use the exterior angle rule. n = 360 ÷ 20 = 18
3
Check: interior = 180 − 20 = 160°, and 160 ÷ 20 = 8 ✓
18 sides
Practice 5The interior angle of a regular polygon P is 140°. (a) Find the number of sides of P. (b) Two copies of P and one other regular polygon Q fit exactly around a point. Find the number of sides of Q.
1
(a) Exterior angle of P. 180 − 140 = 40°  →  n = 360 ÷ 40 = 9 sides
2
(b) The angle left for Q at the point. 360 − 140 − 140 = 80°
3
An interior angle of 80° means an exterior angle of 100°… but 360 ÷ 100 = 3.6 is not a whole number, so check with the triangle: an equilateral triangle has interior 60°, which does not fit. Test interior 80° directly: exterior = 180 − 80 = 100°  →  n = 360 ÷ 100 = 3.6
4
No regular polygon has interior angle 80°, so no such Q exists — state this conclusion.
(a) 9 sides  (b) no such regular polygon exists, since 360 ÷ (180 − 80) = 3.6 is not a whole number

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Forgetting the “+ 2” After solving n − 2 = 13, writing n = 13 is the single most common error on “how many sides?” questions. Always add the 2 back.
Using 180° where the picture makes a full turn Angles meeting at a point total 360°; angles on a straight line total 180°. Picking the wrong one gives a negative angle — treat that as an alarm bell, not an answer.
Accepting a non-integer number of sides If 360 ÷ exterior is not a whole number, no such regular polygon exists. Say so explicitly — that statement is the mark.
Exam tip Always add your finished angles back up to the required total ((n − 2) × 180°). It costs ten seconds and catches an arithmetic slip that would otherwise lose every later mark.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1What is the sum of the interior angles of a decagon?
(10 − 2) × 180 = 1440°.
Exit 2Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is 30°. How many sides?
360 ÷ 30 = 12 sides — a regular dodecagon.
Exit 3Name the three regular polygons that tessellate on their own.
Equilateral triangle, square and regular hexagon — their interior angles divide exactly into 360°.
