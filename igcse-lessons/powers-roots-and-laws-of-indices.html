Powers, Roots and Laws of Indices

Squares, cubes, higher powers and their roots, plus the index laws that let you simplify and evaluate powers — including negative and fractional indices — without a calculator.

Learning objectives What you will be able to do

  • Recall squares up to 15², cubes up to 10³, and their roots.
  • Apply the index laws for multiplying, dividing and raising a power to a power.
  • Evaluate zero and negative indices, using the reciprocal correctly.
  • Evaluate fractional indices such as 272/3 without a calculator, root first.
  • Solve equations with the unknown in the index by rewriting both sides with the same base.

1 ·  Powers and roots

Definition In 54 the base is 5 and the index is 4: 54 = 5 × 5 × 5 × 5 = 625. Taking the nth root undoes this: if bn = a then b is an nth root of a.

Squares and square roots

1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144, 169, 196, 225

√144 = 12 because 12² = 144. Learn the squares up to 15² = 225, plus 20² = 400 and 25² = 625.

Cubes and cube roots

1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, 343, 512, 729, 1000

∛64 = 4 because 4³ = 64. Cube roots of negatives do exist: ∛(−27) = −3.

Watch out Every positive number has two square roots: x² = 49 gives x = ±7. But the symbol √49 means only the positive one, 7. When you solve an equation write ±; when you evaluate a root sign, do not.
Exam tip Knowing the powers of 2 (2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256) and of 3 (3, 9, 27, 81, 243) turns many index questions into one-line answers, because you can rewrite both sides with the same base.

2 ·  The laws of indices

All the laws come from counting factors: a³ × a² = (a·a·a)(a·a) = a5 — you simply add the number of factors. Every rule below can be checked the same way.

LawRuleWorked example
Multiplyingam × an = am+n2³ × 24 = 27 = 128
Dividingam ÷ an = am−n56 ÷ 54 = 5² = 25
Power of a power(am)n = amn(3²)4 = 38 = 6561
Power of a product(ab)n = anbn(2x)³ = 8x³

Negative indexa−n = 1 ÷ an3−2 = 19
Unit fraction indexa1/n = the nth root of a81/3 = ∛8 = 2
General fraction indexam/n = (n√a)m272/3 = 3² = 9
Watch out The multiplying and dividing laws only work when the bases are the same. 2³ × 3² does not simplify to a single power — it is just 8 × 9 = 72. And am + an has no index law: you cannot add indices when the terms are added.
a³ ÷ a³ = a0  and  a³ ÷ a³ = 1 So a0 must equal 1 for every non-zero a — the same trick explains negative indices
Example 1Simplify (3x4y)² × 2xy³ ÷ (6x³y²).
1
Deal with the bracket first: the index 2 applies to every factor inside, including the 3. (3x4y)² = 3² × x8 × y² = 9x8y²
2
Multiply: numbers with numbers, then add the indices of each letter. 9x8y² × 2xy³ = 18x9y5
3
Divide: divide the numbers, subtract the indices. 18 ÷ 6 = 3,   x9−3 = x6,   y5−2 = y³
3x6y³
Common slip Writing (3x4y)² = 3x8y². The number in front must be squared too.

3 ·  Negative and fractional indices

A negative index means reciprocal, not “negative answer”. A fractional index means root. Put them together and you can evaluate 16−3/4 in your head.

a−n = 1an     (ab)−n = (ba)n     am/n = (n√a)m Flip for a negative index; the denominator is the root, the numerator is the power
Exam tip Always take the root before the power. 272/3 done as (∛27)² = 3² = 9 is easy; done as ∛729 it is not. Both give 9, but only one is doable without a calculator.
Example 2Evaluate 16−3/4, giving your answer as a fraction.
1
The index is negative, so take the reciprocal first and make the index positive. 16−3/4 = 1 ÷ 163/4
2
The denominator 4 says fourth root. 4√16 = 2  (since 24 = 16)
3
Now raise to the power 3. 2³ = 8
16−3/4 = 18
Watch out The answer is ⅛, not −8. A negative index never makes the answer negative.
Example 3Evaluate (254)−3/2.
1
For a fraction, a negative index simply turns it upside down. (25/4)−3/2 = (4/25)3/2
2
Square root the top and the bottom separately. √4 = 2,   √25 = 5  →  2/5
3
Cube the result. (2/5)³ = 8/125
(254)−3/2 = 8125

4 ·  Unknown in the index

Whenever an unknown sits in the index, the first move is always the same: make the bases the same, then equate the indices.

Example 4Solve 4x+1 = 8x−1.
1
Write both sides with the same base — both 4 and 8 are powers of 2. 4 = 2²  and  8 = 2³
2
Use (am)n = amn on each side. (2²)x+1 = 22x+2
(2³)x−1 = 23x−3
3
Same base and equal, so the indices must be equal. 2x + 2 = 3x − 3  →  x = 5
4
Check: 46 = 4096 and 84 = 4096 ✓
x = 5

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Forgetting to raise the coefficient (3x4)² is 9x8, not 3x8. An index outside a bracket applies to every factor inside.
Making a negative index negative 16−3/4 = 18, not −8. A negative index means reciprocal — it never changes the sign of the answer.
Using the laws across different bases or added terms 2³ × 3² is just 72, and am + an has no index law at all. The add-and-subtract rules need the same base and multiplication or division.
Exam tip For a fractional index, take the root before the power: 272/3 = (∛27)² = 9 keeps the numbers small enough to do in your head. And when the unknown is in the index, rewrite both sides with the same base first.
