Using Letters

Letters stand for numbers we do not yet know — learn the notation examiners expect, turn words and diagrams into correct expressions, and name what you have written.

1 ·  Why letters, and how to write them

Arithmetic answers one question at a time; algebra answers every version at once. If a taxi charges a $3 fixed fee plus $2 per kilometre, you could work out the fare for 4 km, then 9 km, then 12 km — or write the rule once:

C = 3 + 2d Cost in dollars for a journey of d kilometres
Definition A variable is a letter standing for a number that can change (d above). A constant is a fixed number (the 3 and the 2). An expression is a collection of numbers and letters joined by +, −, ×, ÷ and powers, with no equals sign.

Algebra also has a house style. Writing 3 × n is not wrong, but no examiner writes it, and multiplication signs are easily mistaken for the letter x.

Instead of…WriteRule
3 × n3nDrop the ×; the number goes in front of the letter
1 × nnA coefficient of 1 is not written
n ÷ 4n4Division is written as a fraction, never with ÷
n × nn²Repeated multiplication becomes a power
b × aabLetters in alphabetical order
2 × (n + 5)2(n + 5)Brackets hold a whole expression together
Watch out 2n and n² are completely different: 2n means n + n while n² means n × n. When n = 5 they give 10 and 25.

2 ·  Turning words into algebra

Most exam algebra begins as a sentence. Sum means add, difference means subtract, product means multiply, quotient means divide — and two phrases reverse the order of the words:

More than / less than

3 more than n → n + 3
3 less than n → n − 3

Order matters

“subtract n from 12” → 12 − n
“n subtract 12” → n − 12

Consecutive numbers

integers: n,  n+1,  n+2
even: 2n,  2n+2,  2n+4
odd: 2n+1,  2n+3

Example 1Write an expression for each: (a) 7 more than x; (b) subtract y from 20; (c) the product of 3 and (m + 2); (d) half of n, then add 5; (e) 4 less than three times t.
1
(a) “More than” means add. x + 7
2
(b) “Subtract y from 20” — 20 is the starting amount. 20 − y
3
(c) Product means multiply, and the whole bracket is multiplied, so keep the bracket. 3(m + 2)
4
(d) Half of n first, then add 5 — only the n is halved. n2 + 5
5
(e) Three times t is 3t; 4 less than that subtracts 4 from it. 3t − 4
(a) x + 7   (b) 20 − y   (c) 3(m + 2)   (d) n2 + 5   (e) 3t − 4
Exam tip Compare (d) with “half of (n add 5)”, which is n + 52. Read to the end of the sentence before you write.

3 ·  Terms, coefficients and constants

An expression is built from terms — the chunks separated by + and − signs. The sign in front belongs to the term.

5x²  −  3x  +  7 Three terms: 5x², −3x and 7

Coefficient

The number multiplying the letter part. Here the coefficient of x² is 5 and the coefficient of x is −3 — take the minus sign with it.

Constant term

A term with no letter in it. Here the constant is 7. Its value never changes, whatever x is.

Example 2For the expression 9y³ − y + 6x − 11, write down (a) the number of terms, (b) the coefficient of y³, (c) the coefficient of y, (d) the constant term.
1
Split at every + or −, keeping each sign with the term that follows it. 9y³  |  −y  |  +6x  |  −11
2
(a) Count them: 4 terms. (b) The number in front of y³ is 9.
3
(c) −y means −1 × y, so the coefficient is −1, not 1.
4
(d) The term with no letter, sign included, is −11.
(a) 4   (b) 9   (c) −1   (d) −11

4 ·  Expressions from diagrams — and what to call the result

Perimeter is the distance all the way round, so you add every side. If a side is not labelled, work it out from the sides that are.

Two shapes with algebraic sides

Example 3Find, in terms of x, the perimeter of the L-shape in the diagram. Then find the perimeter when x = 2.5 cm.
1
Go round the shape once, listing every side in order and missing none. 5x + 2x + 2x + 3x + 3x + 5x
2
Add the coefficients: 5 + 2 + 2 + 3 + 3 + 5 = 20. P = 20x
3
Substitute x = 2.5. P = 20 × 2.5 = 50 cm
P = 20x, and P = 50 cm when x = 2.5
Exam tip Mark each side with a small tick as you use it. Candidates lose marks far more often by missing a side than by adding up wrongly.

Finally, name what you have written — questions use these five words precisely, and “simplify” belongs to an expression while “solve” belongs to an equation.

NameTestExampleWhat you do with it
Expressionno = sign at all4x − 7simplify, expand, factorise, substitute
Equationhas =; true only for particular values4x − 7 = 5solve — here x = 3 only
Formulahas =; a rule linking quantities, with a subjectA = πr²substitute, or rearrange the subject

Example 4State whether each is an expression, an equation, a formula, an identity or an inequality: (a) 7 − 2k; (b) v = u + at; (c) 3(x − 1) ≡ 3x − 3; (d) 5m + 2 = 17; (e) 2y − 1 ≤ 9.
1
(a) No relation symbol of any kind — an expression.
2
(b) A rule connecting four quantities with v as its subject — a formula.
3
(c) Expanding the left gives exactly the right, so it holds for every x — an identity.
4
(d) True only for one value (m = 3), so it can be solved — an equation.
5
(e) Uses ≤, giving a range of solutions — an inequality.
(a) expression   (b) formula   (c) identity   (d) equation   (e) inequality

1
Translate each phrase, watching the order in (b).
(a) t + 9   (b) 30 − k   (c) 5(m − 2)   (d) w3
Practice 2For 7x² − x + 4y − 9 write down (a) the number of terms, (b) the coefficient of x², (c) the coefficient of x, (d) the constant term.
1
Split into terms, signs attached. 7x²  |  −x  |  +4y  |  −9
(a) 4   (b) 7   (c) −1   (d) −9
Practice 3A taxi charges a fixed $3 plus $2 per kilometre. Write a formula for the cost C of a journey of d km, and use it to find the cost of a 7 km journey.
1
Fixed part 3, variable part 2 × d. C = 3 + 2d
2
Substitute d = 7. C = 3 + 2 × 7 = 3 + 14 = 17
C = 3 + 2d, and the 7 km journey costs $17
Practice 4n is an integer. Write down the next two consecutive even numbers after 2n, and show that the sum of the three is a multiple of 6.
1
Even numbers rise in steps of 2. 2n + 2  and  2n + 4
2
Add all three and collect. 2n + (2n + 2) + (2n + 4) = 6n + 6
3
Factorise so the factor of 6 is visible — this earns the “show that” mark. 6n + 6 = 6(n + 1)
Sum = 6(n + 1), which is 6 × an integer, so it is a multiple of 6
Practice 5A rectangle has width x cm and length 3 cm more than twice the width. Write and simplify expressions for (a) the perimeter, (b) the area. (c) Find the perimeter when x = 5.
1
Length = 2x + 3. (a) P = 2x + 2(2x + 3) = 6x + 6
2
(b) Area = width × length. A = x(2x + 3) = 2x² + 3x
3
(c) Substitute x = 5, then check with the actual sides (5 and 13). P = 6(5) + 6 = 36    check: 2(5 + 13) = 36 ✓
(a) P = 6x + 6   (b) A = 2x² + 3x   (c) 36 cm

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Reversing “less than” and “subtract from” The words appear in the opposite order to the algebra. “5 less than k” is k − 5, not 5 − k, and “subtract n from 12” is 12 − n.
Confusing 2n with n² 2n is n + n; n² is n × n. When n = 5 they give 10 and 25 — only at n = 2 do they happen to agree.
Dropping the bracket when subtracting a whole cost Change from $50 for a bill of 4x + 6y is 50 − (4x + 6y) = 50 − 4x − 6y. Writing 50 − 4x + 6y is the classic error — the minus applies to both terms.
Exam tip When a question says “let n be the number of…”, write that sentence down before you start — it earns method marks and stops you confusing two quantities. And if you are unsure of an expression, test it with a small number: for the ages check, s = 10 settles it in seconds.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1Write “3 more than twice n” in algebra.
2n + 3 — double first, then add 3.
Exit 2In 5x² − 3x + 7, what is the coefficient of x?
−3 — the sign in front belongs to the term.
Exit 3Is A = πr² an expression, an equation or a formula?
