Different Types of Numbers

Natural numbers, integers, rationals, irrationals and reals — learn the language and notation of number sets, then use it to classify any number you meet.

Learning objectives What you will be able to do

  • Name the number sets — natural numbers, integers, rationals, irrationals and reals — and use their symbols ℕ, ℤ, ℚ, ℝ.
  • Decide whether any given number is rational or irrational, simplifying roots first.
  • Use the notation ∈, ∉ and ⊂, and the inequality symbols, correctly.
  • Convert any recurring decimal into a fraction in its lowest terms.

1 ·  The number sets

Every number in IGCSE belongs to one or more number sets. Exam questions use the names without explanation — “write down an irrational number between 3 and 4” — so the vocabulary itself earns marks.

SetSymbolWhat it containsExamples
Natural numbersℕCounting numbers, starting at 11, 2, 3, 4, …
IntegersℤPositive and negative whole numbers and 0−3, 0, 2
Rational numbersℚAnything that can be written as a fraction ab of two integers, b ≠ 0¾, −5, 0.25, 0.3̇
Irrational numbers—Cannot be written as a fraction; decimals never stop and never repeat√2, π
Real numbersℝAll rationals and irrationals — every point on the number lineeverything above
Key idea The sets are nested: ℕ ⊂ ℤ ⊂ ℚ ⊂ ℝ. Every natural number is an integer, every integer is rational, every rational is real. Only the irrationals sit outside ℚ.
Example 1From the list −6, 0, √9, √10, 3.75, π, 22/7 write down (a) the integers, (b) the irrational numbers.
1
Simplify anything you can first. √9 = 3  →  an integer, not irrational
2
(a) Integers are whole numbers — positive, negative or zero. −6,  0,  √9
3
(b) Irrationals cannot be written as fractions. 3.75 = 15/4 and 22/7 are fractions, so they are rational. √10  and  π
(a) −6, 0, √9     (b) √10, π
Exam trap 22/7 is only an approximation to π. It is a fraction, so it is rational — π itself is not.

2 ·  Notation you must recognise

Set membership

  • 5 ∈ ℕ — 5 is an element of the naturals
  • −5 ∉ ℕ — −5 is not a natural number
  • ℤ ⊂ ℚ — the integers are a subset of the rationals

Comparing numbers

  • a ≠ b not equal  ·  a ≈ b approximately equal
  • a < b less than  ·  a > b greater than
  • a ≤ b / a ≥ b — less/greater than or equal to
Watch out With negatives the inequality can feel backwards: −7 < −2 is true, because −7 is further left on the number line. Always picture the line.

3 ·  Recurring decimals are rational

A rational number always gives a decimal that either terminates (0.625) or recurs (0.272727… = 0.2̇7̇). The dots sit above the first and last digit of the repeating block — and every recurring decimal can be turned back into a fraction with one piece of algebra.

0.3̇ = 13    0.1̇2̇ = 433    0.4̇5̇ = 511 Recurring decimals are always rational
Example 2Write 0.4̇5̇ as a fraction in its lowest terms.
1
Call the decimal x. x = 0.454545…
2
Two digits repeat, so multiply by 10² = 100. 100x = 45.454545…
3
Subtract — the recurring tails cancel exactly. 100x − x = 45.4545… − 0.4545…
99x = 45
4
Divide and simplify (top and bottom by 9). x = 45/99 = 5/11
0.4̇5̇ = 511
Example 3Write 0.16̇ (only the 6 recurs) as a fraction.
Careful One digit does not recur, so you need two multiplications before subtracting.
1
x = 0.16666…
2
Multiply by 10 to push the non-recurring digit past the point. 10x = 1.6666…
3
Multiply by 100 so the recurring tails line up. 100x = 16.6666…
4
Subtract the smaller from the larger, then simplify. 90x = 15  →  x = 15/90 = 1/6
0.16̇ = 16

4 ·  Special numbers worth knowing by heart

Square numbers

1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144

Cube numbers

1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, 1000

Triangular numbers

1, 3, 6, 10, 15, 21, 28

Reciprocals

The reciprocal of a is 1a; a number times its reciprocal is always 1. Zero has no reciprocal.

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Calling √9 irrational A root is only irrational when the number underneath is not a perfect square. Always simplify the root before you classify it.
Treating 22/7 as π Any fraction of two integers is rational — including 22/7. π is irrational; 22/7 is just an approximation to it.
Saying −9 is a natural number The natural numbers start at 1. Negative numbers are integers, but never natural.
Exam tip When converting a recurring decimal, multiply by 10ⁿ where n is the length of the recurring block — then subtracting always cancels the tail exactly. Show the subtraction line: it is where the method mark lives.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1True or false: every integer is a rational number.
True — any integer k can be written as k/1.
Exit 2Write 0.5̇ as a fraction.
9x = 5, so 0.5̇ = 59
Exit 3Give one number that is real but not rational.
Any irrational, e.g. √2 or π.
