1 ·  The language of the circle

Every circle question begins with a word: chord, sector, tangent, segment. If you misread one of them you solve the wrong problem, so learn this diagram until you can redraw it from memory.

Lines in and around a circle

Arcs and regions

Definition An arc is part of the circumference. A sector is the pizza-slice region between two radii and an arc. A segment is the region between a chord and an arc. A semicircle is half a circle (180°) and a quadrant is a quarter (90°).
Watch out Sector and segment sound alike but are cut differently: a sector is cut by two radii, a segment by a chord. Remember “seCtor has a Centre in it”.

2 ·  Circumference and area

Two formulas do most of the work — and you must know which one is a length (cm) and which is an area (cm²).

Circumference — a length

C = πd = 2πr

Use πd when given the diameter, 2πr when given the radius. Answer in cm, m, km.

Area — a region

A = πr²

Always the radius, and always squared before multiplying by π. Answer in cm², m².

Watch out The single most expensive circle error is putting the diameter into A = πr². If a question says “a circle of diameter 10 cm”, write r = 5 on the page before you touch the calculator.
Example 1A circle has radius 7.5 cm. Find its circumference and its area.
1
Circumference uses 2πr. C = 2 × π × 7.5 = 47.1238… = 47.1 cm (3 s.f.)
2
Area uses πr² — square the radius first. A = π × 7.5² = π × 56.25 = 176.714… = 177 cm² (3 s.f.)
C = 47.1 cm     A = 177 cm²
Note In terms of π these are 15π cm and 56.25π cm².
Example 2(a) A circle has circumference 44 cm. Find its radius and its area. (b) A different circle has area 200 cm². Find its radius.
1
(a) Start from the formula and substitute what you know, then divide by 2π. 2πr = 44  →  r = 44 ÷ (2π) = 7.00281… = 7.00 cm
2
Now the area, using the unrounded radius. A = π × 7.00281…² = 154.056… = 154 cm²
3
(b) Reverse A = πr²: divide by π, then square root. r² = 200 ÷ π = 63.6619…  →  r = √63.6619… = 7.97884…
(a) r = 7.00 cm, A = 154 cm²     (b) r = 7.98 cm
Exam tip Working backwards is “undo in reverse order”: for area, divide by π first, then square root — never square root first.

3 ·  Arcs and sectors: fractions of a circle

A sector of angle θ is simply the fraction θ360 of the whole circle. Work out the whole circle first, then take that fraction — every sector question is that one idea.

arc length = θ360 × 2πr      sector area = θ360 × πr² θ is the angle at the centre, in degrees

Perimeter of a sector

The boundary is the arc plus the two straight radii:

P = arc + 2r

Forgetting the + 2r is the classic dropped mark.

Semicircle and quadrant

Semicircle (θ = 180°): area ½πr², perimeter πr + 2r (the curve plus the diameter).

Quadrant (θ = 90°): area ¼πr², perimeter πr2 + 2r.

Example 3A sector has radius 9 cm and angle 140°. Find its arc length, its area and its perimeter.
1
Write down the fraction of the circle. 140360 = 718
2
Arc length is that fraction of the whole circumference. arc = 140360 × 2 × π × 9 = 21.9911… = 22.0 cm
3
Sector area is the same fraction of the whole area. A = 140360 × π × 9² = 98.9601… = 99.0 cm²
4
Perimeter = arc + the two radii. Use the unrounded arc. P = 21.9911… + 9 + 9 = 39.9911… = 40.0 cm
arc = 22.0 cm    area = 99.0 cm²    perimeter = 40.0 cm
Watch out 22.0 and 40.0 keep the trailing zero — that is what 3 significant figures looks like here.

4 ·  Composite shapes

Exams rarely give you a plain circle. They give you a rectangle with a semicircular end, or a square with a circle cut out. The method never changes:

The three-step method

  1. Split the shape into a rectangle, triangle, semicircle, quadrant … and mark the radius of each curved piece on the diagram.
  2. Add or subtract the areas. A hole is a subtraction.
  3. For a perimeter, walk round the outside and add only the edges you actually travel along. Cut edges that are now inside the shape do not count.
Example 4A shape is a 20 cm by 12 cm rectangle with a semicircle joined to one short end. Find its area and its perimeter.
1
The semicircle sits on the 12 cm end, so its diameter is 12 and its radius is 6. r = 12 ÷ 2 = 6 cm
2
Add the two areas: rectangle plus half of πr². 20 × 12 = 240 cm²
½ × π × 6² = 56.5486… cm²
A = 240 + 56.5486… = 296.548… = 297 cm²
3
Perimeter: walk round. You travel the left end (12), two long sides (20 each) and the curve — but not the dashed join, because it is inside the shape. curve = ½ × 2π × 6 = 18.8495…
P = 12 + 20 + 20 + 18.8495… = 70.8495… = 70.8 cm
Area = 297 cm²     Perimeter = 70.8 cm
Watch out Adding the dashed 12 cm join into the perimeter is the standard trap. Trace the outline with your pencil before you add anything up.

Practice Now you try

Do every question on paper first, then open the solution and mark your own working.

Practice 1A circle has diameter 18 cm. Find its circumference and its area, correct to 3 significant figures.
1
Circumference from the diameter; radius for the area. C = π × 18 = 56.548… = 56.5 cm
r = 9  →  A = π × 81 = 254.469… = 254 cm²
C = 56.5 cm    A = 254 cm²
Practice 2A circle has circumference 62 cm. Find its radius.
1
Reverse C = 2πr. r = 62 ÷ (2π) = 9.8676…
r = 9.87 cm (3 s.f.)
Practice 3A sector has radius 12 cm and angle 45°. Find its arc length, its area and its perimeter.
1
Write the fraction first. 45360 = 18
2
Take that fraction of the circumference and of the area. arc = ⅛ × 2π × 12 = 9.4247… = 9.42 cm
A = ⅛ × π × 144 = 56.548… = 56.5 cm²
3
Perimeter = arc + two radii. P = 9.4247… + 24 = 33.4247… = 33.4 cm
arc = 9.42 cm    area = 56.5 cm²    perimeter = 33.4 cm
Practice 4Find the perimeter of a semicircle of diameter 20 cm.
1
Radius 10. The boundary is the curved half plus the straight diameter. P = π × 10 + 20 = 31.4159… + 20 = 51.4159…
P = 51.4 cm (3 s.f.)
Practice 5A running track is made from a 40 m by 25 m rectangle with a semicircle of diameter 25 m at each end. Find the total area and the total perimeter of the shape.
1
The two semicircles make one full circle of radius 12.5 m. A = 40 × 25 + π × 12.5² = 1000 + 490.87… = 1490.87… = 1490 m² (3 s.f.)
2
Perimeter: the two straight 40 m sides plus one full circumference. The 25 m ends are inside the shape. P = 40 + 40 + 2π × 12.5 = 80 + 78.539… = 158.539… = 159 m
Area = 1490 m²     Perimeter = 159 m

Common mistakes Lose no easy marks

Putting the diameter into A = πr² The area formula wants the radius, squared before multiplying by π. Given a diameter, halve it on paper first — “diameter 10” means write r = 5 before anything else.
Forgetting the straight edges of a sector The perimeter of a sector is arc + 2r, and a semicircle’s perimeter is πr + 2r. The curve alone is only part of the boundary.
Counting internal joins in a composite perimeter When a semicircle is glued to a rectangle, the join line is inside the shape. Trace the outline with a pencil and add only the edges you actually walk along.
Exam tip Use the π key, keep the full value in the calculator, and round once at the very end — to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise. If the answer is wanted “in terms of π”, do not press the π key at all: write 36π.

Exit check Prove it before you leave

Exit 1Write down the formulas for the circumference and the area of a circle.
C = πd = 2πr (a length);   A = πr² (an area).
Exit 2What is the difference between a sector and a segment?
A sector is cut off by two radii (it contains the centre); a segment is cut off by a chord.
Exit 3Find the arc length of a quadrant of radius 6 cm.
¼ × 2π × 6 = 3π ≈ 9.42 cm
